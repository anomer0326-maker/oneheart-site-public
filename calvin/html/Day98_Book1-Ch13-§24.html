Day 98 — Book 1, Chapter 13, §24

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday Calvin handed the sober reader a pocket card. Today he looks up from the desk and says: "Now the enemy." Printed §21 is the hinge of Chapter 13. Everything before it = teaching (to stretch out our hand for the guidance of such as are disposed to learn). Everything after it (printed §§22–29) = defence (the truth … must be vindicated from the calumnies of the ungodly). Servetus, Gentile, the Italian anti-trinitarians — they are coming. But look what Calvin does first. Before he fires one shot, he disarms HIMSELF. Rules of engagement: sobriety, moderation, not one step past the Word. The polemicist starts by fencing in the polemic. That's the lesson. A fight about God that is not fought inside God’s word is already lost — whichever side "wins".

Second — this section is a grammar obstacle course. ⚠️ The first sentence runs 72 words before its main clause arrives (it will be proper here to dispose of …). Two since-clauses, separated by a semicolon, hold you at the door. ⚠️ Two printer's traps: Still, however it will be (missing comma — changes the meaning if you read it literally) and the human minds which has (plural noun, singular verb — a slip). ⚠️ And one sentence where the object sits at the front but belongs to the if-clause: This knowledge, then, if we would leave to God, we must conceive of him ….

Third — comparison is today's weapon. no more modesty … than they are wont to do · more docility than acumen · anywhere but in his sacred word · farther than we have it for our guide · no other quarter than his word · more trouble … than is meet. Six fences, every one built out of than or but. Calvin's whole method in one construction: this far, and no farther.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. The long causal frame: since …; since …, it will be proper here to V (S1) + Hitherto … has been to V …, not to V …; but now … must be p.p. (S2) + Still, however it will be … (S3, printed without the comma). ⚠️ since = because (no time point follows); the semicolon separates two causes inside ONE subordinate frame; it in it will be proper is a dummy subject pointing to to dispose of. In S3 read however, — without the comma, however it will be would mean in whatever way it will be. Formula 1.
  2. Rhetorical questions and concessive machines: how can the human mind[s] … bring down …? (S5) · Nay, how can it … penetrate … while unable …? (S6) · though it is daily presented (S5) · if any where (S4) · how much soever the mystery may be (S13). ⚠️ how can …? = it cannot; the verb after can is always the bare infinitive (bring, penetrate) — however far away it sits. how much soever … may = however much. Formula 2.
  3. Fences of comparison and exclusion: no more X than … (S11) · more X than Y (S12) · more X than is meet (S13) · no other quarter than (S9) · anywhere but in (S12) · farther than we have it for our guide (S12) + the fronted object This knowledge, … if we would leave ∅ to God (S9). ⚠️ no more modesty than they are wont to do = just as little modesty as usual (not "less"); than is meet = than is fitting (than acts like a subject); anywhere but = nowhere except. Formula 3.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 13 sentences –

But since Satan, in order to pluck up our faith by the roots, has always provoked fierce disputes, partly concerning the divine essence of the Son and Spirit, and partly concerning the distinction of persons; since in almost every age he has stirred up impious spirits to vex the orthodox doctors on this head, and is attempting in the present day to kindle a new flame out of the old embers, it will be proper here to dispose of some of these perverse dreams. Hitherto our chief object has been to stretch out our hand for the guidance of such as are disposed to learn, not to war with the stubborn and contentious; but now the truth which was calmly demonstrated must be vindicated from the calumnies of the ungodly. Still, however it will be our principal study to provide a sure footing for those whose ears are open to the word of God. Here, if any where, in considering the hidden mysteries of Scripture, we should speculate soberly and with great moderation, cautiously guarding against allowing either our mind or our tongue to go a step beyond the confines of God’s word. For how can the human minds which has not yet been able to ascertain of what the body of the sun consists, though it is daily presented to the eye, bring down the boundless essence of God to its little measure? Nay, how can it, under its own guidance, penetrate to a knowledge of the substance of God while unable to understand its own? Wherefore, let us willingly leave to God the knowledge of himself. In the words of Hilary (De Trinit. lib. 1), “He alone is a fit witness to himself who is known only by himself.” This knowledge, then, if we would leave to God, we must conceive of him as he has made himself known, and in our inquiries make application to no other quarter than his word. On this subject we have five homilies of Chrysostom against the Anomoei (De Incomprehensit. Dei Natura), in which he endeavoured, but in vain, to check the presumption of the sophists, and curb their garrulity. They showed no more modesty here than they are wont to do in everything else. The very unhappy results of their temerity should be a warning to us to bring more docility than acumen to the discussion of this question, never to attempt to search after God anywhere but in his sacred word, and never to speak or think of him farther than we have it for our guide. But if the distinction of Father, Son, and Spirit, subsisting in the one Godhead (certainly a subject of great difficulty), gives more trouble and annoyance to some intellects than is meet, let us remember that the human mind enters a labyrinth whenever it indulges its curiosity, and thus submit to be guided by the divine oracles, how much soever the mystery may be beyond our reach.

Sentence count: 13 (S1–S13). The spine in one line: Satan attacks the Trinity because it is the root of faith → so Calvin switches from teaching to defending — but for the sake of the teachable → and the defence has one rule: not a step beyond Scripture → because a mind that cannot fathom the sun or itself cannot fathom God → God alone can testify about God, so we know him only as he has revealed himself → the Anomoeans prove what happens otherwise → curiosity is a labyrinth; the Word is the way out.

Examiner's Eye. ⚠️⚠️⚠️ Six traps. (1) S2–S3 — has Calvin abandoned the teachable for the heretics? ⚠️⚠️ NO. S2 shifts the MODE (teaching → vindicating), but S3 keeps the AUDIENCE: it will be our principal study to provide a sure footing for those whose ears are open to the word of God. An option saying from now on Calvin's main purpose is to refute the heretics is a direction flip. The polemic serves the flock. (2) S1 — what is Satan's target? ⚠️ our faith, by the roots. The disputes about the Son, Spirit and Persons are the MEANS; the uprooting of faith is the END (in order to). An option saying Satan's aim was to split the church into sects is off-target. (3) S4 — what exactly is forbidden? ⚠️⚠️ Going a step beyond the confines of God’s word — with mind OR tongue. NOT speculation as such (we should speculate soberly). An option saying Calvin forbids all inquiry into the hidden mysteries of Scripture is scope overreach. in considering the hidden mysteries of Scripture — he assumes we DO consider them. (4) S5–S6 — the logic. ⚠️ Lesser to greater (a fortiori): if not the sun (visible, daily), and not the mind itself — then certainly not God. NOT because the sun is divine or because astronomy is useless. The sun is a measuring stick, not a topic. (5) S10 — did Chrysostom succeed? ⚠️⚠️ NO — he endeavoured, but in vain, to check the presumption of the sophists. Option Chrysostom's homilies silenced the Anomoeans = direction flip. (6) S12–S13 — is Calvin anti-intellectual? ⚠️⚠️ NO. more docility than acumen = a RANKING, not a ban on acumen. And S13: the distinction of Persons is certainly a subject of great difficulty — Calvin concedes it; he does not tell the troubled to stop thinking, but to submit to be guided by the divine oracles. The labyrinth is entered by curiosity, not by thought.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

Where we are. Chapter 13 has two halves. Printed §§1–20 (our §§4–23) = positive teaching: the terms (person, hypostasis), the deity of the Son and Spirit, the distinction, order and relation of the Persons. Printed §§21–29 (our §§24–32) = polemic. Today's section is the hinge — and it announces the hinge in so many words: Hitherto … but now. Tomorrow (printed §22) Calvin names Servetus; after that (printed §§23–29) he answers the newer anti-trinitarians who taught that the Father alone is the "essentiator" and the Son and Spirit derive their essence from him.

a new flame out of the old embers — Geneva in the 1550s. Michael Servetus (De Trinitatis Erroribus, 1531; Christianismi Restitutio, 1553) attacked the Trinity as a philosophical invention; he was condemned and burned at Geneva on 27 October 1553. Within a few years the Italian refugee congregation in Geneva produced new anti-trinitarian voices — Matteo Gribaldi, Giorgio Biandrata, Giovanni Valentino Gentile. In 1558 the Italian church was required to sign a Trinitarian confession; Gentile signed, recanted, fled. So when Calvin writes is attempting in the present day, he is not being rhetorical. The 1559 edition was written in the smoke. Behind that: Calvin's own bruise — in 1537 Pierre Caroli had accused him and Farel of Arianism because they would not subscribe on demand to the Athanasian Creed. Calvin knew from experience that Trinitarian polemic can be weaponised. That helps explain why his rules of engagement (S4, S12) bind himself as much as his opponents.

Hilary of Poitiers (c. 315–367) — S8. The "Athanasius of the West", exiled to Phrygia by the pro-Arian emperor Constantius, wrote De Trinitate (12 books) there. Book 1 is autobiographical and methodological: we must receive what God says of himself, because only God is a competent witness about God. Calvin quotes him not as a proof-text but as a PRINCIPLE: God's self-testimony is the only admissible evidence. The line fits I.7 (the Spirit's testimony) perfectly.

John Chrysostom (c. 347–407) — S10. Preaching at Antioch (c. 386–387), Chrysostom delivered a series On the Incomprehensible Nature of God against the Anomoeans. Their leader Eunomius taught that God's essence is simply "unbegottenness" (agennēsia) — and therefore that the begotten Son must be of a different essence; and he claimed that human beings can know God's essence as God knows it. Chrysostom's reply: even the seraphim cover their faces (Isa 6:2); Paul himself says God dwells in unapproachable light (1 Tim 6:16). Calvin's in vain (S10) is historically fair: the Anomoean party was not argued out of existence; it was outlasted.

The a fortiori from the sun (S5–S6). Calvin's argument is old — Wisdom 9:16 (hardly do we guess aright at things that are upon earth … but the things that are in heaven who hath searched out?), John 3:12 (If I have told you earthly things, and ye believe not, how shall ye believe, if I tell you of heavenly things?). And it links back to I.1.1: knowledge of God and knowledge of self are bound together. S6 turns that into a diagnosis: the mind that does not understand its own substance should not claim to understand God's.

The labyrinth, once more (S13). I.5.12: the mind is itself a kind of labyrinth from which idols pour. I.6.3: the brightness of God's face is a labyrinth to us inextricable, if the Word do not serve us as a thread (Day 30). Today: CURIOSITY is the door in. Calvin will use the image again at the start of the doctrine of predestination (III.21.1–2), with the same rule: go as far as Scripture goes, and stop where it stops. One vocabulary line through all of them: labyrinth (the problem) · guidance/oracles/thread (the solution) · sobriety (the posture).

Reception. The principle He alone is a fit witness to himself — God known only through God — became a cornerstone of Reformed prolegomena (the principium cognoscendi externum = Scripture) and was taken up forcefully by Karl Barth (Church Dogmatics I/1: God is known through God alone). Bavinck (Reformed Dogmatics II) opens the doctrine of God with the same double theme as today's section: God is incomprehensible, yet truly knowable as he reveals himself.

Over-reading guardrails. ⚠️ (1) S4/S12 ≠ anti-intellectualism: Calvin commands us to speculate soberly and ranks acumen below docility — he does not ban it. (2) S13 ≠ agnosticism: the mystery … beyond our reach does not mean God is unknown; S9 says we know him as he has made himself known. Incomprehensible ≠ unknowable. (3) S2 ≠ abandonment of the flock: S3 keeps the teachable as the principal study. (4) S5 ≠ disdain for natural science: the sun is a measuring stick for the mind's limits, not a target. (5) S10–S11 ≠ contempt for argument: Chrysostom's homilies were argument — Calvin praises the effort and blames the listeners. (6) S1 Satan ≠ demonising every questioner: Calvin distinguishes such as are disposed to learn (S2) and some intellects troubled more than is meet (S13) from the stubborn and contentious.

The one-line takeaway. ⚠️ Before the fight about God, the rule for the fight: know God as he has made himself known — not a step less, not a step more.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① 어법 — 밑줄 넷 중 어색한 것 하나

Here, if any where, we should speculate soberly, cautiously guarding against (A) allowing either our mind or our tongue (B) to go a step beyond the confines of God’s word. For how can the human mind, which has not yet been able to ascertain of what the body of the sun (C) consists, though it is daily presented to the eye, (D) brings down the boundless essence of God to its little measure?

✨ 답안 보기 (클릭)정답: (D).

출제 의도. ⚠️ Formula 2(A) — after how can, the main verb is the bare infinitive, however far away: how can the human mind … bring down …? (D) brings agrees with mind as if there were no modal → ✗. Why the others are sound. (A) guard against + gerund ✓. (B) allow + object + to V ✓. (C) ⚠️ the "feels wrong, is right" option — embedded question with fronted preposition keeps statement order and agrees with the body (singular): of what the body of the sun consists ✓ (Formula 2B).

② 내용일치 — 다음 중 §24의 내용과 일치하는 것은?

(1) From this point on, Calvin's principal aim is to refute heretics rather than to help those who are willing to learn. (2) Calvin forbids believers to consider the hidden mysteries of Scripture, since they lie beyond human reach. (3) Chrysostom's five homilies succeeded in silencing the Anomoeans and curbing their presumption. (4) Since the human mind cannot even discover what the sun is made of, or understand its own nature, it cannot reduce God's boundless essence to its own measure.

✨ 답안 보기 (클릭)정답: (4).

출제 의도. (1) ✗ Direction flip. S2 changes the mode, but S3: it will be our principal study to provide a sure footing for those whose ears are open to the word of God. (2) ✗ Scope overreach. S4 assumes consideration (in considering the hidden mysteries of Scripture, we should speculate soberly); what is forbidden is going a step beyond the confines of God’s word. (3) ✗ Direction flip. S10: he endeavoured, but in vain, to check the presumption of the sophists. (4) ✓ S5–S6 — how can the human minds which has not yet been able to ascertain of what the body of the sun consists … bring down the boundless essence of God to its little measure? Nay, how can it … penetrate to a knowledge of the substance of God while unable to understand its own?

③ 영작 — 다음을 오늘의 공식을 적용해 영어로

이 지식을 하나님께 맡기고자 한다면, 우리는 그분이 자신을 알리신 대로 그분을 생각해야 하며, 그분의 말씀 외에는 어디서도 그분을 찾지 말아야 한다. 신비가 아무리 우리 손에 닿지 않더라도 말이다.

Model answer:

This knowledge, if we would leave to God, we must conceive of him as he has made himself known, and never search after him anywhere but in his word, how much soever the mystery may be beyond our reach.

출제 의도. Three checks. ① Formula 3(F) — fronted object with the gap after leave + volitional would. ⚠️ This knowledge, if we would leave it to God is acceptable modern English, but the target is the gap ∅; we must conceive this knowledge ✗ (wrong verb). ② Formula 3(D) — anywhere but = only. ⚠️ anywhere except for his word is fine in meaning; anywhere but his word loses the in (search in the word). ③ Formula 2(E) — how much soever … may be. ⚠️ how much the mystery soever ✗ (soever follows the adj./adv. directly).

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary. since Satan, in order to pluck up our faith by the roots, has always provoked fierce disputes* about the deity of the Son and Spirit and the distinction of Persons, it will be proper here to dispose of some of these perverse dreams; the mode shifts — Hitherto … to stretch out our hand for the guidance of such as are disposed to learn, not to war with the stubborn and contentious; but now the truth … must be vindicated — yet the aim stays pastoral (a sure footing for those whose ears are open); the rule: we should speculate soberly … guarding against allowing either our mind or our tongue to go a step beyond the confines of God’s word, because a mind that cannot tell what the sun is made of, or what it is itself, cannot bring down the boundless essence of God to its little measure; so let us willingly leave to God the knowledge of himself (Hilary: He alone is a fit witness to himself), conceive of him as he has made himself known, bring more docility than acumen, and when the distinction troubles us more … than is meet, remember the human mind enters a labyrinth whenever it indulges its curiosity and submit to be guided by the divine oracles, how much soever the mystery may be beyond our reach*.

The three grammar points, once more. ① The long causal frame — since …; since …, it will be proper here to dispose of (S1: since = because; main verb will be), Hitherto … has been to V, not to V; but now … must be vindicated (S2), Still, however it will be (S3, printed; read however,), and the pair dispose of / be disposed to. ② Rhetorical questions and concessives — how can the human mind[s] … bring down …? (S5: bare verb after can), of what the body of the sun consists (no inversion), Nay, how can it … while unable … (S6), if any where (S4), how much soever … may be (S13). ③ Fences of comparison and exclusion — no other quarter than (S9), no more modesty … than they are wont to do (S11), more docility than acumen, anywhere but, farther than we have it for our guide (S12), more … than is meet (S13), plus the fronted object This knowledge, … if we would leave ∅ to God (S9).

And the structural fact you now own: ⚠️ S1–S3 the declaration of war (two causes → the duty to refute; mode shift; priority kept), S4–S6 the rules of engagement (sobriety; the a fortiori from the sun and the soul), S7–S9 the principle (God alone witnesses to God; know him as he has made himself known; no other source than the Word), S10–S13 the cautionary tale and the exit (Chrysostom vs the Anomoeans; docility over acumen; curiosity = labyrinth; submit to the oracles).

Homework (10 minutes).

  1. Structure restoration (4 min). Rewrite S1 as two plain sentences in base order: Satan has always … . Therefore it will be proper here to … Then underline the main verb of the original sentence and explain why since is causal, not temporal.
  2. Composition (4 min). Write one sentence using Formula 3(A) and 3(C): They showed no more … than …, and gave more … than is meet (topic: a modern debate about God's nature). Check: no more = as little; meet = fitting.
  3. Preview (2 min). Tomorrow (our §25 = printed §22): It were tedious, and to no purpose toilsome, to form a catalogue of the errors by which, in regard to this branch of doctrine, the purity of the faith has been assailed. Expect: (a) It were tedious = it would be tedious (archaic subjunctive — Day 97 S10 were puerile); (b) Arius and Sabellius shut out by the two-part rule (one simple essence + distinct properties); (c) Servetus by name.
