Day 36 — Book 1, Chapter 7, §5

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday Calvin named the thing: the secret testimony of the Spirit. Today he seals the chapter with the word the whole Reformed tradition will build on — except that he never writes the word. He writes its definition and lets you supply the term.

Scripture, carrying its own evidence along with it, deigns not to submit to proofs and arguments

That is αὐτόπιστος — autopistos, self-authenticating, believed on its own account. Nine words. Underline them now.

But here is why §5 is a harder section than §4, and why most readers get it wrong. Look at the shape:

S1–S2    THE FIXED POINT: Scripture does not stoop to proof; it owes its full
         conviction to the Spirit — and we feel assured AS IF we saw God's
         image stamped on it
S3–S4    ⚠️ THE DOUBLE GUARDRAIL: we ask no proofs — BUT this is not credulity
         (not fastening on an unknown object) and NOT superstition
         (we obey WILLINGLY and KNOWINGLY)
S5       Proof text: Isa 43:10 — "that ye may know and believe me"
S6       THE PARADOX SENTENCE: a conviction which asks not for reasons;
         a knowledge which ACCORDS WITH THE HIGHEST REASON
S7–S8    Modesty + deferral (we return to this in Book 3)
S9–S16   THE CHAPTER'S CLOSE: why so few? — taught of the Lord (Isa 54:13),
         the elect only, the word already nigh (Deut 30), and even the elect
         are in the dark UNTIL ingrafted — Isa 53:1 — none but those to whom
         it is given

Two-thirds of this section is Calvin defending himself against the charge his own doctrine invites. If Scripture needs no proof and the Spirit does the sealing, then is faith just a feeling? Is it blind? Calvin saw the objection coming and spent S3, S4, and S6 slamming that door. not like miserable men, whose minds are enslaved by superstition. willingly indeed, and knowingly. a knowledge which accords with the highest reason. Do not let anyone tell you Calvin's doctrine of the Spirit's testimony is fideism. He anticipated you by four hundred and fifty years.

Then the chapter turns sombre. S13 to S16 asks the pastoral question that has been sitting under the whole chapter: if it is this clear, why do so few believe? And Calvin's answer is not "the evidence is weak." It is election, and it comes with a stinger — S14 puts the elect themselves in the general darkness until they are ingrafted. Nobody gets to sit above this sentence.

Today's 3 Big Points — write them down now:

  1. SUCH ... IS ... — complement-fronting inversion, three times, with gapping (S6). Such, then, **is** a conviction...; such, ‹is› a knowledge...; such in fine, ‹is› the conviction... The verb disappears in legs two and three and you must put it back. This is the single most quotable sentence of the chapter and the one most often mis-parsed.
  2. The fronting family — Beveridge front-loads pointers constantly. Object–Subject–Verb: **This, however, we do** (S4), **This singular privilege** God bestows (S10). Fronted participle: **Enlightened by him,** we no longer believe (S2), **carrying** its own evidence along with it (S1). And the nominative absolute returns in its perfect-passive dress: **God having been pleased** to reserve the treasure of intelligence... (S13).
  3. Exceptive but and save — both mean EXCEPT — what is the beginning of true doctrine **but** prompt alacrity to hear? (S11) and none comprehend the mysteries of God **save** those to whom it is given (S16). Two different words, one function, and both of them are trap words of the first rank.

Sixteen sentences. Chapter 7 ends today. Let's go.

1 — Full Text (the whole § — 16 sentences, verbatim) –

5. Let it therefore be held as fixed, that those who are inwardly taught by the Holy Spirit acquiesce implicitly in Scripture; that Scripture, carrying its own evidence along with it, deigns not to submit to proofs and arguments, but owes the full conviction with which we ought to receive it to the testimony of the Spirit. Enlightened by him, we no longer believe, either on our own Judgment or that of others, that the Scriptures are from God; but, in a way superior to human Judgment, feel perfectly assured—as much so as if we beheld the divine image visibly impressed on it—that it came to us, by the instrumentality of men, from the very mouth of God. We ask not for proofs or probabilities on which to rest our Judgment, but we subject our intellect and Judgment to it as too transcendent for us to estimate. This, however, we do, not in the manner in which some are wont to fasten on an unknown object, which, as soon as known, displeases, but because we have a thorough conviction that, in holding it, we hold unassailable truth; not like miserable men, whose minds are enslaved by superstition, but because we feel a divine energy living and breathing in it—an energy by which we are drawn and animated to obey it, willingly indeed, and knowingly, but more vividly and effectually than could be done by human will or knowledge. Hence, God most justly exclaims by the mouth of Isaiah, “Ye are my witnesses, saith the Lord, and my servant whom I have chosen, that ye may know and believe me, and understand that I am he,” (Isa. 43:10). Such, then, is a conviction which asks not for reasons; such, a knowledge which accords with the highest reason, namely knowledge in which the mind rests more firmly and securely than in any reasons; such in fine, the conviction which revelation from heaven alone can produce. I say nothing more than every believer experiences in himself, though my words fall far short of the reality. I do not dwell on this subject at present, because we will return to it again: only let us now understand that the only true faith is that which the Spirit of God seals on our hearts. Nay, the modest and teachable reader will find a sufficient reason in the promise contained in Isaiah, that all the children of the renovated Church “shall be taught of the Lord,” (Isaiah 54:13). This singular privilege God bestows on his elect only, whom he separates from the rest of mankind. For what is the beginning of true doctrine but prompt alacrity to hear the Word of God? And God, by the mouth of Moses, thus demands to be heard: “It is not in heavens that thou shouldest say, Who shall go up for us to heaven, and bring it unto us, that we may hear and do it? But the word is very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth and in thy heart,” (Deut. 30:12, 14). God having been pleased to reserve the treasure of intelligence for his children, no wonder that so much ignorance and stupidity is seen in the generality of mankind. In the generality, I include even those specially chosen, until they are ingrafted into the body of the Church. Isaiah, moreover, while reminding us that the prophetical doctrine would prove incredible not only to strangers, but also to the Jews, who were desirous to be thought of the household of God, subjoins the reason, when he asks, “To whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?” (Isaiah 53:1). Examiner's Eye — the traps they will set:

Trap 1 (S3 + S4 + S6): "asks not for reasons" ≠ "against reason." This is the number-one comprehension trap of §5 and probably of the whole chapter. S3 says We ask not for proofs or probabilities. Left alone, that reads like anti-intellectualism. But S4 immediately rules out credulity (not in the manner in which some are wont to fasten on an unknown object) and superstition (not like miserable men, whose minds are enslaved by superstition), and adds **willingly indeed, and knowingly**; and S6 states the paradox flat out — the conviction asks not for reasons yet is a knowledge which **accords with the highest reason**. An option reading "Calvin holds that faith is contrary to reason and requires the mind to be set aside" is dead on arrival. The claim is about the ground of assurance, not about the rationality of the belief.

Trap 2 (S2): where does no longer believe attach? we no longer believe, **either on our own Judgment or that of others**, that the Scriptures are from God — the negation attaches to the ground (on our own Judgment or that of others), not to the content (that the Scriptures are from God). We believe it more than ever; we no longer believe it on that footing. The distractor will read: "Calvin says the enlightened no longer believe that the Scriptures are from God." Ridiculous when stated baldly — which is why they will bury it in a subordinate clause.

Trap 3 (S2): by the instrumentality of men AND from the very mouth of God. Both, in the same breath, joined by nothing more than a comma. This is dual authorship in eleven words. An option saying "Calvin denies that human agency had any part in the giving of Scripture" is contradicted by half of Calvin's own sentence. Beware also the reverse over-read: "Calvin regards the human writers as the source" — from the very mouth of God says otherwise. Instrument, not source.

Trap 4 (S10 vs. S14): election does not exempt. S10 gives the privilege to his elect only, whom he separates from the rest of mankind. Then S14: In the generality, I include **even those specially chosen**, **until** they are ingrafted into the body of the Church. The elect are, before their ingrafting, in exactly the same darkness as everyone else. An option reading "Calvin holds the elect are marked off from mankind by a native capacity for divine things" has been beheaded by S14. The separation is God's act in time, not a quality in them.

Trap 5 (S11 and S16): but and save = EXCEPT. what is the beginning of true doctrine **but** prompt alacrity to hear = what is it except a prompt readiness to hear? — i.e. it is nothing else. Read but as the ordinary adversative and the sentence turns to mush. Same with none comprehend the mysteries of God **save** those to whom it is given = except those. Neither has anything to do with rescuing or with contrast.

Trap 6 (S9): Nay again, and teachable. Nay is upward revision — more than that, not no. Second day running; they will keep testing it because it keeps working. And teachable = docilis, capable of being taught — a cognitive posture, not a moral compliment. S11 defines it for you: prompt alacrity to hear.

Trap 7 (S15): incredible. = not believable, incapable of being believed. Not "amazing." the prophetical doctrine would prove incredible not only to strangers, but also to the Jews — the doctrine would fail to be believed. Every modern reader stumbles here once.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas (시험 직전 이것만) –

Formula 1 — SUCH + BE + S : 보어 도치 + 연속 gapping

   SUCH  ( , then / in fine )  +  IS / ARE  +  [ 진짜 주어 = 명사구 ]
   = 이런 것이 바로 ~이다        (보어 SUCH 를 문두로 → 주어·동사 도치)

   ▸ SUCH, then, IS  a conviction which asks not for reasons;                (S6)
     SUCH,     ‹is› a knowledge which accords with the highest reason;
     SUCH in fine, ‹is› the conviction which revelation from heaven
                   alone can produce.

   ⚠️ ① 두 번째·세 번째 다리에서 IS 가 통째로 생략된다(gapping).
        쉼표 하나가 사라진 동사의 화석이다. 반드시 복원해서 읽을 것.
   ⚠️ ② SUCH 를 한정사로 읽지 말 것.
        ✗ "such a knowledge" (그런 지식) ← 최다 오답
        ○ "SUCH ‹is› a knowledge" (이런 것이 바로 ~한 지식이다)
   ⚠️ ③ 주어는 SUCH 가 아니라 뒤의 명사구다. 수일치도 그쪽을 따른다.

   ── 같은 가족 ──
   Bright as is the manifestation ...              (I.5.11)   ← Adj + as + BE + S
   Blessed is the man that walketh not ...         (Ps 1:1)

Transformation drill: Write three legs with gapping in legs 2 and 3 — Such is the assurance which asks no argument; such, a certainty which shames every argument; such in fine, the peace which the Spirit alone can seal upon the heart.

Formula 2 — 전치(fronting) 3형제: OSV · 분사 전치 · 독립분사구문

   ① OSV — 목적어 전치 (지시사가 앞으로 나온다)
      ▸ THIS, however, WE DO, not in the manner ...                          (S4)
      ▸ THIS SINGULAR PRIVILEGE  GOD  BESTOWS  on his elect only.           (S10)
      복원:  We do this, however, ... / God bestows this singular privilege ...
      ⚠️ 문두 명사구를 주어로 오인하는 것이 1순위 함정.
         진단법: 동사가 요구하는 '행위자'가 될 수 있는 명사를 찾아라.
         (privilege 가 bestow 할 수는 없다 → God 이 주어)

   ② 분사구 전치 — 주어가 주절과 같을 때 (주어 없음)
      ▸ ENLIGHTENED BY HIM, we no longer believe ...                         (S2)
      ▸ Scripture, CARRYING ITS OWN EVIDENCE ALONG WITH IT, deigns not ...   (S1)
      ▸ Isaiah, WHILE REMINDING US THAT ..., subjoins the reason.            (S15)

   ③ 독립분사구문(nominative absolute) — 분사가 제 주어를 데리고 있을 때
      ▸ GOD HAVING BEEN PLEASED to reserve the treasure of intelligence
        for his children,  ‹it is› no wonder that ...                        (S13)
      = Since God has been pleased to reserve ...

      ⚠️ 여기서 정동사(has been)를 쓰면 즉시 오답 — 콤마 접속(comma splice)이 된다.
         독립분사구문은 종속절이므로 반드시 분사형(having been p.p.)이어야 한다.
      ⚠️ ②와 ③의 차이는 오직 하나: 분사 앞에 제 주어가 있는가.

Transformation drill: Use all three in one paragraph — This the Spirit does, not by overpowering the mind but by opening it. This singular office God reserves to himself alone. And the Spirit having once sealed the word upon the heart, no wonder that argument adds nothing to a certainty already complete.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The word Calvin does not write. Scripture, carrying its own evidence along with it, deigns not to submit to proofs and arguments — that is αὐτόπιστος, autopistos, self-authenticating. Calvin gives the doctrine in §5 and leaves the label to his heirs; by Turretin's Institutio Elencticae Theologiae (I.iv) it is a section heading, and it stays one. The pairing that runs through all later Reformed prolegomena is set right here: Scripture is αὐτόπιστος objectively (it needs nothing outside itself to make it credible), and the testimonium Spiritus Sancti internum works subjectively (it makes us credit what is already credible). ⚠️ Keep those two apart. Conflate them and you get the caricature: "Calvin says the Bible is true because I feel it is." He says the opposite — the evidence is Scripture's own property (S1), and the Spirit supplies the conviction, not the evidence.

Calvin's own footnote — the 1560 French, worth having. Our text carries a note at S1 that is not an editor's: it is Calvin's own addition in the French edition. He writes —

"Car jacoit qu'en sa propre majesté elle ait assez de quoy estre reuerée, neantmoins elle commence lors à nous vrayement toucher, quand elle est scellée en nos coeurs par le Sainct Esprit." — "For though in its own majesty it has enough to command reverence, nevertheless it then begins truly to touch us when it is sealed in our hearts by the Holy Spirit."

⚠️ That sentence is the best one-line gloss on the whole chapter, and it is Calvin's own. Note the structure: though ... in its own majesty ... enough (= αὐτόπιστος, objective) nevertheless ... begins truly to touch US ... when sealed in our hearts (= testimonium, subjective). Objective sufficiency, subjective application. He wrote the distinction into his own vernacular text because he could see readers collapsing it.

Why the anti-fideism guardrails (S3, S4, S6) had to be there. By 1559 Calvin had been attacked from two sides simultaneously for exactly twenty years. Rome said: deny the Church's judgment and you have no criterion left but private fancy — every man his own pope (this is the burden of Eck's Enchiridion and, after 1546, of Trent's fourth session). The Anabaptists and spiritualists — Müntzer's heirs, and closer to home the Libertins spirituels Calvin attacked in a 1545 treatise — said something Calvin found more dangerous because it sounded like his own doctrine: the inner Spirit, the inner word, needs no letter at all. Calvin's §5 answers both in one breath: willingly indeed, and **knowingly** (against the charge of blind fancy) and **a knowledge which accords with the highest reason** (against the charge of irrationalism), while S16's **the same** Spirit-logic from §4 blocks any inner light that runs off from the text. The two guardrails in S4 are not throat-clearing. They are the load-bearing answers to the two live objections of 1559.

Where election comes from, and why here. Readers are often surprised that a chapter on the authority of Scripture ends in election (S10–S16). But follow the logic: if the ground of faith is neither the Church's verdict nor the force of argument, then the difference between the man who believes and the man who does not cannot lie in either — so it must lie in God's giving. **those to whom it is given** (S16, = Matt 13:11) is not an intrusion; it is the only remaining answer. ⚠️ Note the restraint, though: Calvin gives you the fact and explicitly defers the doctrine (we will return to it again, S8 — Book 3). And note S14's brake: election confers no native superiority, since the elect are in the general darkness until they are ingrafted. Anyone who quotes S10 without S14 is quoting half a thought.

Where it goes afterwards. Straight into the confessions again. Belgic Confession 5: we believe these books "without any doubt... not so much because the Church receives and approves them as such, but more especially because the Holy Ghost witnesseth in our hearts that they are from God" — that is S2, nearly word for word. Westminster 1.5 ends with our full persuasion and assurance of the infallible truth and divine authority thereof, is from the inward work of the Holy Spirit bearing witness by and with the Word in our hearts — and note by and with the Word, which is the Westminster divines writing S14's and §4 S16's anti-enthusiasm brake into the creed. Later: Bavinck systematises the αὐτόπιστος / testimonium pair; Barth, CD I/1–I/2, rebuilds self-attestation on very different ground (revelation attesting itself, with a much thinner doctrine of the text); Plantinga re-describes S4's willingly indeed, and knowingly in analytic terms — a belief can be warranted without being inferred from other beliefs, so asks not for reasons and accords with the highest reason are not in tension at all. Plantinga's whole "Reformed epistemology" is, in effect, a 300-page commentary on S6.

⚠️ Guardrail — four over-readings to refuse. 1. "Calvin is a fideist; faith is against reason." S6: a knowledge which **accords with the highest reason**, in which the mind rests more firmly and securely **than in any reasons**. S4: willingly indeed, and **knowingly**. The claim is about the ground of assurance, not about its rationality. And Chapter 8 — thirteen sections of evidences — is next. 2. "The Spirit supplies Scripture's evidence." No: Scripture, **carrying its own evidence along with it** (S1). The evidence is Scripture's own; the full conviction is what is owed to the Spirit. Two nouns, two owners. 3. "So the Church is irrelevant." S14: the elect themselves remain in the general darkness until they are **ingrafted into the body of the Church**. Calvin denies the Church's authority over Scripture (§§1–3) while affirming the Church as the place where the elect are joined in. Book 4.1 makes the Church mother. Both claims, held together — that is the Reformed position, and §5 is where they touch. 4. "Election means the elect are naturally more perceptive." S13's intelligence is **reserved** — kept back and given, not innate — and S14 puts the elect in the generality until God acts. The separation is God's act in time (separates, present tense, S10), not a quality in them.

7 — Scripture Connections –

Four explicit citations (S5, S9, S12, S15), and the closing clause is an unmarked quotation. As always with Calvin, look at the grammar of each proof text, because that is usually what he is after.

  1. S5 — “Ye are my witnesses... that ye may know and believe me, and understand that I am he” ← Isaiah 43:10, explicit. What Calvin wants is the purpose clause: God chose them **that** ye may know and believe me. The knowing is the goal of the choosing, not its precondition. That is S10 arriving five sentences early. ⚠️ Note the Hebrew close: אֲנִי הוּא ʾănî hûʾ, "I am he" — the same formula Jesus takes on his own lips in John 8:24, 8:58, 13:19. And witnesses = עֵדַי, the root of testimony (S1) in every language involved.

  2. S9 — “shall be taught of the Lord” ← Isaiah 54:13, explicit. Hebrew לִמּוּדֵי יְהוָה limmûdê YHWH, "taught-ones of the LORD." ⚠️ Jesus quotes this exact verse in John 6:45 — καὶ ἔσονται πάντες διδακτοὶ θεοῦ — and he quotes it in the middle of the passage that says No man can come to me, except the Father which hath sent me draw him (6:44) and no man can come unto me, except it were given unto him of my Father (6:65). John 6:44–65 is §5 in miniature: taught of God, and given of the Father. Calvin does not cite John 6 here. He does not have to; anyone who knows Isaiah 54:13 in the New Testament is already there.

  3. S12 — “It is not in heavens that thou shouldest say...” ← Deuteronomy 30:12, 14, explicit — with v. 13 deliberately skipped (see S12 note). Paul reuses these same verses in Romans 10:6–8, redirecting them to the word of faith, which we preach, and then — three verses later — quotes Isaiah 53:1 (Lord, who hath believed our report?, Rom 10:16). ⚠️ Calvin's S12 and S15 cite the same two texts, in the same order, that Paul cites in the same paragraph of Romans 10. That is not coincidence. Calvin is walking Paul's own path from the word is near to who has believed?

  4. S15 — “To whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?” ← Isaiah 53:1, explicit. Quoted in John 12:38 and Romans 10:16, and in both New Testament uses the point is unbelief in the face of full evidence — John 12:37: though he had done so many miracles before them, yet they believed not on him. ⚠️ זְרוֹעַ יְהוָה, the arm of the LORD = God's effective power in action (Exod 6:6, Isa 51:9, 52:10). To have it revealed is not to be shown information but to have power land on you. Calvin's exegetical move — that Isaiah subjoins the reason for the unbelief — depends entirely on reading 53:1b as the cause of 53:1a.

  5. S16 — none comprehend the mysteries of God save those to whom it is given ← Matthew 13:11 (ὅτι ὑμῖν δέδοται γνῶναι τὰ μυστήρια τῆς βασιλείας τῶν οὐρανῶν, ἐκείνοις δὲ οὐ δέδοται) and Matthew 19:11 (ἀλλ᾽ οἷς δέδοται). Unmarked — no quotation marks, no reference. ⚠️ Calvin keeps Matthew's divine passive (it is given, agent unnamed) and Matthew's noun (mysteries). Also 1 Corinthians 2:14: ψυχικὸς δὲ ἄνθρωπος οὐ δέχεται τὰ τοῦ πνεύματος τοῦ θεοῦ... οὐ δύναται γνῶναι, ὅτι πνευματικῶς ἀνακρίνεται — "neither can he know them, because they are spiritually discerned." The chapter's last clause is a quotation the reader is expected to finish from memory.

  6. S1, S8 — carrying its own evidence / the Spirit of God seals on our hearts ← 2 Corinthians 1:22 (σφραγισάμενος... τὸν ἀρραβῶνα) and Ephesians 1:13. Yesterday's seal, still working. ⚠️ And note that Calvin's mature definition of faith in III.2.7 will end with the very words of S8: revealed to our minds and sealed upon our hearts by the Holy Spirit. He is laying the phrase down here to pick it up two books later.

  7. S2 — the divine image visibly impressed on it ← 2 Corinthians 3:3 (written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God; not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart) and Hebrews 1:3 (χαρακτὴρ τῆς ὑποστάσεως αὐτοῦ — the stamp of his substance). Also 2 Corinthians 4:6 (God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts, to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God) — ⚠️ which is S2's Enlightened by him and in our hearts in a single verse.

  8. S4 — a divine energy living and breathing in it ← Hebrews 4:12: ζῶν γὰρ ὁ λόγος τοῦ θεοῦ καὶ ἐνεργής — "quick [= living], and powerful." ⚠️ Beveridge's two words living and energy are the Greek's two words ζῶν and ἐνεργής, in order. Also 1 Thessalonians 2:13: τὸν λόγον θεοῦ, ὃς καὶ ἐνεργεῖται ἐν ὑμῖν τοῖς πιστεύουσιν — "which effectually worketh also in you that believe" — which supplies S4's other key word, effectually.

  9. S4 — willingly indeed, and knowingly... drawn and animated ← Philippians 2:13 (θεὸς γάρ ἐστιν ὁ ἐνεργῶν ἐν ὑμῖν καὶ τὸ θέλειν καὶ τὸ ἐνεργεῖν — "it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do") and Psalm 110:3 (Thy people shall be **willing** in the day of thy power). ⚠️ ἐνεργῶν again — the third appearance of that root under this one sentence. Philippians 2:13 is S4's exact logic: God works, and what he works is our willing. Not instead of the will; in it.

  10. S11 — prompt alacrity to hear the Word of God ← Acts 17:11 (the Bereans, οἵτινες ἐδέξαντο τὸν λόγον μετὰ πάσης προθυμίας — "with all readiness of mind") and James 1:19 (ταχὺς εἰς τὸ ἀκοῦσαι, "swift to hear"). ⚠️ And behind both, 1 Samuel 3:9: Speak, LORD; for thy servant heareth. Calvin's prompt alacrity is προθυμία, and Luke uses it to praise readers who searched the scriptures daily — which is to say, the teachable reader of S9 is the Berean.

  11. S14 — ingrafted into the body of the Church ← Romans 11:17–24 (ἐνεκεντρίσθης ἐν αὐτοῖς — "wert graffed in among them") with 1 Corinthians 12:13 (by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body) and Ephesians 4:15–16. ⚠️ Paul's verb is passive throughout Romans 11 and so is Calvin's. And Paul's warning in 11:20 (be not highminded, but fear) is S14's pastoral force exactly.

  12. S15 — desirous to be thought of the household of God ← Ephesians 2:19 (οἰκεῖοι τοῦ θεοῦ) and Romans 9:6 (they are not all Israel, which are of Israel). ⚠️ Romans 9:6 is the doctrine of S15 in six words, and it opens the chapter where Paul works out precisely the problem of S16 — the small number of those who believe.

  13. S3 — too transcendent for us to estimate ← Romans 11:33 (ὡς ἀνεξεραύνητα τὰ κρίματα αὐτοῦ καὶ ἀνεξιχνίαστοι αἱ ὁδοὶ αὐτοῦ — "how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past finding out") and Psalm 139:6 (Such knowledge is too wonderful for me; it is high, I cannot attain unto it). ⚠️ Note that Psalm 139:6 in the KJV is almost Beveridge's sentence: too wonderful / too transcendent, I cannot attain / for us to estimate.

  14. S6 — the conviction which revelation from heaven alone can produce ← Matthew 16:17: **flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee, but my Father which is in heaven** — and Galatians 1:11–12 (οὐδὲ γὰρ ἐγὼ παρὰ ἀνθρώπου παρέλαβον αὐτό... ἀλλὰ δι᾽ ἀποκαλύψεως Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ). ⚠️ Matthew 16:17 is §5's thesis in Jesus's own mouth, and it is spoken to Peter — the very apostle Rome's argument in §§1–3 rested on. Calvin does not point this out. Read §§1–5 as a unit and you will not need him to.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① 어법 — 밑줄 넷 중 어법상 틀린 것은?

Such, then, ⓐ is a conviction which asks not for reasons; such, a knowledge which accords with the highest reason. This singular privilege ⓑ God bestows on his elect only, whom he separates from the rest of mankind. ⓒ God has been pleased to reserve the treasure of intelligence for his children, no wonder that so much ignorance is seen in the generality of mankind. For what is the beginning of true doctrine ⓓ but prompt alacrity to hear the Word of God?

정답: ⓒ

The clause no wonder that so much ignorance is seen... is the main clause (with it is elided). What precedes it must therefore be subordinate — a nominative absolute, which requires the participle: God having been pleased to reserve..., exactly as Calvin writes it in S13. As printed in ⓒ, God has been pleased is a finite verb, giving two main clauses joined by nothing but a comma. Comma splice.

출제 의도 — 셋을 미끼로 깔았다. ⓐ is the complement-fronted inversion of S6 (Such ... is a conviction) — it looks wrong because the subject follows the verb, and it is the most-chosen wrong answer. It is correct: Such is the fronted complement, a conviction... is the subject. ⓑ is OSV from S10 — This singular privilege is the object, God the subject; students who take the fronted noun phrase for the subject will flag it. It is correct. ⓓ is the exceptive but of S11 (= except); students who read it as the adversative will call it a fragment. It is correct. Only ⓒ breaks a rule rather than a habit — and the rule is: 독립분사구문의 동사는 반드시 분사형이어야 한다.

② 내용일치 — §5의 내용과 일치하는 것은?

  1. Calvin teaches that the believer's certainty runs contrary to reason, so that the intellect and the will must be set aside if the Spirit is to seal the word on the heart.
  2. Calvin holds that Scripture carries its own evidence and does not stoop to be proved by argument, yet insists that the resulting conviction is neither credulity nor superstition: the believer obeys willingly and knowingly, and the knowledge accords with the highest reason.
  3. Since God bestows this singular privilege on his elect only, Calvin concludes that the elect are exempt from the ignorance which marks the generality of mankind.
  4. Calvin denies that human agency played any part in the giving of Scripture, since he holds that it came from the very mouth of God.

정답: ②

S1 (carrying its own evidence along with it, deigns not to submit to proofs and arguments) + S4 (not in the manner in which some are wont to fasten on an unknown object / not like miserable men, whose minds are enslaved by superstition / willingly indeed, and knowingly) + S6 (a knowledge which accords with the highest reason). Thesis and both guardrails, in Calvin's own words.

출제 의도 — 네 개의 서로 다른 오독을 한 문제에 심었다. ① 방향 뒤집기 — 이 절 최대의 함정. It takes S3 (We ask not for proofs) and runs it past S4 and S6, which say the opposite about reason. willingly indeed, and **knowingly** explicitly keeps the will and the intellect engaged; accords with the highest reason explicitly denies the contrariety. 3절만 읽고 4·6절을 안 읽으면 반드시 걸린다. ③ S10만 읽고 S14를 안 읽은 경우. In the generality, **I include even those specially chosen**, until they are ingrafted into the body of the Church — the elect are inside the generality until God acts. This distractor is the single most tempting one, because its first clause is a verbatim quotation of S10. 인용이 정확하다고 추론까지 옳은 것은 아니다. ④ 범위 초과 + 접속의 무시. S2 says both: it came to us, **by the instrumentality of men**, **from the very mouth of God**. Calvin affirms human instrumentality and divine origin in one clause. 이 선지는 문장의 절반만 인용한 것이다.

③ 영작 — 오늘의 공식을 적용해 영작하시오.

(a) [Formula 1] "이런 것이 바로 근거를 구하지 않는 확신이며, 이런 것이 어떤 논증보다도 더 든든히 마음이 안식하는 지식이다." — Such ... is ... 도치를 쓰되, 두 번째 다리에서는 동사를 생략할 것. (b) [Formula 2] "이것을 우리는 우리 자신의 판단으로 하지 않는다. 이 특권을 하나님은 자기 자녀에게만 주신다. 그리고 성령께서 이미 말씀을 마음에 인치셨으므로, 어떤 논증도 더할 것이 없는 것은 당연하다." — ①OSV, ②OSV, ③독립분사구문을 각각 한 문장씩. (c) [Formula 3] "참된 순종의 시작이란 하나님의 말씀을 기꺼이 들으려는 마음 말고 무엇이겠는가? 그리고 성령께서 가르치신 자들 외에는 아무도 그렇게 듣지 못한다." — what is X but Y? 와 none ... save ... 를 각각 쓸 것.

모범답안: (a) Such is the conviction which asks not for reasons; such, a knowledge in which the mind rests more firmly than in any argument. (b) This we do not on our own judgment. This privilege God bestows on his children only. And the Spirit having already sealed the word upon our hearts, no wonder that no argument can add anything to it. (c) For what is the beginning of true obedience but a willing readiness to hear the Word of God? And none so hear it save those whom the Spirit has taught.

채점 포인트. (a) 두 번째 다리에 is를 다시 쓰면 감점 — 오늘 공식의 핵심은 gapping이다. 그리고 Such a conviction is...로 쓰면 0점: such가 한정사가 되어 도치가 사라진다. (b) ①②는 반드시 목적어를 문두에 둘 것 (This we do... / This privilege God bestows...) — 정상 어순으로 쓰면 공식 미적용. ③은 반드시 분사형 (the Spirit having sealed) — the Spirit has sealed로 쓰면 콤마 접속이 되어 즉시 0점. no wonder that의 it is 생략은 유지해도 좋고 복원해도 좋다. (c) but과 save를 역접·구원으로 오해한 답안(예: but we need also..., except we are saved...)은 즉시 0점. 두 단어 모두 except의 자리에 있어야 한다. what is X but Y?는 의문부호까지 살릴 것 — 수사의문이 곧 단언이다.

One-line summary. Chapter 7 closes by fixing the point it has been driving at since §1: those who are inwardly taught by the Holy Spirit acquiesce implicitly in Scripture, because Scripture, carrying its own evidence along with it, deigns not to submit to proofs and arguments, but owes the full conviction with which we ought to receive it to the testimony of the Spirit (αὐτόπιστος + testimonium, objective sufficiency and subjective sealing) — so that we feel perfectly assured—as much so as if we beheld the divine image visibly impressed on it—that it came to us, **by the instrumentality of men, from the very mouth of God**; and lest anyone call this credulity or superstition, Calvin bolts on both guardrails (not... to fasten on an unknown object / not like miserable men, whose minds are enslaved by superstition) and the three words that decide the whole debate, **willingly indeed, and knowingly**, yielding the paradox of S6 — a conviction which asks not for reasons; ... a knowledge which accords with the highest reason — after which the chapter turns pastoral and asks why so few believe: the children of the renovated Church shall be taught of the Lord, this singular privilege God bestows on his elect only, God having been pleased to reserve the treasure of intelligence for his children — yet **I include even those specially chosen, until they are ingrafted into the body of the Church** — and Isaiah, seeing the doctrine prove incredible not only to strangers, but also to the Jews, subjoins the reason: To whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed? So: **none comprehend the mysteries of God save those to whom it is given.**

And the three grammar points, once more:

  1. SUCH + BE + S (S6) — complement fronted, subject and verb inverted, and the verb gapped in legs two and three. Such is never the subject and never a determiner here. Restore the is and the sentence turns simple.
  2. The fronting family — OSV (This, however, we do S4; This singular privilege God bestows S10: the fronted noun phrase is the object); fronted participle (Enlightened by him S2; carrying its own evidence S1; while reminding us S15); nominative absolute with its own subject and an obligatory participle (God having been pleased... S13 — a finite verb here is a comma splice).
  3. Exceptive but and save = EXCEPT — what is the beginning of true doctrine **but** prompt alacrity to hear? (S11, rhetorical question = strong assertion) and none comprehend... **save** those to whom it is given (S16). Plus the running old-style negation with no do and no inversion: deigns not, ask not, asks not.
