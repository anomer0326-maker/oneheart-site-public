Day 84 — Book 1, Chapter 13, §10

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday the Son was proved to bear God's names. Today he is proved to have appeared — and to have been worshipped. That is the step up, and you must see why it is a step up or the section will read like more of the same. A name can be delegated. An ambassador speaks in the king's name; a prophet says thus saith the Lord; Moses was a God to Pharaoh. So the Jewish reader has one last move left after §9: the Angel bore the name in virtue of his office. Calvin lets that move be made — S3, Should it be alleged that this is done in respect of the office which he bears — and then answers it with something that cannot be delegated: sacrifice. No servant would rob God of his honour, by allowing sacrifice to be offered to himself. (S4) A servant may carry the king's name; no servant may take the king's worship. The Angel of Judges 13 does exactly that — and the whole first half of the section (S1–S10) is that one syllogism worked out on Manoah's altar. Learn the syllogism and you have the section.

Then a name appears that has been waiting since §4: Servetus — Hence the impiety of Servetus was the more detestable (S11). ⚠️ This is the first time Calvin names him in the body of the Institutes. The synopsis mentioned him; §4 alluded to we of the present day; today he is on the page. And what Servetus said tells you what the Angel argument is for: God was never manifested to Abraham and the Patriarchs, but … an angel was worshipped in his stead (S11); God personated an angel (S16). If the Old Testament theophanies were creatures playing God, then there is no pre-existent Son to be found in them. So the second half of the section (S11–S19) argues the positive thesis of the orthodox doctors: the Word of God was the supreme angel, who then began, as it were by anticipation, to perform the office of Mediator (S12). ⚠️⚠️ Read the hedges. as it were. by anticipation. as in an intermediate form. not clothed with flesh. a type of the office. Calvin is walking a line: the Angel is truly Jehovah (against the Jews and Servetus) and is not yet the incarnate Mediator (against anyone who would put the incarnation under the Law). The examiner's overreach option lives in that gap. Then a coda (S20–S26): Zechariah's angel who is also the Lord of hosts; and two texts Calvin says he is passing over — Isaiah 25:9 and Malachi 3:1 — because they may not have much weight with the Jews (S21). ⚠️ That is a rhetorical figure, paralipsis: "I omit X — for instance, X." The two texts he "omits" are the two he closes on.

Grammatically, today is the day of conditions and concessions. Three forms of the conditional in one section: the inverted **Should** it be alleged … (S3), the present-subjunctive **if** … **be** candidly considered (S20), and — inside a quotation — the past counterfactual **If** Jehovah **had wished** to slay us, he **would not have received** the sacrifice (S9). Four concessions: **though** he **were** not clothed with flesh, **yet** … (S13), **still, however** (S14), **Although** … had not yet arrived (S19), **though** they **may not** have much weight (S21) — and a the + comparative that hangs on a when-clause (S11). And three sentences with no main verb or with the verb before the subject: **Hence** Manoah's exclamation, … (S8), **Hence also** Paul's statement, **that** … (S18), **Still clearer and stronger is** the passage of Malachi (S24).

The section straddles a page-break, and the seam falls inside S3 — between by no and means solved, where a margin number 118, the page number 122, and the running header are all dropped. Today's 3 Big Points

  1. Three conditionals, three forms — the inverted Should, the subjunctive be, and the past counterfactual. **Should it be alleged** that this is done in respect of the office which he bears, the difficulty is by no means solved. (S3) ⚠️⚠️ No if. The auxiliary should is fronted and the subject follows it — Should it be alleged = If it should be alleged = Suppose someone objects that …. You met this on Day 12. It is not the should of obligation; it marks a hypothetical the speaker is entertaining in order to dismiss it, and by no means solved dismisses it. Then S20: if the first chapter of Zechariah … and the second … **be** candidly considered, it will be seen that … ⚠️ be, not is — the present subjunctive in an open condition, formal period English. Do not "correct" it. And S9, inside Manoah's wife's mouth: **If** Jehovah **had wished** to slay us, he **would not have received** the sacrifice at our hand — the standard past counterfactual, and ⚠️ it is an argument: the sacrifice was received, therefore he did not wish to slay them — modus tollens in a peasant woman's sentence. Three shapes, one section. Formula 1.
  2. Concession stacked four ways, plus the more … when …. For **though** he **were** not clothed with flesh, **yet** he descended as in an intermediate form, **that** he **might** have more familiar access to the faithful. (S13) ⚠️⚠️⚠️ Three things. though … yet — the correlative pair you met at §9 S1, yet = nevertheless, not still now. he **were** — the subjunctive after though: a concession Calvin is treating as counter-to-expectation (though he was not, as one might suppose he had to be, clothed with flesh); it is not a past-plural error. And the tail **that** he **might** have = purpose, in order that he might (§7 S9's unless that … might). Then S14 doubles the adverb — **still, however**, he retained the character — S19 opens **Although** the time of humiliation **had not yet** arrived, S21 concedes **though** they **may not** have much weight with the Jews. ⚠️ And S11 gives you **the more** detestable, **when** he maintained … = all the more detestable, given that he maintained — the + comparative with the measure supplied by the when-clause — the old instrumental the (by that much more), the same the as in the more, the merrier, with its second half replaced by a when. Concession is the grammar of a man drawing a line: this much I grant, and no more. Formula 2.
  3. Sentences without a verb, and a verb before its subject. **Hence** Manoah's exclamation, “We shall die; for we have seen the Lord.” (S8) **Hence also** Paul's statement, **that** Christ led the people in the wilderness (S18). ⚠️⚠️ Neither sentence has a finite verb. Hence + NP = from this comes / hence arises — the verb is elided, and the noun phrase (exclamation, statement) carries the quotation or the that-clause as its content. Restore: Hence [arises] Manoah's exclamation. Then S24: **Still clearer and stronger is** the passage of Malachi — the comparative predicate is fronted, so the subject comes after is. ⚠️ Agreement follows the subject that follows the verb: is because the passage is singular. And S1's I **know not what** cavils will enable them to evade — the do-less negative on an ordinary verb (§9 S15 sees not) with an embedded question as its object; ⚠️ it is a rhetorical certainty — there are no cavils that will. When the verb is missing, restore it; when the verb is first, find the subject behind it. Formula 3.

Trap words I will test. cavils (S1) ⚠️⚠️ = captious, frivolous objections (Lat. cavillari, to jeer, to quibble) — the noun cousin of yesterday's verb quibble; what cavils will enable them to evade = what quibbles will let them dodge. in respect of (S3) = with regard to, on account of — in respect of the office which he bears = because of the office he holds. Not "respect." bears (S3) = holds, carries — of an office: bear office. by no means (S3) = not at all — a whole-clause negation, not "by no method." rob X of Y (S4) = deprive X of Y — Day 82's rob him of his eternity, back again with honour. sacrifice (S4, S5, S9) = the burnt offering of Judges 13:16–20 — a cultic act reserved to God; that reservation is the whole argument. infer from the sign (S7) = conclude from the miracle — sign = the angel ascending in the flame. previously (S9) ⚠️ = earlier in the narrative — he who is previously called an angel = the one the text has been calling an angel until now. Not "formerly in time." impiety (S11) = ungodliness, sacrilege (in- + pius) — Calvin's heaviest noun for Servetus. maintained (S11) ⚠️ = asserted, held as a position — not "kept up." manifested (S11) = shown, made visible — Day 81/82's word (not yet manifested). in his stead (S11) = in his place, instead of him. doctors (S12) ⚠️⚠️ = teachers (Lat. doctor < docere) — the orthodox doctors of the Church = the orthodox teachers, the Fathers. No physicians. expounded (S12) = explained, set forth as an interpretation. by anticipation (S12) ⚠️ = ahead of the appointed time, as a foretaste — the office was previewed, not begun in full. intermediate form (S13) = a form between — between pure Godhead and flesh; note as in. familiar access (S13) = closer, more intimate approach — familiar = intimate, not "well-known." intercourse (S14) ⚠️⚠️⚠️ = dealings, communication, converse (inter- + currere, to run between) — this closer intercourse = this closer communication with men. The period sense; the modern narrowing is nowhere in view. procured for him (S14) = obtained for him, won him — procured for him the name of the Angel = earned him the title "Angel". character (S14) ⚠️⚠️ = distinctive nature, stamp (Gk χαρακτήρ, an engraved mark — Hebrews 1:3) — the character which justly belonged to him—that of the God of ineffable glory = the nature that was rightly his, namely that of the God of unspeakable glory. Not "personality." ineffable (S14) = unspeakable — yesterday it was the name; today it is the glory. intimated (S15) = hinted, signified — Day 82's verb. memorial (S15) ⚠️⚠️ = the name by which one is remembered (Heb. zeker, Exod 3:15) — the Lord is his memorial = YHWH is the name he is remembered by. Not a monument. insinuates (S16) = slips in the suggestion, hints underhandedly (in- + sinus, a fold) — Calvin's verb for Servetus's method. personated (S16) ⚠️⚠️ = played the part of, impersonated (persona, a mask — §6's word, now as a verb) — God personated an angel = God put on an angel as a mask. That is Servetus's claim, and Calvin's noun person is being turned against him. confession (S17) = declaration, avowal — Jacob's I have seen God; not a confession of sin. fulness of the Godhead (S17) = the whole of deity (Col 2:9). type (S19) ⚠️⚠️ = a prefiguration, a foreshadowing pattern (Gk τύπος, the impress of a stamp) — exhibited a type of the office = displayed a foreshadowing of the office. Not "kind." was to fulfil (S19) = was destined to fulfil — be to + infinitive of destiny. candidly (S20) ⚠️ = fairly, without prejudice (candidus, white) — be candidly considered = be read in good faith. Not "frankly." ascribed to him (S20) = attributed to him. numberless (S21) = countless. rests secure (S21) = stands on safe ground. have much weight with X (S21) = carry much force in X's eyes. precludes the idea that (S23) = rules out the possibility that (prae- + claudere, to shut beforehand). emphatic (S23) = stressed, marked — the emphatic description, twice repeated = the pointed wording, said twice. claims it for Christ (S25) = assigns it to Christ as his.

10. But if this does not satisfy the Jews, I know not what cavils will enable them to evade the numerous passages in which Jehovah is said to have appeared in the form of an Angel (Judges 6:7; 13:16-23, &c). This Angel claims for himself the name of the Eternal God. Should it be alleged that this is done in respect of the office which he bears, the difficulty is by no means solved. No servant would rob God of his honour, by allowing sacrifice to be offered to himself. But the Angel, by refusing to eat bread, orders the sacrifice due to Jehovah to be offered to him. Thus the fact itself proves that he was truly Jehovah. Accordingly, Manoah and his wife infer from the sign, that they had seen not only an angel, but God. Hence Manoah’s exclamation, “We shall die; for we have seen the Lord.” When the woman replies, “If Jehovah had wished to slay us, he would not have received the sacrifice at our hand,” she acknowledges that he who is previously called an angel was certainly God. We may add, that the angel’s own reply removes all doubt, “Why do ye ask my name, which is wonderful?” Hence the impiety of Servetus was the more detestable, when he maintained that God was never manifested to Abraham and the Patriarchs, but that an angel was worshipped in his stead. The orthodox doctors of the Church have correctly and wisely expounded, that the Word of God was the supreme angel, who then began, as it were by anticipation, to perform the office of Mediator. For though he were not clothed with flesh, yet he descended as in an intermediate form, that he might have more familiar access to the faithful. This closer intercourse procured for him the name of the Angel; still, however, he retained the character which justly belonged to him—that of the God of ineffable glory. The same thing is intimated by Hosea, who, after mentioning the wrestling of Jacob with the angel, says, “Even the Lord God of hosts; the Lord is his memorial,” (Hosea 12:5). Servetus again insinuates that God personated an angel; as if the prophet did not confirm what had been said by Moses, “Wherefore is it that thou dost ask after my name?” (Gen. 32:29, 30). And the confession of the holy Patriarch sufficiently declares that he was not a created angel, but one in whom the fulness of the Godhead dwelt, when he says, “I have seen God face to face.” Hence also Paul’s statement, that Christ led the people in the wilderness (1 Cor. 10:4. See also Calvin on Acts 7:30, and infra, chap. 14, s. 9). Although the time of humiliation had not yet arrived, the eternal Word exhibited a type of the office which he was to fulfil. Again, if the first chapter of Zechariah (ver. 9, &c). and the second (ver. 3, &c). be candidly considered, it will be seen that the angel who sends the other angel is immediately after declared to be the Lord of hosts, and that supreme power is ascribed to him. I omit numberless passages in which our faith rests secure, though they may not have much weight with the Jews. For when it is said in Isaiah, “Lo, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us; this is the Lord: we have waited for him, we will be glad and rejoice in his salvation,” (Isa. 25:9), even the blind may see that the God referred to is he who again rises up for the deliverance of his people. And the emphatic description, twice repeated, precludes the idea that reference is made to any other than to Christ. Still clearer and stronger is the passage of Malachi, in which a promise is made that the messenger who was then expected would come to his own temple (Mal. 3:1). The temple certainly was dedicated to Almighty God only, and yet the prophet claims it for Christ. Hence it follows, that he is the God who was always worshipped by the Jews.

Sentence count: 26 (S1–S26). One paragraph, four movements: the Angel who is worshipped — Manoah's altar as the proof that a name can be delegated but a sacrifice cannot (S1–S10) · Servetus named, and the orthodox thesis: the Word as the supreme angel, Mediator by anticipation, not yet in flesh (S11–S14) · the Hosea–Genesis witness (Jacob at Peniel), Servetus again, Paul on the wilderness, and the "type of the office" (S15–S19) · Zechariah's sending angel, and two texts "passed over" — Isaiah 25:9 and Malachi 3:1 — closing on the God the Jews always worshipped (S20–S26).

Examiner's Eye. Five traps, and four of them are about how much Calvin is claiming. - Trap 1 — him in S5. orders the sacrifice due to Jehovah to be offered to **him**. ⚠️⚠️ Who is him? If you read it as Jehovah (a third party), S5 merely says the Angel told Manoah to sacrifice to God — which any servant could say, and S4's principle would be irrelevant. him = the Angel himself. S4 sets the rule (no servant lets sacrifice be offered to himself); S5 reports the breach (this Angel orders it offered to himself); S6 draws the conclusion (so he was Jehovah). The narrative confirms it — the Angel receives the offering by ascending in the flame of the altar (Judges 13:20). An option that says the Angel directed the sacrifice away from himself to Jehovah has inverted the argument. - Trap 2 — the hedges in S12–S13, S19. **as it were** by anticipation · **as in** an intermediate form · **not clothed with flesh** · a **type** of the office which he **was to** fulfil. ⚠️⚠️⚠️ Calvin does not say the Son was incarnate under the Law, nor that the Mediator's office was already in force. He says the Word previewed it, in a form between Godhead and flesh. An option that puts the incarnation in Judges 13 is the overreach option — and it is the one Book 2 will explicitly forbid. - Trap 3 — the two audiences in S21. I omit numberless passages in which **our faith** rests secure, though they **may not have much weight with the Jews**. ⚠️⚠️ Isaiah 25:9 and Malachi 3:1 are offered as examples of the class he is passing over — texts that secure Christian faith but that a Jewish reader will not grant. An option that says Calvin presents Isaiah 25:9 as the text most likely to convince the Jews has flipped S21. even the blind may see (S22) is Calvin's confidence, not a claim about Jewish assent. - Trap 4 — what Servetus actually said (S11, S16). God was **never manifested** to Abraham and the Patriarchs, but … **an angel** was worshipped **in his stead**; God **personated** an angel. ⚠️ Servetus does not deny that the patriarchs saw something or worshipped someone; he denies that what they saw was God. An option that has Servetus denying the theophanies altogether, or denying that an angel was worshipped, misreports him. - Trap 5 — always in S26. he is the God who was **always** worshipped by the Jews. ⚠️ The conclusion is continuity, not novelty: Christ is not a second God introduced alongside the God of Israel; he is the God of Israel. An option that has Calvin concluding that Christ is a God distinct from the one Israel worshipped has reversed the last sentence.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

Where §10 sits. The synopsis reads: The angel who appeared to the fathers under the Law asserts that he is Jehovah. That angel was the Λόγος of the Eternal Father. The Son being that Λόγος is Eternal God. Impiety of Servetus refuted. Why the Son appeared in the form of an angel. ⚠️ Five clauses, and they map the section: the Angel asserts he is Jehovah (S1–S10, Manoah) — that Angel was the Word (S12, the orthodox doctors; S18, Paul) — so the Son is eternal God (S26) — Servetus refuted (S11, S16) — why the form of an angel (S13–S14, more familiar access). The proof-block of the chapter now stands: §§7–8 the Word's eternity, §9 the divine names, §10 the theophanies, §§11–13 the New Testament. Today is the last Old Testament section, and the first in which a living opponent is named.

The Angel of the LORD, and the "interchange." The Old Testament has a recurring figure, mal'ak YHWH, who appears to Hagar (Gen 16), Abraham (Gen 22), Jacob (Gen 31–32), Moses (Exod 3), Gideon and Manoah (Judg 6; 13). ⚠️ The textual fact Calvin builds on is real and undisputed: in these narratives the text slides between the angel and the LORD without warning — the angel of the LORD appeared … and the LORD said (Judg 6:12, 14); Hagar names the angel Thou God seest me (Gen 16:13). Scholars call it the interchange. What is disputed is what it means. Calvin's reading — the Angel is the pre-incarnate Word — is the ante-Nicene consensus: Justin Martyr's Dialogue with Trypho (chs. 56–62) argued exactly this to a Jewish interlocutor, from exactly these texts (Genesis 18–19, 32; Exodus 3); Irenaeus, Tertullian (Against Praxeas 16), Novatian, and Hilary followed. Calvin's orthodox doctors of the Church (S12) are these men, and his rhetorical situation — arguing with the Jews — is Justin's.

Augustine's reversal — the guardrail Calvin does not name. ⚠️⚠️ The reading Calvin endorses is not Augustine's. In De Trinitate II–III Augustine argued that the theophanies were produced through created means (angels, or shaped creatures) and could not be assigned to the Son as such — any person of the Trinity might be signified, and none was seen in his own nature. His reason was Arian misuse: if the Son is the visible God who appears and the Father the invisible God who does not, the Son looks lesser and mutable. Calvin knows the danger and answers it by hedging, not by abandoning the old reading: as in an intermediate form (S13), as it were by anticipation (S12), a type of the office (S19), and above all S14 — he retained the character which justly belonged to him—that of the God of ineffable glory. The Angel is not a lesser, visible deity; he is the God of glory condescending. That sentence is Calvin's Augustinian insurance on a pre-Augustinian thesis. ⚠️ Do not write Calvin follows Augustine here. He follows Justin and Tertullian, with Augustine's worry in view.

Servetus. Michael Servetus (1511–1553), Spanish physician and theologian, author of De Trinitatis erroribus (1531) and Christianismi restitutio (1553), burned at Geneva in October 1553 after a trial in which Calvin was the principal accuser. ⚠️ The 1559 Institutes is thick with anti-Servetus material added after the trial; §22 of this chapter is devoted to him. His position, as Calvin reports it here: the Son had no personal pre-existence, so the Old Testament theophanies could not be the Son; they were God personating (S16) an angel, or an angel worshipped in God's stead (S11). Guardrail: Calvin's summary is polemical and compressed; Servetus's own Christology (a divine mode of self-disclosure that becomes a man, not a subsisting second person) is harder to state than S11 suggests. But S11's charge is fair on its own terms: on Servetus's account the patriarchs worshipped a creature, and Scripture approved. That is why the word is impiety.

The office-objection is not a straw man. S3's in respect of the office which he bears is the standard Jewish answer, and it has a legal form: the rabbinic principle that a man's agent is as himself (shaliach) — the envoy speaks and is addressed as the sender. ⚠️ Calvin does not deny the principle; he finds its limit. An agent may bear the name; an agent may not receive the worship. That is why the section's evidence is not a saying but a sacrifice (S4–S6), not a title but a confession by those who met him (S9, S17). The method of §9 — argue from what the opponent grants — continues: the Jew grants that sacrifice belongs to God alone.

Later reception, in three lines. Reformed orthodoxy (Turretin, Owen) kept the Christophany reading as standard. Modern critical study of the Old Testament reads the mal'ak YHWH as a literary mode of God's self-presence — the interchange is granted; the identification with a second divine person is not. And study of Second Temple Judaism (the "two powers in heaven" debates of the rabbis) shows that Jewish readers themselves found the interchange troubling enough to legislate against binitarian readings — which is indirect evidence that the texts read the way Calvin says they read. ⚠️ Over-reading guardrail: §10 proves from the texts that the Angel is Jehovah; it identifies the Angel with the Word on the authority of the Church's exposition (S12) and Paul (S18). Two steps; do not collapse them, and do not claim Judges 13 alone names the Son.
