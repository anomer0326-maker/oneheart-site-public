Day 107 — Book 1, Chapter 14, §4

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday Calvin said: "stay within the bounds of piety." Today he gives the bounds a shape — two rules — and then names the culprit who broke them: the author of the Celestial Hierarchy. Angels are God's creatures — settled in one clause. When they were made and in what order? Calvin: "more perverseness than industry." Then comes the passage every seminary student quotes: one rule of modesty and soberness (don't speak, think, or even long to know beyond the Word) and a second rule (aim your study at edification, not curiosity). Fourteen sentences. Compact. Quotable. Underline it.

Why examiners love it. The logic is a clean staircase: admit the fact → refuse the question → state Rule 1 → state Rule 2 → apply to angelology → target Dionysius → contrast with Paul → resume the Scripture-only path. And the grammar is dense: an absolute participial opening (Angels being …, S1), a parenthetical (whoever he may have been, S10), a not only … but even chain (S9), a so far from V-ing idiom (S13), a fronted/adverbial Bidding adieu (S14).

Whose voice? S7 is Calvin reporting what others do (I know that many fasten on these topics) — he is not praising it. S12 you would think … is a hypothetical reader's impression of Dionysius, not Calvin's own belief. Keep the voices separate.

Text traps. ⚠️ bespeaks (S1) = indicates, betrays (not "speaks for"). avails (S2) = is the use of. nugatory (S14) = worthless, trifling. disquisitions (S10) = elaborate discussions. tickle the ear (S11) = amuse rather than instruct (cf. 2 Tim 4:3).

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. Absolute participial opening: Angels being the ministers …, must, of course, be admitted to be his creatures (S1) → ⚠️ Angels being … is not the subject of the main clause as a participle-subject; it is a fronted participial phrase, with Angels doing double duty as the subject of must be admitted. Meaning: Since angels are the ministers …, they must be admitted to be his creatures. Formula 1.
  2. not … but … as a rule of method: not to speak or think, or even long to know, more than … (S3); not indulge in curiosity (S4); not to tickle the ear, but confirm the conscience (S11) → ⚠️ Calvin builds Rule 1 and Rule 2 on negated infinitives and on correlative not … but. Watch the scope of the not. Formula 2.
  3. so far from V-ing and though-concession: Paul, however, though he was carried to the third heaven, so far from delivering any thing of the kind, positively declares … (S13) → so far from V-ing = far from V-ing; not only did he not V, but … — the opposite of what you expect. Formula 3.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 14 sentences –

Angels being the ministers appointed to execute the commands of God, must, of course, be admitted to be his creatures, but to stir up questions concerning the time or order in which they were created (see Lombard, lib. 2 dist. 2, sqq.), bespeaks more perverseness than industry. Moses relates that the heavens and the earth were finished, with all their host; what avails it anxiously to inquire at what time other more hidden celestial hosts than the stars and planets also began to be? Not to dwell on this, let us here remember that on the whole subject of religion one rule of modesty and soberness is to be observed, and it is this, in obscure matters not to speak or think, or even long to know, more than the Word of God has delivered. A second rule is, that in reading the Scriptures we should constantly direct our inquiries and meditations to those things which tend to edification, not indulge in curiosity, or in studying things of no use. And since the Lord has been pleased to instruct us, not in frivolous questions, but in solid piety, in the fear of his name, in true faith, and the duties of holiness, let us rest satisfied with such knowledge. Wherefore, if we would be duly wise, we must renounce those vain babblings of idle men, concerning the nature, ranks, and number of angels, without any authority from the Word of God. I know that many fasten on these topics more eagerly, and take greater pleasure in them than in those relating to daily practice. But if we decline not to be the disciples of Christ, let us not decline to follow the method which he has prescribed. In this way, being contented with him for our master, we will not only refrain from, but even feel averse to, superfluous speculations which he discourages. None can deny that Dionysus (whoever he may have been) has many shrewd and subtle disquisitions in his Celestial Hierarchy, but on looking at them more closely, every one must see that they are merely idle talk. The duty of a Theologian, however, is not to tickle the ear, but confirm the conscience, by teaching what is true, certain, and useful. When you read the work of Dionysus, you would think that the man had come down from heaven, and was relating, not what he had learned, but what he had actually seen. Paul, however, though he was carried to the third heaven, so far from delivering any thing of the kind, positively declares, that it was not lawful for man to speak the secrets which he had seen. Bidding adieu, therefore, to that nugatory wisdom, let us endeavour to ascertain from the simple doctrine of Scripture what it is the Lord’s pleasure that we should know concerning angels.

2 — Structure at a Glance – 

[FACT]       S1  Angels = creatures (they serve) — BUT asking when/in what order = perverseness
[ILLUSTRATE] S2  Moses: "the heavens and the earth were finished, with all their host" — don't dig further
[RULE 1]     S3  Modesty & soberness: in obscure matters don't speak/think/long-to-know beyond the Word
[RULE 2]     S4  Aim all study at edification, not curiosity
[WHY]        S5  The Lord teaches solid piety, the fear of his name, true faith, holiness — be satisfied
[APPLY]      S6  So: renounce the vain babblings about the nature, ranks, number of angels
[CONCESSION] S7  I know many love these topics more than daily practice
[APPEAL]     S8–S9  Disciples of Christ follow his method → we'll even feel averse to speculation
[TARGET]     S10–S12  Dionysus: shrewd, but idle talk; the theologian confirms the conscience; he writes as if he'd seen heaven
[CONTRAST]   S13  Paul, caught up to the third heaven, was forbidden to tell
[RESUME]     S14  Say goodbye to nugatory wisdom; learn from Scripture's simple doctrine what the Lord wants us to know

Examiner's Eye. - ⚠️ Scope overreach: S1 does not forbid all study of angels. It forbids anxious inquiry into time and order of creation (S1–S2) and nature, ranks, number "without any authority from the Word" (S6). The sections that follow (§§5–12) do teach about angels — from Scripture. Choosing "Calvin bans angelology" is the classic trap. - ⚠️ Direction-flip: Rule 1 is not "know less"; it is "do not go beyond what the Word has delivered." Within the Word, Calvin wants full knowledge. - ⚠️ Voice trap: S12 (you would think that the man had come down from heaven) describes the impression Dionysus gives, not Calvin's own view that he actually did. S7 describes others' eagerness. - ⚠️ Target trap: Calvin criticizes Dionysus's method and content, not his intelligence — S10 concedes shrewd and subtle disquisitions. Concede, then disarm (same move as Day 106 S12).

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • fasten on — physical → mental "latch on."
전체 해설 더보기

One point. A concession: Calvin knows the temptation (I know that …) — theological speculation is more fun than obedience. The parallel more eagerly / greater pleasure … than in those relating to daily practice sets speculation vs. practice as rivals; fasten on = latch onto. Mark the contrast; Calvin's real target is the imbalance. Literal rendering: I know that many latch on to these topics more eagerly, and take greater pleasure in them, than in those that bear on daily practice.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — Fronted participial reason clause (S1, S9, S14)

[N + being + complement], S + V …   =   Since N is complement, S + V …
Angels being the ministers appointed to execute God's commands, [they] must be admitted to be his creatures.

⚠️ Trap. being does not make a complete clause. There must be exactly one finite verb in the rest (must be admitted). Also: S9 being contented with him for our master, we will … — the participle's implied subject must equal the subject of the main clause (we). S14 Bidding adieu …, let us … — the implied subject is we (inside let us). Drill. Because the Spirit is the teacher of the church, the church must be admitted to be dependent on him. → The Spirit being the teacher of the church, the church must be admitted to be dependent on him.

Formula 2 — not (to) A, but (to) B and not only A, but even B (S3, S4, S9, S11)

X is not to A, but (to) B            ⇒ the duty is B, not A (second `to` often dropped)
not only V1 (prep), but even V2 (prep) OBJ   ⇒ shared object, both prepositions stranded

⚠️ Trap. Calvin often suspends the object to the end (S9): refrain from, but even feel averse to, superfluous speculations. Restore the object after each preposition. And track the scope of not — S4: not indulge in curiosity, or in studying … — not covers both in-phrases. Drill. The preacher must please the ear. The preacher must also fail to confirm the conscience. → Negate: The preacher's task is not to please the ear, but confirm the conscience.

Formula 3 — so far from V-ing and concessive though (S13)

so far from V-ing, S + V     =   not only did S not V; S did the opposite
S, though + clause, so far from V-ing O, positively V2 …

⚠️ Trap. so far from is an idiom for reversal, not distance. Do not translate "to such a degree far from". The though clause carries the expectation (carried to heaven → would tell) that so far from then reverses. Drill. Calvin did not offer a map of heaven. He warned against such maps. → Calvin, so far from offering a map of heaven, warned against all such maps.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

1. Doctrine at stake. Calvin has just said in I.14.3: angels are creatures (against angel-worship and Manichean dualism). Now, in §4, he closes the door on curiosity before entering the room of Scripture in §5. The creaturehood of angels is a given; the timing and order of their creation are unrevealed, therefore not to be explored.

2. The Scholastic target. (see Lombard, lib. 2 dist. 2, sqq.) — Peter Lombard's Sentences II.2 ff. discuss whether the angels were created before the visible world or simultaneously (Augustine: simultaneously, with "Let there be light", De Civ. Dei XI.9; Jerome and the Greek Fathers: before). Calvin does not decide; he refuses to play the game. Compare Augustine's own sobriety on the subject (cf. Enchiridion 9.58, where he declines to settle the matter).

3. "Pseudo-Dionysius." The Celestial Hierarchy (c. AD 500, Syria) arranges angels in three triads of three orders (Seraphim–Cherubim–Thrones; Dominions–Virtues–Powers; Principalities–Archangels–Angels), attributed to Dionysius the Areopagite (Acts 17:34). It was massively influential in the Latin West (Eriugena, Hugh of St Victor, Aquinas's ST I q.108). By Calvin's day, Valla (1457) and Erasmus had questioned the authorship; Calvin hedges (whoever he may have been). Beveridge's Dionysus is a spelling, not another man. Over-reading guardrail: Calvin is not rejecting hierarchy as such — he rejects the claim to certainty beyond Scripture; in §8 he will say the Bible gives no definitive numbers and orders.

4. The two rules, in the later tradition. Rule 1 is the locus classicus of Reformed "scriptural sobriety" (cf. III.21.1–2 on predestination; Westminster Confession I.6: "the whole counsel of God … is either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture"). Rule 2 is the Reformed principle that doctrine serves piety (pietas): Barth, though sharply critical of angelological speculation, reopens the topic in CD III/3 §51 under Scriptural control — which is arguably Calvin's own pattern in §§5–12. Guardrail: do not turn Rule 1 into "no theology of anything unseen."

5. Reception note. The contrast of S11 (ear vs. conscience) is one of the most quoted lines of the Institutes in Reformed preaching manuals; treat any claim about its exact edition history as outside today's source text.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. S2 — Gen 2:1 "Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them" (כָּל־צְבָאָם). Calvin reads host broadly enough to include hidden hosts — and uses its silence as a boundary.
  2. S3 / S11 — Deut 29:29 "The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us…" — the background for Rule 1 (and for the secrets which he had seen). Implicit, not quoted.
  3. S4 — Eph 4:12; 1 Cor 14:26 (οἰκοδομή, "edification") — the standard of Rule 2. Calvin uses edification as a hermeneutical criterion for which questions to ask.
  4. S5 — Ps 111:10; Prov 1:7 ("the fear of the LORD") — the fear of his name joins fear–faith–holiness as the curriculum.
  5. S6 — 1 Tim 6:20; 2 Tim 2:16 (κενοφωνίας, "vain babblings") — Calvin imports Paul's phrase to label the Celestial Hierarchy.
  6. S8 — Matt 11:29; 23:8 ("one is your Master, even Christ") — the disciple/method frame; the Master prescribes the method.
  7. S11 — 2 Tim 4:3 (κνηθόμενοι τὴν ἀκοήν, "itching ears") — tickle the ear; Calvin turns the hearers' vice into the teacher's temptation.
  8. S12 — Jn 3:13 ("no man hath ascended up to heaven, but he that came down from heaven") — the background irony: Dionysus claims to have come down; only the Son can say that.
  9. S13 — 2 Cor 12:2–4 (ἁρπαγέντα … ἕως τρίτου οὐρανοῦ; ἄρρητα ῥήματα … οὐκ ἐξὸν ἀνθρώπῳ λαλῆσαι) — Calvin's central proof-text: even Paul's heavenly visions were not to be told.
  10. S14 — Col 2:18 ("worshipping of angels, intruding into those things which he hath not seen") — the hidden warning against angel-speculation, which Calvin picks up in I.14.10 ff.

8 — Exam Problems –

① Grammar — one underlined item is wrong.

(A) Angels being the ministers appointed to execute God's commands, they must be admitted to be his creatures. (B) The theologian's duty is not to tickle the ear, but to confirm the conscience. (C) We will not only refrain from, but even feel averse to, superfluous speculations. (D) Paul, though he was carried to the third heaven, so far from to deliver anything of the kind, positively declares that it was not lawful to speak of it.

Answer: (D). Correct: so far from delivering (so far from + gerund; Formula 3). Examiner's intent: from is a preposition — it takes V-ing, never an infinitive. (A) fronted participial reason clause ✓ (Formula 1). (B) not to A, but (to) B — second to may be dropped or kept ✓. (C) stranded prepositions with shared object ✓ (Formula 2).

② Comprehension — choose the statement consistent with §4.

(A) Calvin forbids every inquiry about angels because the Bible never mentions them. (B) Calvin denies that angels are creatures, since their creation is not dated in Genesis. (C) Calvin grants that Dionysus's discussions are clever, but denies that they confirm the conscience or rest on the Word. (D) Calvin holds that Paul revealed to the church what he saw in the third heaven.

Answer: (C). Examiner's intent: (A) scope overreach — S6 bans only speculation without authority from the Word; S14 promises a Scripture-based doctrine. (B) direction-flip — S1 says angels must be admitted to be his creatures; what's refused is the question of when/in what order. (D) direction-flip — S13: Paul declared it was not lawful to speak those secrets.

③ Composition — use Formula 2 or Formula 3.

Compose one English sentence (≈20 words): Many theologians love speculation more than obedience. Calvin does not want his readers merely to avoid such speculation; he wants them to dislike it.

Model answer: Calvin would have his readers not only refrain from, but even feel averse to, speculations that tend little to edification. Examiner's intent: the suspended shared object after two stranded prepositions (refrain from … feel averse to … speculations; Formula 2). Alternative accepted: Calvin, so far from praising such speculation, urges us to lose our taste for it (Formula 3).

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary: Angels are God's creatures, but the time and order of their creation are not revealed, so Calvin lays down two rules — nothing beyond the Word on obscure matters, and all study aimed at edification — and, by contrasting Pseudo-Dionysius's heavenly "eyewitness" prattle with Paul's enforced silence, sends us to Scripture's plain doctrine. Three grammar points: ① fronted participial reason clause (Angels being …) ② not (to) A, but (to) B / not only … but even … with a suspended shared object ③ so far from V-ing (reversal) with concessive though.
