Day 62 — Book 1, Chapter 11, §7

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Third and final answer to the "books of the unlearned" plea — and Calvin saves his most dangerous witnesses for last: the images themselves. Scripture ruled in §5. The fathers ruled in §6. Today the pictures and statues take the stand — and they incriminate their owners by their own wardrobe. Look at the audacity of the comparison Calvin dares to print: brothels exhibit their inmates more chastely and modestly dressed than churches do images intended to represent virgins. Sixteenth-century readers gasped at that line. He is saying: walk your "holy" images into the street and they would be arrested for indecency (S5 — the pillory!). Some "books."

But don't stop at the demolition, because the second half of the section is the keeper. Calvin pivots (S10) from what God forbids to what God commands: the doctrine set forth by the preaching of the Word and the administration of the sacraments. Underline that pairing. Calvin is not replacing pictures with mere talk — he is replacing man-made images with God-instituted signs. And then the thunderbolt (S17): Paul says that in true preaching Christ is portrayed and in a manner crucified before our eyes (Gal. 3:1). Paul's own Greek verb is a painting verb. The Reformed church is not against the picture of Christ — it possesses the only portrait God ever commissioned: the preached cross. One doctrine, says S19, teaches more than a thousand crosses of wood and stone.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. The Were + S + to-V inverted conditional — TWICE (S2, S5). were I to admit the plea, it would not be a valid defence / Were any one to dress himself after their model, he would deserve the pillory. Drop the if, front the were, follow with a to-infinitive: the hypothetical-future conditional. Two live specimens in one section means this is today's exam certainty.
  2. Fronted-complement inversion (S10). Very different from these follies is the doctrine… — the adjective phrase marches to the front, the verb follows, and the true subject hides at the END. Ask a student "what is the subject of is?" and half the room grabs follies. Don't be half the room.
  3. Archaic want = LACK (S14). there are not a few in the present day who cannot want such books — cannot want = cannot do without, cannot lack. Read it as "cannot desire" and the sentence flips into nonsense. This is the direction-reversing trap word of the day.

Bonus traps I will test: dumb (S16) = mute, speechless — not stupid; and watch the irony, because in the prophets it is the idols that are dumb (Hab. 2:18), and Calvin hangs the word on the clergy. too and fro (S11) — the source's own spelling of to and fro; we quote texts as they stand. becoming (S7) = fitting, seemly (from become = to suit). in a manner (S17) = as it were. viz. (S18) = videlicet, "namely." rudeness (S12) = lack of training — nobody is being impolite.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 20 sentences –

7. Let Papists, then, if they have any sense of shame, henceforth desist from the futile plea, that images are the books of the unlearned—a plea so plainly refuted by innumerable passages of Scripture. And yet were I to admit the plea, it would not be a valid defence of their peculiar idols. It is well known what kind of monsters they obtrude upon us as divine. For what are the pictures or statues to which they append the names of saints, but exhibitions of the most shameless luxury or obscenity? Were any one to dress himself after their model, he would deserve the pillory. Indeed, brothels exhibit their inmates more chastely and modestly dressed than churches do images intended to represent virgins. The dress of the martyrs is in no respect more becoming. Let Papists then have some little regard to decency in decking their idols, if they would give the least plausibility to the false allegation, that they are books of some kind of sanctity. But even then we shall answer, that this is not the method in which the Christian people should be taught in sacred places. Very different from these follies is the doctrine in which God would have them to be there instructed. His injunction is, that the doctrine common to all should there be set forth by the preaching of the Word, and the administration of the sacraments,—a doctrine to which little heed can be given by those whose eyes are carried too and fro gazing at idols. And who are the unlearned, whose rudeness admits of being taught by images only? Just those whom the Lord acknowledges for his disciples; those whom he honours with a revelation of his celestial philosophy, and desires to be trained in the saving mysteries of his kingdom. I confess, indeed, as matters now are, there are not a few in the present day who cannot want such books. But, I ask, whence this stupidity, but just because they are defrauded of the only doctrine which was fit to instruct them? The simple reason why those who had the charge of churches resigned the office of teaching to idols was, because they themselves were dumb. Paul declares, that by the true preaching of the gospel Christ is portrayed and in a manner crucified before our eyes (Gal. 3:1). Of what use, then, were the erection in churches of so many crosses of wood and stone, silver and gold, if this doctrine were faithfully and honestly preached—viz. Christ died that he might bear our curse upon the tree, that he might expiate our sins by the sacrifice of his body, wash them in his blood, and, in short, reconcile us to God the Father? From this one doctrine the people would learn more than from a thousand crosses of wood and stone. As for crosses of gold and silver, it may be true that the avaricious give their eyes and minds to them more eagerly than to any heavenly instructor.

Sentence count: 20 (S1–S20). One paragraph.

Source housekeeping (page furniture removed, text otherwise character-exact): the page number 96 (after innumerable, S1) was removed; the §6 footnote block (29–31), the page number 100, and the running chapter header fell mid-section between The dress of the and martyrs (S7) and were removed; the end-of-line hyphenations injunc-tion (S11) and acknow-ledges (S13) were rejoined to injunction and acknowledges. too and fro (S11) is the source's own reading and is retained verbatim. No footnote markers fall inside this §. The em-dashes in unlearned—a plea, sacraments,—a doctrine, and preached—viz. are the source's own.

Examiner's Eye — three traps I would build from this section

Trap 1 — the want flip on S14. A test-writer will offer: "Calvin denies that anyone in his day needs images as books." False. S14 confesses the opposite — "there are not a few in the present day who cannot want such books" = many today cannot do without them. But watch the second layer: the option "many people today have no desire for such books" is equally false, because it mistranslates the archaic want. One trap word, two dead options. This is why S14 wears three stars.

Trap 2 — the function flip on S8. "Calvin advises the church to adorn its images more beautifully." False. S8 is an ironic entrance requirement, not interior-decorating advice: IF the "books of sanctity" claim is to have the least plausibility, decency is the minimum — and the plea already fails the minimum. Quoting real words while reversing their function: the concessive trap you drilled yesterday, back in a new coat.

Trap 3 — the irony flip on S20. "Calvin concedes that crosses of gold and silver at least have instructive value." False — sarcasm read straight. The only students gold crosses win are the avaricious, and the only lesson taught is covetousness. If an option turns a sneer into a concession, kill it.

3 — Sentence-by-Sentence Live Teaching –

S1★★the jussive verdict — let X desist + the em-dash resumptive›

Let Papists, then, if they have any sense of shame, henceforth desist from the futile plea, that images are the books of the unlearned—a plea so plainly refuted by innumerable passages of Scripture.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • desist < de- + sistere (to stand) — stand down, stop; cf. resist, consist. futile < futilis (a leaky vessel) — pours everything out, holds nothing: the perfect adjective for a failed argument.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • desist from = ~을 그만두다; the books of the unlearned = Gregory the Great's famous tag (libri idiotarum) — the slogan under trial since §5.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

So if the Papists have any sense of shame, let them stop using the empty excuse that images are the books of the uneducated — an excuse so clearly refuted by countless passages of Scripture.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • henceforth desist from the futile plea → from now on stop using the empty excuse
  • plea, that → excuse that
  • the unlearned → the uneducated
  • innumerable → countless.
S2★★★inversion #1 — were I to admit the plea›

And yet were I to admit the plea, it would not be a valid defence of their peculiar idols.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • peculiar < peculium (one's private property, orig. one's cattle, pecus) — "their peculiar idols" = the idols peculiarly THEIRS; cf. pecuniary. valid < validus (strong) — an argument that stands.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • were I to V = 설령 내가 ~한다 하더라도 (hypothetical concession); and yet = 그럼에도.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

And yet even if I accepted that excuse, it would not justify the particular idols they actually have.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • were I to admit the plea → even if I accepted that excuse
  • a valid defence of → a justification for
  • their peculiar idols → the particular idols they actually have.
S3★anticipatory it + obtrude›

It is well known what kind of monsters they obtrude upon us as divine.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • obtrude < ob- (against) + trudere (to thrust) — to thrust upon someone uninvited; kin: intrude, protrude, thrust-family by sense.
전체 해설 더보기

One point: anticipatory it + wh-clause subject — restore: what kind of monsters they obtrude upon us as divine is well known. The verb is the venom: obtrude (< ob- against + trudere thrust; cf. intrude, extrude) — these "gods" are not offered, they are shoved at us. Move on.

S4★★what are X but Y? — the rhetorical "nothing but"›

For what are the pictures or statues to which they append the names of saints, but exhibitions of the most shameless luxury or obscenity?

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • append < ad- + pendere (to hang) — to hang onto; cf. appendix, pendant, suspend. obscenity < obscenus (offensive, ill-omened) — what should stay off-stage.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • what is X but Y? = X는 Y에 지나지 않는다; append A to B = A를 B에 붙이다.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

After all, what are the pictures and statues they label with saints' names, except displays of utterly shameless luxury and indecency?

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • to which they append the names of saints → they label with saints' names
  • exhibitions of → displays of
  • obscenity → indecency.
S5★★inversion #2 — the pillory›

Were any one to dress himself after their model, he would deserve the pillory.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • pillory < Old French pilori — the public punishment-frame; to pillory someone survives as a metaphor in modern English (to expose to public scorn).
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • after the model of = ~을 본떠서 (cf. named after); dress oneself = 옷을 입다 (reflexive, archaic flavor).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

If anyone dressed the way those images are dressed, he would deserve to be put in the pillory.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • Were any one to dress himself after their model → If anyone dressed the way those images are dressed
  • he would deserve the pillory → he would deserve to be publicly shamed.
S6★★the scandal comparison — than churches do images›

Indeed, brothels exhibit their inmates more chastely and modestly dressed than churches do images intended to represent virgins.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • chaste < castus (pure); inmate = in + mate — one housed within (a resident; only later a prisoner). exhibit < ex- + habere — to hold out for display.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • intended to represent = ~을 표상하도록 만들어진; the do of comparison = 앞 동사의 대용.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

In fact, brothels display the women who live there more chastely and modestly dressed than churches display images that are supposed to represent virgins.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • exhibit their inmates → display the women who live there
  • than churches do images → than churches display images
  • intended to represent → supposed to represent.
S7★in no respect more becoming — the trap adjective›

The dress of the martyrs is in no respect more becoming.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • becoming — from become in its old sense "to suit, befit"; the fossil survives in "a very becoming dress" and its negative unbecoming ("conduct unbecoming an officer").
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • In no respect = in no way
전체 해설 더보기

One point: becoming = fitting, seemly — the adjective from the old verb become = to suit ("that coat becomes you"). Nothing is "turning into" anything. In no respect = in no way. The virgins failed the decency test; the martyrs' images fail it too — the indictment is inventory-wide. Next.

S8★★the ironic entrance requirement — if they would›

Let Papists then have some little regard to decency in decking their idols, if they would give the least plausibility to the false allegation, that they are books of some kind of sanctity.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • deck < Du. dekken (to cover, adorn); plausibility < plaudere (to clap) — what can win applause, as distinct from what is true; cf. applaud, plaudits.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • have regard to = ~을 고려하다; if they would = ~할 생각이라면 (volitional); give plausibility to = ~을 그럴듯하게 만들다.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

So if the Papists want their false claim — that these are books of some kind of holiness — to have even the slightest plausibility, let them at least show a little decency in how they dress up their idols.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • have some little regard to decency in decking their idols → show a little decency in how they dress up their idols
  • if they would give the least plausibility to → if they want … to have even the slightest plausibility
  • allegation, that → claim that
  • sanctity → holiness.
S9★the even-then verdict — we shall answer, that›

But even then we shall answer, that this is not the method in which the Christian people should be taught in sacred places.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • method < Gk. methodos (meta + hodos, way) — a way of pursuit; the fight is over the God-appointed way of teaching.
전체 해설 더보기

One point: even then completes the concession ladder that S2 began — grant the plea (S2), grant even the decency (S8): still no. First-person shall = firm resolve, a standing answer. The word method quietly hands the baton to S10, where God's method enters. Move on.

S10★★★fronted-complement inversion — the pivot of the section›

Very different from these follies is the doctrine in which God would have them to be there instructed.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • folly < OFr. folie (< fol, fool); instruct < in- + struere (to build) — to build INTO someone; cf. structure, construct: doctrine as edification, literally.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • would have X (to be) p.p. = X가 ~되기를 원하다 (archaic causative); very different from = ~와 전혀 다른.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

The teaching by which God wants his people instructed in church is completely different from these follies.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • Very different from these follies is the doctrine → The teaching … is completely different from these follies (inversion undone)
  • in which God would have them to be there instructed → by which God wants his people instructed in church.
S11★★★God's method — mandative should + the resumptive apposition, upgraded›

His injunction is, that the doctrine common to all should there be set forth by the preaching of the Word, and the administration of the sacraments,—a doctrine to which little heed can be given by those whose eyes are carried too and fro gazing at idols.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • injunction < in- + jungere (to join) — an order that binds; cf. enjoin, junction. heed — OE hēdan, to guard, attend to.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • set forth = 제시·선포하다; give heed to = ~에 주의를 기울이다; to and fro = 이리저리 (here spelled too and fro in the source).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

His command is that the teaching meant for everyone should be presented there through the preaching of the Word and the administration of the sacraments — a teaching that gets little attention from people whose eyes wander back and forth, gazing at idols.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • His injunction is, that → His command is that
  • the doctrine common to all → the teaching meant for everyone
  • should there be set forth → should be presented there
  • little heed can be given by those → gets little attention from people
  • carried too and fro → wander back and forth.
S12★★admits of being taught — the archaic pattern›

And who are the unlearned, whose rudeness admits of being taught by images only?

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • rude < rudis (raw, untrained) → rudiments (first raw elements), erudite (polished OUT of rawness — the exact antonym).
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • admit of ~ing = ~의 여지가 있다, ~될 수 있다 (cf. admits of no doubt).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

And who exactly are these uneducated people, whose ignorance can supposedly be taught only by images?

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • the unlearned → these uneducated people
  • whose rudeness admits of being taught → whose ignorance can supposedly be taught
  • by images only → only by images.
S13★★the rank-flip — those whom the Lord acknowledges for his disciples›

Just those whom the Lord acknowledges for his disciples; those whom he honours with a revelation of his celestial philosophy, and desires to be trained in the saving mysteries of his kingdom.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • disciple < discere (to learn) — a learner by definition (the pun is Calvin's whole point); celestial < caelum (heaven).
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • acknowledge X for Y (archaic) = X를 Y로 인정하다 (= acknowledge as); celestial philosophy = 하늘의 철학 — Calvin's favorite name for gospel doctrine.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

Precisely those whom the Lord recognizes as his disciples — those he honours with the revelation of his heavenly philosophy and wants trained in the saving mysteries of his kingdom.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • acknowledges for his disciples → recognizes as his disciples
  • celestial → heavenly
  • desires to be trained → wants trained.
S14★★★the trap of the day — cannot want such books›

I confess, indeed, as matters now are, there are not a few in the present day who cannot want such books.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • want < ON vanta (to lack) — LACK first, desire later; the old sense survives in for want of (= for lack of) and he wants for nothing. confess < con- + fateri (to acknowledge).
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • as matters now are = 지금 형편으로는; not a few = 적지 않은 수 (litotes = 많다); cannot want = ~없이는 안 된다 (= cannot lack/do without).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

I admit, in fact, that as things now stand, there are quite a few people today who cannot do without such books.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • as matters now are → as things now stand
  • not a few → quite a few
  • cannot want such books → cannot do without such books (⚠️ want = lack, NOT desire).
S15★★whence this stupidity, but just because… — the verbless indictment›

But, I ask, whence this stupidity, but just because they are defrauded of the only doctrine which was fit to instruct them?

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • defraud < de- + fraus (deceit) — to rob by deceit; cf. fraud. whence — OE hwanon, "from where"; answers thence.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • whence X, but because Y? = X가 Y 때문이 아니면 어디서 왔겠는가; fit to V = ~하기에 합당한.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

But where, I ask, does this ignorance come from, if not simply from the fact that they have been robbed of the only teaching that was fit to instruct them?

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • whence this stupidity → where does this ignorance come from
  • but just because → if not simply from the fact that
  • defrauded of → robbed of.
S16★★because they themselves were dumb — the word-level irony›

The simple reason why those who had the charge of churches resigned the office of teaching to idols was, because they themselves were dumb.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • dumb — OE, mute (cf. Ger. dumm drifted to "stupid"; English kept "mute" longer — the trap is the drift); resign < re- + signare (to unseal, give back) — to hand back one's commission.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • have the charge of = ~을 맡고 있다; resign X to Y = X를 Y에게 넘겨주다; the office of teaching = 가르치는 직무.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

The simple reason why those in charge of churches handed the office of teaching over to idols was that they themselves were mute.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • had the charge of churches → were in charge of churches
  • resigned the office of teaching to idols → handed the office of teaching over to idols
  • was, because → was that
  • dumb → mute (⚠️ not "stupid").
S17★★Gal. 3:1 — the portrait God commissioned›

Paul declares, that by the true preaching of the gospel Christ is portrayed and in a manner crucified before our eyes (Gal. 3:1).

🔤 Morphology · 어형

💬 Idiom · 관용

쉬운 영어 / Modern English

S18★★★the counterfactual challenge — Of what use, then, were… + the viz.-creed›

Of what use, then, were the erection in churches of so many crosses of wood and stone, silver and gold, if this doctrine were faithfully and honestly preached—viz. Christ died that he might bear our curse upon the tree, that he might expiate our sins by the sacrifice of his body, wash them in his blood, and, in short, reconcile us to God the Father?

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • expiate < ex- + piare (to appease, make good; cf. pious) — to pay out guilt in full; erection < erigere (to raise up — the setting-up of the crosses, echoing §6's erected); viz. = videlicet < videre licet, "one may see."
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • of what use is X? = X가 무슨 소용인가; in short = 요컨대; bear the curse = 저주를 짊어지다.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • Of what use, then, were the erection → What use, then, would it be to set up
  • viz. → namely
  • that he might bear / expiate → to bear / to atone for
  • in short → 그대로 (already modern).
S19★the arithmetic — one doctrine, a thousand crosses›

From this one doctrine the people would learn more than from a thousand crosses of wood and stone.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • doctrine < docere (to teach) — the thing taught; the whole section's fight compressed into one etymology: only doctrina teaches.
전체 해설 더보기

One point: the fronted From this one doctrine sets up the naked arithmetic — one doctrine outweighs a thousand crosses; would learn continues S18's hypothetical mood. One sermon of the cross teaches more than a forest of them. Move on.

💬 Idiom · 관용

  • the avaricious → greedy people
  • heavenly instructor → heavenly teacher
  • it may be true that → it may well be true that.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — the Were + S + to-V inverted conditional (S2, S5)

Were + S + to-V ~ , S′ + would/might/should + V
= If + S + were + to-V ~ , S′ + would + V
  (hypothetical future; drop "if," invert "were"; WERE for every person)

⚠️ Traps:
  ① were → was 교체 함정: inversion에서는 인칭 불문 WERE.
  ② 주절 조동사는 과거형(would/might) — will/may가 오면 오답.
  ③ "Were I to admit" = 양보적 가정(arguendo)일 수 있다 — 인정이 아니다.

Transformation drill: If the church were to lose the preaching of the Word, no army of statues would restore her sight. → Were the church to lose the preaching of the Word, no army of statues would restore her sight.

Formula 2 — fronted-complement inversion (S10)

[Adj/Comp phrase] + BE + Subject
e.g. Very different from these follies IS the doctrine (…).
= The doctrine (…) is very different from these follies.

⚠️ Traps:
  ① BE의 수일치는 "뒤에 오는" 진짜 주어와 한다 (doctrine → is; follies 아님!).
  ② 무거운 주어(관계절 달린)를 뒤로 보내는 end-weight + 화제 전환 기능.

Transformation drill: The reverence that springs from true knowledge is very different from superstition. → Very different from superstition is the reverence that springs from true knowledge.

Formula 3 — the mandative that-clause after nouns of command (S11)

His injunction/command/order + is + that + S + (should) + V원형/be p.p.
e.g. His injunction is, that the doctrine … should there be set forth …

⚠️ Traps:
  ① that절 안은 should + V 또는 원형(가정법 현재) — 직설법 is/are가 오면 어색.
  ② Beveridge식 "is, that"의 콤마는 house style — 오류 아님.

Transformation drill: His command is that the gospel should be set forth to all nations by the preaching of the Word.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The plea on trial. Around 599–600 Gregory the Great wrote two letters to Serenus, bishop of Marseilles, who had been smashing images in his churches: don't adore them, Gregory said, but don't destroy them either — "what writing presents to readers, a picture presents to the unlearned who behold it." That sentence became the medieval charter for church imagery: images are the books of the unlearned. Calvin has spent three sections dismantling it in courtroom order — §5: Scripture rules images out as teachers of falsehood; §6: the older fathers and Elvira rule against them, with Augustine and even pagan Varro concurring; §7 today: (3) the images themselves testify — their "meretricious ornaments" disqualify them on sight — and (4) God has appointed a different method altogether.

The positive doctrine is the real center. Notice that Calvin's decisive move is not iconoclasm but pedagogy: S11's pairing of Word preached + sacraments administered is the Reformed definition of how God teaches his church (it will flower in Book 4 as the marks of the true church). The sacraments matter enormously here: Calvin's God is not anti-visual — baptism and the Supper are visible words (Augustine's own phrase, verbum visibile). The antithesis is man-devised images vs. God-instituted signs. And S17's reading of Gal. 3:1 (προεγράφη — Christ "placarded" in preaching) supplies the positive theory of the only authorized portrait.

Reception — hear the echo in the catechism. Heidelberg Catechism (1563), Q. 98: "But may not images be tolerated in churches as books for the laity? No; for we must not pretend to be wiser than God, who will have his people taught, not by dumb idols, but by the lively preaching of his Word." That is this very section, compressed — down to the word dumb. The Second Helvetic Confession (1566) runs the same line. On the other side, Trent (Session 25, 1563) reaffirmed images' didactic use with abuses corrected — the two confessional roads split exactly at this argument. Modern heirs of Calvin's point (preaching as the church's true visibility) run through Barth's doctrine of proclamation.

Over-reading guardrail. Do not turn Calvin into an enemy of art. In §12 — five days from now — he will say plainly that sculpture and painting are gifts of God and mark out their lawful use. Today's target is precise: images of God, images in worship, and the claim that they can do the Word's teaching job. Nor does S6's brothel comparison make Calvin a prude scoring cheap points: he is testing the plea's own premise (a book of sanctity must at minimum be decent) against the actual inventory of late-medieval and Renaissance church art. Keep the argument's edges sharp and its scope narrow — that's how Calvin cut it.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. Gal. 3:1 — S17 (explicit citation). "O foolish Galatians… before whose eyes Jesus Christ hath been evidently set forth, crucified among you." The verb προεγράφη (proegraphē) = publicly placarded, painted up on a notice-board. Calvin's use: the apostle's own metaphor makes preaching the God-commissioned portraiture of Christ — the plank on which the whole positive argument stands.
  2. Gal. 3:13 / Deut. 21:23 / 1 Pet. 2:24 — S18 ("bear our curse upon the tree"). "Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us" — with Peter's ἐπὶ τὸ ξύλον ("on the tree") supplying the wording. Calvin's creed-in-a-question quotes Scripture's own atonement grammar.
  3. Hab. 2:18–19 (cf. 1 Cor. 12:2) — S16 (echo). The prophet mocks the maker who says to the dumb stone, "Arise, it shall teach!" — and Paul remembers the Corinthians "carried away unto these dumb idols." Calvin's use: savage transfer — the idol's defining defect (mute) is pinned on the clergy who hired idols to teach.
  4. Matt. 13:11 — S13 ("the saving mysteries of his kingdom"). "It is given unto you to know the mysteries of the kingdom (τὰ μυστήρια τῆς βασιλείας)." Calvin's use: the "unlearned" hold Christ's own student ID — picture-books demote whom God promoted.
  5. 1 Cor. 1:21; 2:6–7 — S13, S15 (echo). God's wisdom, hidden from the wise, given by "the foolishness of preaching" — behind both celestial philosophy and the claim that preaching is the only doctrine fit to instruct.
  6. 2 Cor. 5:18–19; Rev. 1:5 — S18 ("reconcile us to God… wash them in his blood"). καταλλάσσω (reconcile) and "washed us from our sins in his own blood" — the last two clauses of the viz.-creed, each a verbatim biblical cadence.

8 — Exam Problems –

① Grammar — one of the four underlined parts is WRONG. Find it.

(A) Were the church to lose true preaching, images (B) would not supply the loss; very different from such helps (C) are the doctrine which God has appointed; his injunction is that the Word (D) be set forth to all.

Answer: (C). Formula 2: in fronted-complement inversion the verb agrees with the TRUE subject, which follows — the doctrine (singular) → is, not are. The plural bait helps sits right before the verb, exactly as follies does in S10. (A) correct inversion (Formula 1); (B) correct past-form modal in the consequence; (D) correct mandative subjunctive after injunction is that (Formula 3).

② Comprehension — which statement agrees with the passage?

(a) Calvin denies that anyone in his day genuinely needs images as books. (b) Calvin grants that crosses of gold and silver, at least, provide useful instruction. (c) Calvin urges churches to adorn their images more beautifully so that they may serve as books of sanctity. (d) Calvin admits that many people now cannot do without such books, but blames this on their being deprived of true preaching.

Answer: (d). S14 (not a few… cannot want such books — want = lack) + S15 (defrauded of the only doctrine). (a) is the want-flip (Trap 1); (b) reads S20's sarcasm straight (Trap 3 — the avaricious learn only greed); (c) reverses S8's ironic conditional into advice (Trap 2).

③ Composition — translate using Formula 1 (inverted conditional):

만약 교회가 십자가의 설교를 잃는다면, 천 개의 십자가상도 그 자리를 채우지 못할 것이다.
