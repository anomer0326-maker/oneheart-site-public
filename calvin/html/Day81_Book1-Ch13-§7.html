Day 81 — Book 1, Chapter 13, §7

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday Calvin defined the person. Today he starts proving that there are three of them who are God — and he tells you the running order of the next nine sections in his first two sentences: Before proceeding farther, it will be necessary to prove the divinity of the Son and the Holy Spirit. Thereafter, we shall see how they differ from each other. Underline that. Unity first, distinction second. It is not a stylistic choice; it is the whole Reformed method of Trinitarian argument in one line, and the examiner will absolutely offer you an option that reverses it.

Now look at what he actually does. The section is one argument run four times, from four witnesses, in ascending order of clarity: Peter (S5–S7), Moses (S8–S11), Solomon (S12–S13), and finally John, who gives — Calvin's own superlative — the clearest explanation (S17). Between Moses and Solomon he stops to fight off one objection (I know prattlers would easily evade this, by saying that Word is used for order or command — S10) and answers it not with a counter-argument but with an appeal to authority: **the apostles are better expositors**. Watch that move. Calvin does not out-argue the objector; he out-quotes him. That is the deepest thing in the section and it is easy to read past.

And the thesis the four witnesses are driving at is a single negation: the Word is not a noise. it were certainly most absurd to imagine that it is only a fleeting and evanescent voice, which is sent out into the air, and comes forth beyond God himself (S3). Against that: the wisdom ever dwelling with God (S4) — yesterday's dwelling (§6 S6, of the Word in the Father) now reappearing in Proverbs 8. A voice leaves the speaker. Wisdom stays with him. The whole section hangs on that contrast.

Grammatically, today is the day of the counterfactual copula and the rhetorical question. it **were** certainly most absurd to imagine… (S3) — that is the sixth were in six days, exactly as previewed yesterday, and then, eleven sentences later, Calvin does it again: it **were** trifling and foolish to imagine… (S13). Same frame, same verb imagine, twice in one section. Learn it once, own it forever. Then the question that is not a question: For **why does** he distinctly narrate…, **unless that** the unsearchable glory of God **might** shine forth in his image? (S9) — an assertion wearing a question mark. Then a whole family of inversions and interruptions: just **as did** the apostles (S5), **whose organs the prophets were** (S7), **To this** our Saviour's words **refer** (S14), and the monster of S17, where the subject the Word and its verb was are separated by eleven words and two commas.

One warning about the page. Three page-break seams fall inside words (unsearch-|able S9, myster-|ious S13, to-|gether S17), and a page number plus the running header sit between S17 and S18, with a further margin number inside S19. All joined silently. Details in the text note.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. The counterfactual copula: it were + ADJ + to V — twice today (S3, S13). it **were** certainly most absurd **to imagine** that it is only a fleeting and evanescent voice (S3); for it **were** trifling and foolish **to imagine** any temporary command (S13). ⚠️⚠️ were is the past subjunctive of the counterfactual, not a past indicative: it were X = it would be X. The apodosis-without-a-protasis: the if-clause is swallowed into the infinitive — if one were to imagine this, it would be absurd. Restore it and the sentence becomes ordinary: to imagine X would be absurd. Don't translate it it was absurd; the absurdity is hypothetical, not historical. ⚠️ Note the intensifier stack in S3: certainly (speaker's certainty) + most (superlative degree) — it would surely be utterly absurd. And in the same family, watch the delayed appositive that-clause in S7: the inference is irresistible, **that** the Word was truly God — the that-clause is in apposition to the inference, held back behind the predicate by a comma. Modern English wants the inference that … is irresistible; Beveridge extraposes. Comma + that after an abstract noun = apposition, not a relative. Same shape in S16: The meaning therefore is, **that** God spoke…. Formula 1.
  2. The rhetorical question of purpose: why does S V …, unless that … might …? (S9). For **why does** he distinctly narrate that God … said, Let there be this—let there be that, **unless that** the unsearchable glory of God **might shine forth** in his image? ⚠️⚠️⚠️ Three things at once. (i) why …? + unless …? is not a request for information; it is an assertion in interrogative dress: he narrates it for no other reason than that…. Flip it to a statement before you translate. (ii) **unless that** = except in order that — that here is the purpose-that (Lat. nisi ut), not the complementiser. Modern English has lost the pairing; do not read it as unless it is the case that. (iii) **might** is the purpose subjunctive that follows it (ut … refulgeat) — not "perhaps", not past-tense "was able to". might shine forth = should shine forth, so as to shine forth. ⚠️ And distinctly = expressly, in so many words — not "clearly to the eye". Formula 2.
  3. Inversion and interruption — four specimens, one drill: find the finite verb. (a) the ancient prophets spake by the Spirit of Christ just **as did** the apostles (S5) — inversion in a comparative clause; as did the apostles = as the apostles did; the auxiliary did stands for spake. (b) that Spirit, **whose organs the prophets were** (S7) — a possessive relative with a predicate nominative fronted: restore to the prophets were whose organs → the prophets were the organs of that Spirit. ⚠️ The subject of the relative clause is the prophets, not organs. (c) **To this** our Saviour's words **refer** (S14) — fronted prepositional phrase, subject delayed; the verb agrees with words (plural), and the exam will offer you refers. (d) S17, the big one: the Word—which was from the beginning, God and with God, **was**, together with God the Father, the maker of all things — subject the Word, then a dash, a relative clause, a comma, the finite verb was, then another interruption, then the complement. Eleven words between subject and verb. ⚠️ Cover the interruptions and the spine is three words: the Word was the maker. Formula 3. Plus two correlatives in the same family: in such a manner **as** left the Word his peculiar part (S16 — the relative as, subject of left, after such) and **as** all revelations … are duly designated…, **so** the highest place must be assigned (S19 — the as … so proportional).

Trap words I will test. organs (S7) ⚠️⚠️⚠️ = Gk ὄργανον, instruments, tools — the prophets are the Spirit's instruments; nothing to do with body parts or with music. nod (S11) ⚠️⚠️⚠️ = Lat. nutus, an authoritative signal of the will, a beck — the nod or command of the Son = the Son's sovereign signal; nothing to do with drowsiness or agreement. prattlers (S10) ⚠️⚠️ = idle chatterers (Lat. garruli) — a dismissive term for shallow disputants, not children. counsel (S13) ⚠️⚠️ = purpose, decree (consilium) — not advice. substantial (S18, S19) ⚠️⚠️ = having real, subsisting being (from substantia) — not "considerable, sizeable"; that substantial Word = the Word that actually subsists, as against a mere utterance. simply (S15) ⚠️⚠️⚠️ = merely, only in passing — what Moses simply touched = what Moses merely touched on. This is the same adverb you learned yesterday meaning absolutely, without qualification (§6 S4, S8). One day apart, opposite work. Let context decide. touched (S15) = touched on, mentioned lightly (attigit). evanescent (S3) = vanishing (e- + vanescere). intermediate (S8) = standing in the middle (as the mediating factor). expositors (S10) = interpreters, expounders. evade (S10) = get round, slip out of (e- + vadere, go out). oracles (S4) = divine utterances (orare) — not fortune-tellers. presiding at (S12) = taking the chief place at. sane (S12) = sound, healthy (sanus). trifling (S13) = frivolous, not serious. duly (S19) = rightly, as is fitting. liable to (S19) = subject to, open to (ligare, to bind). manifested (S6) = made visible, brought into the open. spake (S5) = archaic past of speak. hitherto (S14) = until now. peculiarity (S18) = a distinguishing property — yesterday's peculiar, nominalised. peculiar part (S16) = his own part.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 19 sentences –

7. Before proceeding farther, it will be necessary to prove the divinity of the Son and the Holy Spirit. Thereafter, we shall see how they differ from each other. When the Word of God is set before us in the Scriptures, it were certainly most absurd to imagine that it is only a fleeting and evanescent voice, which is sent out into the air, and comes forth beyond God himself, as was the case with the communications made to the patriarchs, and all the prophecies. The reference is rather to the wisdom ever dwelling with God, and by which all oracles and prophecies were inspired. For, as Peter testifies (1 Pet. 1:11), the ancient prophets spake by the Spirit of Christ just as did the apostles, and all who after them were ministers of the heavenly doctrine. But as Christ was not yet manifested, we necessarily understand that the Word was begotten of the Father before all ages. But if that Spirit, whose organs the prophets were, belonged to the Word, the inference is irresistible, that the Word was truly God. And this is clearly enough shown by Moses in his account of the creation, where he places the Word as intermediate. For why does he distinctly narrate that God, in creating each of his works, said, Let there be this—let there be that, unless that the unsearchable glory of God might shine forth in his image? I know prattlers would easily evade this, by saying that Word is used for order or command; but the apostles are better expositors, when they tell us that the worlds were created by the Son, and that he sustains all things by his mighty word (Heb. 1:2). For we here see that word is used for the nod or command of the Son, who is himself the eternal and essential Word of the Father. And no man of sane mind can have any doubt as to Solomon’s meaning, when he introduces Wisdom as begotten by God, and presiding at the creation of the world, and all other divine operations (Prov. 8:22). For it were trifling and foolish to imagine any temporary command at a time when God was pleased to execute his fixed and eternal counsel, and something more still mysterious. To this our Saviour’s words refer, “My Father worketh hitherto, and I work,” (John 5:17). In thus affirming, that from the foundation of the world he constantly worked with the Father, he gives a clearer explanation of what Moses simply touched. The meaning therefore is, that God spoke in such a manner as left the Word his peculiar part in the work, and thus made the operation common to both. But the clearest explanation is given by John, when he states that the Word—which was from the beginning, God and with God, was, together with God the Father, the maker of all things. For he both attributes a substantial and permanent essence to the Word, assigning to it a certain peculiarity, and distinctly showing how God spoke the world into being. Therefore, as all revelations from heaven are duly designated by the title of the Word of God, so the highest place must be assigned to that substantial Word, the source of all inspiration, which, as being liable to no variation, remains for ever one and the same with God, and is God.

The engine, in one line: a voice leaves its speaker; the Word never does — therefore the Word is not a thing God made but a subsistence God is. Every sentence in the section is a variation on the contrast set up in S3–S4: sent out into the air, and comes forth beyond God himself versus ever dwelling with God and, at the very end, remains for ever one and the same with God, and is God. ⚠️ Notice the ring structure: dwelling with God (S4) → remains for ever one and the same with God (S19). The section opens and closes on the same preposition, and the closing clause escalates it — with God becomes is God, which is exactly John 1:1's own move (πρὸς τὸν θεόν → θεὸς ἦν). Calvin has built §7 in the shape of the verse he is going to quote at the end of it.

And notice the method, which is the most examinable thing here. The argument is cumulative and graded: Moses clearly enough shown (S8) → Christ a clearer explanation (S15) → John the clearest explanation (S17). Positive, comparative, superlative, in that order, spread across ten sentences. ⚠️ This is not decoration. Calvin is telling you that the Old Testament witnesses are real but incomplete, and that the New Testament is the authoritative commentary on them — which is precisely the principle he deploys against the objector in S10: the apostles are **better expositors**.

Examiner's Eye — six traps.

  1. ⚠️⚠️⚠️ The order in S1–S2 is divinity first, difference second — and the exam will reverse it. it will be necessary **to prove the divinity** of the Son and the Holy Spirit. **Thereafter**, we shall see **how they differ**. An option reading "Calvin now turns to explain how the three persons are distinguished from one another" is a programme-flip: that is §§17–20, not §7. Thereafter is the whole point of S2's existence.
  2. ⚠️⚠️⚠️ S3 does not deny that God ever spoke by a transient voice. Read the tail: **as was the case with** the communications made to the patriarchs, and all the prophecies. Calvin grants that those were passing voices; what he denies is that the Word of John 1 is that kind of thing. An option reading "Calvin denies that God addressed the patriarchs by an audible voice" has over-reached the scope. The as was the case with clause is a concession, not a target.
  3. ⚠️⚠️ prattlers would easily evade this — Calvin concedes the evasion is EASY (S10). He does not say the objection is stupid or impossible; he says it is available, and then defeats it by authority, not by logic: but the apostles are better expositors. An option reading "Calvin shows that the objection is self-contradictory" or "no one could seriously maintain…" has misread the move. ⚠️ Compare no man of sane mind can have any doubt (S12) — there the dismissal is total. Two different registers, two sentences apart. Don't blend them.
  4. ⚠️⚠️ S11 holds two senses of word in one sentence — lowercase and capital. word is used for the **nod or command** of the Son, **who is himself** the eternal and essential **Word** of the Father. So Calvin concedes the objector's lexicography and denies his conclusion: yes, word can mean a command; that command is the Son's, and the Son is the Word. An option reading "Calvin agrees that the Word in Genesis means simply God's command" has collapsed the two senses and thrown away who is himself.
  5. ⚠️⚠️ simply in S15 means merely, not absolutely. he gives a clearer explanation of what Moses **simply** touched = what Moses merely touched on. Yesterday (§6 S4, S8) the same adverb meant without qualification. ⚠️ An option that makes S15 say "what Moses stated absolutely" has imported yesterday's sense. The giveaway is touched — a light verb; simply touched = only brushed against.
  6. ⚠️⚠️ John, not Moses, gives the clearest explanation (S17). The superlative is deliberate and the section is built to reach it. An option ranking Moses's account highest, or treating John as merely corroborating Moses, is a direction-flip on the graded series (clearly enough S8 → a clearer S15 → the clearest S17).

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

What §7 is, in the architecture. The chapter's synopsis for this section: Proofs of the eternal Deity of the Son. The Son the λόγος of the Eternal Father, and, therefore, the Son Eternal God. Objection. Reply. §§2–5 defended the words; §6 defined the thing; §7 opens the proof. And the proof block runs long: §§7–13 on the Son (7–8 the Word's eternity, 9–10 the divine names, 11 the divine works, 12–13 the worship and the New Testament testimonies), §§14–15 on the Spirit, and only then §§16–20 on the distinctions — which is exactly the order S1–S2 announced. ⚠️ So S2's Thereafter is not a throwaway; it is a table of contents, and it holds for thirteen sections.

1. Why "the Word is not a voice" is the load-bearing claim. The target is a family of positions that make the Logos something less than a subsistent person: (a) the Sabellian/modalist reading — the Word as God's self-expression in time, with no eternal standing (§6's opponent, still in view); (b) the reading that makes the Word an emanation, real but derivative; and above all (c) Michael Servetus (De Trinitatis Erroribus, 1531; Christianismi Restitutio, 1553), who held that the eternal Logos was not a person but a disposition or utterance of God, personalised only in the incarnate Christ. ⚠️ Calvin does not name him in §7 — he names him openly at §§22–23 — but the phrase only a fleeting and evanescent voice, which is sent out into the air is aimed squarely at that family. Guardrail: do not identify prattlers (garruli) with Servetus specifically. It is a general term of dismissal for shallow disputants, and Calvin uses it freely.

2. The Word in the Old Testament, and what Calvin does not argue. There was a well-worn Jewish and patristic path from Genesis 1's and God said to a personified divine Word — the Targums' Memra, Philo's Logos, Justin's Logos spermatikos. ⚠️ Calvin takes almost none of it. Notice what he actually appeals to: not to Philo, not to speculation about the Hebrew dabar, but to the apostles' own reading — the apostles are better expositors (S10), Heb. 1:2–3, John 1:1–3. His method is to read the Old Testament backwards from the New, not forwards from philosophy. This is the same instinct that made him refuse Boethius's definition yesterday: a doctrine cannot rest on a term the Bible does not control.

3. Proverbs 8:22 — the single most contested verse of the Arian controversy. Hebrew יְהוָה קָנָנִי רֵאשִׁית דַּרְכּוֹ — the LORD possessed/acquired me at the beginning of his way. LXX: κύριος ἔκτισέν με ἀρχὴν ὁδῶν αὐτοῦ — the Lord created me. ⚠️⚠️ Arius built his case on that Greek verb: if Wisdom is created, and Wisdom is Christ, then there was when he was not. Athanasius answered it at length (Orations against the Arians II.18–82), arguing the verse speaks of the incarnate economy, not the eternal generation. ⚠️ So Calvin is doing something bold here: he cites the Arians' own proof-text as a proof of the Son's eternity — and he can, because he reads it with 8:24–25's I was brought forth (חוֹלָלְתִּי, twice) and 8:30's אָמוֹן (as one brought up with him, or as a master workman). begotten by God, and presiding at the creation is 8:24–25 plus 8:30, filed under the reference 8:22. That is why no man of sane mind can have any doubt is a stronger claim than it looks — Calvin is taking territory, not conceding it.

4. 1 Peter 1:11, and the argument from whose Spirit. the Spirit of Christ which was in them (τὸ ἐν αὐτοῖς πνεῦμα Χριστοῦ). ⚠️ The inference of S5–S7 is compact and worth laying out because the exam can attack any joint: (i) the Spirit at work in the prophets is called Christ's; (ii) Christ was not yet manifested, so the possession cannot date from the incarnation; (iii) therefore what possessed that Spirit existed before; (iv) and since only God has a Spirit that is his own, the inference is irresistible, that the Word was truly God. ⚠️ Step (iv) is the one Calvin leaves implicit. It rests on the Old Testament identification of the Spirit as the Spirit of the LORD: to have that Spirit as one's own is to be that LORD. Guardrail: this is an argument for the Word's deity from a relation, not a proof-text saying "the Word is God." Don't oversell it as the latter.

5. Afterlife — the Word after Calvin. Two receptions worth knowing, and one warning. Barth (Church Dogmatics I/1 §§4–6) built his prolegomena on the threefold form of the Word of God — revealed, written, preached — and S19 is startlingly close to the move: as all revelations from heaven are duly designated by the title of the Word of God, **so** the highest place must be assigned to that substantial Word. ⚠️ Calvin ranks the senses within one name; Barth systematises the ranking. Warfield (Calvin's Doctrine of the Trinity, 1909) read §7 as the opening of Calvin's characteristically exegetical rather than speculative Trinitarianism — every step in the section is a citation. And the warning: modern Old Testament scholarship does not read Genesis 1's and God said as implying a personal Word, and reads Proverbs 8 as personified Wisdom, a literary figure. ⚠️ Calvin's argument does not actually require the modern reading to be false — his real claim (S10) is that the apostles' reading is decisive, which is a claim about authority, not about Hebrew semantics. Note the difference; the exam may test whether you have.

Over-reading guard-rail. ⚠️ Six limits. (a) intermediate (S8) does not make the Word an instrument outside God. Yesterday's indissoluble tie, being incapable of separation (§6 S6) stands; S16 says the operation is common to both. (b) S3 does not deny that God spoke to the patriarchs by transient voices. as was the case with is a concession. The denial attaches to only. (c) his image (S9) is not settled. The Latin in eius imagine is as ambiguous as the English. Do not resolve it to the Son and build on it. (d) before all ages (S6) is not a date. Tomorrow's §8 makes the point explicitly: if any inquire how long, he will find it was without beginning. (e) (Heb. 1:2) in S10 covers 1:2–3; (Prov. 8:22) in S12 covers 8:22–31. Period citation practice references the passage. Not slips — do not correct them inside a quotation. (f) S7 proves the Word's deity, not the Word's distinction. The distinction is §§16–20 — exactly what S2 deferred. Keep the two questions apart or the whole chapter collapses into a blur.

① Grammar — which underlined part is wrong?

ⓐ When the Word of God is set before us in the Scriptures, it were certainly most absurd to imagine that it is only a fleeting and evanescent voice. ⓑ For, as Peter testifies, the ancient prophets spake by the Spirit of Christ just as did the apostles, and all who after them were ministers of the heavenly doctrine. ⓒ For why does he distinctly narrate that God, in creating each of his works, said, Let there be this—let there be that, unless that the unsearchable glory of God might shine forth in his image? ⓓ To this our Saviour’s words refers, “My Father worketh hitherto, and I work,” (John 5:17).

Answer: ⓓ.

출제 의도. Formula 3(c): agreement after a fronted prepositional phrase. The subject is our Saviour's **words** — plural — so the verb must be **refer**. The bait is the possessive Saviour's sitting immediately before the verb slot, which makes the eye take a singular subject; the fronted To this removes the usual left-to-right cue. ⚠️ Fix the habit, not the instance: when a sentence opens with a prepositional phrase, find the subject after the verb before you check agreement. Now check why the others stand. ⓐ is Formula 1: it **were** … to imagine is the counterfactual copula (= it would be); it was would date a hypothetical, and it is would flatten the subjunctive — were with a singular dummy it is correct precisely because it is subjunctive, not indicative. ⓑ is Formula 3(a): comparative inversion, as did the apostles = as the apostles did, with did standing for the whole predicate spake by the Spirit of Christ; as the apostles did is also grammatical but loses the period cadence. ⓒ is Formula 2: unless that … **might** shine forth — that is the purpose-that (nisi ut) and might is the purpose subjunctive it requires; may would be the present-sequence form and can would destroy the construction.

ⓐ Calvin argues that since every revelation from heaven is called "the word of God," the Word of John 1 should likewise be understood as God's spoken command. ⓑ Because the prophets spoke by the Spirit of Christ although Christ was not yet manifested, Calvin concludes that the Word was begotten of the Father before all ages and was truly God. ⓒ Calvin holds that Moses's creation account gives the clearest explanation of the Word's part in creation, John's prologue merely confirming what Moses had already established. ⓓ Calvin dismisses the objection that "Word" means order or command as an evasion that no serious person could possibly make.

출제 의도. Four options, four failure modes. - ⓐ is the direction-flip on S19 and S11. S19's as … so runs the other way: because all revelations share the title word of God, **the highest place must be assigned to that substantial Word** — the shared name ranks the substantial Word first, it does not reduce him to an utterance. And S11 has already located the "command" sense inside the Son (the nod or command of the Son, **who is himself** the eternal and essential Word). ⓐ reverses the inference of the section's last sentence. - ⓑ is true, and note it requires all three sentences of the syllogism. Drop S6 and the argument has no middle term; drop S7 and it stops short of deity. ⚠️ Note also that ⓑ says although Christ was not yet manifested — S6's causal as. The manifestation clause is the reason, not a concession about Peter. - ⓒ is the direction-flip on the graded series. clearly enough shown by Moses (S8) → a **clearer** explanation (S15) → **the clearest** explanation is given by John (S17). John is at the top and Moses at the bottom. ⚠️ The wording merely confirming also inverts S10's principle: the apostles are **better** expositors. - ⓓ is the over-reach on S10. Calvin says prattlers would **easily** evade this — he concedes the evasion is available and answers it by authority, not by calling it impossible. ⚠️ The bait is S12's no man of sane mind can have any doubt, two sentences later, which is a total dismissal — but of Solomon's meaning, not of the objection. Options that transplant one sentence's temperature onto another are the commonest trap in this section.

③ Composition — apply Formulas 1, 2 and 3

Render into Beveridge-style English, using all three of today's formulas:

"성령이 하나님의 깊은 것을 통달하신다고 성경이 우리 앞에 제시할 때, 그것이 단지 피조된 능력일 뿐이라고 상상하는 것은 참으로 가장 불합리한 일일 것이다. 바울은 어찌하여 그 영이 하나님의 것들을 아신다고 명시적으로 진술하는가 — 하나님의 헤아릴 수 없는 영광이 그분 안에서 빛나게 하려 함이 아니고 무엇이겠는가? 선지자들이 그 도구였던 그 영이 하나님께 속하였으니, 사도들이 그러했던 것처럼. 그러므로 그 결론은 저항할 수 없다, 곧 그 영은 참으로 하나님이시라는 것."

Model answer:

When the Spirit is set before us in the Scriptures as searching the deep things of God, it were certainly most absurd to imagine that he is only a created power. For why does Paul distinctly narrate that the Spirit knoweth the things of God, unless that the unsearchable glory of God might shine forth in him? That Spirit, whose organs the prophets were, belonged to God, just as did the apostles. Therefore the inference is irresistible, that the Spirit was truly God.

출제 의도 및 채점 기준. Six things are being checked. 1. Formula 1, the counterfactual copula. **it were** certainly most absurd **to imagine** that…. ⚠️ Three checks: were (not was, not is, not would be it); the stacked intensifiers certainly most in that order; and the to-infinitive carrying the absorbed condition. Writing it would be most absurd is correct modern English and forfeits the point — the exercise is the period form. 2. Formula 1, the delayed appositive. the inference **is** irresistible, **that** the Spirit was truly God. ⚠️ The comma before that is required; the inference that the Spirit was truly God is irresistible is grammatical and loses the Beveridge shape. Test yourself with namely. 3. Formula 2, the rhetorical question of purpose. For **why does** Paul **distinctly narrate** that…, **unless that** … **might** shine forth…? ⚠️ Four checks: the interrogative why does, the adverb distinctly (= expressly), the pairing unless that (not unless, not except that), and **might** (not may, not can, not would). Drop the question mark and the whole construction dies. 4. Formula 3(a) and 3(b), both. **whose organs** the prophets **were** — the fronted predicate nominative, with the prophets as subject and were plural to match; and just **as did** the apostles — the comparative inversion. ⚠️ Writing whose organs were the prophets inverts the wrong element; writing just as the apostles did forfeits the inversion point. 5. Vocabulary control. set before us (S3), organs (S7, = instruments), distinctly (S9), unsearchable (S9), shine forth (S9), irresistible (S7), truly God (S7). ⚠️ searching the deep things of God is 1 Cor. 2:10 in KJV form; knoweth is the KJV of 2:11. Keeping the archaic third-singular -eth inside a scriptural allusion is part of the register. 6. Argumentative accuracy — heaviest. The negation must fall on **only** (only a created power), exactly as S3's falls on only a fleeting … voice — the point is not that the Spirit is not a power but that he is not merely one. The whose organs clause must run from the possessive (whose Spirit it is) to the conclusion (what he therefore is), reproducing S5→S7. And the final sentence must predicate deity of the Spirit, not of the prophets or the apostles. ⚠️ Write it were absurd to imagine that he is a created power without only and you have made Calvin deny that the Spirit is powerful — the exact over-reach of Trap 2.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary. Calvin sets the programme — it will be necessary **to prove the divinity** of the Son and the Holy Spirit. **Thereafter**, we shall see **how they differ** (S1–S2) — and then destroys the reading that makes the Word a noise: **it were** certainly most absurd **to imagine** that it is only a fleeting and evanescent voice … as was the case with the communications made to the patriarchs (S3), for the reference is rather to **the wisdom ever dwelling with God** (S4). Four witnesses follow, in ascending clarity. Peter: the prophets spake by **the Spirit of Christ** just as did the apostles (S5), and since Christ was not yet manifested, we necessarily understand that the Word was **begotten of the Father before all ages** (S6), so that if that Spirit, **whose organs the prophets were**, belonged to the Word, **the inference is irresistible, that the Word was truly God** (S7). Moses, clearly enough (S8), places the Word as **intermediate** — for **why does** he distinctly narrate that God … said, Let there be this—let there be that, **unless that** the unsearchable glory of God **might** shine forth in his image? (S9); and when prattlers **would easily evade** this, by saying that Word is used for order or command, the answer is not a counter-argument but an authority — but **the apostles are better expositors** (S10), so that word is used for the **nod** or command of the Son, **who is himself** the eternal and essential Word (S11). Solomon: no man of sane mind can have any doubt that Wisdom is **begotten** by God, and **presiding** at the creation (S12), since **it were** trifling and foolish **to imagine** any temporary command where God executes his **fixed and eternal counsel** (S13). Christ himself: **To this** our Saviour's words **refer** (S14), and in affirming that he constantly worked with the Father he gives **a clearer** explanation of what Moses **simply touched** (S15) — God spoke **in such a manner as left** the Word his **peculiar part** in the work, and thus made the operation **common to both** (S16). And John, **the clearest** explanation: the Word—which was from the beginning, God and with God, **was**, together with God the Father, **the maker of all things** (S17), attributing to it a **substantial** and **permanent** essence, a certain **peculiarity**, and the how of creation (S18). Therefore — **as** all revelations from heaven are duly designated by the title of the Word of God, **so** the highest place must be assigned to that **substantial** Word … which,as being liable to no variation, remains for ever one and the same with God, and is God` (S19). A voice leaves its speaker; the Word never does.

And the three grammar points, once more. ① The counterfactual copula and the delayed appositive — **it were** certainly most absurd **to imagine** (S3) and **it were** trifling and foolish **to imagine** (S13): it were X = it would be X, the if absorbed into the infinitive; plus the inference is irresistible, **that**… (S7) and The meaning therefore is, **that**… (S16): abstract noun + predicate + comma + appositive that-clause, testable with namely. ② The rhetorical question of exclusive purpose — **why does** he distinctly narrate …, **unless that** the … glory **might** shine forth…? (S9): flip it into an assertion first; unless that = except in order that; might = purpose subjunctive, never perhaps. ③ Inversion and interruption — just **as did** the apostles (S5), **whose organs** the prophets **were** (S7), **To this** our Saviour's words **refer** (S14), and the eleven-word subject–verb gap of S17: cover the interruptions, read the spine; plus the relative as after such (S16) and the proportional as … so (S19). And five things to keep: organs = instruments, nod = sovereign signal, counsel = decree, substantial = really subsisting, simply (S15) = merely — the opposite of yesterday's simply; the concession in as was the case with (S3, Trap 2); the concession in would easily evade (S10, Trap 3); the graded series clearly enough → a clearer → the clearest (S8, S15, S17); and Thereafter (S2) — divinity first, difference later.
