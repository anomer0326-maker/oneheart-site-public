Day 37 — Book 1, Chapter 8, §1

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday Calvin shut the door: Scripture, carrying its own evidence along with it, deigns not to submit to proofs and arguments. Today he opens a chapter of thirteen sections made entirely of proofs and arguments.

Look at that for one second before we go further. If you have understood Chapter 7 and you now read Chapter 8's title —

THE CREDIBILITY OF SCRIPTURE SUFFICIENTLY PROVED IN SO FAR AS NATURAL REASON ADMITS

— you should feel a jolt. Isn't this the very thing he just said was preposterous (§4) and beneath Scripture's dignity (§5)? Every hostile reader of Calvin since Bellarmine has said: caught you.

He is not caught. He told you the answer four days ago and he tells you again in the first sentence of today's section. preposterous never meant false — it meant prae + posterus, out of order. So Calvin does not delete the proofs. He puts them second, and today's §1 is where he shows you what a proof looks like once it has been put second. Watch the first sentence do it:

   In vain WERE the authority of Scripture
        fortified by argument,                  ← that's Chapter 8 (this chapter!)
        or supported by the consent of the Church,  ← that's Chapter 7 (refuted)
        or confirmed by any other helps,
   IF unaccompanied by an assurance higher and stronger
        than human Judgment can give.           ← that's Chapter 7 (testimonium)

Calvin opens his chapter of arguments by putting his own chapter of arguments under the knife first. That is not a contradiction; that is a man who knows exactly what he is doing. And then S3 gives you the hinge word: those same proofs, received after the Spirit's work, **become** most appropriate helps. Not are. Become. One verb carries the whole two-tier structure.

Then, from S4 to S12, the chapter's real business begins — and it is a business you would not expect from a systematic theologian. Calvin, trained humanist, author of a commentary on Seneca, turns literary critic. Arrangement. Harmony. Majesty. Style. And the most startling move of the section: he argues that Scripture's bad prose is a providential design feature (S6), and that the plainest thing in the Bible hits harder than the whole of Greek oratory (S8, S12). If you have ever been embarrassed by the roughness of the Bible's Greek, S6 is written for you.

And then he ends by handing you a homework assignment that is also an experiment: Read Demosthenes or Cicero, read Plato, Aristotle... but turn from them to the reading of the Sacred Volume. ⚠️ That is not a rhetorical flourish. That is Augustine's own biography, running in reverse — and Period 6 will show you the passage.

Today's 3 Big Points — write them down now:

  1. The counterfactual machine, three moves in eight sentences. **In vain were** the authority of Scripture fortified... **if unaccompanied by** (S1: fronted adverbial + inversion + were = would be + a subjectless reduced conditional) → **Had they been** adorned with a more splendid eloquence (S7: if-deletion with inversion, past perfect) → **But now**, when an unpolished simplicity... makes a deeper impression (S8). ⚠️ That last one is the trap of the day: But now here does not mean "at the present time." It is Latin nunc vero — "but as things actually stand." It is the marker that cancels the counterfactual.
  2. The embedded how-clause — and the fact that it can be the SUBJECT. Four of them in a dash cascade in S4 (how admirably the system... is arranged—how perfectly free the doctrine is—how beautifully it harmonises—how rich it is), and then the killer in S11: **How peculiarly this property belongs to Scripture** appears from this, that.... That entire eight-word clause is the subject of appears. It is not a question. There is no question mark. If you read it as a question the sentence has no main verb and you are finished.
  3. The result-construction family, all three members present. not **so** strong **as to** produce and rivet (S3) · **too** mighty in the power of truth **to** need the rhetorician's art (S8) · and the triple in S12, **so** affect you, **so** pierce your heart, **so** work its way into your very marrow, **that**... — with the whole thing hung on a conditional imperative: turn from them..., **and** ... it will so affect you.

Twelve sentences. New chapter. The gloves come off. Let's go.

1 — Full Text (the whole § — 12 sentences, verbatim) –

1. In vain were the authority of Scripture fortified by argument, or supported by the consent of the Church, or confirmed by any other helps, if unaccompanied by an assurance higher and stronger than human Judgment can give. Till this better foundation has been laid, the authority of Scripture remains in suspense. On the other hand, when recognising its exemption from the common rule, we receive it reverently, and according to its dignity, those proofs which were not so strong as to produce and rivet a full conviction in our minds, become most appropriate helps. For it is wonderful how much we are confirmed in our belief, when we more attentively consider how admirably the system of divine wisdom contained in it is arranged—how perfectly free the doctrine is from every thing that savours of earth—how beautifully it harmonises in all its parts—and how rich it is in all the other qualities which give an air of majesty to composition. Our hearts are still more firmly assured when we reflect that our admiration is elicited more by the dignity of the matter than by the graces of style. For it was not without an admirable arrangement of Providence, that the sublime mysteries of the kingdom of heaven have for the greater part been delivered with a contemptible meanness of words. Had they been adorned with a more splendid eloquence, the wicked might have cavilled, and alleged that this constituted all their force. But now, when an unpolished simplicity, almost bordering on rudeness, makes a deeper impression than the loftiest flights of oratory, what does it indicate if not that the Holy Scriptures are too mighty in the power of truth to need the rhetorician’s art? Hence there was good ground for the Apostle’s declaration, that the faith of the Corinthians was founded not on “the wisdom of men,” but on “the power of God,” (1 Cor. 2:5), this speech and preaching among them having been “not with enticing words of man’s wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power,” (1 Cor. 2:5). For the truth is vindicated in opposition to every doubt, when, unsupported by foreign aid, it has its sole sufficiency in itself. How peculiarly this property belongs to Scripture appears from this, that no human writings, however skilfully composed, are at all capable of affecting us in a similar way. Read Demosthenes or Cicero, read Plato, Aristotle, or any other of that class: you will, I admit, feel wonderfully allured, pleased, moved, enchanted; but turn from them to the reading of the Sacred Volume, and whether you will or not, it will so affect you, so pierce your heart, so work its way into your very marrow, that, in comparison of the impression so produced, that of orators and philosophers will almost disappear; making it manifest that in the Sacred Volume there is a truth divine, a something which makes it immeasurably superior to all the gifts and graces attainable by man. ⚠️ Text note (transcription, not Calvin) — five housekeeping items from our local Beveridge file. Examiner's Eye — the traps they will set:

Trap 1 (S1 + S3): the relation between the proofs and the Spirit is ORDER, not VALUE. This is the whole chapter in one trap, and it will be the number-one comprehension item for the next thirteen days. S1 says the proofs are in vain ... if unaccompanied by the higher assurance. S3 says that once the higher assurance is there, those same proofs become most appropriate helps. Two distractors get built out of this, and they are mirror images:

  • ✗ "Calvin dismisses rational proofs of Scripture's authority as worthless." — False. most appropriate helps is a commendation, and he is about to spend twelve more sections producing them.
  • ✗ "Calvin holds that the arrangement, harmony, and majesty of Scripture are sufficient to produce full conviction." — False, and it is refuted by S3's own relative clause: those proofs **which were not so strong as to produce and rivet a full conviction in our minds**. He says the quiet part out loud inside the very sentence that praises them.

Trap 2 (S8): But now is not temporal. They will offer an option reading "Calvin observes that in his own day plain style had come to move readers more than oratory" — a historical claim about the sixteenth century. Wrong. But now = nunc vero, "but as it actually is", and it is the standard logical answer to a Had they been... counterfactual. The sentence is not about 1559; it is about the way the Bible in fact reads.

Trap 3 (S6): contemptible meanness of words is a claim about STYLE, not about content — and it is a compliment to Providence, not a complaint. Note how the sentence is built: the subject is the **sublime** mysteries of the kingdom of heaven and the manner is a **contemptible meanness** of words. Maximum content, minimum packaging, on purpose (not without an admirable arrangement of Providence). Any option saying "Calvin concedes that parts of Scripture are of little worth" has confused verba with res — which is exactly the confusion S5 was written to prevent.

Trap 4 (S12): who is that in that of orators and philosophers? It is a pro-form for the impression. in comparison of the impression so produced, **that** of orators and philosophers will almost disappear = compared with the impression Scripture produces, the impression made by orators and philosophers all but vanishes. If you read that as the orators themselves, or as the Sacred Volume, the comparison inverts and you will pick the wrong option.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas (시험 직전 이것만) –

Formula 1 — 반사실 3단 기계: In vain were S p.p. → Had S been p.p. → But now

   ① 가정 제시 — 문두 부사구 + 가정법 were + 도치
      IN VAIN  +  WERE  +  [S]  +  p.p. ...   ,  IF ‹S were› + p.p. ...
      ▸ IN VAIN WERE the authority of Scripture FORTIFIED by argument ...,
        IF UNACCOMPANIED by an assurance ...                             (S1)
      = It WOULD BE in vain to fortify ... if it WERE NOT accompanied by ...

      ⚠️ ① were = WOULD BE (과거 가정법). 과거시제 아님. 사실 진술 아님.
      ⚠️ ② if절이 주어·동사 없이 축약된다 → `if unaccompanied by` / `when
           unsupported by` / `however skilfully composed`.
           복원 시 접두사 UN- 을 반드시 부정으로 살릴 것.
           ✗ if it were accompanied  ○ if it were NOT accompanied

   ② 반사실 심화 — if 삭제 + 조동사 도치
      HAD  +  S  +  BEEN p.p. ...  ,  S + MIGHT/WOULD HAVE p.p.
      ▸ HAD THEY BEEN ADORNED with a more splendid eloquence,
        the wicked MIGHT HAVE CAVILLED, and ‹might have› ALLEGED ...      (S7)
      ⚠️ 도치 가능 조동사는 HAD / WERE / SHOULD 뿐. 둘째 동사는 조동사 gapping.

   ③ 반사실 취소 — 실제 상황으로 복귀
      BUT NOW ( = 라틴 nunc vero ) , when ... , ‹결론›
      ▸ BUT NOW, when an unpolished simplicity ... makes a deeper
        impression than the loftiest flights of oratory, what does it
        indicate if not that ...?                                        (S8)
      ⚠️⚠️ 오늘 최대 함정: BUT NOW = 「그러나 실제로는」. 시간 부사 아님.
           cf. 고전15:20 νυνὶ δέ "But now is Christ risen"; 롬3:21.
           「그런데 지금은」으로 읽으면 논리 연결자를 날짜로 바꾸는 것.

Transformation drill: Run all three moves on one theme — In vain were the gospel commended by learning, or recommended by its beauty, if unaccompanied by the Spirit's seal. Had it been delivered in a nobler tongue, the scoffer might have said that the tongue was all its power. But now, when the plainest sentence in it outweighs a library, what does that prove if not that the power was never in the words?

Formula 2 — 내포 how절: 도치하지 않는다 · 그리고 주어 자리에 들어간다

   how + ADV/ADJ  +  [S]  +  [V] ...          ← 어순 그대로, 도치 없음
   ▸ ... consider  HOW ADMIRABLY  the system ... IS ARRANGED               (S4①)
                   HOW PERFECTLY FREE  the doctrine  IS  from ...           (S4②)
                   HOW BEAUTIFULLY  it  HARMONISES  in all its parts        (S4③)
                   HOW RICH  it  IS  in all the other qualities ...         (S4④)

   ⚠️ ① 직접의문·감탄은 도치(How admirably IS the system arranged!),
        내포절은 도치 없음(how admirably the system IS arranged).
        ✗ how perfectly free IS the doctrine   ← 최다 오답
   ⚠️ ② how 가 형용사를 데려갈 때 be동사는 주어 뒤에 남는다.
        how rich + it + is  /  how perfectly free + the doctrine + is

   ── 그리고 이 절은 명사 자리 전체를 차지할 수 있다 ──
   ▸ 목적어:   we consider [ how admirably the system is arranged ]         (S4)
   ▸ 보어:     it is wonderful [ how much we are confirmed ]                (S4)
   ▸ ★ 주어:   [ HOW PECULIARLY THIS PROPERTY BELONGS TO SCRIPTURE ]
               APPEARS from this, that ...                                  (S11)

   ⚠️⚠️ S11 유형이 오늘의 최고 출제 포인트.
        물음표 없음 + 도치 없음 → 의문문이 아니다. appears 의 주어다.
        진단법: 주절 동사(appears)를 먼저 찾고, 그 앞 전체를 주어로 묶어라.

   ── 사촌: 축약 양보절 ──
   however skilfully ‹they are› composed  (S11) / how great soever (I.6.3)
   / though he be ignorant (I.5.2)

Transformation drill: Put a how-clause in all three slots — How exactly the parts of Scripture answer to one another appears from this, that no forgery has ever matched it. Consider how carefully the promises are placed, and it is wonderful how little the ablest critic has been able to unsettle.

Formula 3 — 정도·결과 3형제 + 조건 명령문

   ① NOT SO + ADJ + AS TO + V     = ~할 정도로 ~하지는 않다
      ▸ those proofs which were NOT SO STRONG AS TO produce and rivet
        a full conviction                                                  (S3)
      ⚠️ 부정형이 훨씬 어렵다. "약하다"가 아니라 "그 결과를 낼 만큼은 아니다".
      ⚠️ full 이 한정어다. 확신을 전혀 못 준다는 말이 아니다(§4의 a FULL faith).

   ② TOO + ADJ + TO + V          = 너무 ~해서 ~할 필요/수 없다
      ▸ the Holy Scriptures are TOO MIGHTY in the power of truth
        TO NEED the rhetorician's art                                      (S8)
      ⚠️ 힘의 소재를 보라: too mighty IN THE POWER OF TRUTH. 문체가 아니다.

   ③ SO V, SO V, SO V, THAT ...   = 다리 셋, 착지 하나
      ▸ it will SO affect you, SO pierce your heart, SO work its way
        into your very marrow, THAT ... that of orators and philosophers
        will almost disappear                                             (S12)
      ⚠️ 첫 so 에서 that 을 찾지 말 것. 세 다리가 하나의 that절을 공유한다.
      ⚠️ that of orators = THE IMPRESSION of orators (대명사 that).

   ── 그리고 이 전체를 매달고 있는 틀 ──
   ④ 명령문 + AND + S will V      = IF you V, S will V (조건 명령문)
      ▸ TURN from them to the reading of the Sacred Volume, AND ...
        it WILL so affect you ...                                          (S12)
      ⚠️ and = 「그러면」(결과). 등위 명령 둘로 읽으면 조건 논리가 사라진다.
      ⚠️ whether you WILL or not → will = 원하다(본동사). 바로 뒤 it WILL 은 조동사.
         같은 철자, 다른 단어, 열 단어 간격. cf. willy-nilly < will he, nill he.

Transformation drill: Use all four — The argument is not so strong as to settle the matter by itself; the truth in it is too plain to need decoration. Open the book, and whether you will or not it will so search you, so expose you, so hold you, that every other page you read today will seem thin by comparison.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

① The scandal of the chapter, and why it isn't one. Read the title of Chapter 8 straight after Chapter 7 and it looks like a self-contradiction. Bellarmine and generations after him pressed exactly that: if Scripture is self-authenticating and the Spirit does all the sealing, why thirteen sections of proofs? The answer is in S1 and S3, and it is structural, not apologetic:

   TIER 1   TESTIMONIUM SPIRITUS INTERNUM   — the ground of assurance   (Ch.7)
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   TIER 2   INDICIA / ARGUMENTA             — "most appropriate HELPS"  (Ch.8)

preposterous (§4 S10) named the error as one of order, never of validity. Today S1 subordinates the whole of Chapter 8 before it begins (in vain ... if unaccompanied by), and S3 states the payoff with one verb: the proofs **become** most appropriate helps. Later Reformed prolegomena systematised this into a standing distinction — Turretin (Institutio I.iv–vi) treats the criteria of divine authority at length and then insists the ratio formalis of faith is the Spirit's testimony. Bavinck (Reformed Dogmatics I) runs the same two tiers under the names indicia and testimonium. Calvin's §1 is the seed of a two-tier prolegomenon that lasted three centuries.

② The list in S4 became a creed. Read them side by side.

Calvin, I.8.1 (S4): how admirably the system of divine wisdom contained in it is arranged—how perfectly free the doctrine is from every thing that savours of earth—how beautifully it harmonises in all its parts—and how rich it is in all the other qualities which give an air of majesty to composition

Westminster Confession 1.5: "...the heavenliness of the matter, the efficacy of the doctrine, the majesty of the style, the consent of all the parts, the scope of the whole ... are arguments whereby it doth abundantly evidence itself to be the Word of God: yet notwithstanding, our full persuasion and assurance of the infallible truth and divine authority thereof, is from the inward work of the Holy Spirit bearing witness by and with the Word in our hearts."

the consent of all the parts = Calvin's harmonises in all its parts. the majesty of the style = Calvin's an air of majesty. the heavenliness of the matter = Calvin's free ... from every thing that savours of earth + S5's the dignity of the matter. And **yet notwithstanding** is S1's if unaccompanied by. The Divines are not summarising Calvin loosely; they are versifying I.7.5 and I.8.1 in order. If you know today's section you effectively know WCF 1.5, and the Belgic Confession art. 5 is the same architecture in a shorter compass.

③ Sermo humilis — and the fact that S12 is Augustine's autobiography reversed. The claim that Scripture's low style is providential rather than embarrassing has a long history:

  • Augustine, Confessions III.5 — as a young rhetorician, fresh from Cicero's Hortensius, he opened the Bible and recoiled: it seemed to him "unworthy to be compared to the dignity of Cicero" (indigna quam Tullianae dignitati compararem), and he says his swelling pride shrank from its lowliness (humilitas).
  • Augustine, De Doctrina Christiana IV.6–7 — the mature answer: the sacred writers have an eloquence of their own, fitted to the matter, and their plainness is a form of divine condescension.
  • Erich Auerbach, "Sermo Humilis" (in Literary Language and Its Public, 1958) — the classic modern study of how Christian Latin deliberately inverted the classical hierarchy of styles.

★ Now look at S12 again: Read Demosthenes or Cicero, read Plato, Aristotle, or any other of that class ... but turn from them to the reading of the Sacred Volume. That is precisely Augustine's itinerary in Confessions III.5 — Cicero first, then Scripture — with the verdict reversed. Augustine turned and was repelled; Calvin says: turn, and whether you will or not you will be pierced. Whether Calvin is consciously answering that page or not, the shape is unmistakable, and the ninth-most-quoted sentence of the Institutes is sitting on top of the most famous conversion narrative in Christian literature.

④ The concession is a humanist's, and that is the point. Do not read you will, I admit, feel wonderfully allured, pleased, moved, enchanted as a grudging throwaway. Calvin's first publication (1532) was a commentary on Seneca's De Clementia; he wrote some of the finest French prose of the century; the 1541 French Institutio is a monument of vernacular style. The man conceding Cicero's power is a professional. That is what makes the argument evidence rather than posturing — and it is also why §2 will open by conceding even more (in elegance and beauty, nay, splendour, the style of some of the prophets is not surpassed by the eloquence of heathen writers). Calvin's method throughout Chapter 8 is: grant the opponent's strongest ground, then show it does not reach.

⑤ ⚠️ 과대해석 방지 — four lines you must not cross with today's text.

  1. Not anti-rational. S3's become most appropriate helps is praise, and thirteen sections of argument follow. Anyone quoting Calvin as saying evidences are worthless has skipped the verb.
  2. Not a claim that Scripture is badly written. S6 says the plainness is providential; for the greater part limits the scope; and §2 will concede outright that some prophets rival any pagan writer. Calvin's claim is about where the power lives, not about literary quality per se.
  3. Not an aesthetic apologetic. S5 pre-empts this: admiration is elicited more by the dignity of the matter than by the graces of style. The argument is not "the Bible is beautiful, therefore divine."
  4. Not "faith comes against your will." S12's whether you will or not describes the impression the text makes on any reader, including a hostile one. Faith's obedience, per I.7.5, is willingly indeed, and knowingly. Two different claims. Do not merge them.

⑥ A word on the modern conversation. Plantinga's Warranted Christian Belief uses Calvin's structure (belief in Scripture as properly basic, not inferred from evidences, yet not irrational and not incompatible with argument) — S10 and S11 are the sentences most often cited in that connection. On the other side, the "evidentialist Calvin" reading leans on Chapter 8 as a whole. The text refuses both extremes, and it refuses them in the first sentence. That is why §1 exists.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. 1 Cor. 2:5 — S9, cited explicitly. that your faith should not stand in the wisdom of men, but in the power of God. Calvin's founded not on "the wisdom of men," but on "the power of God" is a direct quotation, and it supplies the not A but B seam that runs through S5, S8, and S9 alike.

  2. 1 Cor. 2:4 — S9, cited but mis-numbered as 2:5. οὐκ ἐν πειθοῖς σοφίας λόγοις, ἀλλ’ ἐν ἀποδείξει πνεύματος καὶ δυνάμεως. ★ ἀπόδειξις is Aristotle's term for demonstrative proof (the subject of the Posterior Analytics), and Paul assigns it to the Spirit while giving πειθοῖς λόγοις — persuasive rhetoric — to the opposition. Paul, not Calvin, invented the two-tier structure's vocabulary. How Calvin uses it: as the apostolic warrant for writing a chapter of arguments while denying that arguments produce faith.

  3. Hebrews 4:12 — S12, quoted without attribution. ζῶν γὰρ ὁ λόγος τοῦ θεοῦ καὶ ἐνεργής ... διϊκνούμενος ... ἁρμῶν τε καὶ μυελῶν — piercing ... of the joints and marrow. so pierce your heart, so work its way into your very **marrow** is this verse. ⚠️ And note that I.7.5 S4's a divine energy **living and breathing** in it is ζῶν καὶ ἐνεργής, the first half of the same verse. Two consecutive sections, one verse split in two. How Calvin uses it: the effect of the text is offered as the empirical residue of its divinity.

  4. 2 Cor. 4:7 — S6, the unstated logic. we have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us. Sublime cargo, contemptible container, and the cheapness of the container is the proof. How Calvin uses it: without citation, as the providential rationale — not without an admirable arrangement of Providence.

  5. 2 Cor. 10:10 — S6, an echo worth marking. The Corinthians' own complaint against Paul: his bodily presence is weak, and his **speech contemptible** (ὁ λόγος ἐξουθενημένος). Calvin's a **contemptible** meanness of words reuses the insult and reassigns it as a credential. How Calvin uses it: the charge is admitted and converted.

  6. 1 Cor. 1:17 — behind S8. not with wisdom of words, lest the cross of Christ should be made of none effect (ἵνα μὴ κενωθῇ ὁ σταυρός). Exactly S7's worry — that eloquence would be credited with the power. How Calvin uses it: as the Pauline form of the counterfactual.

  7. Matt. 13:11 / 1 Cor. 2:7 — S6's phrase. the sublime mysteries of the kingdom of heaven = τὰ μυστήρια τῆς βασιλείας τῶν οὐρανῶν. ⚠️ Yesterday's chapter closed on Matt 13:11's logic (none comprehend the mysteries of God save those to whom it is given); today's S6 picks the same noun up and gives it the plainest possible wrapping. Deliberate seam between the chapters.

  8. Acts 4:13 — behind S8. The council's estimate of Peter and John: unlearned and ignorant men (ἀγράμματοι καὶ ἰδιῶται). The unpolished simplicity, almost bordering on rudeness has a New Testament pedigree — and so does the astonishment that follows it.

  9. Jer. 23:29 — behind S12's violence. Is not my word like as a fire? and like a hammer that breaketh the rock in pieces? A rhetorical question, exactly like S8's, asserting that the word's force is intrinsic. How Calvin uses it: the register of pierce, work its way, marrow comes from this family of texts, not from rhetoric manuals.

  10. Ps. 34:8 / Ps. 19:10 — the taste vocabulary of S4. O taste and see that the LORD is good; sweeter also than honey. savours of earth belongs to Calvin's running sensory epistemology (I.5.3 tasted his paternal love; I.7.2 sweet and bitter ... of taste; Heb 5:14 αἰσθητήρια). How Calvin uses it: recognition of the divine is treated as a perception, not a deduction — which is why S12 can end in an experiment rather than a syllogism.

  11. Matt. 7:7 / Jas. 4:8 — the form of S12's invitation. Ask, and it shall be given you; Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Conditional imperative — command + and + promise. Calvin's turn from them to the reading of the Sacred Volume, and ... it will so affect you is built on this biblical pattern. How Calvin uses it: it converts his literary claim into a testable command.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① 어법 — 밑줄 넷 중 틀린 것을 고르시오.

In vain ①were the authority of Scripture fortified by argument, if ②unaccompanied by an assurance higher than human judgment can give. Consider ③how beautifully is it harmonised in all its parts; and had it been adorned with a more splendid eloquence, the wicked ④might have cavilled.

정답: ③

how beautifully **is it** harmonised → how beautifully **it is** harmonised. 내포 how절은 도치하지 않는다. 오늘 Formula 2 의 정면 출제이고, S4 의 네 다리가 전부 이 어순을 지킨다(how beautifully **it harmonises**).

출제 의도 — 나머지 셋이 왜 옳은가를 아는 것이 실력이다. ① were = would be(과거 가정법) + 문두 부사구 In vain 에 의한 도치. 사실을 말하는 was 로 바꾸면 문장의 무드가 붕괴한다(S1). ② unaccompanied — 축약 조건절(if ‹it were› unaccompanied). 접두사 un- 이 부정을 지고 있으므로 not 을 추가로 넣으면 이중부정이 되어 뜻이 뒤집힌다. ④ might have cavilled — Had it been adorned 라는 과거 반사실 조건에 호응하는 완료 조동사. might cavil 로 쓰면 시간축이 어긋난다(S7). 함정 설계: ③은 "감탄문이니 도치"라는 반쯤 맞는 규칙을 자극한다. 규칙의 조건절이 「독립 감탄·의문일 때」임을 아는 학생만 걸러진다.

② 내용일치 — §1의 내용과 일치하는 것은?

  1. Calvin argues that the arrangement, harmony, and majesty of Scripture are of themselves strong enough to produce and fix a full conviction in the reader's mind, so that the Spirit's testimony merely confirms what the evidences have already settled.
  2. Calvin admits that the sublime mysteries of the kingdom of heaven have for the most part been delivered in language of a contemptible plainness, and treats this very plainness as a providential arrangement, since a more splendid eloquence would have let the wicked claim that the eloquence was the whole of Scripture's force.
  3. Calvin holds that the classical orators and philosophers have no real power to move a reader, and that anyone who claims to be delighted by Cicero or Plato is deceived by mere ornament.
  4. Since Scripture is exempt from the common rule, Calvin concludes that arguments for its credibility are of no use to the believer, and he offers the marks of divinity in this chapter only as a concession to unbelievers.

정답: ②

S6 (not without an admirable arrangement of Providence, that the sublime mysteries ... have for the greater part been delivered with a contemptible meanness of words) + S7 (Had they been adorned with a more splendid eloquence, the wicked might have cavilled, and alleged that this constituted all their force). 양보와 그 양보의 목적이 정확히 결합되어 있다.

출제 의도 — 네 개의 오답이 §1의 네 가지 서로 다른 오독을 각각 대표한다. ① 방향 뒤집기, 그리고 이 절 최대의 함정. S3 의 관계절이 이 선지를 한 방에 죽인다 — those proofs **which were not so strong as to produce and rivet a full conviction in our minds**. 증거는 확신을 만들지도 못박지도 못한다. 그리고 S1 의 in vain ... if unaccompanied by 는 성령을 확인자가 아니라 전제로 놓는다. 순서가 뒤집힌 선지다(= §4의 preposterous 그 자체). ③ 범위 초과 + 양보 삭제. S12 는 정반대를 말한다 — you will, **I admit**, feel wonderfully allured, pleased, moved, enchanted. 칼빈은 고전 웅변의 힘을 인정하고서 비교한다. 인정을 지우면 논증이 사라진다. ④ 반쪽 인용. exemption from the common rule(S3) 는 맞게 가져왔지만, 같은 문장의 결론이 become **most appropriate helps** 다 — 신자에게 유용하다는 말이다. 그리고 이 챕터가 불신자 전용이라는 말은 본문에 없다. ⚠️ 채점 관점: ①과 ④는 방향이 정반대인데 둘 다 오답이다. 오늘의 요지는 "증거가 전부다"도 "증거는 무용하다"도 아니라 순서라는 것. 양극단 오답을 한 문제에 넣는 것은 이 구조를 이해했는지 확인하는 표준 수법이다.

③ 영작 — 오늘의 공식을 적용해 영작하시오.

(a) [Formula 1] "복음이 학식으로 뒷받침되고 교회의 동의로 지지된다 해도, 성령의 인치심이 함께하지 않는다면 헛일일 것이다. 만일 복음이 더 화려한 언어로 꾸며졌더라면, 조롱하는 자들은 그 언어가 그 힘의 전부라고 주장했을 것이다. 그러나 실제로는, 가장 평범한 한 문장이 웅변의 절정보다 더 깊이 박힌다." — ①In vain were ... if unaccompanied by (축약 조건절 유지) ②Had ... been p.p., ... might have p.p. ③But now(시간 아님) 순서로.

(b) [Formula 2] "성경의 각 부분이 서로 얼마나 정확히 응답하는지는 다음 사실에서 드러난다. 즉, 아무리 솜씨 좋게 지어진 어떤 인간의 글도 같은 방식으로 우리에게 조금도 영향을 주지 못한다는 것이다." — 내포 how절을 주어 자리에 놓고, appears from this, that ... 과 축약 양보절 however skilfully composed 를 쓸 것.

(c) [Formula 3] "그 논증은 그 자체로 문제를 끝낼 만큼 강하지는 않다. 그 안의 진리는 너무 강해서 장식을 필요로 하지 않는다. 그 책을 펴 보라, 그러면 원하든 원하지 않든 그것이 너를 그토록 파고들고, 그토록 붙잡아, 오늘 읽은 다른 모든 것이 비교하면 얇아 보일 것이다." — not so ... as to V, too ... to V, 조건 명령문 + whether you will or not + so ..., so ..., that ... 을 각각 쓸 것.

모범답안: (a) In vain were the gospel supported by learning, or upheld by the consent of the Church, if unaccompanied by the seal of the Spirit. Had it been adorned with a more splendid diction, the scoffers might have alleged that the diction constituted all its force. But now, when its plainest sentence sinks deeper than the summit of oratory, what does that indicate if not that the power was never in the words? (b) How exactly the parts of Scripture answer to one another appears from this, that no human writing, however skilfully composed, is at all capable of affecting us in a similar way. (c) The argument is not so strong as to settle the question by itself; the truth in it is too mighty to need ornament. Open the book, and whether you will or not it will so search you, so hold you, that everything else you have read today will seem thin in comparison.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary. Chapter 8 opens by putting itself in its place: **In vain were** the authority of Scripture fortified by argument, or supported by the consent of the Church, or confirmed by any other helps, **if unaccompanied by** an assurance higher and stronger than human Judgment can give — until that foundation is laid the authority remains **in suspense** — but once we receive Scripture reverently, recognising its **exemption from the common rule**, then those same proofs, **which were not so strong as to produce and rivet a full conviction**, become most appropriate helps (one verb, and the whole two-tier structure with it); whereupon Calvin turns literary critic and names the marks — how admirably the system of divine wisdom ... is arranged—how perfectly free the doctrine is from every thing that **savours of earth**—how beautifully it **harmonises in all its parts**—and how rich it is in all the other qualities which give **an air of majesty** to composition (the list the Westminster Divines will versify) — but locates their force in the dignity of **the matter** rather than the graces of **style**, and then makes the audacious move: it was **not without an admirable arrangement of Providence**, that the **sublime mysteries** of the kingdom of heaven have for the greater part been delivered with a **contemptible meanness of words**, since **Had they been adorned** with a more splendid eloquence, the wicked might have cavilled; **But now** — as things actually stand — an unpolished simplicity, almost **bordering on rudeness** outweighs the loftiest flights of oratory, and what does it indicate **if not** that the Holy Scriptures are **too mighty** in the power of truth **to need** the rhetorician's art? Paul ratifies it (not on "the wisdom of men," but on "the power of God"; in **demonstration** of the Spirit and of power), the principle is generalised (unsupported by **foreign** aid, it has its **sole sufficiency in itself**), narrowed (**How peculiarly** this property belongs to Scripture appears from this, that...), and finally put to the reader as an experiment: Read Demosthenes or Cicero ... **but turn** from them to the reading of the Sacred Volume, **and whether you will or not**, it will **so** affect you, **so** pierce your heart, **so** work its way into your very **marrow**, **that** ... that of orators and philosophers will almost disappear — leaving a truth divine, **a something** that no list of marks can name.

And the three grammar points, once more:

  1. The counterfactual machine, in three moves. **In vain were** S p.p. ... **if unaccompanied by** (S1: fronted adverbial forces inversion; were = would be; the if-clause is reduced to conjunction + participle, with the negation hiding in un-) → **Had they been** adorned ..., the wicked **might have** cavilled (S7: if-deletion, only HAD/WERE/SHOULD may invert; second modal gapped) → **But now** (S8: ⚠️ nunc vero, "but as things actually stand" — a logical operator, never a date).
  2. The embedded how-clause: no inversion, any noun slot. Four in a dash cascade in S4 (how admirably the system **is arranged**, how perfectly free the doctrine **is**, how beautifully **it harmonises**, how rich **it is**) — subject before verb, every time. Then the high-value version in S11: **How peculiarly this property belongs to Scripture** appears from this, that..., where the whole clause is the subject of appears. Diagnostic: find the main verb first, then bracket everything in front of it.
  3. Degree-and-result, three members plus the frame. **not so** strong **as to** produce and rivet a full conviction (S3 — and full is the limiting word, as in §4's a full faith) · **too** mighty in the power of truth **to** need the rhetorician's art (S8) · **so** affect you, **so** pierce your heart, **so** work its way into your very marrow, **that**... (S12 — three legs, one landing) — all hung on the conditional imperative turn ..., **and** it **will** ..., inside which sits the trap of the section: whether you **will** or not, where will is the full verb "to want."
