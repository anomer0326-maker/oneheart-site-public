Day 99 — Book 1, Chapter 13, §25

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday Calvin fenced in his own polemic. Today the polemic begins. Look at the opening move: he REFUSES to fight on every front. It were tedious … to form a catalogue. No list of heresies. Two principles, and the door is shut. That's an economy of force — one wall instead of a hundred patrols.

Then the first name falls: Servetus. Michael Servetus was burned at Geneva on 27 October 1553 — six years before this edition. When Calvin writes in our day, the smoke has hardly cleared. Read what follows with that in mind: this is not a classroom exercise. It is a theologian summarising, in the present tense, the man he helped send to the stake. Your job today is NOT to decide who was right in 1553. Your job is to read the argument — what Calvin says Servetus taught, and how Calvin answers from three verses.

Second — the grammar is archaic, and it will bite. ⚠️ It were tedious (were = would be). ⚠️ needs not (= does not need). ⚠️ I say nothing of the insulting terms — and then he mentions them: the classic praeteritio. ⚠️ Sentences 9 and 10 are 73 and 93 words — Calvin reporting an opponent's view with he would have it that and maintaining that, one clause stacked inside another.

Third — a printer's trap in the Scripture proof. ⚠️ S15 prints the shapeless mass was unborn by him (Gen. 1:2). Read it as printed and the sentence is nonsense. The sense is upborne — the Spirit "brooded over" and sustained the unformed mass. We quote it as printed, and flag it.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. The economy-of-force machine: It were tedious … to form a catalogue (S1) · deeming it enough if (S2) · But if we hold, what has already been demonstrated from Scripture, that A …, and again, that B …, the door will be shut (S4) · it may be worthwhile briefly to discuss (S5). ⚠️ were = would be (old subjunctive); what has already been demonstrated is a PARENTHETICAL, not the object of hold; the real objects are the two that-clauses; the Son from the Spirit = gapping (differs is understood). Formula 1.
  2. Reporting an opponent: The sum of his speculations was, that … (S8) · he would have it that … (S9) · maintaining that … (S10) · charged … with being Atheists (S6) · inasmuch as (S8) · although he sometimes cloaks his absurdities … he at last reduces … (S10). ⚠️ he would have it that = he maintains / insists that (volitional would, not a conditional — Day 34 machine). Every that after says / maintaining is HIS claim, not Calvin's. Formula 2.
  3. 1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 23 sentences –

It were tedious, and to no purpose toilsome, to form a catalogue of the errors by which, in regard to this branch of doctrine, the purity of the faith has been assailed. The greater part of heretics have with their gross deliriums made a general attack on the glory of God, deeming it enough if they could disturb and shake the unwary. From a few individuals numerous sects have sprung up, some of them rending the divine essence, and others confounding the distinction of Persons. But if we hold, what has already been demonstrated from Scripture, that the essence of the one God, pertaining to the Father, Son, and Spirit, is simple and indivisible, and again, that the Father differs in some special property from the Son, and the Son from the Spirit, the door will be shut against Arius and Sabellius, as well as the other ancient authors of error. But as in our day have arisen certain frantic men, such as Servetus and others, who, by new devices, have thrown every thing into confusion, it may be worthwhile briefly to discuss their fallacies. The name of Trinity was so much disliked, nay detested, by Servetus, that he charged all whom he called Trinitarians with being Atheists. I say nothing of the insulting terms in which he thought proper to make his charges. The sum of his speculations was, that a threefold Deity is introduced wherever three Persons are said to exist in his essence, and that this Triad was imaginary, inasmuch as it was inconsistent with the unity of God. At the same time, he would have it that the Persons are certain external ideas which do not truly subsist in the Divine essence, but only figure God to us under this or that form: that at first, indeed, there was no distinction in God, because originally the Word was the same as the Spirit, but ever since Christ came forth God of God, another Spirit, also a God, had proceeded from him. But although he sometimes cloaks his absurdities in allegory, as when he says that the eternal Word of God was the Spirit of Christ with God, and the reflection of the idea, likewise that the Spirit was a shadow of Deity, he at last reduces the divinity of both to nothing; maintaining that, according to the mode of distribution, there is a part of God as well in the Son as in the Spirit, just as the same Spirit substantially is a portion of God in us, and also in wood and stone. His absurd babbling concerning the person of the Mediator will be seen in its own place. The monstrous fiction that a Person is nothing else than a visible appearance of the glory of God, needs not a long refutation. For when John declares that before the world was created the Logos was God (John 1:1), he shows that he was something very different from an idea. But if even then, and from the remotest eternity, that Logos, who was God, was with the Father, and had his own distinct and peculiar glory with the Father (John 17:5), he certainly could not be an external or figurative splendour, but must necessarily have been a hypostasis which dwelt inherently in God himself. But although there is no mention made of the Spirit antecedent to the account of the creation, he is not there introduced as a shadow, but as the essential power of God, where Moses relates that the shapeless mass was unborn by him (Gen. 1:2). It is obvious that the eternal Spirit always existed in God, seeing he cherished and sustained the confused materials of heaven and earth before they possessed order or beauty. Assuredly he could not then be an image or representation of God, as Servetus dreams. But he is elsewhere forced to make a more open disclosure of his impiety when he says, that God by his eternal reason decreeing a Son to himself, in this way assumed a visible appearance. For if this be true, no other Divinity is left to Christ than is implied in his having been ordained a Son by God’s eternal decree. Moreover, those phantoms which Servetus substitutes for the hypostases he so transforms as to make new changes in God. But the most execrable heresy of all is his confounding both the Son and Spirit promiscuously with all the creatures. For he distinctly asserts, that there are parts and partitions in the essence of God, and that every such portion is God. This he does especially when he says, that the spirits of the faithful are co-eternal and consubstantial with God, although he elsewhere assigns a substantial divinity, not only to the soul of man, but to all created things.

Examiner's Eye. ⚠️⚠️⚠️ Six traps. (1) S1–S3 — does Calvin say the heresies are unimportant? ⚠️⚠️ NO. He says a CATALOGUE would be tedious and toilsome. S2: the heretics made a general attack on the glory of God. An option saying Calvin thinks the early heresies are harmless is a direction flip. (2) S4 — what are the two principles? ⚠️ (a) the one essence is simple and indivisible; (b) the Father differs in some special property from the Son, and the Son from the Spirit. An option naming only the unity, or only the distinction, is scope under-reach; an option saying the Persons are three essences is the opposite error. Also: ⚠️ the principles are already … demonstrated from Scripture — Calvin is summarising, not proving. (3) S3 — who rends, who confounds? ⚠️ rending the divine essence = dividing God (Arians ranked the Son as a lesser being); confounding the distinction of Persons = blurring them (Sabellius). Do not swap. The two principles of S4 are the mirror image of these two errors. (4) S9–S10 — whose claims are these? ⚠️⚠️ Servetus's, as Calvin reports them — introduced by he would have it that and maintaining that. An option attributing "the Persons are external ideas" to Calvin is a direction flip. (5) S12–S15 — what do the verses prove? ⚠️ Not that the Persons are three Gods. That the Word and the Spirit were IN God, as God, BEFORE creation — so cannot be mere appearances (which need something to appear to). An option saying John 1:1 proves the Son was created is scope overreach in the wrong direction. (6) S21 — what is the most execrable heresy of all? ⚠️⚠️ his confounding both the Son and Spirit promiscuously with all the creatures — NOT the denial of the Trinity, NOT the insults of S7. Calvin ranks the errors, and this is first.

One point: ⚠️ so much … that = result (so intensely disliked that …); nay detested = indeed, even worse than disliked; and charged all … with being Atheists = accused of being. Grammar: I say nothing of … = I pass over; in which = in the language in which; thought proper = saw fit (= thought it right to). Examiner trap: Calvin lists Servetus's insults — ✗. He declines to.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — Old subjunctive & dummy-it frames (S1, S2, S5, S19)

It were + ADJ + to V              = It would be ADJ to V          (S1  It were tedious … to form a catalogue)
deem it ADJ + if-clause           = it is a dummy object          (S2  deeming it enough if they could …)
it may be worthwhile (briefly) to V  = it is worth V-ing          (S5)
if this be true, …                = if this is true               (S19)

⚠️ Traps. (a) were here is NOT past: it is hypothetical would be. (b) In deeming it enough if …, it points forward to the if-clause — do not look for a noun it refers to. (c) if this be true is a present subjunctive, not an error. (d) needs not (S12) belongs to the same old-grammar family: main-verb negation without do. Drill. Rewrite in Calvin's old style: It would be foolish to list every heresy. → It were foolish to list every heresy. And: He thinks it enough if the unwary are shaken. → He deems it enough if the unwary be shaken (subjunctive possible, but indicative is also correct).

Formula 2 — Fronting, inversion, and the parenthetical what (S3, S4, S5, S20, S23)

PP + V + S (long subject last)     = as in our day have arisen certain frantic men      (S5; base: as certain frantic men have arisen in our day)
Object-first (+ SV)                = those phantoms … he so transforms … (S20); This he does … (S23; base: He does this)
we hold, [what has been shown,] that A, and again, that B, C(main)   = what-clause is a parenthesis, not the object   (S4)
the Father differs from the Son, and the Son ⟨differs⟩ from the Spirit   = gapping                                (S4)

⚠️ Traps. (a) Inversion after a fronted PP with have arisen — restore the base order before parsing. (b) what has already been demonstrated is NOT the object of hold; the two that-clauses are. (c) This he does — this is an object, he the subject. (d) Gapping: the repeated from is a clue that differs is understood. Drill. Restore the base order: In this chapter has Calvin defended the unity of the essence. → Calvin has defended the unity of the essence in this chapter. Gap: The Father begets, and the Son is begotten. → fine without gapping; with gapping: the Father begets the Son, and the Son ⟨begets⟩ nothing — and the Spirit proceeds. (identify the gap).

Formula 3 — Reporting an opponent, and the if–then proof (S6, S8, S9, S10, S14, S19, S20)

he would have it that …            = he maintains that …                 (volitional would, S9)
charge X with V-ing                = accuse X of V-ing                   (S6 charged all … with being Atheists)
inasmuch as                        = because                             (S8)
could not be A, but must have been B   = it cannot be A; it must be B     (logical modals, S14)
no other N is left than (what) is p.p.  = only what is implied remains     (S19; than = hidden subject)
so V as to V                       = in such a way as to …               (S20)

⚠️ Traps. (a) would is not conditional — it reports insistence. (b) Everything after says that / maintaining that is Servetus's, not Calvin's. (c) could not be = impossibility, must have been = deduction — not ability or obligation. (d) than is implied has no written subject (compare Day 98 than is meet). Drill. Report a heretic's view with the pattern: He would have it that the Persons are mere masks. Then transform: The Son cannot have been a mere idea; he must have been a real Person. → He could not have been a mere idea, but must necessarily have been a real Person.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

1. The man. Michael Servetus (c. 1511–1553), Spanish physician and theologian. De Trinitatis erroribus (1531) and Dialogorum de Trinitate (1532) attacked the traditional Trinity; his last work, Christianismi Restitutio (1553), was the occasion of his arrest. He was condemned at Geneva and burned on 27 October 1553 at Champel — having already been condemned in absentia by the Catholic authorities in Vienne. Calvin was a prosecuting voice, not the sole one: the Geneva council, and Zurich, Bern, Basel and Schaffhausen, were consulted and agreed. Calvin urged a less cruel form of execution (beheading) and was refused. How to read this: it is context for why this paragraph is written in the present tense. It is not the lesson of the paragraph — and the later Protestant tradition itself has repudiated the practice (cf. Calvin's own Defensio orthodoxae fidei, 1554, and modern regret).

2. What Servetus taught (as far as we can tell). Not a simple Sabellian. His view: God is one; the Word is God's self-expression and the Spirit his breath; the "persons" are dispensations (dispositiones, oikonomiai) by which the one God shows himself; Christ's flesh participates in the divine substance. Calvin's report in S8–S10 and S23 is a polemical compression. ⚠️ Always label it: Calvin's account of Servetus.

3. Calvin's method — and its lineage. The two principles of S4 are the classic Nicene–Cappadocian formula: one ousia, three hypostaseis — unity of essence, distinction of Persons by their properties (the Cappadocian Fathers; Augustine, De Trinitate). Calvin's contribution here is rhetorical economy: no catalogue, only the two limits. Compare his rule from Day 98: no step beyond the Word — S13–S15 are exactly that: three verses (Jn 1:1; Jn 17:5; Gen 1:2).

4. Why the proofs run through time. Servetus said the Persons emerged in history (S9: first no distinction; later another Spirit). Calvin's answer: the sources put Word and Spirit in God before creation. That is the exegetical core: pre-existence proves real subsistence.

5. Later reception. Servetus's execution gave Calvin's reputation its darkest mark (Sebastian Castellio's Contra libellum Calvini 1554: "To kill a man is not to defend a doctrine, it is to kill a man"). The Reformed confessions (Belgic Confession 8–9; Westminster Confession 2.3) restate the two principles of S4. Barth (CD I/1) makes the Trinity the root of the doctrine of revelation — a modern echo of Calvin's "root of faith" in Day 98.

6. Over-reading guardrail. ⚠️ Six limits. (i) This is not Calvin's complete treatment of Servetus — the Christology comes later (S11). (ii) Not a verdict on the execution — S1–S23 never mention it. (iii) Not a claim that Servetus lacked piety — only that his premises, by Calvin's reading, dissolve the Persons. (iv) Not "never use the word Persons" — Calvin keeps Persons, and defends hypostasis. (v) Not "Scripture proves the doctrine by three verses alone" — S13–S15 are the quickest illustrations; the full proof is §§1–24. (vi) Not a scientific claim about Gen 1:2 — unborn is a misprint.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. Matt 28:19 / 2 Cor 13:14 (background to S4's two principles). Calvin has already "demonstrated from Scripture" the unity and the distinction; S4 only recalls it. Use: summary, not argument.
  2. John 1:1 (explicit, S13). Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ λόγος, καὶ ὁ λόγος ἦν πρὸς τὸν θεόν, καὶ θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος. Two phrases: πρὸς τὸν θεόν (with God = distinction) and θεὸς ἦν (was God = deity). Use: the imperfect ἦν + the time (ἐν ἀρχῇ) rules out "idea that appeared".
  3. John 17:5 (explicit, S14). δόξα … πρὸ τοῦ τὸν κόσμον εἶναι — glory before the world was. Use: a prayer from the Son TO the Father, about glory he already had — two subjects, one divine glory.
  4. Heb 1:3 (S14, hypostasis). Son = χαρακτὴρ τῆς ὑποστάσεως αὐτοῦ. Use: the Greek term Servetus resisted is Scripture's own.
  5. John 14:10–11 (echo, S14 dwelt inherently in God himself). the Father that dwelleth in me. Use: indwelling language as the shape of divine relation.
  6. Gen 1:2 (explicit, S15–S16). the Spirit of God moved (מְרַחֶפֶת, merachefet) upon the face of the waters. Use: Calvin hears "brooding / sustaining" — ⚠️ unborn is a misprint of upborne.
  7. Heb 10:1 / Col 2:17 (echo, S10 a shadow of Deity). shadow is the biblical word for the provisional (Law), not for a Person. Use: Servetus's word turned against him.
  8. Gen 3:5 (echo, S23). ye shall be as gods. Use: the old temptation reappears when creatures are called co-eternal and consubstantial with God.
  9. Mal 3:6 / Jas 1:17 (echo, S20 new changes in God). I am the LORD, I change not; no variableness, neither shadow of turning. Use: divine immutability against the God of S9.

Calvin's habit today. He uses Scripture here as chronology: before creation, before the world — so the Persons cannot be later appearances. That is a different use from Day 98's guided by the divine oracles: there, Scripture limits; here, Scripture refutes.

8 — Exam Problems –

① Grammar — which underlined expression is used INCORRECTLY in the sense of the passage?

(a) It were tedious = It would be tedious (S1) (b) in deeming it enough if they could disturb and shake the unwary, the word it refers back to the glory of God (S2) (c) in But if we hold, what has already been demonstrated from Scripture, that …, the what-clause is a parenthesis, not the object of hold (S4) (d) he would have it that the Persons are certain external ideas = he maintains that … (S9)

Answer: (b). it in deeming it enough if … is a dummy object anticipating the if-clause — it refers to no noun. (a) ✓ hypothetical were; (c) ✓ the real objects of hold are the two that-clauses; (d) ✓ volitional would. Examiner's rationale: (b) looks natural — students want it to point to a noun (here the glory of God is the nearest). Calvin's it points forward.

② Comprehension — which statement is CONSISTENT with S1–S23?

(a) Calvin refuses to treat the ancient heresies because they are harmless. (b) Calvin holds that two principles — the one simple, indivisible essence and the real distinction of Persons — are enough to shut the door on Arius and Sabellius. (c) Calvin argues from Genesis 1:2 that the Spirit was created before the other creatures. (d) Calvin says Servetus's worst error is that he disliked the word "Trinity".

Answer: (b). (a) ✗ direction flip — S1: tedious and to no purpose toilsome, not harmless; S2 calls them a general attack on the glory of God. (c) ✗ S16: the Spirit always existed in God — not created. (d) ✗ scope: the most execrable heresy is S21's confounding the Son and Spirit with all creatures, not S6's dislike of a name. Examiner's rationale: direction-flip (a); reversal of the proof (c); wrong ranking (d).

③ Composition — apply Formula 3

Using he would have it that and could not … but must have been, put into English: 세르베투스는 위격들이 한낱 외적인 관념이라고 주장했다. 그러나 요한복음 1:1에 따르면 말씀은 창조 이전부터 하나님이셨으므로, 한낱 관념일 수는 없고 반드시 실재하는 휘포스타시스였어야 한다.

Model answer: Servetus would have it that the Persons are mere external ideas. But since, according to John 1:1, the Word was God before the world was created, he could not have been a mere idea, but must necessarily have been a real hypostasis. Examiner's rationale: (i) volitional would have it that, (ii) logical could not have been … but must have been, (iii) since (not time-since) for the premise.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary. Calvin refuses a catalogue of heresies because two principles — one simple, indivisible essence and the real distinction of Persons — shut out Arius and Sabellius; then he answers Servetus's "Persons as appearances" from John 1:1, John 17:5 and Genesis 1:2, and ends with the charge he thinks worst: Servetus pours the Son and the Spirit into all creatures. Grammar: ① old subjunctive & dummy-it frames (It were, deeming it enough if, if this be true, needs not), ② fronting/inversion, object-first and the parenthetical what, ③ reporting machinery (would have it that) and the logical modals of the if–then proof.
