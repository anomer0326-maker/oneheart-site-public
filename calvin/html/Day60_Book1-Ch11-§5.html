Day 60 — Book 1, Chapter 11, §5

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Here it comes — the most famous slogan in the entire image controversy, and Calvin has been saving it for the chapter's third movement. "Images are the books of the unlearned." Who said it? Gregory the Great, pope, doctor of the church, writing to a bishop who had started smashing pictures. For nine hundred years that one sentence was the Western church's standing defence of every fresco, every statue, every painted wall. Can't read? The pictures will teach you.

Watch what Calvin does with it, because the method is the lesson. He does not sneer at Gregory. He does not pretend the slogan doesn't exist — S1 opens "I am not ignorant, indeed" — I know it perfectly well; everyone does; it is more than threadbare. Instead he files an appeal to a higher court. Gregory said it — fine. But the Holy Spirit has ruled differently, and the ruling is in writing: Jeremiah calls the wooden idol "a doctrine of vanities"; Habakkuk calls the molten image "a teacher of lies." Do you see the move? Scripture itself talks about idols in classroom vocabulary — doctrine, teacher. So Calvin grants Gregory his metaphor and turns it into the indictment: yes, images are books — books whose every page is false. A book that only teaches lies is not a book for the unlearned. It is a school for the deceived.

And mark S2's picture: had Gregory "got his lesson in this matter in the Spirit's school," he never would have spoken so. The pope, sent back to class. That is not mockery for its own sake — it is the whole Reformation doctrine of authority in one image: even a doctor of the church sits at a desk below Scripture.

The section's centre of gravity is S4. An objector says: the prophets were only attacking superstitious abuse of images. Calvin's answer is the most instructive sentence of the day — "I admit it to be so; but I add…" He concedes the point, really concedes it, and then shows the concession changes nothing: the prophets condemn not just the abuse but the axiom — the claim that images can substitute for books at all. Learn that machine. Concede the fact; defeat the inference.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. Inverted conditionals — the if-less subjunctive (S2, S7). had Gregory got his lesson…, he never would have spoken (past counterfactual) and were it not true that…, the prophets would not condemn (present counterfactual). When if is deleted, had / were / should march to the front. Restore the if before you translate, and remember: the inverted form is were for every person — was is disqualified.
  2. The concession machine — litotes + concessio (S1, S4). I am not ignorant of… = "I know perfectly well" (double negative as emphasis), and the full frame: If it is objected that…, I admit it to be so; but I add (what must be obvious to all), that… — grant the premise, then out-argue it. Calvin's favourite courtroom rhythm.
  3. The inference frame (S3, S6). the general doctrine to be inferred certainly is, that… / the conclusion drawn is, that… — a subject noun carrying a passive modifier, then Beveridge's comma, then the that-clause holding the payload. Plus the archaic causal seeing (that) = since (S6) — not a participle of see.

Bonus traps I will test: stock (S3) = a tree trunk, a block of wood — no inventories, no shares in 1559. doctrine (S3) = teaching (what a thing teaches), not a theology textbook. viz. (S4) = videlicet, "namely" — read it namely. seeing (S6) = since. And one source-scan anomaly in S2 you must not "correct" silently — details at S2.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 8 sentences –

5. I am not ignorant, indeed, of the assertion, which is now more than threadbare, “that images are the books of the unlearned.” So said Gregory: but the Holy Spirit goes a very different decision; and had Gregory got his lesson in this matter in the Spirit’s school, he never would have spoken as he did. For when Jeremiah declares that “the stock is a doctrine of vanities,” (Jer. 10:8), and Habakkuk, “that the molten image” is “a teacher of lies,” the general doctrine to be inferred certainly is, that every thing respecting God which is learned from images is futile and false. If it is objected that the censure of the prophets is directed against those who perverted images to purposes of impious superstition, I admit it to be so; but I add (what must be obvious to all), that the prophets utterly condemn what the Papists hold to be an undoubted axiom—viz. that images are substitutes for books. For they contrast images with the true God, as if the two were of an opposite nature, and never could be made to agree. In the passages which I lately quoted, the conclusion drawn is, that seeing there is one true God whom the Jews worshipped, visible shapes made for the purpose of representing him are false and wicked fictions; and all, therefore, who have recourse to them for knowledge are miserably deceived. In short, were it not true that all such knowledge is fallacious and spurious, the prophets would not condemn it in such general terms. This at least I maintain, that when we teach that all human attempts to give a visible shape to God are vanity and lies, we do nothing more than state verbatim what the prophets taught.

Sentence count: 8 (S1–S8). One paragraph.

Source housekeeping (page furniture removed, text otherwise character-exact): marginal page number 95 (inside S6, after seeing there is), page marker 99, the running header CHAPTER 11. - IMPIETY OF ATTRIBUTING A VISIBLE FORM TO GOD.—THE SETTING… (both fall inside S7, between fallacious and and spurious), and footnote texts 27–28 were all stripped. Curly quotes and apostrophes (Spirit’s) are the source's own.

2 — Structure at a Glance (board work) – 

[SLOGAN]      S1  the threadbare plea: images = "the books of the unlearned"
[HIGHER       S2  Gregory said it — the SPIRIT rules otherwise;
 COURT]           had Gregory learned in the Spirit's school → never said it
[SCRIPTURE    S3  Jer 10:8 "stock = a doctrine of vanities" + Hab "teacher of lies"
 RULES]           → general doctrine: ALL image-derived knowledge of God = futile & false
[OBJECTION    S4  "prophets only hit superstitious ABUSE" — granted (I admit it);
 HANDLED]         BUT they also condemn the AXIOM: images ≠ substitutes for books
              S5  proof: prophets CONTRAST images with the true God — opposite natures
[VERDICT]     S6  one true God (seeing…) → representing shapes = false, wicked fictions;
                  knowledge-seekers there = miserably deceived
              S7  were it not ALL false, prophets wouldn't condemn in GENERAL terms
[DEFENCE]     S8  "we state VERBATIM what the prophets taught" — no novelty, pure echo

Examiner's Eye — three traps I would build from this section

Trap 1 — the direction flip on S4. A test-writer will offer: "Calvin denies that the prophets' censure was aimed at those who abused images superstitiously." False — he does the opposite. S4: "I admit it to be so." The concession is genuine; the argument is that the prophets also condemn the axiom itself. An option that erases the concession, or one that stops at the concession and misses the but I add, are two versions of the same trap.

Trap 2 — the partial-knowledge soften. "Calvin allows that images convey some true, though imperfect, knowledge of God." False. S3 is a universal: "every thing respecting God which is learned from images is futile and false" — and S7 argues from that totality: if any of it were sound, the prophets would not condemn it "in such general terms." No remainder, no salvage.

Trap 3 — the novelty smear. "Calvin presents his rejection of images as a new doctrinal insight of the Reformation." False — S8 claims the exact opposite. "We do nothing more than state verbatim what the prophets taught." The Reformers' self-understanding throughout: not innovation, repetition. An exam item that has Calvin claiming originality reverses the closing sentence.

3 — Sentence-by-Sentence Live Teaching –

S1★★the litotes opener — "I am not ignorant" + the threadbare slogan›

I am not ignorant, indeed, of the assertion, which is now more than threadbare, “that images are the books of the unlearned.”

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • ignorant = in- + gnarus (knowing; cf. Greek gnōsis) — un-knowing; the litotes stacks not + in- into a plus. threadbare = thread + bare — cloth worn to the warp; the metaphor of an argument worn out by over-use.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • I am not ignorant of = ~을 모르는 바 아니다 (litotes); more than threadbare = 닳고 닳은 정도를 넘어선.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

I'm well aware, of course, of the claim — by now worn completely thin — “that images are the books of the unlearned.”

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • I am not ignorant, indeed, of → I'm well aware, of course, of
  • the assertion → the claim
  • more than threadbare → worn completely thin
  • the unlearned → people who can't read.
S2★★★the higher court — So said Gregory + the inverted conditional had Gregory got…›

So said Gregory: but the Holy Spirit goes a very different decision; and had Gregory got his lesson in this matter in the Spirit’s school, he never would have spoken as he did.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • decision < de- + caedere (to cut; cf. incision, concise) — a cutting-off of debate; a ruling. school < Greek scholē (leisure → place of learned leisure) — the Spirit's schola, where Scripture is the syllabus.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • So said X = X는 그렇게 말했다 (도치 귀속); had S p.p. = 만약 ~했더라면 (if 생략 도치); get one's lesson = 배움을 받다.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

That's what Gregory said — but the Holy Spirit gives a very different ruling; and if Gregory had learned his lesson on this subject in the Spirit's school, he never would have said what he did.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • So said Gregory → That's what Gregory said
  • goes a very different decision → gives a very different ruling (scan artifact repaired)
  • had Gregory got his lesson → if Gregory had learned his lesson
  • spoken as he did → said what he did.
S3★★★Scripture's own classroom vocabulary — Jeremiah + Habakkuk → the general doctrine›

For when Jeremiah declares that “the stock is a doctrine of vanities,” (Jer. 10:8), and Habakkuk, “that the molten image” is “a teacher of lies,” the general doctrine to be inferred certainly is, that every thing respecting God which is learned from images is futile and false.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • infer = in- + ferre (to carry) — to carry a conclusion in from evidence (Day 59's S2 word — second appearance, learn it once, score twice). futile < futilis (leaky, that pours out; of a vessel that holds nothing) — knowledge that cannot hold water. molten — archaic past participle of melt: cast metal, poured in a mould.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • the N to be p.p. = 마땅히 ~되어야 할 N; respecting = ~에 관한 (formal, = concerning); futile and false = 헛되고 거짓된 (내용 없음 + 내용이 틀림).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

For when Jeremiah declares that “the wooden block is a teaching of empty things” (Jer. 10:8), and Habakkuk that “the molten image” is “a teacher of lies,” the general lesson to draw is certainly this: everything about God that people learn from images is worthless and wrong.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • the stock → the wooden block
  • a doctrine of vanities → a teaching of empty things
  • the general doctrine to be inferred certainly is, that → the general lesson to draw is certainly this:
  • every thing respecting God which is learned from → everything about God that people learn from
  • futile and false → worthless and wrong.
S4★★★the concession machine — I admit it to be so; but I add… + the axiom, viz.›

If it is objected that the censure of the prophets is directed against those who perverted images to purposes of impious superstition, I admit it to be so; but I add (what must be obvious to all), that the prophets utterly condemn what the Papists hold to be an undoubted axiom—viz. that images are substitutes for books.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • censure < censere (to assess, judge; cf. census, censor) — the judge's adverse finding. pervert = per- (thoroughly, astray) + vertere (to turn) — to twist to a wrong use; cf. convert, invert. axiom < Greek axios (worthy) — a claim "worthy" of acceptance without proof; that unearned worthiness is exactly what Calvin attacks.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • if it is objected that = ~라는 반론이 제기된다면; admit X to be so = X가 사실임을 인정하다; hold X to be Y = X를 Y로 여기다; viz. = 즉 (= namely).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

If someone objects that the prophets' rebuke was aimed at people who twisted images into tools of godless superstition, I admit that's true; but I add — and this must be obvious to everyone — that the prophets utterly condemn what the Papists treat as an unquestioned axiom: namely, that images are substitutes for books.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • If it is objected that → If someone objects that
  • the censure of the prophets is directed against → the prophets' rebuke was aimed at
  • perverted images to purposes of impious superstition → twisted images into tools of godless superstition
  • I admit it to be so → I admit that's true
  • an undoubted axiom—viz. → an unquestioned axiom: namely.
S5★the proof in one stroke — as if the two were of an opposite nature›

For they contrast images with the true God, as if the two were of an opposite nature, and never could be made to agree.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • contrast < contra- + stare (to stand) — to stand things against each other; the prophets' method in one etymology.
전체 해설 더보기

One point: as if + past subjunctive — as if the two were of an opposite nature — the were holds for every subject, and here the as if clause is not dismissing the comparison but reporting the prophets' own framing: they set images and the true God face to face as opposites — two natures that never could be made to agree, no treaty possible. That irreconcilability is why the axiom of S4 dies: a thing cannot be a book about God if it is by nature God's opposite. Move on.

S6★★the verdict from §4's texts — seeing there is + fictions and the deceived›

In the passages which I lately quoted, the conclusion drawn is, that seeing there is one true God whom the Jews worshipped, visible shapes made for the purpose of representing him are false and wicked fictions; and all, therefore, who have recourse to them for knowledge are miserably deceived.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • recourse = re- + currere (to run; cf. current, concourse) — a running-back to something for help. fiction < fingere (to mould, fabricate) — cf. figment, feign; a shaped falsehood.
💬 Idiom · 관용

S7★★the clincher — were it not true that… + the argument from general terms›

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — the if-less conditional: subjunctive inversion (S2, S7)

PAST counterfactual:    Had + S + p.p. …, S + would have p.p.
                        S2: Had Gregory got his lesson…, he never would have spoken…
PRESENT counterfactual: Were + S (+ not) …, S + would + V(bare)
                        S7: Were it not true that…, the prophets would not condemn…

⚠ Only had / were / should invert. No other auxiliary fronts.
⚠ The inverted subjunctive is WERE for every person — "was it not true" is wrong.
⚠ Match the timelines: Had…p.p. → would have p.p. / Were… → would V.
   A crossed pair (Had…, would condemn) is the exam's favourite corruption.

Transformation drill. Theme: the church and the Word. → Had the church remained in the Spirit's school, she never would have exchanged the living Word for painted walls; and were it not true that God alone teaches savingly, such an exchange would be harmless.

Formula 2 — the concession machine: concessio (S1, S4)

OPENER (litotes):  I am not ignorant (of the assertion) that …   = I know it perfectly well
FULL FRAME:        If it is objected that …, I admit it to be so;
                   but I add (what must be obvious to all), that …

⚠ "it is objected" — impersonal passive: the objection is generic, no straw man named.
⚠ "admit X to be so" — the concession must be REAL; sarcasm breaks the machine.
⚠ The "but I add" clause must outweigh the concession — concede the fact,
   defeat the inference.

Transformation drill. Theme: ceremonies of the old law. → If it is objected that ceremonies were once commanded by God himself, I admit it to be so; but I add (what no reader of the apostle can deny), that they were commanded only until the time of reformation.

Formula 3 — the inference frame + archaic causal seeing (S3, S6)

the + N(doctrine / conclusion) + (to be inferred | drawn) + certainly is, + that-clause
    S3: the general doctrine to be inferred certainly is, that…
    S6: the conclusion drawn is, that…

⚠ The subject noun carries a passive modifier (to be inferred / drawn = which must
   be inferred / which is drawn).
⚠ Beveridge's comma before "that" is house style — not an error to "fix".
⚠ seeing (that) + clause = SINCE (causal conjunction, archaic) — not V-ing of "see".

Transformation drill. Theme: providence. → The doctrine to be gathered from the whole of Scripture certainly is, that seeing God governs all things by his secret counsel, nothing befalls the faithful by chance.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

Where we are in the chapter's plan. Beveridge's analysis: §§1–2 refuted the visible-form party; §§3–4 answered the objection from God's symbolic manifestations; §§5–7 demolish Gregory's "books of the unlearned" — §5 by Scripture, §6 by the ancient theologians (Lactantius, Eusebius, Augustine — tomorrow), §7 by the actual character of the Papists' images. Today is answer one: the Spirit's own ruling.

Gregory and Serenus — where the slogan comes from. Around 599–600, Serenus, bishop of Marseilles, alarmed at parishioners venerating the images in his churches, had them smashed and thrown out. Gregory the Great wrote him twice (the letters footnoted in our source): a careful two-front ruling — Serenus was right that images must not be adored, wrong to destroy them, "for a picture provides for the unlearned what Scripture provides for readers" (quod legentibus scriptura, hoc idiotis praestat pictura). From that sentence grew the medieval axiom — images as libri idiotarum, the laity's Bible — the working defence of the entire Western image-apparatus for nine centuries. Notice what Calvin concedes to Gregory without saying so: Gregory also forbade adoration. The quarrel is over the remainder — whether the unadored image can teach.

Calvin's actual argument — epistemology, not vandalism. The strategy of §5 is easy to misread as mere proof-texting. It is sharper than that. Gregory's plea is a claim about media: images can carry true knowledge of God to those who cannot read. Calvin answers with Scripture's own media-criticism: Jer 10:8 and Hab 2:18 describe idols in pedagogical terms — a "doctrine," a "teacher" — and grade the instruction: vanities, lies. So the question is not whether images teach (they do; everyone in the debate agrees they do) but what they are capable of teaching about God — and the prophetic answer is: only falsehood. A book all of whose sentences are false is not a book for the unlearned. That epistemological turn — from "may we have images?" to "what can an image know?" — is Calvin's signature contribution to the old controversy.

Where the argument goes next in history. The Heidelberg Catechism (1563), Q98, reads like this section versified: "But may not images be tolerated in the churches as books to the laity? No: for we must not pretend to be wiser than God, who will have his people taught, not by dumb images, but by the lively preaching of his word." The positive half of that answer — lively preaching — is Calvin's implicit counter-proposal throughout: God appointed a medium for the unlearned; it is the audible Word, not the visible shape. On the other side, the Council of Trent (Session 25, 1563) reaffirmed the instructional and commemorative use of images, with abuses to be corrected — Gregory's position, fortified. The two confessional lines harden exactly along the fault Calvin exposes today.

Guardrail — do not over-read. Three fences. (1) Calvin is not condemning painting and sculpture as arts — §12, two days from now, calls them gifts of God; the target is images of God and images as media of the knowledge of God. (2) He concedes (S4) that the prophets' immediate polemic hit superstitious abusers — do not flatten his careful concessio into "the prophets wrote against Gregory." (3) The "Spirit's school" is not a claim to private illumination — its curriculum is precisely the written texts Calvin cites. Quote him for what he argues: images cannot serve as books about God, because everything they teach of him is false.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. Jeremiah 10:8 — S3 (quoted). Hebrew mûsar hăḇālîm ʿēṣ hûʾ — literally "the instruction of vapors is wood": the idol's whole curriculum, when you strip it down, is a piece of timber. hebel (vapor, breath) is Ecclesiastes' "vanity" — teaching with the substance of fog. Beveridge/AV render the wood-word with stock. Calvin's use: the verse hands him Gregory's own metaphor pre-graded — the idol is indeed in the teaching business, and its subject-matter is emptiness.
  2. Habakkuk 2:18 — S3 (quoted). "What profiteth the graven image… the molten image, and a teacher of lies…?" — Hebrew môreh šāqer, "teacher of falsehood." môreh is from the root yrh, the same root that gives Torah: the idol is an anti-Torah, a rival instruction whose content is šeqer, lie. Calvin's use: the second witness that Scripture's own vocabulary for idols is pedagogical — and damning.
  3. Psalm 115:4–8 / 135:15–18 — S6 ("the passages which I lately quoted"). Yesterday's spine, still load-bearing: the one true God set against the works of men's hands, and the makers becoming like the made. Calvin's use: the contrast structure of those psalms (S5: "as if the two were of an opposite nature") is itself the refutation of the substitute-book axiom — you cannot learn a thing from its opposite.
  4. Romans 1:21–23 — S3, S6 (echo, uncited). "They became vain in their imaginations" — Greek emataiōthēsan, from mataios (futile) — "and changed the glory of the uncorruptible God into an image made like to corruptible man." The Pauline analysis underneath Calvin's "futile and false": image-making is not a failed attempt at true knowledge but the exchange of it. Calvin's use: silent warrant for the totality claim — the medium does not dilute the knowledge of God; it replaces it.
  5. Deuteronomy 4:12, 15 — S8 (echo). "Ye heard the voice of the words, but saw no similitude; only ye heard a voice." The positive ground under the whole section: at Sinai God gave his people a voice, deliberately no shape — the divinely chosen medium for the unlearned is audible, not visible. Calvin's use: when S8 claims to repeat the prophets verbatim, this is the Mosaic bedrock the claim stands on.

8 — Exam Problems –

① Grammar — find the wrong underline

①Had Gregory got his lesson in this matter in the Spirit’s school, he never ②would have spoken as he did; and ③was it not true that all such knowledge is fallacious and spurious, the prophets ④would not condemn it in such general terms.

Answer: ③ — inverted subjunctive conditionals take were for every person: were it not true that… The indicative was cannot front in an if-less conditional. (① correct had-inversion for the past counterfactual; ② correct would have + p.p. matching the Had…got timeline; ④ correct present-counterfactual would + V matching were it not true.)

Exam-setter's rationale: Formula 1's second warning verbatim. Students who read ③ aloud and feel nothing have never internalised that inversion is a subjunctive privilege — was is grammatical in a plain question ("Was it not true?"), which is exactly the camouflage the item exploits.

② Comprehension — which statement matches the passage?

(a) Calvin denies that the prophets' censure had anything to do with those who abused images superstitiously. (b) While admitting that the prophets aimed at superstitious abuse, Calvin holds that they also condemn the very axiom that images may serve as substitutes for books. (c) Calvin allows that images convey some true knowledge of God, though imperfectly and mixed with error. (d) Calvin presents the Reformed rejection of images as a doctrinal advance beyond what the prophets themselves taught.

Answer: (b) — the precise shape of S4: concession ("I admit it to be so") plus the addition ("the prophets utterly condemn what the Papists hold to be an undoubted axiom—viz. that images are substitutes for books").

Exam-setter's rationale: (a) is Trap 1, the direction flip — it deletes Calvin's genuine concession. (c) is Trap 2, the partial-knowledge soften — S3's "every thing respecting God which is learned from images is futile and false" and S7's argument from "such general terms" leave no remainder. (d) is Trap 3, the novelty smear — S8 claims the exact opposite: "nothing more than state verbatim what the prophets taught." Every wrong option is one of Period 2's traps, worded to sound scholarly.

③ Composition — apply Formula 2

Translate into Calvin-style English, using the full concessio frame (impersonal objection → real concession → outweighing addition):

"교부들이 형상을 용인했다는 반론이 제기된다면, 나는 그것이 사실임을 인정한다. 그러나 나는 덧붙인다 — 누구에게나 명백할 수밖에 없는 사실이지만 — 그들은 형상이 예배당 안에서 경배의 대상이 되는 것을 결코 용납하지 않았다."

Model answer: If it is objected that the fathers tolerated images, I admit it to be so; but I add (what must be obvious to all), that they never suffered images to become objects of adoration within the church.

Exam-setter's rationale: Checklist — impersonal passive If it is objected that ✓; concession by admit + it + to be so (not "admit that it is so-called" or a sarcastic hedge) ✓; but I add + parenthetic (what must be obvious to all) in apposition to the coming that-clause ✓; the addition genuinely outweighing the concession ✓. Commonest student errors: naming the objector ("If the Papists object…"), which breaks the impersonal frame; and writing admit it is so without the to-infinitive, losing the formal pattern.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary: Gregory called images the books of the unlearned, but the Spirit's school teaches from a different textbook (S1–S2): Scripture itself labels the idol a doctrine of vanities and a teacher of lies (S3), so even granting that the prophets' fire fell on superstitious abusers, they still burn down the axiom that images substitute for books (S4–S5) — image-knowledge of God is fiction wholesale, or the prophets would not condemn it in general terms (S6–S7), and in saying so the Reformers merely repeat the prophets verbatim (S8). Grammar to keep: ① the if-less conditional — Had + S + p.p. / were it not true that, and it is were, never was; ② the concession machine — litotes opener + I admit it to be so; but I add…; ③ the inference frame the doctrine to be inferred / conclusion drawn is, that… with archaic causal seeing (that) = since.
