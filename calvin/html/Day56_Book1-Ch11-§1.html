Day 56 — Book 1, Chapter 11, §1

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

New chapter. And look where we are standing.

Yesterday (I.10.3) Calvin closed with Habakkuk 2:20: after condemning all idols, seek God in his temple — and the temple turned out to be his word. That was the door. Today we walk through it. Chapter 11 is Calvin's iconoclasm treatise, and §1 is the load-bearing wall: God is the only fit witness to himself, therefore no shape.

But here is why this section is dangerous for a careless reader. Calvin opens with a concession about how Scripture talks — popularly, coarsely, for coarse minds. A hasty student reads "accommodation" and thinks: ah, so Scripture's language is soft, negotiable, culturally relative. Then Calvin drops a stone: "as often as any form is assigned to God, his glory is corrupted by an impious lie." Not negotiable. Not soft. The accommodation is in the register, never in the rule.

And then the closing hammer — the one that kills the sophisticated defence forever: "God makes no comparison between images." No gradations. No "ours is more refined." The Greeks tried that. Calvin levels them with the Egyptians and their onions.

Three sentences today are grammatically gorgeous. Do not skim them.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. As A, so B correlative — with the so-arm delayed by a wedge clause (S1), and its counting twin As many X as …, so many Y … (S7). Same skeleton, two completely different jobs. Find the so before you translate anything.
  2. not that …, but that … may … (S1) — the double-purpose correction frame, riding on the adverbial the better (= all the more effectively). This is not "not because."
  3. Constructions with no finite verb of their own: nominative absolute God himself **being** the only fit witness (S2) and the three-deep absolute stack men **longing** … and so **forming** … (S3). If you hunt for the main verb inside these, you lose the sentence.

Bonus trap I will make you pay for if you miss it: S10's shared predicate. as if one were more, and another less befitting — one word befitting is doing two jobs.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 10 sentences –

1. As Scripture, in accommodation to the rude and gross intellect of man, usually speaks in popular terms, so whenever its object is to discriminate between the true God and false deities, it opposes him in particular to idols; not that it approves of what is taught more elegantly and subtilely by philosophers, but that it may the better expose the folly, nay, madness of the world in its inquiries after God, so long as every one clings to his own speculations. This exclusive definition, which we uniformly meet with in Scripture, annihilates every deity which men frame for themselves of their own accord—God himself being the only fit witness to himself. Meanwhile, seeing that this brutish stupidity has overspread the globe, men longing after visible forms of God, and so forming deities of wood and stone, silver and gold, or of any other dead and corruptible matter, we must hold it as a first principle, that as often as any form is assigned to God, his glory is corrupted by an impious lie. In the Law, accordingly, after God had claimed the glory of divinity for himself alone, when he comes to show what kind of worship he approves and rejects, he immediately adds, “Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image, or any likeness of any thing that is in heaven above, or in the earth beneath, or in the water under the earth,” (Exod. 20:4). By these words he curbs any licentious attempt we might make to represent him by a visible shape, and briefly enumerates all the forms by which superstition had begun, even long before, to turn his truth into a lie. For we know that the Sun was worshipped by the Persian. As many stars as the foolish nations saw in the sky, so many gods they imagined them to be. Then to the Egyptians, every animal was a figure of God. The Greeks, again, plumed themselves on their superior wisdom in worshipping God under the human form (Maximum Tyrius Platonic. Serm. 38). But God makes no comparison between images, as if one were more, and another less befitting; he rejects, without exception, all shapes and pictures, and other symbols by which the superstitious imagine they can bring him near to them.

Sentence count: 10 (S1–S10). One paragraph.

2 — Structure at a Glance (board work) – 

[METHOD]     S1  Scripture accommodates → speaks popularly → opposes God to IDOLS
                 ⤷ not endorsing philosophers, but exposing the world's madness
[THESIS]     S2  This exclusive definition ANNIHILATES all self-made deities
                 ⤷ ground: God = only fit witness to himself   (← I.7 testimonium)
[PRINCIPLE]  S3  Because the disease is universal → FIRST PRINCIPLE:
                 any form assigned to God = his glory corrupted by an impious lie
[LAW]        S4  Exod 20:4 quoted — the Second Commandment as the divine ruling
             S5  Function of the words: CURBS the attempt + ENUMERATES the forms
[EXHIBITS]   S6  Persians  — the Sun
             S7  star-worshippers — as many stars, so many gods
             S8  Egyptians — every animal
             S9  Greeks    — the human form (the "refined" defence)
[VERDICT]    S10 God makes NO COMPARISON — rejects all, without exception

Examiner's Eye — three traps already loaded:

  1. S1 direction-flip. not that it approves of what is taught more elegantly and subtilely by philosophers — a test option will say "Scripture prefers the philosophers' refined account to popular speech." Dead wrong, and it is the exact opposite of the clause. Calvin declines to endorse the philosophers in the very act of attacking the idolaters. He is fighting on two fronts, and he tells you so in a subordinate clause.
  2. S9 → S10 scope trap. The Greeks' "superior wisdom" is reported, then demolished. An option will read "Calvin concedes the human form is a less objectionable representation." S10 exists to kill that: no comparison, without exception.
  3. S3 excuse trap. men longing after visible forms of God describes the disease, not a mitigating circumstance. An option will say the accommodation principle excuses this longing. It does not — it is precisely what the first principle condemns.

3 — Sentence-by-Sentence Live Teaching –

S1★★★the correlative with a delayed so-arm + not that … but that … may›

As Scripture, in accommodation to the rude and gross intellect of man, usually speaks in popular terms, so whenever its object is to discriminate between the true God and false deities, it opposes him in particular to idols; not that it approves of what is taught more elegantly and subtilely by philosophers, but that it may the better expose the folly, nay, madness of the world in its inquiries after God, so long as every one clings to his own speculations.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • accommodation < ad + commodus (fitting) — Calvin's technical term accommodatio; rude < rudis (raw, unworked — not "impolite"); gross < grossus (thick, coarse — not "disgusting"); popular < populus (of the common people — not "well-liked"); discriminate < dis + crimen (a separating judgement — not "prejudice"); subtilely < sub + tela (under the web → finely woven — met yesterday as subtle, I.10.3 S10).
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • not that A, but that B = "I don't mean A; the point is B"; the better V = all the more effectively V; nay = indeed, or rather (upward correction); so long as = as long as / for as long as; cling to = 집착하다.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

Scripture normally uses everyday language because human understanding is coarse, so when it wants to separate the true God from false gods, it specifically contrasts him with idols. That isn't an endorsement of the philosophers' more refined theories; it's a way of showing up the world's folly — really, its insanity — in its search for God, as long as everyone keeps trusting his own private ideas.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • in accommodation to → adapting itself to
  • rude and gross intellect → coarse and limited understanding
  • popular terms → everyday language
  • subtilely → subtly
  • the better expose → show up all the more clearly
  • nay → in fact, even.
S2★★★the thesis + nominative absolute›

This exclusive definition, which we uniformly meet with in Scripture, annihilates every deity which men frame for themselves of their own accord—God himself being the only fit witness to himself.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • annihilate < ad + nihil (to nothing); exclusive < ex + claudere (to shut out); frame = OE framian (to be useful → to construct) — here fabricate; fit = suitable, competent, admissible (legal register).
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • meet with = encounter, come across; of one's own accord = spontaneously, on one's own initiative (accord < ad + cor, "to the heart").
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

This exclusive definition, which we find everywhere in Scripture, destroys every god that people invent for themselves on their own — since God alone is qualified to testify about himself.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • uniformly meet with → find everywhere
  • frame for themselves → invent for themselves
  • of their own accord → on their own initiative
  • fit witness → qualified/competent witness
  • God himself being → since God alone is.
S3★★★the absolute stack — and the first principle›

Meanwhile, seeing that this brutish stupidity has overspread the globe, men longing after visible forms of God, and so forming deities of wood and stone, silver and gold, or of any other dead and corruptible matter, we must hold it as a first principle, that as often as any form is assigned to God, his glory is corrupted by an impious lie.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • brutish < brutus (heavy, dull, insensible — the "dullness" thread from I.5.11–12); overspread = spread over, cover; corruptible < cor + rumpere (to break utterly) = liable to decay, φθαρτός; impious < in + pius — the negation of pietas, Calvin's key virtue since I.2.1.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • seeing that = since, given that; long after = crave, yearn for; hold X as a first principle = lay down X as an axiom; as often as = whenever.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

Meanwhile, since this animal-like dullness has spread across the whole world — people craving visible forms of God and therefore making gods out of wood, stone, silver, gold, or any other lifeless, perishable material — we have to take it as a basic rule that any time a form is assigned to God, his glory is ruined by a blasphemous lie.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • brutish stupidity → animal-like dullness
  • overspread the globe → spread across the whole world
  • longing after → craving
  • dead and corruptible matter → lifeless, perishable material
  • hold it as a first principle → take it as a basic rule
  • as often as → any time / whenever.
S4★★the Law arrives — and note the sequencing of clauses›

In the Law, accordingly, after God had claimed the glory of divinity for himself alone, when he comes to show what kind of worship he approves and rejects, he immediately adds, “Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image, or any likeness of any thing that is in heaven above, or in the earth beneath, or in the water under the earth,” (Exod. 20:4).

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • graven = p.p. of grave (to carve, engrave) — Hebrew פֶּסֶל pesel, a carved thing. Note the arc: God engraves on hearts (I.3.1, I.5.1, I.10.3); men grave in stone. Same verb, opposite direction; likeness = Hebrew תְּמוּנָה tᵉmûnâ, form/shape.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • claim the glory for himself alone = assert exclusive right to; come to V = reach the point of V-ing; unto thee = for yourself (archaic reflexive dative).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

So in the Law, after God had claimed the honour of divinity for himself alone, when he gets to the point of showing which kind of worship he accepts and which he rejects, he immediately goes on: "You shall not make for yourself a carved image, or any likeness of anything in heaven above, or on the earth beneath, or in the water under the earth" (Exod. 20:4).

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • accordingly → so
  • had claimed the glory of divinity → had claimed the honour of being God
  • when he comes to show → when he gets to showing
  • immediately adds → immediately goes on
  • unto thee → for yourself
  • graven image → carved image.
S5★★zero relative + a split infinitive phrase + Paul under the surface›

By these words he curbs any licentious attempt we might make to represent him by a visible shape, and briefly enumerates all the forms by which superstition had begun, even long before, to turn his truth into a lie.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • curb < OFr courbe (the curb-chain of a bridle) = restrain; licentious < licere (to be permitted) → license = unrestrained, presumptuous (not lewd); enumerate < ex + numerus = count out; superstition < super + stare (to stand over/survive) = excessive or misdirected religious observance.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • by these words = through this wording; turn A into B = 바꾸다 (here echoing Rom 1:25); even long before = long beforehand, already.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

With this wording he restrains any presumptuous attempt we might make to portray him in a visible shape, and briefly lists all the forms through which superstition had already, long before, begun turning his truth into a lie.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • curbs → restrains
  • licentious attempt → presumptuous attempt
  • represent him by a visible shape → portray him in a visible form
  • enumerates → lists
  • even long before → long before that, already.
S6★generic singular›

For we know that the Sun was worshipped by the Persian.

전체 해설 더보기

One point: the Persian = the generic singular — the Persian as a type, i.e. Persians as a class. Same construction as "the Englishman loves his garden." Do not translate as one specific man. (Capital Sun is the source's own; Beveridge capitalises the worshipped object.)

Historically: Mithra and the Zoroastrian fire-and-light cult, as the 16th century understood it via Herodotus (I.131) and Cicero.

S7★★★the counting correlative + fronted complement + ECM›

As many stars as the foolish nations saw in the sky, so many gods they imagined them to be.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • foolish < OE fōl — cf. Ps 14:1 נָבָל (Day 10's `The fool hath said in his heart`); imagine < imago — literally "to form an image of," so the verb itself is doing iconography. Calvin's word choice is not accidental: the sin of the section is imagining, i.e. image-making in the mind before it is image-making in wood.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • as many X as …, so many Y … = however many X …, that many Y …; imagine O to be C = suppose O to be C (5형식).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

However many stars the foolish nations could see in the sky, that's how many gods they supposed those stars to be.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • As many … as … so many … → however many … that's how many …
  • the foolish nations → those foolish peoples
  • imagined them to be → supposed them to be.
S8★the shortest exhibit›

Then to the Egyptians, every animal was a figure of God.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • figure — figura. Not "number," not "a notable person."
전체 해설 더보기

One point: figure = figura, a representation/likeness, not "a number" and not "a person of note." Egyptian theriomorphism is the third rung down the ladder — sun, stars, beasts.

(Beveridge's footnote here preserves Calvin's French edition, which pushes the joke one rung further: "voire jusques aux oignons et porreaux" — "they have gone even to onions and leeks." That is Juvenal, Satire XV, on Egyptians worshipping their vegetables. Calvin kept the jab in French and dropped it in Latin. See Period 6.)

S9★★the sophisticated defence — stated at its strongest›

The Greeks, again, plumed themselves on their superior wisdom in worshipping God under the human form (Maximum Tyrius Platonic. Serm. 38).

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • plume oneself on < pluma (feather) — to preen; superior < super (comparative of superus); figure/form pairing continues the imago family of S7.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • plume oneself on X = pride oneself on X (with a sneer); again here = in their turn, next in the list (not "once more" — cf. Day 47 S6 `again` = in his turn).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

The Greeks, for their part, congratulated themselves on their more refined wisdom in worshipping God in human form (Maximus of Tyre, Platonic Discourses, Serm. 38).

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • plumed themselves on → congratulated themselves on
  • again → for their part / in their turn
  • superior wisdom → more refined wisdom
  • under the human form → in human form.
S10★★★the verdict — shared predicate + as if subjunctive›

But God makes no comparison between images, as if one were more, and another less befitting; he rejects, without exception, all shapes and pictures, and other symbols by which the superstitious imagine they can bring him near to them.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • befitting < be- (intensive) + fit — picking up S2's `fit witness`: God is the only fit witness, and no image is fit (the same root closes the frame); superstitious < super + stare; symbol < συμβάλλειν (to throw together) — a token that stands in for the absent thing, which is exactly the fraud Calvin names.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • make a comparison between = rank against each other; as if + were = as though (counterfactual); without exception = 예외 없이; bring X near to Y = bring X within Y's reach.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

But God draws no distinction between images, as though one were more and another less appropriate; he rejects, without exception, every shape, picture, and other symbol that superstitious people think can bring him close to them.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • makes no comparison between → draws no distinction between
  • as if one were more, and another less befitting → as though one were more and another less appropriate
  • without exception → with no exceptions at all
  • the superstitious → superstitious people
  • imagine → think.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — The As … so … correlative, and how to tell which one you have

As  + S₁ + V₁ … ,   so  ( ‹wedge clause› )  S₂ + V₂ …          → manner / consequence
As many + N + as + S + V … ,   so many + N   S + V …            → quantity / one-for-one count

⚠️ Trap A: the so-arm may be delayed by a subordinate wedge (so **whenever its object is to discriminate…**, it opposes). Read past the wedge to find the finite verb. ⚠️ Trap B: the skeleton is identical in both types. The word right after as decides: a clause → manner; many/much + N → quantity. ⚠️ Trap C: in the quantity type the complement is fronted (so many gods they imagined them to be). Un-front before translating.

Transformation drill. Combine into one correlative sentence: Calvin adapts his Latin to unlearned readers. Calvin therefore attacks popular idolatry rather than academic error. → As Calvin adapts his Latin to unlearned readers, so, whenever he sets out to distinguish true worship from false, he attacks popular idolatry rather than academic error.

And in the quantity type: The nations counted stars. They multiplied gods to the same number. → As many stars as the nations counted, so many gods they supposed them to be.

Formula 2 — The correction frame not that A, but that B (may) … + adverbial the

… X …  ;   not that [S + V]  ,   but that [S + may + V]
                 ↑                        ↑
        "I don't mean that…"      "the purpose is that…"

adverbial THE:   S + may + the + COMPARATIVE + V   =  V all the more ADV-ly
                 (the better expose = expose all the more effectively)

⚠️ Trap A: not that ≠ not because. It denies an inference, not a cause. ⚠️ Trap B: the better expose is correct. Do not "repair" it to to better expose. ⚠️ Trap C: nay inside this frame is upward correction (folly → nay, madness), never negation.

Transformation drill. Render in the frame: Calvin condemns idols. He is not endorsing the philosophers. His aim is to display the world's madness more sharply. → Calvin condemns idols; not that he approves what the philosophers teach, but that he may the better display the folly, nay, the madness of the world.

Formula 3 — Non-finite constructions that own the sentence

NOMINATIVE ABSOLUTE:      NOUN + PARTICIPLE  (no finite verb, comma-fenced)
                          "God himself BEING the only fit witness to himself"
                          → since / because God himself is …

ABSOLUTE STACK:           NOUN + PART₁ , and so + PART₂ …   (coordinated, still no finite verb)
                          "men LONGING …, and so FORMING …"

ANTICIPATORY IT:          V + it + as + N ,  that [ … ]
                          "hold IT as a first principle, THAT …"

⚠️ Trap A: the noun in an absolute is not the sentence's subject. Keep hunting. (Today: the subject is we, sixty words in.) ⚠️ Trap B: never "fix" an absolute into a finite clause — you produce a comma splice and lose the subordination that makes the main clause the verdict. ⚠️ Trap C: with anticipatory it, the that-clause is the real object. Do not translate it as a pronoun.

Transformation drill. Convert to absolutes and one main clause: Superstition has covered the world. Men crave visible forms. They therefore carve gods from timber. We must lay down an axiom: every assigned form is a lie. → Superstition having covered the world, men craving visible forms and so carving gods from timber, we must hold it as a first principle, that every form assigned to God is a lie.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

Where this chapter sits, and why it sits here. The 1559 architecture is deliberate. Ch. 10 established that Scripture presents the true God exclusively, rejecting the gods of the nations. Ch. 11 draws the operational consequence: if God is known exclusively by his own self-attestation, then every visible substitute is a rival witness. Chapter 10's conclusion is Chapter 11's premise. Calvin is not changing the subject; he is collecting a debt.

Accommodation — Calvin's most borrowed and most abused idea. Accommodatio is the principle that God adapts his self-disclosure to human capacity. Its patristic ancestor is Chrysostom's συγκατάβασις (condescension); its most famous Calvinian image comes two chapters on (I.13.1): God "lisps with us as nurses do with infants." The guardrail: accommodation for Calvin governs the manner of divine speech, not its truth. God speaks simply; he does not speak falsely. In S1 the accommodation explains why Scripture argues against idols rather than against metaphysics — a choice of opponent, not a softening of doctrine. Modern appeals to accommodation as a warrant for treating Scripture's content as culturally negotiable are a very long way from what Calvin does with it here, where accommodation is immediately followed by an absolute prohibition.

Why Calvin can cite "the Second Commandment" as a freestanding law. This matters and is regularly missed. Reformed (with Orthodox and Jewish) numbering counts Exod 20:4 — "no graven image" — as the Second Commandment. Roman Catholic and Lutheran catechetical numbering folds it into the First and splits coveting into Ninth and Tenth. Calvin's argument in S4 depends on the prohibition being a distinct commandment with its own force, immediately consequent on the claim to exclusive divinity. The numbering is not pedantry; it is the joint the argument turns on.

The pagan witnesses, and why Calvin ranks them. The exhibits ascend deliberately — Persian sun, star cults, Egyptian animals, Greek anthropomorphism. Maximus Tyrius (c. 125–185), the Platonist rhetorician cited in S9, offers in his Dissertationes (Calvin's Serm. 38 = modern Or. 2) the strongest pagan defence of images: they are a concession to weakness, a ladder for minds that cannot ascend to the intelligible God. Calvin quotes the best available opponent, then denies the premise the defence needs — that images admit of degrees. Note also the footnote Beveridge preserves at S8: Calvin's French edition adds "voire jusques aux oignons et porreaux" — "even to onions and leeks," the old jibe from Juvenal (Sat. XV) at Egyptian vegetable-worship. Calvin kept the joke in French for a lay readership and dropped it from the Latin. A small window into how he wrote for two audiences.

Downstream. The Second Commandment's exclusivity becomes the regulative principle of worship — Westminster Confession 21.1, "the acceptable way of worshipping the true God is instituted by himself, and so limited by his own revealed will, that he may not be worshipped according to the imaginations and devices of men… or any other way not prescribed in the holy Scripture." That last clause is S3's first principle in confessional dress. Heidelberg Catechism Q.96–98 works the same ground, including the "books of the unlearned" objection Calvin takes up in §§5–7.

⚠️ Over-reading guardrails — three fences, and I want all three standing.

  1. This is not a condemnation of visual art. §12 will say explicitly that sculpture and painting are gifts of God, and that the question is use and abuse. The prohibition here is about representing God and about worship. Flattening §1 into "Calvin banned pictures" misrepresents the chapter you are about to read.
  2. This is not fideism or anti-intellectualism. S1 declines to endorse the philosophers; it does not condemn philosophy as such. Same fence as I.10.3 (the target was evasions, not reason) and II.2.15 (do not despise the gifts of the natural mind). Calvin fights on two fronts and confuses neither.
  3. This is not a warrant for iconoclastic riot. Calvin's own position on image removal was magisterial — action by lawful authority, not mob demolition. §1 supplies a doctrine, not a crowbar. Reading the 1566 Beeldenstorm back into 1559 is an anachronism, and it is a favourite of hostile summaries.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. Exodus 20:4 — quoted verbatim, S4. פֶּסֶל pesel (a carved thing) + תְּמוּנָה tᵉmûnâ (form, likeness); LXX εἴδωλον … ὁμοίωμα. Note the reflexive dative לְךָ lᵉḵā, "for yourself" — the self-serving element is legislated into the text, matching S2's frame for themselves of their own accord. How Calvin uses it: as the divine ruling that settles the case, positioned to follow immediately on the First Commandment.

  2. Romans 1:25 — S5, to turn his truth into a lie. μετήλλαξαν τὴν ἀλήθειαν τοῦ θεοῦ ἐν τῷ ψεύδει. Beveridge's phrasing tracks the KJV ("changed the truth of God into a lie"). How Calvin uses it: unmarked — the proof text is embedded in the predicate rather than cited. His reader is expected to hear it.

  3. Romans 1:23 — S3, dead and corruptible matter + his glory is corrupted. ἤλλαξαν τὴν δόξαν τοῦ ἀφθάρτου θεοῦ ἐν ὁμοιώματι εἰκόνος φθαρτοῦ ἀνθρώπου καὶ πετεινῶν καὶ τετραπόδων καὶ ἑρπετῶν. How Calvin uses it: the pairing δόξα + φθαρτός is reproduced exactly as glory + corruptible, and Paul's descending list of creatures (man → birds → beasts → reptiles) is the template for Calvin's own exhibit list in S6–S9.

  4. Acts 17:29 — S3, wood and stone, silver and gold. τὸ θεῖον … χρυσῷ ἢ ἀργύρῳ ἢ λίθῳ, χαράγματι τέχνης καὶ ἐνθυμήσεως ἀνθρώπου. How Calvin uses it: Paul's Areopagus list becomes Calvin's list, which is fitting — Paul at Athens is arguing against exactly the "refined" Greek position S9 reports.

  5. Deuteronomy 4:15–16 — behind S5, and quoted in full in §2 tomorrow: "ye saw no manner of similitude." How Calvin uses it: here only as the logic (no form was seen, therefore no form may be made); the text itself is held back one section.

  6. Isaiah 40:18 — behind S10. "To whom then will ye liken God? or what likeness will ye compare unto him?" Hebrew תְּדַמְּיוּן tᵉdammᵉyûn (liken) — the very act of comparison is what the prophet forbids, which is Calvin's God makes no comparison between images.

  7. Psalm 115:4–8 (and its twin 135:15–18) — behind S3's material list. "Their idols are silver and gold, the work of men's hands." The psalm's sting — they that make them are like unto them — is the theology behind Calvin's brutish stupidity.

  8. Isaiah 44:9–20 — the carpenter who burns half the log and worships the other half. Held for §4, where Calvin turns to the materials of idols.

  9. Jeremiah 10:3–5 — the tree cut from the forest, decked with silver and gold, fastened so it cannot totter. Same family as S3's dead and corruptible matter.

  10. John 4:24 — πνεῦμα ὁ θεός. The ontological premise never stated in §1 but assumed throughout: a spirit has no shape to copy.

  11. 1 Timothy 1:17 — ἀοράτῳ, "invisible." With Col 1:15 (εἰκὼν τοῦ θεοῦ τοῦ ἀοράτου), this is the pressure point Calvin will have to handle in Book 2: the one authorised image of God is the Son. §1 is silent on this by design — I.10.1 ruled the Mediator out of scope until Book 2.

8 — Exam Problems –

① Grammar

Choose the incorrect underlined portion.

As Scripture ⓐspeaks in popular terms, so whenever its object is to discriminate between the true God and false deities, it opposes him to idols; not that it approves of what the philosophers teach, but that it may ⓑthe better to expose the folly of the world — God himself ⓒbeing the only fit witness to himself, and men ⓓlonging after visible forms.

Answer: ⓑ.

Exam-setter's rationale. ⓑ mixes two constructions that cannot coexist. The adverbial the + comparative attaches directly to the bare verb after a modal: may **the better expose**. Inserting to produces may the better to expose — ungrammatical. The two legitimate options are may the better expose (Calvin's) or may better expose. ⓐ is the finite verb of the as-arm; ⓒ and ⓓ are nominative absolutes (noun + participle, no finite verb) and are correct exactly as they stand — indeed ⓒ and ⓓ are the distractors most students will pick, because a bare participle looks like a missing verb. That instinct is the trap. Learn to see the absolute and let it alone.

② Comprehension

Which statement agrees with §1?

ⓐ Because Scripture accommodates itself to coarse minds, it endorses the philosophers' more refined account of God. ⓑ God permits certain representations — such as the human form — as less objectionable than the worship of animals. ⓒ Scripture defines God by exclusion, and on that basis every form assigned to God corrupts his glory. ⓓ Idolatry began with the giving of the Law, when men first learned what forms were forbidden.

Answer: ⓒ.

Exam-setter's rationale. ⓐ is a direction flip built straight out of S1's own words. not that it approves of what is taught more elegantly and subtilely by philosophers says the reverse. The option is seductive because the first half ("Scripture accommodates itself to coarse minds") is true — a half-true stem is the most effective distractor there is. ⓑ is scope overreach, and it is the option S10 was written to destroy: God makes no comparison between images, as if one were more, and another less befitting; he rejects, **without exception**, all shapes and pictures. The Greeks made this claim (S9); Calvin reported it in order to level it. ⓓ is a chronology flip. S5 says superstition had begun **even long before** to turn God's truth into a lie. The Law diagnoses an existing disease; it does not create the category. ⓒ tracks S2 (This exclusive definition … annihilates every deity) and S3 (as often as any form is assigned to God, his glory is corrupted by an impious lie) — the definition and the principle, in that order.

③ Composition

Render into English using the quantity correlative (As many … as …, so many …) with a fronted complement + ECM, and then attach a nominative absolute giving the reason.

사람들이 자기 마음속에서 만들어 낸 형상이 많으면 많을수록, 그만큼 많은 신을 그들은 상상해 냈다 — 하나님만이 자신에 대한 합당한 증인이시기 때문이다.

Model answer:

As many forms as men framed in their own hearts, so many gods they imagined them to be — God himself being the only fit witness to himself.

Exam-setter's rationale. Three things are being tested at once, and each has a failure mode. (1) The fronted complement. so many gods must precede they imagined; writing they imagined them to be so many gods is grammatical English but loses the correlative's balance and would not score. (2) The ECM frame. imagine + O + to be + C. Dropping to be (imagined them so many gods) breaks the construction; substituting a that-clause (imagined that they were) abandons the pattern being tested. (3) The absolute. God himself **being** — a participle, not a finite verb. Writing God himself is the only fit witness creates a comma splice after a dash and converts subordinate ground into an independent assertion. The whole point of the absolute is that the reason is subordinated so the main clause keeps the weight.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary. Scripture, adapting its register to coarse minds, fights idols rather than philosophies — not to endorse philosophy but to expose the world's madness the better (S1); and its exclusive definition annihilates every home-made god, because God alone is a fit witness to himself (S2), from which follows the chapter's first principle: any form assigned to God corrupts his glory with an impious lie (S3) — legislated in Exod 20:4 (S4–S5), illustrated up an ascending ladder of pagan refinement (S6–S9), and closed by the verdict that God makes no comparison and rejects all images without exception (S10).

The three grammar points, once more: ① As … so … in two flavours — manner (S1) and quantity (S7) — with the so-arm often delayed by a wedge, and the complement often fronted. ② not that A, but that B may … + the adverbial the better V. ③ Constructions with no finite verb: nominative absolute (God himself being…) and absolute stacks (men longing …, and so forming …) — the noun in an absolute is never the sentence's subject.

Homework (10 minutes).

  1. Structure restoration. Take S3 and rewrite it as four short finite sentences, in this order: (a) the state of the world, (b) what men crave, (c) what they therefore make, (d) the principle. Then read your version against Calvin's and answer in one line: what did the absolute construction buy him that four sentences cannot? (Hint: look at which clause ends up carrying the sentence's weight.)

  2. Composition. Write one sentence using all three of today's formulas — the As … so … correlative, the not that A, but that B may … frame, and one nominative absolute. Subject: why the Reformers preached rather than painted.

  3. Preview for §2 (Day 57). Tomorrow opens: "This may easily be inferred from the reasons which he annexes to his prohibition." Calvin moves from the command to the reasons attached to it — Deuteronomy 4:15 (ye saw no manner of similitude) comes in first, then Isaiah, then Paul, and then a heathen complaint that Calvin says should shame the idolaters. Watch for two things: (i) the logic of ye saw no manner of similitude — the prohibition is grounded in what Israel did not see at Horeb, an argument from the mode of revelation (voice, not form); and (ii) annexes, a legal-drafting verb — reasons attached to a statute. Read S1 of §2 with the question: is Calvin arguing from authority, or from the nature of the revelation itself?
