Day 106 — Book 1, Chapter 14, §3

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Moses told the creation story in "popular language" — sun, moon, earth, plants, animals. No angels in Genesis 1. So are angels outside God's creation? Calvin's answer today: no — and he says it fast, then spends the rest of the section on two jobs: (1) angels are another window onto the Creator, and (2) the doctrine is a firewall against two errors — angel-worship and Manichean dualism. Then he slams on the brakes: "I will go no further than piety allows."

This is one of the most important "method" paragraphs in the Institutes. Calvin states the boundaries of theological curiosity here, and he will repeat them in §4. Examiners love it because the logic is compact and the grammar is dense: a concessive For although …, yet, seeing …, we infer (S2), an inverted conditional Were this delirium to take possession … (S9), a rhetorical question hiding an embedded seeing clause (S10), and a monster sentence with a nor … did inversion (S12).

Whose voice? In S6 the angel-worshippers think angels would be "insulted" by subordination — Calvin reports their psychology, he doesn't share it. In S8 fabricating to himself two principles is Manes' invention; S12 starts with the only good ground which the Manichees have — Calvin concedes their one good point (God can't create evil) and then disarms it.

Text traps. ⚠️ αυ τουσι α = αὐτουσία (OCR). peculiar to (S10) = belonging exclusively to, not "strange". militates against (S12) = tells against (not "fights"). cursorily (S16) = quickly, superficially. notwithstanding of (S11) is an old idiom = in spite of.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. Inverted conditional Were this delirium to take possession …, God would be denied … (S9) → Were S to V = If S were to V. ⚠️ No if — the verb moves to the front. Formula 1.
  2. seeing (that) … as a reason clause + rhetorical question: For, seeing there is nothing more peculiar to God than …, do not those who … invest him …? (S2, S10) → seeing here = since, not the participle of "see". ⚠️ A negative question (do not those who …) = a strong positive claim. Formula 2.
  3. Negative inversion and limits on speculation: nor, at first, did anything whatever exist … (S12); lest … I bewilder (S15); of all such matters … it is our duty to remain … (S16). → After nor the auxiliary jumps ahead of the subject; lest = for fear that, with a subjunctive/should-less verb. Formula 3.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 16 sentences –

But before I begin to treat more fully of the nature of man (chap. 15 and B. 2 c. 1), it will be proper to say something of angels. For although Moses, in accommodation to the ignorance of the generality of men, does not in the history of the creation make mention of any other works of God than those which meet our eye, yet, seeing he afterwards introduces angels as the ministers of God, we easily infer that he for whom they do service is their Creator. Hence, though Moses, speaking in popular language, did not at the very commencement enumerate the angels among the creatures of God, nothing prevents us from treating distinctly and explicitly of what is delivered by Scripture concerning them in other places. For if we desire to know God by his works, we surely cannot overlook this noble and illustrious specimen. We may add that this branch of doctrine is very necessary for the refutation of numerous errors. The minds of many are so struck with the excellence of angelic natures, that they would think them insulted in being subjected to the authority of God, and so made subordinate. Hence a fancied divinity has been assigned them. Manes, too, has arisen with his sect, fabricating to himself two principles—God and the devil, attributing the origin of good things to God, but assigning all bad natures to the devil as their author. Were this delirium to take possession of our minds, God would be denied his glory in the creation of the world. For, seeing there is nothing more peculiar to God than eternity and αυ τουσι α, i.e. self-existence, or existence of himself, if I may so speak, do not those who attribute it to the devil in some degree invest him with the honour of divinity? And where is the omnipotence of God, if the devil has the power of executing whatever he pleases against the will, and notwithstanding of the opposition of God? But the only good ground which the Manichees have—viz. that it were impious to ascribe the creation of any thing bad to a good God, militates in no degree against the orthodox faith, since it is not admitted that there is any thing naturally bad throughout the universe; the depravity and wickedness whether of man or of the devil, and the sins thence resulting, being not from nature, but from the corruption of nature; nor, at first, did anything whatever exist that did not exhibit some manifestation of the divine wisdom and justice. To obviate such perverse imaginations, we must raise our minds higher than our eyes can penetrate. It was probably with this view that the Nicene Creed, in calling God the creator of all things, makes express mention of things invisible. My care, however, must be to keep within the bounds which piety prescribes, lest by indulging in speculations beyond my reach, I bewilder the reader, and lead him away from the simplicity of the faith. And since the Holy Spirit always instructs us in what is useful, but altogether omits, or only touches cursorily on matters which tend little to edification, of all such matters, it certainly is our duty to remain in willing ignorance.

2 — Structure at a Glance – 

          ─── I. WHY TALK ABOUT ANGELS HERE? (S1–S5) ───
[TRANSITION] S1      before the nature of man (Ch.15): a word on angels
[ARGUMENT]   S2–S3   Moses omits them in the creation story (accommodation), but later shows them serving God
                     → their Creator is the God they serve → we may treat them separately
[REASON 1]   S4–S5   a creature worth knowing: a "noble specimen" of God's works; and the doctrine refutes errors
          ─── II. THE TWO ERRORS (S6–S12) ───
[ERROR 1]    S6–S7   angel-worship: awe at their excellence → "fancied divinity"
[ERROR 2]    S8–S9   Manes: two principles (God/devil) → God robbed of glory
[REFUTE]     S10–S11 aseity belongs to God alone; a self-sufficient devil = a rival god, and omnipotence dies
[CONCEDE+    S12     Manichees' one good point (God cannot create evil) is granted — but nothing is
 DISARM]             "naturally bad": evil = corruption of nature, not nature
          ─── III. THE RULE OF PIETY (S13–S16) ───
[ASCEND]     S13–S14 raise minds above the visible (Nicene Creed: "things invisible")
[LIMIT]      S15–S16 bounds of piety; the Spirit teaches what edifies → "willing ignorance" of the rest

Examiner's Eye.

① ⚠️ Whose claim? "Calvin says angels are insulted by being subject to God." ✗ S6: that is what the minds of many think. would think = their imagined reaction.

② ⚠️ Concession ≠ agreement. "Calvin agrees with the Manichees that God cannot create bad things." ✗/✓ trap: S12 grants only the premise (it were impious to ascribe the creation of any thing bad to a good God) and denies the conclusion (that therefore there must be a second principle): nothing is naturally bad.

③ ⚠️ What willing ignorance means. "Calvin forbids all study of angels." ✗ S3 says the opposite: nothing prevents us from treating … what is delivered by Scripture. What he refuses is speculation beyond Scripture (S15–S16).

④ ⚠️ Scope. "Angels are mentioned in Genesis 1." ✗ S2–S3: Moses does not name them at the outset (did not at the very commencement enumerate); they appear afterwards.

S4★For if we desire to know God by his works, we surely cannot overlook this noble and illustrious specimen — first reason›

For if we desire to know God by his works, we surely cannot overlook this noble and illustrious specimen.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • specimen — specere (look at) → spectacle, inspect
전체 해설 더보기

One point. A plain open conditional (if we desire … we cannot overlook). specimen (L. specere, to look at) = a sample — angels are a sample of God's works of the finest kind. This links back to Ch. 5: God is known from his works. Reason #1: angels, too, show God. Literal rendering: For if we want to know God from his works, we certainly cannot pass over this noble and splendid example of them.

S5★We may add that … — second reason: a doctrine that corrects errors›

We may add that this branch of doctrine is very necessary for the refutation of numerous errors.

전체 해설 더보기

One point. We may add that … introduces reason #2: the doctrine of angels is polemically necessary. refutation of numerous errors — the errors come in the next sentences (angel-worship, Manicheeism). branch of doctrine = topic within theology. Literal rendering: We may add that this part of doctrine is greatly needed to refute many errors.

S11★And where is the omnipotence of God, if …? — second rhetorical question›

전체 해설 더보기

One point. 전체 해설 더보기

One point. To obviate (L. obviare, to meet in the way) = to head off, to prevent — the infinitive of purpose at the front. higher than our eyes can penetrate — comparative + than + a clause: reach above the visible. This is the epistemic move: to answer these errors, theology must go beyond what we can see — which is why Scripture must teach us about invisible things. Literal rendering: To forestall such perverse notions, we must lift our minds higher than our eyes can reach.

S14★It was probably with this view that the Nicene Creed … makes express mention of things invisible — it-cleft›

It was probably with this view that the Nicene Creed, in calling God the creator of all things, makes express mention of things invisible.

전체 해설 더보기

One point. It-cleft: It was … with this view that … = the Nicene Creed does this with this aim (emphasis on with this view). probably = Calvin's caution (a historical inference, not a documented intention). The Creed reads: "maker of heaven and earth, and of all things visible and invisible" (Nicene-Constantinopolitan). ⚠️ Present tense makes = the Creed as a standing text. express mention = explicit mention. Literal rendering: It was probably with this aim that the Nicene Creed, in calling God the maker of all things, expressly mentions things invisible.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — The inverted conditional (Were / Had / Should + S)

Were  + S + to V,   S would V        =  If S were to V, S would V        (S9)
Had   + S + p.p.,   S would have p.p. =  If S had p.p., ...               (Day 105 S6)
Should+ S + V,      S would V        =  If S should V, ...                (Day 12 S4)

⚠️ Trap: the if is gone — never write If were this delirium … or Were this delirium *to took* …. After were to, the verb is the bare infinitive (to take). Drill. Rewrite with inversion: If the devil were to act independently of God, divine omnipotence would collapse. → Were the devil to act independently of God, divine omnipotence would collapse.

Formula 2 — seeing (that) = since, and the rhetorical negative question

Seeing (that) S V, [main clause]      =  Since S V, ...            (S2, S10)
Do not those who V ...?               =  Those who V ... certainly do.   (S10)

⚠️ Trap: seeing + clause is a conjunction (cause), not "seeing something". ⚠️ A negative question in this style is an assertion, not a real question. Drill. Since nothing is more proper to God than aseity, those who give it to creatures rob him. → Seeing nothing is more proper to God than aseity, do not those who give it to creatures rob him of his honour?

Formula 3 — Negative inversion after nor, and lest

..., nor + AUX + S + V           =  and S did not V either          (S12: nor ... did anything exist)
lest + S + (should) V            =  for fear that S might V         (S15: lest ... I bewilder)

⚠️ Trap: after nor, the auxiliary precedes the subject (nor did anything exist, not nor anything did exist). ⚠️ lest already carries negation: lest I confuse, not lest I do not confuse. Drill. Combine with nor: Evil is not from nature. Evil did not exist at first. → Evil is not from nature; nor did it exist at first. Combine with lest: I keep to the Word. I do not want to lead you astray. → I keep to the Word lest I lead you astray.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

Why angels here? After the creation of the world (§§1–2), Calvin treats angels (§§3–12) and devils (§§13–19) before man (Ch. 15). Reason: Moses' account is in "popular language" (accommodation), so he omits the invisible creatures; but Scripture elsewhere teaches about them, and they are part of what God made (Col 1:16 visible and invisible). So the Institutes inserts the doctrine between creation-of-the-world and creation-of-man.

The two errors Calvin has in view. ① Angel-worship / angel-divinization — rooted in Neoplatonic hierarchies and late-antique cults (cf. Col 2:18; Rev 19:10), alive in the medieval cult of saints and angels; Calvin will answer in §§10–12. ② Manichean dualism — the 3rd-century religion of Mani: two eternal principles (light/good vs darkness/evil). Augustine, a former Manichee, wrote against it; Calvin follows Augustine's metaphysics: evil is a privation/corruption of a good nature, not a substance (cf. Confessions VII, City of God XI–XII).

Key doctrinal point: aseity. αὐτουσία (autousia, here OCR-mangled) is the Greek counterpart of Latin aseitas. For Calvin, only God exists from himself; everything else is dependent. If the devil had independent being, he would share the one attribute that marks God as God (S10).

The method rule (S15–S16). Willing ignorance (S16) is Calvin's epistemological discipline: where Scripture is silent or only touches cursorily, we are silent. It anticipates §4 (the "garrulity" of Pseudo-Dionysius' Celestial Hierarchy) and echoes his stance on predestination (III.21.1–3). Cf. Deut 29:29.

Reception. Barth (CD III/3 §51) treats angels at length but warns against both "angelology as speculation" and "demythologizing"; he shares Calvin's concern for Scripture-bounded limits. Plantinga and the free-will defense debate echo Calvin's evil as corruption in a modern key.

Guardrails (over-reading). ① Calvin does not say Moses is wrong to omit angels — he says Moses accommodated. ② Willing ignorance ≠ ban on study: S3 permits study of what Scripture delivers. ③ Calvin's argument that angels are created (S2) is an inference (we easily infer), not a stated proof-text in this sentence.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. S2–S3 — Gen 1 (creation "in popular language"; "the heavens and the earth, and all the host of them" Gen 2:1). Calvin uses the silence of Genesis 1 about angels as a case of accommodation; Gen 2:1's "host" is where he will park angels (§4).
  2. S2 — Gen 16:7; 19:1; 22:11; Ps 103:20–21; Heb 1:14. Angels introduced as ministers: λειτουργικὰ πνεύματα (Heb 1:14). Calvin reasons: servants of the Creator belong to his creation.
  3. S4 — Rom 1:20. Know God by his works — echo of the ex creatura argument (I.5).
  4. S6–S7 — Col 2:18 (θρησκείᾳ τῶν ἀγγέλων, "worship of angels"); Rev 19:10; 22:8–9. The angel-worship error that §10–11 will take up.
  5. S8–S12 — Gen 1:31 ("very good"). Calvin's ground for no nature is bad; ⚠️ NOT a "theodicy" proof-text but the base of the privation view.
  6. S10 — Isa 42:8; 44:6; Ex 3:14 (ehyeh asher ehyeh). The Scripture undergirding aseity: God's name is "I AM"; glory not given to another.
  7. S11 — Job 1:12; 2:6; 1 Kgs 22:19–23. Satan acts only within God's permission — Calvin's I.14.17.
  8. S13–S14 — Col 1:16 (τὰ ὁρατὰ καὶ τὰ ἀόρατα); Nicene Creed. Things invisible.
  9. S15 — 2 Cor 11:3 (ἁπλότης). The simplicity of the faith.
  10. S16 — Deut 29:29; 1 Cor 14:26; Rom 12:3. Willing ignorance ↔ the secret things belong to the LORD; edification as the norm; sober thinking.

How Calvin uses Scripture here: by inference from role (S2), by silence (S3, S16) and by limit (S15–S16) — never by speculation.
