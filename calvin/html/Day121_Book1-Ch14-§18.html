Day 121 — Book 1, Chapter 14, §18

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday: Satan on a leash — he does only what God permits. Today: what is that leash for? Calvin answers with the most pastoral paragraph in the chapter: God uses the devil to train believers — and the devil can wound but never conquer them. This is the longest section of Ch. 14 so far: 23 sentences, one paragraph, and a clean three-movement shape (the contest S1–S9 → the victory, partial and perfect S10–S17 → whose side owns whom S18–S23).

Why examiners love it. The first sentence is a parade of participles — warring… assailing… urging… pressing… disturbing, alarming, and occasionally wounding, but never conquering or oppressing — and the whole section lives on scope and polarity: often / never, fall / get up, wounded / not mortally, in our Head / in us, believers / the wicked. Constructions: a participial series after by (S1), a whereas contrast (S1), a causal as + I deny that (S5), a never so… as not to negative degree (S6), an in fine summary (S8), a perfect-passive timeline when we shall have put off… and shall have been filled (S12), an absolute clause inside a parenthesis such being our weakness, and such his raging fury (S17), and a not… but only + deigns not to finish (S18). The traps: Calvin does not say believers never fall (S7), and he does not say the victory in us is already complete (S12).

Text traps. ⚠️ hither and thither (S1) = this way and that. ⚠️ thraldom (S1) = slavery. ⚠️ exempt (S3) = excused from. ⚠️ buffet (S3) = strike, beat (not "feast"). ⚠️ vanquished (S5) = defeated. ⚠️ in fine (S8, S22) = in short, finally. ⚠️ instigation (S9) = urging on. ⚠️ unintermitted (S17) = non-stop. ⚠️ manifold (S17) = many-sided. ⚠️ deigns not (S18) = does not condescend/think fit. ⚠️ sway (S18) = rule. ⚠️ impious (S18) = ungodly. ⚠️ recognised (S23) = acknowledged as.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. God thus turning the unclean spirits… employs them in exercising believers by warring against them, assailing them…, but never conquering or oppressing them; whereas they hold the wicked in thraldom… (S1) → participial phrase (same subject, not absolute) + in V-ing + a series of by-participles + but never + whereas contrast. Formula 1.
  2. But as the promise of bruising Satan's head applies alike to Christ and to all his members, I deny that believers can ever be oppressed or vanquished by him. (S5) → causal as-clause + apply alike to A and to B + deny that. Formula 2.
  3. For (such being our weakness, and such his raging fury), how could we withstand his… assaults… if we did not trust to the victory of our leader? (S17) → absolute construction in parentheses + rhetorical question + could… if… did not unreal condition. Formula 3.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 23 sentences –

God thus turning the unclean spirits hither and thither at his pleasure, employs them in exercising believers by warring against them, assailing them with wiles, urging them with solicitations, pressing close upon them, disturbing, alarming, and occasionally wounding, but never conquering or oppressing them; whereas they hold the wicked in thraldom, exercise dominion over their minds and bodies, and employ them as bond-slaves in all kinds of iniquity. Because believers are disturbed by such enemies, they are addressed in such exhortations as these: “Neither give place to the devil;” “Your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about seeking whom he may devour; whom resist steadfast in the faith,” (Eph. 4:27; 1 Pet. 5:8). Paul acknowledges that he was not exempt from this species of contest when he says, that for the purpose of subduing his pride, a messenger of Satan was sent to buffet him (2 Cor. 12:7). This trial, therefore, is common to all the children of God. But as the promise of bruising Satan’s head (Gen. 3:15) applies alike to Christ and to all his members, I deny that believers can ever be oppressed or vanquished by him. They are often, indeed, thrown into alarm, but never so thoroughly as not to recover themselves. They fall by the violence of the blows, but they get up again; they are wounded, but not mortally. In fine, they labour on through the whole course of their lives, so as ultimately to gain the victory, though they meet with occasional defeats. We know how David, through the just anger of God, was left for a time to Satan, and by his instigation numbered the people (2 Sam. 24:1); nor without cause does Paul hold out a hope of pardon in case any should have become ensnared by the wiles of the devil (2 Tim. 2:26). Accordingly, he elsewhere shows that the promise above quoted commences in this life where the struggle is carried on, and that it is completed after the struggle is ended. His words are, “The God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly,” (Rom. 16:20). In our Head, indeed, this victory was always perfect, because the prince of the world “had nothing” in him (John 14:30); but in us, who are his members, it is now partially obtained, and will be perfected when we shall have put off our mortal flesh, through which we are liable to infirmity, and shall have been filled with the energy of the Holy Spirit. In this way, when the kingdom of Christ is raised up and established, that of Satan falls, as our Lord himself expresses it, “I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven,” (Luke 10:18). By these words, he confirmed the report which the apostles gave of the efficacy of their preaching. In like manner he says, “When a strong man armed keepeth his palace, his goods are in peace. But when a stronger than he shall come upon him, and overcome him, he taketh from him all his armour wherein he trusted, and divideth his spoils,” (Luke 11:21, 22). And to this end, Christ, by dying, overcame Satan, who had the power of death (Heb. 2:14), and triumphed over all his hosts that they might not injure the Church, which otherwise would suffer from them every moment. For (such being our weakness, and such his raging fury), how could we withstand his manifold and unintermitted assaults for any period, however short, if we did not trust to the victory of our leader? God, therefore, does not allow Satan to have dominion over the souls of believers, but only gives over to his sway the impious and unbelieving, whom he deigns not to number among his flock. For the devil is said to have undisputed possession of this world until he is dispossessed by Christ. In like manner, he is said to blind all who do not believe the Gospel, and to do his own work in the children of disobedience. And justly; for all the wicked are vessels of wrath, and, accordingly, to whom should they be subjected but to the minister of the divine vengeance? In fine, they are said to be of their father the devil. For as believers are recognised to be the sons of God by bearing his image, so the wicked are properly regarded as the children of Satan, from having degenerated into his image.

2 — Structure at a Glance – 

[THESIS]      S1        God turns unclean spirits at his pleasure to exercise believers (harass, never conquer) — whereas the wicked they hold in thraldom
[WARNINGS]    S2–S4     Believers are told "give place not," "resist steadfast" (Eph 4:27; 1 Pet 5:8); Paul's buffeting messenger (2 Cor 12:7) → the trial is common to all God's children
[PROMISE]     S5        Gen 3:15 applies to Christ AND his members → believers can never be oppressed or vanquished
[CONTEST]     S6–S9     Alarmed, not utterly; fall, get up; wounded, not mortally; labour on to ultimate victory; David left for a time to Satan (2 Sam 24:1); pardon offered to the ensnared (2 Tim 2:26)
[TIMELINE]    S10–S12   Rom 16:20: the promise begins now, completes after the struggle; perfect in our Head (Jn 14:30), partial in us, perfected when mortal flesh is put off
[KINGDOM]     S13–S17   As Christ's kingdom rises Satan falls (Lk 10:18); strong man bound (Lk 11:21–22); Christ by dying overcame him (Heb 2:14); without the leader's victory we could not stand
[CONCLUSION]  S18–S23   God does not give believers over to Satan's dominion — only the impious; the world is his until Christ dispossesses him; the wicked = vessels of wrath, children of the devil by bearing his image

Examiner's Eye. - ⚠️ Scope trap (S5–S7): believers are often alarmed, fall, are wounded — but never oppressed, vanquished, or mortally wounded. An option saying believers are immune from Satan's attacks is wrong; an option saying they are sometimes conquered is wrong too. - ⚠️ Timeline trap (S12): the victory is always perfect in our Head, only partially obtained in us now, and will be perfected when we have put off mortal flesh. An option saying believers already have complete victory is a time-flip. - ⚠️ Target trap (S18): God gives only the impious and unbelieving to Satan's sway; he does not allow dominion over believers' souls. An option extending Satan's dominion to the elect is a direction-flip. - ⚠️ Agent trap (S1): God turns the spirits and employs them; the spirits are not free agents who happen to harass believers. - ⚠️ Source trap (S9): David's census (2 Sam 24:1) shows believers can be left for a time to Satan — it does not contradict S5, because David recovered and the issue is mortal oppression.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — S, (thus) V-ing N, employs them in V-ing X by V-ing A, V-ing B, …, but never V-ing Y; whereas they V … (S1)

S  ,  thus  V-ing  N  (at his pleasure) ,  employs  them  in  V-ing  X  by  V-ing A ,  V-ing B ,  ... ,  but  never  V-ing Y ;  whereas  they  V ...
  → same-subject participial phrase (NOT absolute) + `in`+gerund + `by`+gerund series + `but never` ceiling + `whereas` contrast

⚠️ Trap. (a) God thus turning… shares the main subject — it is not an absolute (compare Formula 3). (b) by governs the whole series, not only the first -ing. (c) but never marks the ceiling: wound — yes; conquer — no. (d) whereas = while on the other hand (contrast), not time. Drill. The Lord, turning trials to the good of his people, uses them to exercise their faith by testing them, humbling them and driving them to prayer, but never destroying them. → God thus turning the trials of the godly at his pleasure, employs them in exercising their faith by testing them, humbling them, and driving them to prayer, but never destroying them.

Formula 2 — But as N (…) applies alike to A and to B, I deny that S can ever be V-ed by X (S5); never so ADV as not to V (S6)

But  as  N (of V-ing ...)  applies  alike  to  A  and  to  B ,  I  deny  that  S  can  ever  be  V-ed  by  X .
They  are  often ... V-ed ,  but  never  so  ADV  as  not  to  V  themselves .
  → causal `as` + `apply alike to A and to B` + `deny that` + `can ever` / degree negative `never so… as not to`

⚠️ Trap. (a) as is causal (since). (b) all his members = Christ's body, not "all people." (c) can ever be in a denial = at no time. (d) never so thoroughly as not to recover = always recover — read the pair as a degree structure, not two separate negatives. Drill. Since the Spirit's promise holds equally for the head and for the members, I say that the Church can never be overcome; she is often shaken, but never so badly that she cannot stand again. → But as the promise of the Spirit applies alike to the Head and to all the members, I deny that the Church can ever be overcome. She is often, indeed, shaken, but never so severely as not to stand again.

Formula 3 — For (such being N1, and such N2), how could S V … if S did not V? (S17)

For  ( such  being  N1 ,  and  such  N2 ) ,  how  could  S  V  ... ,  however  ADJ ,  if  S  did not  V ... ?
  → absolute construction (own subject + `being`) in parentheses + rhetorical `how could` + unreal `if… did not` + `however ADJ` (verb omitted)

⚠️ Trap. (a) The absolute has its own subject (weakness/fury ≠ we). (b) how could…? is rhetorical — the answer is we could not. (c) could… if… did not = unreal condition; don't read did not as a past event. (d) however short = however short it may be (verb omitted). Drill. Since the Church is so frail and the world so hostile, how could she stand for even a day if she did not rely on her Lord's promise? → For (such being the frailty of the Church, and such the hostility of the world), how could she stand for any period, however short, if she did not trust to the promise of her Lord?

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The doctrine at stake. §§13–18 are Calvin's demonology, and §18 is its pastoral payoff: why does the Bible say so much about the devil? Answer (§13): so we will watch and resist. §18 adds the comfort: believers are harassed but never conquered, because the victory already won in Christ is applied to his members (S5, S12). The paragraph is a compact Christus-Victor argument: Gen 3:15 → Christ's dying (Heb 2:14) → the strong man bound (Luke 11) → Satan's fall (Luke 10).

The argument shape. Contest (S1–S9) → timeline (S10–S12: already / not yet) → kingdom reversal (S13–S17) → two destinies (S18–S23: believers = Christ's flock; wicked = Satan's children). Notice the sheep/image symmetry in the last sentence (S23): bearing his image vs degenerating into his image.

Historical lineage. The picture of Christ as victor over Satan runs through the Fathers (Irenaeus, Augustine's City of God) and the Reformers; Gustaf Aulén's 20th-century label Christus Victor names it. The Gen 3:15 reading — the "seed of the woman" crushing the serpent — is the traditional protevangelium reading. Calvin's distinctive move here is the head–members logic: the victory is perfect in the Head, partial in us (S12).

Later reception. Reformed theology keeps the two sides: real warfare (Eph 6, WCF 13's "continual and irreconcilable war") and sure outcome (perseverance of the saints). S9 (David) is regularly cited in discussions of the falls of the saints without loss of final perseverance.

Guardrails against over-reading. (1) §18 does not say believers cannot fall (S7: they fall by the violence of the blows); it denies being finally oppressed or vanquished. (2) S9 (David left to Satan) shows God hands over believers for a time; do not read S18 as saying God never lets believers be sorely tried. (3) S19–S20's undisputed possession of this world is the wicked's relation to Satan until Christ dispossesses him; it is not a claim that Satan owns creation. (4) S22–S23 describe the wicked's moral likeness (image), not a claim that humans are Satan's metaphysical offspring. (5) The Day 119–120 balance holds: Satan's malice is his own, his permission is God's.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. S2 — Eph 4:27; 1 Pet 5:8–9. "Neither give place to the devil" (μηδὲ δίδοτε τόπον τῷ διαβόλῳ); "your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about… whom resist stedfast in the faith." Calvin cites both in the text; the warnings presuppose the contest.
  2. S3 — 2 Cor 12:7. "A messenger of Satan to buffet me" (ἄγγελος σατανᾶ ἵνα με κολαφίζῃ — kolaphizē, "strike with the fist"), "lest I should be exalted above measure." Satan's blow serves God's end — humility.
  3. S5 — Gen 3:15. "It shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel." Calvin extends the promise from Christ to all his members (cf. Rom 16:20).
  4. S9 — 2 Sam 24:1; (cf. 1 Chr 21:1); 2 Tim 2:26. "The anger of the LORD was kindled… and he moved David" (Samuel); "Satan… provoked David" (Chronicles); "recover themselves out of the snare of the devil." Calvin joins the two accounts: God's anger, Satan's instigation.
  5. S10–S11 — Rom 16:20. "The God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly." The victory begins now, is completed later.
  6. S12 — John 14:30. "The prince of this world cometh, and hath nothing in me." Perfect in the Head. (Cf. 2 Cor 5:4; 1 John 3:2 for the "put off mortal flesh" horizon.)
  7. S13 — Luke 10:17–18. "I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven" — Christ's reply to the Seventy's report. (S14 the report which the apostles gave.)
  8. S15 — Luke 11:21–22. The strong man armed / the stronger who divides the spoils. Calvin's hinge from the promise to the work of Christ.
  9. S16 — Heb 2:14; (cf. Col 2:15). "Through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil." The how of Christ's victory.
  10. S19–S20 — John 12:31; 2 Cor 4:4; Eph 2:2. "Now shall the prince of this world be cast out"; "the god of this world hath blinded the minds of them which believe not"; "the spirit that now worketh in the children of disobedience."

How Calvin uses Scripture: he lines up warnings (Eph 4, 1 Pet 5), promises (Gen 3, Rom 16), Christ's words (John 14, Luke 10–11) and doctrinal summary (Heb 2, Rom 9) — a canonical chain from Genesis to Hebrews, with the head–members key unlocking all of them.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① Grammar — which statement about the grammar of §18 is wrong?

(A) In S1, God thus turning the unclean spirits hither and thither at his pleasure, employs them… contains an absolute construction with its own subject. (B) In S5, But as the promise of bruising Satan's head applies alike to Christ and to all his members — as introduces a causal clause meaning since. (C) In S6, never so thoroughly as not to recover themselves means that they never fall so completely that they fail to recover. (D) In S17, such being our weakness, and such his raging fury is an absolute construction with its own subjects.

Answer: (A). Rationale: God in S1 is also the subject of employs — the participial phrase shares the main subject, so it is not absolute (Formula 1). (B) Formula 2. (C) Formula 2 (S6). (D) Formula 3 — weakness/fury ≠ we.

② Comprehension — which statement is consistent with §18?

(A) Calvin says believers are immune from Satan's attacks, because Christ has already crushed his head and so the contest only touches the wicked. (B) Calvin says God employs the unclean spirits to exercise believers — harassing, alarming and even wounding them, but never conquering them — because the promise of Gen 3:15 covers Christ's members; the victory is perfect in the Head and only partial in us now, and God hands over to Satan's sway only the impious and unbelieving. (C) Calvin says the victory in believers is already complete, because the prince of the world had nothing in Christ, and so believers no longer fall or are wounded. (D) Calvin says that God gives Satan dominion over the souls of believers whenever they sin gravely, as with David's census.

Answer: (B). Rationale: (A) scope-flip — S1–S8: believers are attacked, alarmed and wounded. (C) time-flip — S12: in us it is now partially obtained, will be perfected; S7: they fall… are wounded. (D) scope-overreach/direction-flip — S18: God does not allow Satan to have dominion over the souls of believers; S9: David was left for a time, but recovered (S6).

③ Composition — use Formula 2 and Formula 3.

Compose one English sentence (≈40 words): Since the promise holds equally for Christ and for his people, I say the Church can never be overcome. She is often shaken, but never so badly that she cannot rise again. How could she stand if she did not trust her Lord's victory?

Model answer: But as the promise applies alike to Christ and to all his people, I deny that the Church can ever be overcome; she is often, indeed, shaken, but never so thoroughly as not to rise again — for (such being her weakness), how could she stand, if she did not trust to the victory of her Lord? Examiner's intent: causal as + apply alike to A and to B + I deny that… can ever (Formula 2) + never so thoroughly as not to V (Formula 2) + absolute such being + rhetorical how could… if… did not (Formula 3). Alternative accepted (Formula 1): God thus turning the trials of his people at his pleasure, employs them in exercising their faith by testing them, humbling them, and driving them to prayer, but never destroying them.
