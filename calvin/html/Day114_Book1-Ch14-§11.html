Day 114 — Book 1, Chapter 14, §11

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday: don't worship the messenger. Today: so why does God use messengers at all? §10 ended with John on his face and the angel saying worship God. A sharp student asks: "If angels add nothing to God's power, why are they there?" §11 answers in one phrase — a help to our weakness. Not God's need. Ours. Calvin then does something pastoral: he puts the whole doctrine on a trembling servant standing on a mountain surrounded by Assyrian chariots.

Why examiners love it. Twelve sentences, and every one is a grammar showcase: a cleft-like why it is that with a parenthetical instead of acting… (S1), a so far are they from V-ing inversion (S3), a such is our frailty… that inversion plus an inverted conditional did not the Lord proclaim (S6), an it would ill become us to V idiom (S9), and a participial chain in the Elisha story (S10–S12). The traps are direction-flips: God uses angels not from necessity; the angels do not relieve him of difficulty; and Calvin does not say we should disdain angels — he says it would ill become us to overlook the favour.

Text traps. ⚠️ wont (S1) = accustomed (not "won't"). ⚠️ agency (S1) = instrumentality (not "an office"). ⚠️ dispense with (S2) = do without (not "give out"). ⚠️ nod (S3) = a signal of will. ⚠️ effeminacy (S6) = softness, weakness of nerve (a historical sense; not a comment on women). ⚠️ impend (S7) = hang threateningly over. ⚠️ encircled (S7) = surrounded as protection. ⚠️ indulgence (S9) = kindly leniency (not "self-indulgence"). ⚠️ in charge (S11) = as a trust, to guard.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. so far are they from relieving him of any difficulty (S3) → so far (adj/adv) be S from V-ing with inversion after fronted so far. = they are so far from relieving… Formula 1.
  2. such is our frailty and effeminacy, that we might at times be filled with alarm… did not the Lord proclaim (S6) → such is N that… (inverted result) + inverted conditional did not S V = if S had not V-ed. Formula 2.
  3. it would ill become us to overlook the favour (S9) → it ill becomes S to V (impersonal it + become = suit). Formula 3.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 12 sentences –

This danger we will happily avoid, if we consider why it is that Gods instead of acting directly without their agency, is wont to employ it in manifesting his power, providing for the safety of his people, and imparting the gifts of his beneficence. This he certainly does not from necessity, as if he were unable to dispense with them. Whenever he pleases, he passes them by, and performs his own work by a single nod: so far are they from relieving him of any difficulty. Therefore, when he employs them it is as a help to our weakness, that nothing may be wanting to elevate our hopes or strengthen our confidence. It ought, indeed, to be sufficient for us that the Lord declares himself to be our protector. But when we see ourselves beset by so many perils, so many injuries, so many kinds of enemies, such is our frailty and effeminacy, that we might at times be filled with alarm, or driven to despair, did not the Lord proclaim his gracious presence by some means in accordance with our feeble capacities. For this reason, he not only promises to take care of us, but assures us that he has numberless attendants, to whom he has committed the charge of our safety, that whatever dangers may impend, so long as we are encircled by their protection and guardianship, we are placed beyond all hazard of evil. I admit that after we have a simple assurance of the divine protection, it is improper in us still to look round for help. But since for this our weakness the Lord is pleased, in his infinite goodness and indulgence, to provide, it would ill become us to overlook the favour. Of this we have an example in the servant of Elisha (2 Kings 6:17), who, seeing the mountain encompassed by the army of the Assyrians, and no means of escape, was completely overcome with terror, and thought it all over with himself and his master. Then Elisha prayed to God to open the eyes of the servant, who forthwith beheld the mountain filled with horses and chariots of fire; in other words, with a multitude of angels, to whom he and the prophet had been given in charge. Confirmed by the vision he received courage, and could boldly defy the enemy, whose appearance previously filled him with dismay.

2 — Structure at a Glance – 

[ANTIDOTE]     S1       Avoid the danger by asking WHY God is wont to use angels' agency
[NOT NEED]     S2–S3    Not from necessity; he can pass them by with a nod — they relieve him of nothing
[FOR US]       S4–S6    He uses them as a help to our weakness; the word should suffice, but our frailty
                        would give way to alarm/despair unless he proclaimed his presence in a form fitted to us
[PROMISE]      S7       Hence: not only "I will care" but "I have numberless attendants" → beyond all hazard
[CONCESSION]   S8–S9    Strictly, bare assurance should suffice — yet since he graciously provides, it would ill become us to overlook it
[EXAMPLE]      S10–S12  Elisha's servant: terror → prayer → eyes opened → chariots of fire = angels → courage

Examiner's Eye. - ⚠️ Cause trap (S2–S4): God uses angels not from necessity but for our weakness. Any option saying God needs angels is wrong. - ⚠️ Direction trap (S3): so far are they from relieving him of any difficulty — angels do not lighten God's burden. - ⚠️ Concession trap (S5, S8): Calvin concedes that God's word should suffice (S5) and that after a simple assurance looking round is improper (S8) — then (S9) overrules it by grace. Don't read S8 alone as a ban on trusting angels. - ⚠️ Person trap (S10–S12): the one terrified is the servant, not Elisha; Elisha prays, the servant sees. - ⚠️ Wording trap (S11): the chariots of fire are in other words, a multitude of angels — Calvin's gloss, not a new claim about horses.

🔤 Morphology · 어형

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — so far are S from V-ing (S3)

(So far) are S from V-ing O          [inversion after fronted "so far"]
   =  S are so far from V-ing O   =  S are anything but V-ing / far from V-ing

⚠️ Trap. (a) Inversion (are they) is not a question. (b) from takes a gerund. (c) With any (any difficulty) the sense is not at all. Drill. Angels do not lighten God's burden at all. → So far are the angels from lightening God's burden that he passes them by with a nod.

Formula 2 — such is N, that… + inverted conditional did not S V (S6)

2a.  Such is N that S V        =  N is so great that S V
2b.  …S might V, did not S' V' =  …S might V, if S' did not V'  [unreal present]

⚠️ Trap. (a) such fronted → is before the subject. (b) Inverted did not the Lord… is a conditional, not a question; it needs a might/would main clause. (c) The conditional covers all of we might… despair. Drill. We are so weak that we would despair if God did not give us signs. → Such is our weakness that we would despair, did not God give us signs.

Formula 3 — it would ill become S to V (S9)

It (would) ill become(s) S to V   =  It (would) be unseemly for S to V  =  S should not V

⚠️ Trap. (a) become = befit (not "turn into"). (b) ill is an adverb of manner (cf. ill-suited). (c) The to V is the real subject (extraposed it). Drill. We should not neglect God's gift. → It would ill become us to neglect God's gift.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The doctrine at stake. §10 closed one door (angel-worship). §11 opens the other: angels are not decoration. The question is why an omnipotent God uses means. Calvin's answer is accommodation: God stoops to our capacities (in accordance with our feeble capacities). The same logic governs the sacraments (4.14) and the Word itself: means are given for our sake, never for God's need.

The argument shape. (1) Test: why does God use them? (S1). (2) Not need (S2–S3). (3) For our weakness (S4–S6). (4) Hence the promise of "numberless attendants" (S7). (5) Concession and overruling (S8–S9). (6) Elisha's servant (S10–S12). §12 will draw the practical rule: angels must lead us to God, never away.

Historical lineage. Calvin's "means, not need" has Augustine behind it (God works through secondary causes, De Genesi ad litteram) and Aquinas (ST I q.110: angels govern corporeal things by God's disposition). The Reformed tradition keeps the line: angels as ministering spirits (Heb 1:14) who do not mediate or receive prayer. Calvin's pastoral turn — the fearful servant — is characteristic: dogmatics serves consolation.

Later reception. The Heidelberg Catechism (Q&A 27 on providence) similarly roots comfort in God's fatherly hand rather than in intermediaries; the Westminster Confession (5.3) says God uses means "yet is free to work without, above, and against them" — the very point of S3's single nod. Barth (CD III/3) calls angels "the servants of the Word", present for our sake.

Guardrails against over-reading. (1) S8 is a concession (what would be sufficient), not Calvin's rule against trusting in angelic help. (2) Calvin does not say each of us has an individual guardian angel here (that was left open in §7). (3) Chariots of fire is glossed as angels by Calvin; the text itself says "horses and chariots of fire". (4) The passage describes consolation, not a technique for seeing angels.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. S1/S3 — Ps 33:9; Gen 1. "He spake, and it was done" — the single nod; God's power does not depend on means.
  2. S2/S4 — Ps 103:14. "He knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust." The ground of a help to our weakness.
  3. S5 — Ps 121:1–2; Isa 41:10. The Lord as protector ("I am with thee… I will help thee") — what should be sufficient.
  4. S7 — Ps 91:11; Matt 18:10; Heb 1:14. Angels charged to keep us; numberless attendants ↔ "ministering spirits sent forth to minister for them who shall be heirs of salvation".
  5. S7 — Ps 34:7. "The angel of the LORD encampeth round about them that fear him" ↔ encircled by their protection.
  6. S10–S12 — 2 Kgs 6:15–17. "Fear not: for they that be with us are more than they that be with them… LORD, open his eyes… the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire round about Elisha." The core narrative; the Heb. verb wayyiphqach (open) governs the seeing.
  7. S11 — Ps 68:17. "The chariots of God are twenty thousand, even thousands of angels" — Calvin's gloss a multitude of angels.

How Calvin uses Scripture: by narrative proof — he lets the story do the argument, then pins it in one line of gloss (in other words, with a multitude of angels).

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① Grammar — one underlined part is wrong. Which?

Calvin writes: (A) so far are they from relieving him of any difficulty; (B) such our frailty and effeminacy is, that we might at times be filled with alarm; (C) did not the Lord proclaim his gracious presence; and (D) it would ill become us to overlook the favour.

Answer: (B). Rationale: the correct inversion is such is our frailty and effeminacy, that… (Formula 2a). (A) is correct (Formula 1). (C) is correct: inverted conditional = if the Lord did not proclaim. (D) is correct (Formula 3).

② Comprehension — which statement is consistent with §11?

(A) God employs angels because he cannot protect his people without their help. (B) Angels relieve God of difficulties that would otherwise burden him, so believers should honour them. (C) God uses angels as a help to our weakness, so that nothing is lacking to raise our hopes or confidence; he could pass them by with a single nod, and Elisha's servant, terrified by the Assyrian army, was strengthened when his eyes were opened to see the host of angels. (D) Since a simple assurance of divine protection should suffice, Calvin concludes that believers should disregard the ministry of angels.

Answer: (C). Rationale: (A) direction-flip — S2 not from necessity. (B) direction-flip — S3 so far are they from relieving him. (D) scope overreach — S8 is a concession; S9 says it would ill become us to overlook the favour. (C) combines S2–S4, S10–S12.

③ Composition — use Formula 2 and Formula 3.

Compose one English sentence (≈25 words): We are so weak that we would sink into despair if God did not give us visible signs of his care. We should not ignore this kindness.

Model answer: Such is our weakness that we might sink into despair, did not God give us visible signs of his care; it would ill become us to ignore such kindness. Examiner's intent: such is N that + inverted conditional + it would ill become us to V. Alternative accepted (Formula 1): So far are the angels from helping God that he passes them by with a single nod.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary: Calvin disarms angel-worship by asking why God uses angels at all: not from need (he can pass them by with a nod) but as a help to our weakness — a concession to our frailty that it would ill become us to overlook, illustrated by Elisha's terrified servant; the grammar keys are so far are they from V-ing (S3), such is N, that… did not S V (S6), and it would ill become us to V (S9).

Homework (10 min).

  1. Structure restoration. Restore the base pattern of S6's inverted conditional: we might at times be filled with alarm… did not the Lord proclaim his gracious presence. (Answer shape: …if the Lord did not proclaim his gracious presence.)
  2. Composition. Write one sentence using so far are S from V-ing about a means of grace that adds nothing to God's power.
  3. Preview (tomorrow). §12 opens Whatever, therefore, is said as to the ministry of angels, let us employ for the purpose of removing all distrust… — watch the imperative let us employ, the rhetorical How preposterous… is it to…, and Jacob's ladder (Gen 28:12; John 1:51).
