Day 63 — Book 1, Chapter 11, §8

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

New division, new question. Sections 1–7 asked may we image God — answer: no. Today the chapter turns detective: where do idols come from? The received answer of Calvin's day came from the Book of Wisdom — idols began when grieving people honored their dead with portraits. Watch what Calvin does with it, because this is masterclass argumentation: he concedes the theory almost everything — it's ancient, yes; it poured fuel on the fire, yes — and then takes away the one thing it claimed: firstness. Rachel was hiding household gods in a camel-saddle (Gen. 31:19) before any ancestor cult became fashionable. So the origin cannot be a custom. It must be something older than every custom.

And then he names it — the single most quoted sentence Calvin ever wrote about idolatry, S6: "the human mind is, so to speak, a perpetual forge of idols." Not a museum of idols. A forge — a workshop with the fire always lit. Flood wipes the earth clean; a few years pass; "men are forging gods at will" (S7 — present tense, look at it!). Then S12–S15 give you the anatomy of the machine in two strokes: the mind conceives the idol, and the hand gives it birth. Conception, then delivery. Idolatry is not an accident that happened to humanity; it is something humanity is always pregnant with. The golden calf (S16) is just the maternity ward.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. The concession machine — litotes edition (S2, S8). I admit that… and I deny not that… / not without the deepest grief. Double negative = emphatic positive. Calvin concedes with one hand (antiquity? granted; accelerant? granted) so his denial (S3: not the first origin) lands with full weight. Exam setters love to flip this: "Calvin rejects Wisdom's account" — wrong. He rejects only its claim to be first.
  2. The That-clause SUBJECT — twice (S4, S16). That idols were in use before… **is evident** from the words of Moses / That idolatry has its origin in… **appears** from the example of the Israelites. A whole clause sits in the subject seat and the real verb hides twenty-plus words downstream. The drill is always the same: find the verb, then re-read.
  3. OSV object fronting (S14). The god whom man has thus conceived inwardly **he attempts to embody** outwardly. Object first, subject second — plus the adverb pair inwardly/outwardly carrying the whole doctrine. Cousin construction in S12: stuffed **as it is** with presumptuous rashness — the concessive as-wedge.

Bonus traps I will test: Torah (S9) — the source's own misprint for Terah, Abraham's father (Josh. 24:2); we quote texts as they stand, and no, Calvin is not talking about the Pentateuch. we wet not (S16) — the source's rendering of the archaic wot not = "we know not"; read wet literally and the sentence drowns. (Exod. 22:1) (S16) — the golden calf is Exod. 32:1; the citation as printed is a source error — preserve, flag, don't silently fix. profane writers (S4) = secular/pagan authors, not blasphemers. ambitious (S4) = ostentatious, show-off. adverted to (S4) = referred to. given to (S8) = addicted to. figment (S19) < fingere, a thing molded.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 20 sentences –

8. In regard to the origin of idols, the statement contained in the Book of Wisdom has been received with almost universal consent—viz. that they originated with those who bestowed this honour on the dead, from a superstitious regard to their memory. I admit that this perverse practice is of very high antiquity, and I deny not that it was a kind of torch by which the infatuated proneness of mankind to idolatry was kindled into a greater blaze. I do not, however, admit that it was the first origin of the practice. That idols were in use before the prevalence of that ambitious consecration of the images of the dead, frequently adverted to by profane writers, is evident from the words of Moses (Gen. 31:19). When he relates that Rachel stole her father’s images, he speaks of the use of idols as a common vice. Hence we may infer, that the human mind is, so to speak, a perpetual forge of idols. There was a kind of renewal of the world at the deluge, but before many years elapse, men are forging gods at will. There is reason to believe, that in the holy Patriarch’s lifetime his grandchildren were given to idolatry: so that he must with his own eyes, not without the deepest grief, have seen the earth polluted with idols—that earth whose iniquities God had lately purged with so fearful a Judgment. For Joshua testifies (Josh. 24:2), that Torah and Nachor, even before the birth of Abraham, were the worshipers of false gods. The progeny of Shem having so speedily revolted, what are we to think of the posterity of Ham, who had been cursed long before in their father? Thus, indeed, it is. The human mind, stuffed as it is with presumptuous rashness, dares to imagine a god suited to its own capacity; as it labours under dullness, nay, is sunk in the grossest ignorance, it substitutes vanity and an empty phantom in the place of God. To these evils another is added. The god whom man has thus conceived inwardly he attempts to embody outwardly. The mind, in this way, conceives the idol, and the hand gives it birth. That idolatry has its origin in the idea which men have, that God is not present with them unless his presence is carnally exhibited, appears from the example of the Israelites: “Up,” said they, “make us gods, which shall go before us; for as for this Moses, the man that brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we wet not what is become of him,” (Exod. 22:1). They knew, indeed, that there was a God whose mighty power they had experienced in so many miracles, but they had no confidence of his being near to them, if they did not with their eyes behold a corporeal symbol of his presence, as an attestation to his actual government. They desired, therefore, to be assured by the image which went before them, that they were journeying under Divine guidance. And daily experience shows, that the flesh is always restless until it has obtained some figment like itself, with which it may vainly solace itself as a representation of God. In consequence of this blind passion men have, almost in all ages since the world began, set up signs on which they imagined that God was visibly depicted to their eyes.

Sentence count: 20 (S1–S20). One paragraph.

2 — Structure at a Glance – 

[RECEIVED THEORY] S1        Book of Wisdom: idols began as portraits of the dead
[CONCEDE]         S2        ancient? yes — an accelerant torch? yes
[DENY]            S3–S5     …but NOT the first origin: Rachel's teraphim predate the cult (Gen 31:19)
[AXIOM]           S6–S11    the human mind = a PERPETUAL FORGE of idols
                            (flood-cleansed world re-forges gods: Noah's own grandchildren,
                             Terah & Nachor before Abraham; Shem's line → a fortiori Ham's)
[ANATOMY]         S12–S15   two-stage disease: presumption imagines / dullness substitutes;
                            mind CONCEIVES the idol → hand GIVES IT BIRTH
[PROOF-CASE]      S16–S20   golden calf (Exod. 32): "God must be carnally present" —
                            restless flesh + blind passion = signs in almost all ages

Examiner's Eye. The guaranteed comprehension trap: "Calvin rejects the Book of Wisdom's account of the origin of idols." Half true, therefore wrong. He admits the practice's high antiquity and its torch-function (S2); he denies only that it was the first origin (S3). Second trap, direction-flip on S17: "The Israelites no longer believed there was a God." The text says the opposite — They knew, indeed, that there was a God — their sin was demanding his presence be carnally exhibited. Third trap, scope: "idolatry began shortly after the flood" — no; it resumed shortly after the flood (S7 renewal), which is precisely why the forge, not the flood-era, is the origin.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — the That-clause subject (find the verb!)

[That + S + V …(20+ words)…] + is evident / appears + from N
                └── whole clause = SUBJECT; main verb hides downstream
⚠️ Trap 1: picking a noun inside the clause ("idols", "idolatry") as subject.
⚠️ Trap 2: S16 stacks a second that INSIDE (the idea … , that God is not present) — apposition, not repetition.
Verb agreement: the clause counts as SINGULAR → is / appears.

Drill: That the church grew fastest under persecution appears from the second-century record.

Formula 2 — litotes: the double negative that shouts

I deny not that …          = I fully grant that …
not without the deepest grief = with the very deepest grief
⚠️ Not hesitation — EMPHASIS in a lawyer's gown. Paraphrasing "I'm not sure" flips the argument.
Pattern family: deny not / doubt not / not without N / not ignorant (Day 60 S1).

Drill: I deny not that the fathers erred at times; I deny that their errors license ours.

Formula 3 — OSV fronting + the as-wedge

[The N (whom S has V-ed)] + S + V …    → restore: S + V + the N whom …
   e.g. The god whom man has conceived inwardly he attempts to embody outwardly.
⚠️ Fronted object ≠ subject. Test: who does the attempting? "he" (man), not "the god".
Cousin: p.p. + as + S + be = concessive/causal wedge
   stuffed as it is with rashness = being, as it is, stuffed with rashness (NOT a comparison)

Drill: The doctrine which the heart has quietly denied the life will soon openly abandon.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The Book of Wisdom and how Calvin handles it. Wisdom 14:15–16 tells the story Calvin summarizes in S1: a father, crushed by grief for a dead child, makes an image; honor hardens into rite; rite into law. Wisdom of Solomon is deuterocanonical — canonical at Trent (1546), apocryphal for Calvin. Watch his precision: he neither leans on it as Scripture nor wastes breath refuting its canonicity here. He treats it as what it is for his argument — a historical opinion enjoying almost universal consent — and then corrects it FROM canonical Moses (Gen. 31:19). That is the same source-discipline he showed with pseudo-Justin in I.10.3 and Elvira in §6: use the document for exactly what it can bear, nothing more.

From history to anthropology — the strategic move of the section. The ancestor-cult theory (and §6's euhemerism, its pagan twin) explains idols as a custom with a birthday. Calvin's whole point: customs have birthdays; the forge does not. By pushing the evidence behind the ancestor cult (Rachel), behind the flood's fresh start (Noah's grandchildren), behind the covenant line's founding (Terah), he relocates the origin from the calendar into the constitution of fallen man. This is why S6 — perpetua idolorum fabrica — became the section's famous export: it converts iconoclasm from archaeology into anthropology. The line's afterlife is enormous: it stands behind the Reformed insistence that worship be regulated by the Word (the forge never retires, so vigilance never does), and in modern dress behind every "idols of the heart" analysis — money, power, approval as functional gods. That extended application is legitimate offspring, but note the guardrail below.

The two-stage machine and where the chapter goes. S12–S15 give Calvin's psychology of religion in miniature: presumption sizes a god to fit; dullness swaps him for a phantom; the hand prints the file. Tomorrow (§9) adds the fatal step: once the sign stands, adoration forthwith ensues — you cannot erect the image and withhold the knee. That is the hinge on which §§10–16 (Papal evasions, dulia/latria, Nicaea II) will turn.

Over-reading guardrails. (1) Calvin does NOT deny the antiquity or historical reality of ancestor-image cults — S2 concedes both; he denies priority. Reporting "Calvin refutes Wisdom" flattens his surgical concession. (2) The forge axiom is not a license to skip history: Calvin argues from texts (Gen. 31, Josh. 24), not from an armchair. (3) "Idols of the heart" extensions must keep S14–S15's order: this section is about mental conception issuing in physical cult; Calvin has not yet said "your career is an idol" — that broadening has roots here but happens elsewhere (and in later Reformed usage). (4) None of this condemns visual art as such — §12 will say plainly that sculpture and painting are gifts of God; the target is representing and worshipping God.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. Wisdom 14:15–16 — S1 (named source). The grieving-father etiology: "a father afflicted with untimely mourning… made an image of his child soon taken away." Calvin's use: cite the consensus, concede its data, deny its "firstness" — the rare case where he corrects an apocryphal book with the Torah rather than dismissing it outright.
  2. Gen. 31:19 — S4–S5 (explicit). "Rachel had stolen the images that were her father's" — Heb. תְּרָפִים (teraphim), household gods (cf. the camel-saddle scene, 31:34). Calvin's use: a narrative aside as chronological proof — Moses' casualness ("a common vice") is the evidence.
  3. Josh. 24:2 — S9 (explicit). "Your fathers dwelt on the other side of the flood in old time, even Terah… and Nachor: and they served other gods." Calvin's use: idolatry upstream of Abraham — the covenant line itself born in an idolatrous house; grace, not pedigree, explains Abraham.
  4. Gen. 9:25 — S10 (echo). "Cursed be Canaan" — the curse in their father grounding the a fortiori from Shem's line to Ham's.
  5. Gen. 8:21 — S7 (echo). "The imagination of man's heart is evil from his youth" — spoken by God after the flood: the divine post-mortem that makes S7's relapse unsurprising and S6's forge canonical.
  6. Exod. 32:1 — S16 (quoted; source prints "Exod. 22:1"). Heb. קוּם עֲשֵׂה־לָנוּ אֱלֹהִים — "Up, make us gods, which shall go before us" (KJV wording, with "we wot not" behind the source's wet). Calvin's use: the calf as the psychology of presence — a visible guarantee demanded when the mediator vanishes up the mountain.
  7. Rom. 1:21–23 — S12 (unmarked echo). ἐματαιώθησαν ("became vain in their imaginations") behind substitutes vanity; the ἀλλάσσω-exchange of glory for images behind the whole two-stage machine. Paul's sequence (darkened mind → manufactured likeness) IS S12→S14.
  8. Ps. 115:4–8 — S19–S20 (echo). The idols shaped by hands, and the makers growing like them — behind some figment like itself: the god resembles the worshipper because the worshipper is the mold.

8 — Exam Problems –

① Grammar. Choose the INCORRECT underlined portion.

(a) That idols were in use before that consecration is evident from Moses. (b) I deny not that the practice is ancient. (c) The god whom man has conceived inwardly it attempts to embody outwardly. (d) Noah must, not without grief, have seen the earth polluted.

Answer: (c). The fronted object is The god whom man has conceived inwardly; the subject must be personal — he (man) attempts. Writing it mistakes the fronted object for the subject — exactly the OSV trap (Formula 3). (a) correctly treats the that-clause as singular subject (Formula 1); (b) is the emphatic litotes (Formula 2); (d) is epistemic must + perfect infinitive with the litotes wedge (S8).

② Comprehension. Which statement matches the passage?

(a) Calvin rejects the Book of Wisdom's account as historically worthless. (b) Calvin grants that honoring the dead was an ancient accelerant of idolatry, but denies it was idolatry's first origin. (c) The Israelites demanded the calf because they no longer believed a God existed. (d) The flood permanently cleansed mankind of the tendency to make idols.

Answer: (b). Direct restatement of S2–S3: concession (I admit… I deny not) plus the single denial (not… the first origin). (a) is the direction-flip — he concedes antiquity and torch-function; (c) contradicts S17 (They knew, indeed, that there was a God); (d) contradicts S7 (a kind of renewal… but… men are forging gods at will) — scope overreach.

③ Composition. Using Formula 1 (that-clause subject) AND Formula 2 (litotes), render into Calvin-style English: "우상숭배가 관습보다 오래되었다는 것은 라헬의 예에서 분명하며, 나는 그 관습이 불길을 키웠음을 부인하지 않는다."

Model answer: That idolatry is older than any custom is evident from the example of Rachel; and I deny not that the custom fanned the blaze.

Exam-setter rationale: the that-clause must occupy the subject seat with singular is; the litotes I deny not must concede without surrendering — students who write "I do not think" have lost both the register and the logic.
