Day 127 — Book 1, Chapter 15, §2

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday: why we must start with man's glory. Today: the first thing Calvin says that glory consists of — a soul that outlasts the body. And notice the method. This is not a sermon; it is a five-count indictment in which the defendant (the soul-is-just-breath theory) gets cross-examined by conscience, by the knowledge of God, by the mind's powers, by dreams, and finally by Scripture. Calvin even tells you he is going fast (S19–S20): the philosophers did it better; the pious need only a pointer.

Why examiners love it. Inverted conditionals with no if (S21 Were not the soul…, S29 did not the soul… continue…) — and the second one looks like a question. Absolute/participle chains that bury the subject (it being impossible that…, S11; which stupefying the man seems…, S18). Result constructions with a negated so… that (S6) whose scope is a famous trap. And a shelf of archaic words that flip meaning: competent, intimate, endued, honesty, In fine.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. Inverted conditionals (no if) — S21 Were not the soul…, Scripture would not teach…; S29 did not the soul… continue to exist, our Saviour would not have represented…; plus negative-adverb inversion Nor would there be… / neither could… (S25). Formula 1.
  2. Absolute and participial chains that bury the subject — S11 it being impossible that…, S18 which stupefying the man seems…, S14's 45-word subject, S31 Not to dwell on…. Formula 2.
  3. so… as to / negated so… that / such… as — S6 so immersed in darkness as to imagine… and not so completely quenched… that all sense… is lost; S13 such sense as the lower animals possess; S12 so many evidences. Formula 3.

Text traps. ⚠️ competent (S10) = proper to, not "skilled". ⚠️ intimate (v., S22) = hint. ⚠️ endued (S10, S12) = endowed. ⚠️ honesty (S16) = uprightness. ⚠️ In fine (S12) = in short. ⚠️ profane (S19) = non-sacred. ⚠️ no obscure evidence (S18) = clear evidence. ⚠️ a something (S14) = a deliberately vague noun. ⚠️ Dives (S29) = Latin "rich man".

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 31 sentences –

Moreover, there can be no question that man consists of a body and a soul; meaning by soul, an immortal though created essence, which is his nobler part. Sometimes he is called a spirit. But though the two terms, while they are used together differ in their meaning, still, when spirit is used by itself it is equivalent to soul, as when Solomon speaking of death says, that the spirit returns to God who gave it (Eccles. 12:7). And Christ, in commending his spirit to the Father, and Stephen his to Christ, simply mean, that when the soul is freed from the prison-house of the body, God becomes its perpetual keeper. Those who imagine that the soul is called a spirit because it is a breath or energy divinely infused into bodies, but devoid of essence, err too grossly, as is shown both by the nature of the thing, and the whole tenor of Scripture. It is true, indeed, that men cleaving too much to the earth are dull of apprehension, nay, being alienated from the Father of Lights, are so immersed in darkness as to imagine that they will not survive the grave; still the light is not so completely quenched in darkness that all sense of immortality is lost. Conscience, which, distinguishing, between good and evil, responds to the Judgment of God, is an undoubted sign of an immortal spirit. How could motion devoid of essence penetrate to the Judgment-seat of God, and under a sense of guilt strike itself with terror? The body cannot be affected by any fear of spiritual punishment. This is competent only to the soul, which must therefore be endued with essence. Then the mere knowledge of a God sufficiently proves that souls which rise higher than the world must be immortal, it being impossible that any evanescent vigour could reach the very fountain of life. In fine, while the many noble faculties with which the human mind is endued proclaim that something divine is engraven on it, they are so many evidences of an immortal essence. For such sense as the lower animals possess goes not beyond the body, or at least not beyond the objects actually presented to it. But the swiftness with which the human mind glances from heaven to earth, scans the secrets of nature, and, after it has embraced all ages, with intellect and memory digests each in its proper order, and reads the future in the past, clearly demonstrates that there lurks in man a something separated from the body. We have intellect by which we are able to conceive of the invisible God and angels—a thing of which body is altogether incapable. We have ideas of rectitude, justice, and honesty—ideas which the bodily senses cannot reach. The seat of these ideas must therefore be a spirit. Nay, sleep itself, which stupefying the man, seems even to deprive him of life, is no obscure evidence of immortality; not only suggesting thoughts of things which never existed, but foreboding future events. I briefly touch on topics which even profane writers describe with a more splendid eloquence. For pious readers, a simple reference is sufficient. Were not the soul some kind of essence separated from the body, Scripture would not teach that we dwell in houses of clay, and at death remove from a tabernacle of flesh; that we put off that which is corruptible, in order that, at the last day, we may finally receive according to the deeds done in the body. These, and similar passages which everywhere occur, not only clearly distinguish the soul from the body, but by giving it the name of man, intimate that it is his principal part. Again, when Paul exhorts believers to cleanse themselves from all filthiness of the flesh and the spirit, he shows that there are two parts in which the taint of sin resides. Peter, also, in calling Christ the Shepherd and Bishop of souls, would have spoken absurdly if there were no souls towards which he might discharge such an office. Nor would there be any ground for what he says concerning the eternal salvation of souls, or for his injunction to purify our souls, or for his assertion that fleshly lusts war against the soul; neither could the author of the Epistle to the Hebrews say, that pastors watch as those who must give an account for our souls, if souls were devoid of essence. To the same effect Paul calls God to witness upon his soul, which could not be brought to trial before God if incapable of suffering punishment. This is still more clearly expressed by our Saviour, when he bids us fear him who, after he has killed the body, is able also to cast into hell fire. Again when the author of the Epistle to the Hebrews distinguishes the fathers of our flesh from God, who alone is the Father of our spirits, he could not have asserted the essence of the soul in clearer terms. Moreover, did not the soul, when freed from the fetters of the body, continue to exist, our Saviour would not have represented the soul of Lazarus as enjoying blessedness in Abraham’s bosom, while, on the contrary, that of Dives was suffering dreadful torments. Paul assures us of the same thing when he says, that so long as we are present in the body, we are absent from the Lord. Not to dwell on a matter as to which there is little obscurity, I will only add, that Luke mentions among the errors of the Sadducees that they believed neither angel nor spirit.

2 — Structure at a Glance – 

[THESIS]       S1–S2     Man = body + soul; soul = immortal though CREATED essence; also called "spirit"
[TERMS]        S3–S5     "spirit" alone = "soul" (Eccl 12:7; Christ & Stephen); the soul is not a mere
                          breath or energy devoid of essence
[CONCESSION]   S6        Earth-bound men deny survival — still, the light is not wholly quenched
[PROOF 1]      S7–S10    Conscience → fear of judgment → only a soul with essence can feel it
[PROOF 2]      S11       The mere knowledge of God → souls that rise above the world are immortal
[PROOF 3]      S12–S17   Noble faculties: animals vs the human mind (S13–S14); intellect conceives the
                          invisible (S15); ideas of rectitude have a spirit as seat (S16–S17)
[PROOF 4]      S18       Sleep and dreams (a supporting argument)
[PIVOT]        S19–S20   Pagans say it better; pious readers need only a pointer
[PROOF 5]      S21–S31   Scripture: houses of clay (S21), "man" = soul (S22), Paul (S23, S26, S30),
                          Peter (S24–S25), Hebrews (S25, S28), Christ (S27, S29), the Sadducees (S31)

Examiner's Eye. - ⚠️ Created/eternal trap (S1): the soul is immortal though created. An option calling the soul "eternal" or "uncreated" is a direction-flip. - ⚠️ Scope trap (S3): spirit and soul differ when used together but are equivalent when spirit is used alone. "Always identical" is overreach; "never identical" is a flip. - ⚠️ Negation-scope trap (S6): not so completely quenched… that all sense… is lost = the light is dimmed, but not to total extinction. "The light is completely out" and "the light is untouched" are both wrong. - ⚠️ Direction trap (S11): knowledge of God is evidence for the soul's immortality; it does not cause it. - ⚠️ Not-a-question trap (S29): did not the soul… continue to exist is a conditional, not a question. - ⚠️ Weight trap (S18–S20): sleep and the philosophers' arguments are supporting points Calvin treats briefly; the load-bearing proof for him is Scripture (S21–S31).

S1★★there can be no question that… ; meaning by soul, an immortal though created essence›

Moreover, there can be no question that man consists of a body and a soul; meaning by soul, an immortal though created essence, which is his nobler part.

🔤 Morphology · 어형

There is no doubt that a human being is made of a body and a soul. By "soul" I mean an essence that is immortal, though it was created, and it is the better part of us.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — Inverted conditional (no if) and negative-adverb inversion

Were + S + (not) ..., S would (not) V               = If S were (not) ...          (S21)
Did + not + S + V, S would not have V-ed            = If S did not V ... (mixed)  (S29)
Nor / Neither + would/could + S + V                 = and S would/could not V      (S25)
Were not the soul some kind of essence..., Scripture would not teach that…        (S21)
Did not the soul… continue to exist, our Saviour would not have represented…       (S29)
⚠️ No `if` AND no question mark: S29 ends with a period. The first clause is a condition, not a question.
⚠️ Negation sits AFTER the auxiliary/verb: `Were not the soul`, `Did not the soul` (the older style); modern: `Were the soul not…`.
⚠️ Never write `If were not…`: `if` and inversion do not co-occur.
⚠️ In S24/S29 the result is a past-perfect modal (`would not have V-ed`) while the condition is present/timeless: a mixed conditional.

Drill: If the soul were mere breath, conscience would have nothing to fear. → Were the soul mere breath, conscience would have nothing to fear. Now: If souls did not survive, Paul could not have called God to witness. → Did souls not survive, Paul could not have called God to witness.

Formula 2 — Absolute and buried-subject constructions

it being impossible that …, main clause           = since it is impossible that …        (S11)
which, (V-ing X), V …                               = which, because it V-s X, V-s …       (S18)
Not to V …, S V                                     = To be brief, S V                     (S31)
S (long relative clause) V                          = find the main verb first             (S5 err, S14 demonstrates)
⚠️ In S11 `it` is a dummy: the real subject is the that-clause.
⚠️ In S18 `which` is the subject of `seems`; `stupefying the man` is only an interrupter.
⚠️ The same noun in the participle phrase and the main clause = ordinary participle (S6 `men cleaving…, are…`); a different noun = absolute (S11).

Drill: The soul being immortal, the body's dust is no cause for despair. → Conscience speaking of judgment, we conclude that the soul has essence.

Formula 3 — so… as to, negated so… that, such… as

so + ADJ/PP + as to V                                 = so … that it V-s                  (S6 so immersed in darkness as to imagine)
not so + ADV + V-ed + that + clause                   = V-ed, but not to the degree that  (S6 not so completely quenched… that all sense… is lost)
such N as S V                                         = N of the kind that S V            (S13 such sense as the lower animals possess)
so many N                                             = just so many N                    (S12 so many evidences)
⚠️ `not so… that` negates the RESULT, not the main verb: the light is quenched in part, not totally.
⚠️ In `such… as`, `as` is a relative pronoun (object of `possess`), not a conjunction.

Drill: Men are so sunk in darkness as to deny the grave's end. → The light is not so dimmed that conscience falls silent.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The doctrine at stake. Chapter 15 says: before we talk about man's ruin, see what he was. Printed §2 secures the higher half of man: he has an immortal soul. Calvin's definition is deliberately careful — an immortal though created essence. That one clause refuses two opposite mistakes: the view that the soul is just an energy that dies with the body (the S5 opponents), and the view that the soul is a piece of God (the divine-spark idea; compare the Manichee/Servetus/Osiander lines that §5 will refute). Immortal, yes; self-existent, no.

The audience. Calvin wrote this against specific opponents. As far as I know, his early treatise Psychopannychia (written 1534, published in 1542) attacked "soul-sleep", the view some radical groups held that the soul sleeps or ceases between death and resurrection; Beveridge's note 66 (printed between S3's halves and removed above) points readers to that work and to the Instructio adversus Libertinos. Add the classical sceptics (Pliny's quote in the same note: the day after death is like the day before birth) and the Sadducees (S31). So the tone is not abstract metaphysics; it is pastoral defence.

The method. Five arguments, in a deliberate order: conscience (moral), the knowledge of God (religious), the faculties of the mind (rational), sleep (a classical commonplace), and Scripture (decisive). Notice the proportion: S7–S18 are twelve sentences of philosophy, S21–S31 are eleven sentences of Scripture. S19–S20 tell you which counts for the pious reader. Compare Ch. 14: Calvin keeps both lines (reason's pointers, Scripture's authority) but never lets the pointers outrank the Word.

Lineage and later reception. The soul's immortality as a created gift runs through Augustine; the body-soul pair as the basic anthropology is standard in Reformed confessions (Westminster Confession 4.2: God made man "with reasonable and immortal souls"; Belgic Confession art. 14). Later Reformed theology debates whether man is dichotomous (body + soul) or trichotomous (body, soul, spirit); S3 (spirit used alone = soul) is the passage Calvin's heirs cite for dichotomy. Treat that as a later use, not as Calvin's polemic.

Over-reading guardrails. ⚠️ prison-house of the body (S4) and fetters of the body (S29) are borrowed images that echo Platonic language; Calvin's own line in S1 (and in the dust-and-spirit pair of §1) is that the body is a creature of God, a "tabernacle" — a dwelling, not a dungeon. Do not read this paragraph as contempt for the body. ⚠️ S18 (dreams foreboding events) is a classical argument, offered briefly; it is not Calvin's endorsement of dream-divination. ⚠️ S11 is not natural theology standing alone: the knowledge of God it cites is the knowledge Calvin has defended at length in Book 1. ⚠️ Calvin does not here describe the soul's state between death and the resurrection beyond "God becomes its perpetual keeper" (S4) and Lazarus/Dives (S29); do not import later debates.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. S3 — Eccl 12:7. "Then shall the dust return to the earth as it was: and the spirit shall return unto God who gave it." Calvin uses it to show that spirit alone = soul, and that it goes back to its Giver.
  2. S4 — Luke 23:46; Acts 7:59. Christ: "Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit"; Stephen: "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit." Gapped in Stephen his to Christ. Calvin: at death God (or Christ) becomes perpetual keeper.
  3. S6 — Jas 1:17; Eph 4:18; Rom 1:21. the Father of Lights (Jas 1:17); the darkened understanding of the earth-bound (Eph 4:18; Rom 1:21). Calvin concedes the darkness, then insists on the remnant light.
  4. S7–S8 — Rom 2:14–15; Rom 14:10; 2 Cor 5:10. Conscience as witness and the Judgment-seat. Calvin's argument runs from the experience of being accused to the essence of the one accused.
  5. S11 — Ps 36:9. "With thee is the fountain of life." the very fountain of life = God; a fading force could not reach it.
  6. S21 — Job 4:19; 2 Cor 5:1, 4; 2 Pet 1:13–14; 2 Cor 5:10. houses of clay, tabernacle of flesh, put off, the deeds done in the body. Calvin gathers the body-as-tent images (see Beveridge's note 67) to show that a tent presupposes a dweller.
  7. S22 — e.g. Luke 12:20; 2 Cor 4:16. The "inner man" or the soul named as the person (the name of man).
  8. S23 — 2 Cor 7:1. "Let us cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit."
  9. S24 — 1 Pet 2:25. Christ "the Shepherd and Bishop of your souls". Gk. episkopos = overseer.
  10. S25 — 1 Pet 1:9; 1:22; 2:11; Heb 13:17. Salvation of souls; purifying the soul; fleshly lusts that "war against the soul"; pastors who "watch for your souls, as they that must give account".
  11. S26 — 2 Cor 1:23. "I call God for a record upon my soul."
  12. S27 — Luke 12:5; Matt 10:28. "Fear him which, after he hath killed, hath power to cast into hell."
  13. S28 — Heb 12:9. "We have had fathers of our flesh… shall we not much rather be in subjection unto the Father of spirits?"
  14. S29 — Luke 16:19–31. Lazarus in Abraham's bosom; the rich man ("Dives" in the Vulgate tradition) in torment.
  15. S30 — 2 Cor 5:6, 8. "At home in the body… absent from the Lord."
  16. S31 — Acts 23:8. "For the Sadducees say that there is no resurrection, neither angel, nor spirit." Calvin used the same text in Ch. 14 (Day 112).

How Calvin uses Scripture here. First as language (S21–S23: the vocabulary of tents, parts and names presupposes a dweller), then as scenes (S27–S29: Jesus' own words), and finally as a diagnosis of the opposition (S31).

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① Grammar — which one is ungrammatical?

(A) Were not the soul some kind of essence, Scripture would not teach that we dwell in houses of clay. (B) Did not the soul continue to exist, our Saviour would not have represented Lazarus as blessed. (C) If were not the soul some kind of essence, Scripture would not teach that we dwell in houses of clay. (D) Nor would there be any ground for Peter's injunction to purify our souls.

Answer: (C). Rationale: Formula 1 — an inverted conditional replaces if; the two never co-occur. (A) and (B) are the S21 and S29 patterns; (D) is the S25 negative-adverb inversion.

② Comprehension — which statement is consistent with §2?

(A) Calvin says that the soul is immortal because it is uncreated and shares God's essence. (B) Calvin says that "soul" and "spirit" are always interchangeable, whatever the context. (C) Calvin says that the soul must be a real essence, since a mere motion without substance could not feel terror before God's judgment-seat. (D) Calvin says that earth-bound men who deny the afterlife have lost every sense of immortality.

Answer: (C). Rationale: (A) direction flip — S1: immortal though created. (B) scope overreach — S3: they differ while they are used together; they are equivalent when spirit is used by itself. (D) overreach / negation trap — S6: the light is not so completely quenched… that all sense of immortality is lost. (C) restates S7–S10.

③ Composition — apply Formula 1:

Rewrite with inversion and no if: If the soul were not an essence, conscience would not feel terror before God. Then add one sentence using so… as to (Formula 3).

Model answer: Were the soul not an essence, conscience would not feel terror before God. And: Men are so sunk in darkness as to imagine that they will not survive the grave. Rationale: Were moves in front of the subject and if disappears (negation after the subject in modern English: Were the soul not…); so + participle + as to V states the result.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –
