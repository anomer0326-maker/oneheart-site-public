Day 90 — Book 1, Chapter 13, §16

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

The subject changes today, and Calvin tells you so in the first eight words. In asserting the divinity of **the Spirit** … For thirteen days we have been in the doctrine of the Son. That is over. We are now in pneumatology, and it runs to the end of the chapter. But look at what he does not do at the seam. He does not restate his method. He does not re-argue his tools. He writes one clause — the proof must be derived from the **same sources** — and walks straight into the evidence.

⚠️⚠️ Underline the same sources and put a box around it, because that phrase is a promissory note and you are the one holding it. What are the same sources? §§7–15. ① The naming argument — divine names and titles ascribed to him. ② The works proper to God alone — creation, and sending the prophets. ③ The invocation argument — what the Church does toward him in prayer and faith. ④ The inherent-versus-derived axis — does he have this of himself or on loan? Calvin is telling you: everything you learned about the Son, you are now going to watch me re-run on the Spirit. Write it at the top: WHAT I LEARNED YESTERDAY IS TODAY'S TOOLKIT. ⚠️ And here is why that matters for the exam: any question about "Calvin's method for proving the Spirit's divinity" is answerable only if you can name the sources §§7–15 supplied. The section does not list them. It points backward.

Second thing, and it is the sweetest sentence in the section. Genesis 1:2. the Spirit of God was **expanded over** the abyss or **shapeless matter**. Then the for clause explains what that proves — and it proves two things, not one. for it shows **not only that** the beauty which the world displays is maintained by the invigorating power of the Spirit, **but that** even before this beauty existed the Spirit was at work **cherishing the confused mass**. ⚠️⚠️ Yesterday's construction, back within twenty-four hours. not only that … but that … — and as yesterday, BOTH limbs stand. Limb one: the Spirit maintains the beauty that is. Limb two: the Spirit was working before there was any beauty to maintain. That second limb is the whole argument. Anything made belongs to the order of made things; a thing that was already at work before that order had a face cannot be a member of it. Write the two limbs as NOW and BEFORE, one above the other.

Third, and you have been trained for it since Day 88. **Again**, no cavils can explain away the force of what Isaiah says … ⚠️⚠️⚠️ That is the THIRD consecutive Again. Day 88, Day 89, Day 90. Three paragraphs in a row opening on the same word. If Again meant next in the series, Calvin would be building a ladder whose rungs are all at one height — and now he has done it three times running, across a change of subject. It is settled. Again = RESET. Here is another proof, standing on its own legs. Stop arguing with me about it and write it in permanent ink.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. Fronted gerundial adjunct + deontic passive. **In asserting** the divinity of the Spirit, **the proof must be derived** from the same sources. ⚠️ Two items. ① The opening In V-ing … is not a time adverb meaning while; it is the frame that names the task — when it comes to asserting …, i.e. for the purposes of this proof. ② **must be derived** is deontic-methodological, not epistemic. ⚠️⚠️ Trap: reading it as the proof must (surely) come from the same sources — a guess about where the evidence probably lies. Wrong. It is a rule Calvin imposes on himself: the proof is to be drawn from the same sources. Epistemic must reports; deontic must legislates. This one legislates. Formula 1.
  2. Litotes inside an it-cleft, with the predicate in focus. **it is by no means an obscure testimony which Moses bears** in the history of the creation ⚠️ Restore the base pattern before you do anything else: The testimony which Moses bears … is by no means obscure. Calvin has taken the predicate noun phrase — an obscure testimony — and fronted it into cleft-focus position, leaving which Moses bears behind as the cleft relative. ⚠️⚠️ Then he negates it twice over: by no means + obscure = extremely clear. Litotes is not modesty. It is emphasis wearing a raincoat. ⚠️ And watch bears: the idiom is bear testimony (= give evidence), and the cleft has split the idiom in half. Formula 2.
  3. ascribe X to Y stretched across a seven-word object — the garden path of the day. thus ascribing **a share in the sovereign power of sending the prophets** **to the Holy Spirit** ⚠️⚠️⚠️ This is the single most likely grammar item in the section, and almost everyone reads it wrong the first time. The natural but fatal parse: sending the prophets to the Holy Spirit — as though the prophets were being dispatched to the Spirit as their destination. No. Restore: ascribe [a share in the sovereign power of sending the prophets] to [the Holy Spirit]. The indirect object has been shifted to the end because the direct object is too heavy to carry in the middle. Cover the words to the Holy Spirit with your finger, read what remains, and the sentence solves itself.** Formula 3.

Examiner's Eye. ⚠️⚠️⚠️ Four traps, and the third one is the best item in the section. (1) the same sources (S1). Any option that reads this as the same Scripture passages, or the same books of the Bible, has confused sources of proof with proof texts. ⚠️ Note that today's two texts — Genesis 1:2 and Isaiah 48:16 — are brand new; neither has appeared in §§7–15. The texts are different; the instruments are identical. That gap between "same sources" and "new texts" is precisely what a good comprehension item is built on. (2) not only that … but that … (S2). Any option reading Calvin denies that the Spirit maintains the world's present beauty has imported Day 88's not that … but that …, which did cancel its first limb. Today, as yesterday, both limbs stand. ⚠️ Three sections, two constructions, one word apart. The examiner knows this and so do you. (3) ascribing a share in the sovereign power of sending the prophets to the Holy Spirit (S3). ⚠️⚠️ The garden path. Any option that has the prophets sent to the Holy Spirit — or, more subtly, any option that says Isaiah ascribes to the Spirit the power of sending prophets without the word share — has misread. a share in is doing load-bearing work: the sending in Isaiah 48:16 is joint (the Lord God, **and his Spirit**), so what the Spirit receives is a part in a power exercised together, not sole possession of it. Drop share and you have made Calvin say more than he said — and scope-overreach is the examiner's favourite distractor. (4) In this his divine majesty is clear (S4). ⚠️ Watch the antecedent of this. It reaches back to the Isaiah testimony and the inference drawn from it — the nearest complete argument — not to the whole section and not to Genesis. Any option that makes S4 a summary of both witnesses is over-reading a four-word sentence. When a demonstrative is this bare, take the nearest fully-stated argument and stop.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — Fronted gerundial adjunct + deontic passive (the "method declaration" frame)

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  In V-ing  [the task] ,  [the proof / the argument / the case]        │
│                          MUST BE  p.p.  from/on  [the resource].      │
│                                                                       │
│  ① "In V-ing" here = FRAMING, not temporal:                          │
│        = "when it comes to V-ing", "as regards V-ing"                 │
│        ✗ NOT "while V-ing"                                            │
│  ② "must be p.p." = DEONTIC (a rule laid down)                        │
│        TEST: can you substitute "ought to be"?  YES → deontic          │
│        ✗ NOT epistemic ("it must surely be the case that…")           │
│  ③ The gerund's agent is UNEXPRESSED and recovered from context       │
│        — a "dangler" by modern rules, standard in 1845 and in Latin   │
│  ④ Agentless passive = the rule holds WHOEVER is arguing              │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

⚠️ Trap. Converting the deontic must into an epistemic one turns a method into a guess. In a translation item, the proof surely comes from the same sources is wrong even though every word is defensible. ⚠️ Second trap. the same sources means the same lines of proof, not the same proof texts. Today's texts are brand new.

Transformation drill. Rewrite in Formula 1 shape: We prove the Son's eternity. We should use the passages about creation.

Model: In asserting the eternity of the Son, the argument must be drawn from the same passages in which the world is said to have been made by him.

Formula 2 — Litotes inside a predicational it-cleft

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  It is  BY NO MEANS  a(n) [negative-ADJ] [NOUN]  which  S  V …        │
│                                                                       │
│  RESTORE:  The [NOUN] which S V  is  by no means  [negative-ADJ].     │
│                                                                       │
│  ① The PREDICATE has been nominalized and put in focus position       │
│  ② TWO negatives: "by no means" + a negative-meaning adjective        │
│        (obscure / trifling / doubtful / slight)                       │
│        → multiply into a STRONG POSITIVE, not a lukewarm one          │
│  ③ Watch for a SPLIT IDIOM: "bear testimony" divided by the cleft     │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

⚠️ Trap. Flattening the litotes: not an unclear testimony is a translation that survives the grammar and kills the rhetoric. Render the force: about as plain as testimony gets.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

Where we are. Chapter 13 has been running its proof of the Son's deity since §7. Today it turns to the Spirit and does not look back at the Son again except comparatively. §§16–20 (our numbering) are Calvin's compact pneumatology — and "compact" is the word. Four sentences to open it. ⚠️ Do not expect the doctrine of procession here. It is not in this paragraph, and reading it in is the standard over-read. What you get today is one thing only: the Spirit does what only God does, therefore the Spirit is God.

Why so short? Because of the same sources. Calvin has already spent nine sections building the instruments; re-running them on a new subject takes a fraction of the space. ⚠️ This is a feature of the 1559 edition's architecture — the Institutes of 1559 is organized so that earlier argument is capital that later argument spends. The 1536 edition, a sixth the length, could not do this.

Genesis 1:2 and its long history. ⚠️ the Spirit of God was expanded over the abyss sits on top of a genuine translational fault line, and you should know it exists. The Hebrew רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים can be rendered the Spirit of God or a mighty wind (with אֱלֹהִים read as a superlative), and מְרַחֶפֶת can be hovering or sweeping. Calvin knows the alternative and rejects it — in his Commentary on Genesis he argues the personal reading, and this paragraph simply assumes it. ⚠️ Guardrail: today's argument is only as strong as that reading, and Calvin does not defend the reading here. If you are asked what this section proves, the honest answer includes given his reading of Gen 1:2. A careful student notices where an argument's weight rests on an exegetical decision made elsewhere.

The brooding image. The bird over the nest is ancient in this verse's reception — it is in the Syriac tradition, in Basil's Hexaemeron, and it travels with the verse into the Latin West. Calvin's fovēre, which Beveridge gives as cherishing, keeps it. ⚠️ And the French edition's note tells you the function: so as to prevent its being instantly annihilated. That is not incubation toward hatching; it is preservation in being. Theologically this puts the Spirit's pre-formative work under the heading of conservation, alongside the maintained of limb one — the same office, before and after the world has a face. ⚠️ Note how tidy that makes the sentence: one office, two epochs, and the second epoch is the one a creature could not occupy.

Shapeless matter — and the trap of reading Calvin as a dualist. ⚠️ informis materia has a long scholastic pedigree and a longer pagan one, and it would be easy to hear an echo of Plato's receptacle or of a co-eternal matter. It is not there. Calvin holds creation ex nihilo without qualification; Genesis 1:1 makes the mass, Genesis 1:2 finds it unformed, and the forming follows. The informis materia is a creature in an early state, not a principle standing alongside God. ⚠️ Any reading that has Calvin conceding pre-existent matter is an over-read — and it is the kind of over-read that examiners like, because the vocabulary genuinely invites it.

Isaiah 48:16 and its crux. ⚠️ And now the Lord God, and his Spirit, has sent me is one of the most-discussed verses in Isaiah, because the speaker is unidentified — the prophet, the Servant, or (for Calvin and the older tradition) the Messiah. Observe the discipline in Calvin's use: he does not need the crux settled. His premise is about the senders, not the sent. Whoever me is, the sending is predicated of the Lord God and his Spirit together, and a joint share in a divine prerogative is all the argument requires. ⚠️ Learn the technique: locate the minimum your premise needs, and do not buy more controversy than that.

The editor's cross-reference. (Calvin in Acts 20:28) sends you to the Holy Ghost hath made you overseers — the Spirit appointing ministers. The pairing is deliberate: sending prophets (OT) and appointing overseers (NT) are the same act of divine commissioning. ⚠️ This is also, incidentally, the classical Reformed root of the doctrine that ecclesiastical office is the Spirit's appointment mediated through the church's call — a line that runs straight into the Presbyterian ordination formularies.

Later reception, briefly and accurately. Abraham Kuyper's The Work of the Holy Spirit (1888) leans very hard on exactly this Genesis text for a doctrine of the Spirit's work in creation as well as redemption, and he is drawing on this Calvinist line. Barth, Church Dogmatics I/1, treats the Spirit's deity under the same rubric Calvin uses here — the Spirit is Lord because the Spirit does the Lord's works — though Barth reaches it through the doctrine of revelation rather than through proof texts. ⚠️ Guardrail on both: neither is in this paragraph. Calvin is doing something narrower and more forensic than either.

The one-line takeaway. ⚠️ §16 is not a doctrine of the Spirit. It is the opening of a case, conducted with borrowed instruments, and its argumentative move is temporal: the Spirit was at work before there was a creation to belong to.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. Genesis 1:2 — S2 (the Spirit of God was expanded over the abyss or shapeless matter). ⚠️ Not a loose echo — this is the citation, though Calvin gives no reference. Hebrew: וְרוּחַ אֱלֹהִים מְרַחֶפֶת עַל־פְּנֵי הַמָּיִם. ⚠️ The participle מְרַחֶפֶת (mərāḥepet, root רחף) is the hinge: hovering, fluttering. How Calvin uses it: as a statement of activity prior to formation — and therefore prior to the created order as such. The verse is doing chronological work, not merely pictorial work.
  2. Deuteronomy 32:11 — behind expanded over and cherishing, S2. The only other clear OT occurrence of רחף: the eagle that fluttereth over (יְרַחֵף) her young. ⚠️ This is why cherishing is fovēre, the bird-on-the-nest verb, and not a word about affection. The lexicon and the Latin agree. How Calvin uses it: silently — he never cites Deuteronomy here, but the image his verb carries comes from that nest.
  3. Psalm 104:30 — behind limb one, S2 (the beauty which the world displays is maintained by the invigorating power of the Spirit). Thou sendest forth thy spirit, they are created: and thou renewest the face of the earth. ⚠️ invigorating and renewest are the same doctrine — conservation as continuous giving of life. How Calvin uses it: as unstated background; the psalm is the standard locus for the Spirit's work in sustaining the world, and limb one simply assumes it.
  4. Isaiah 48:16 — S3, cited (And now the Lord God, and his Spirit, has sent me). Hebrew: וְעַתָּה אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה שְׁלָחַנִי וְרוּחוֹ. ⚠️ Note two things in the Hebrew that the English hides: (a) the divine Name is present — Adonai YHWH — though Beveridge does not gloss it today; (b) the word order puts and his Spirit after the verb, so the joint agency is, if anything, clearer in Hebrew than in English. How Calvin uses it: to put the Spirit inside an act — the commissioning of prophets — that Scripture reserves to God, thereby assigning him a share in the sovereign power.
  5. Acts 20:28 — S3, editorial cross-reference (the Holy Ghost hath made you overseers). Greek: τὸ πνεῦμα τὸ ἅγιον ἔθετο ἐπισκόπους. ⚠️ ἔθετο (etheto, middle of τίθημι) = appointed, installed of his own act. How it is used: by the editor, not by Calvin in this paragraph — pairing the OT sending with an NT appointing, so the reader sees the instrument work in both Testaments.
  6. Genesis 1:31 — faintly behind the beauty which the world displays, S2. And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. ⚠️ Calvin's word is beauty (pulchritudo), not goodness — but the Genesis verdict on the finished creation is what licenses calling the world's order a beauty in the first place. How Calvin uses it: as an assumed frame — limb one is about the persistence of the goodness Genesis 1:31 pronounced.
  7. John 1:3 / Hebrews 1:2 — implied by the phrase the same sources, S1. ⚠️ Not quoted today, but this is where row ② of the toolkit came from: creation as a work proper to God, used in §7 for the Word. How Calvin uses it: by reference, not by quotation — the same sources is precisely an instruction to go back and re-read §7's use of the creation argument, this time with the Spirit in the frame.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① 어법 — 밑줄 넷 중 어색한 것 하나

In asserting the divinity of the Spirit, the proof (A) must be derived from the same sources. And it is by no means an obscure testimony (B) which Moses bears in the history of the creation; for it shows not only that the beauty of the world is maintained by the Spirit, (C) but even before this beauty existed the Spirit was at work. Again, no cavils can explain away the force of what Isaiah says, thus (D) ascribing a share in the sovereign power of sending the prophets to the Holy Spirit.

✨ 답안 보기 (클릭)정답: (C).

출제 의도. ⚠️ This is today's Formula 2 review box, tested by deletion. The matrix verb is it shows, and it governs two that-clauses: not only **that** A … but **that** B. (C) has dropped the second that, which leaves but even before this beauty existed the Spirit was at work hanging as an independent clause that shows can no longer govern. Correct: but **that** even before this beauty existed …. ⚠️⚠️ Why the deletion is tempting: English often permits that-deletion in a second conjunct — he said A and B is fine. But here sixteen words of fronted adverbial (even before this beauty existed) intervene before the subject arrives, and the parallel collapses without the overt complementizer. Rule to take away: the longer the gap between limbs, the more obligatory the second that becomes. Why the others are sound. (A) deontic passive, Formula 1 — correct as printed. (B) the cleft relative with the split idiom bear testimony — correct; which is the object of bears. (D) heavy-NP shift, Formula 3 — looks wrong, and that is the point: it is the garden path, but it is perfectly grammatical. ⚠️ A well-made grammar item always includes one option that feels wrong and is right.

② 내용일치 — 다음 중 §16의 내용과 일치하는 것은?

(1) Calvin proves the Spirit's divinity from the same Scripture passages he used for the Son. (2) Calvin says that the Spirit maintains the world's present beauty, but denies that the Spirit was active before the world was formed. (3) Calvin says that Isaiah assigns to the Holy Spirit the sovereign power of sending the prophets, which belongs to the Spirit alone. (4) Calvin argues that the Spirit was at work even before the world had any beauty, and that Isaiah gives the Spirit a share in the power of sending prophets.

✨ 답안 보기 (클릭)정답: (4).

출제 의도. ⚠️ Three distractors, three different species of error — learn to name them. (1) is the same sources trap (scope shift). the same sources = the same lines of proof, not the same proof texts. ⚠️ Genesis 1:2 and Isaiah 48:16 are new; neither appeared in §§7–15. The distractor swaps instruments for texts, and it is the subtlest option on the page. (2) is the direction-flip trap. It reads not only that A, but that B as though it were Day 88's not that A, but that B, which cancelled its first limb. Today both limbs stand — and in fact the distractor has flipped the wrong one: it keeps A and denies B, when B is the limb carrying the argument. ⚠️ Double error, single sentence. (3) is the scope-overreach trap, and it is built on one deleted word. Calvin wrote **a share in** the sovereign power. The distractor deletes share and adds alone. ⚠️ Isaiah names two senders — the Lord God, **and** his Spirit — so what the text gives the Spirit is participation in a jointly exercised prerogative. Sole possession is not claimed and is not needed. (4) states both limbs of S2 correctly (with even before intact) and preserves a share in from S3.

③ 영작 — 다음을 오늘의 공식을 적용해 영어로

세례의 증언 역시 결코 모호한 증언이 아니다. 그것은 성령이 성부·성자와 함께 불린다는 것뿐 아니라, 우리가 그 한 이름 안으로 잠기기 전에 이미 성령이 일하고 계셨다는 것도 보여 주기 때문이다. 어떤 궤변도 그 말씀의 힘을 설명해 없앨 수 없으니, 그리하여 성령께 그 한 이름 안의 동등한 자리를 돌리는 것이다.

Model answer:

And it is by no means an obscure testimony which baptism bears; for it shows not only that the Spirit is named together with the Father and the Son, but that even before we are plunged into that one name the Spirit was already at work. No cavils can explain away the force of that word, thus ascribing an equal place in the one name into which we are baptized to the Holy Spirit.

출제 의도. Five things are being checked. ① Formula 2's cleft with the litotes intact — it is by no means an obscure testimony which … bears. ⚠️ Writing baptism's testimony is clear is true and scores nothing; the item is testing whether you can build the cleft and keep the double negative. ② The split idiom bear testimony — the verb must land inside the relative clause (which baptism **bears**), not beside the noun. ③ not only that … but that … with the second that retained, because a fronted adverbial (even before we are plunged …) separates the limbs. ⚠️ Drop it and you have just made problem ① wrong in your own composition. ④ even before — the word even is not optional; it marks the second limb as the stronger one. ⑤ Formula 3: the to-phrase pushed past a nine-word object — ascribing [an equal place in the one name into which we are baptized] **to the Holy Spirit**. ⚠️ Common failure: ascribing to the Holy Spirit an equal place …, which is perfectly good English but does not demonstrate the construction under test. The item asks for the shifted order. ⚠️ And one more, unstated: plunged renders βαπτίζω's literal sense and keeps the register; before we are baptized into that name is acceptable but loses the image.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary. The doctrine of the Spirit opens with a single methodological clause — the proof must be derived from the same sources, a pointer back to the instruments of §§7–15 — and then two witnesses are called: Moses, whose Genesis testimony is by no means obscure and proves two things at once (the Spirit maintains the world's beauty, and — the limb that carries the argument — was at work before that beauty existed, brooding over the shapeless mass); and Isaiah, whom no cavils can explain away, and who gives the Spirit a share in the sovereign power of sending the prophets. Four words close it: In this his divine majesty is clear.**

The three grammar points, once more. ① In V-ing X, the proof must be derived from … — framing gerund (not temporal), deontic must (a rule, not a guess), agentless passive because the rule holds whoever argues. ② it is by no means an obscure testimony which Moses bears — predicational it-cleft, litotes doubled into a strong positive, and the idiom bear testimony split across the cleft; plus the review of not only that … but that …, where both limbs stand and the second that is obligatory across a long gap. ③ ascribing [a share in the sovereign power of sending the prophets] to the Holy Spirit — heavy-NP shift, the garden path of the week; cover the to-phrase, find the incomplete remainder, snap it back; and never delete a share in.

And the structural fact you now own: ⚠️ three consecutive Agains — Day 88, 89, 90 — across a change of subject. Again = RESET, not rung. Use it as a skim-signal for the rest of the chapter.

Homework (10 minutes).

  1. Structure restoration (4 min). Take S2 — the sixty-word sentence — and write out its skeleton on one line: mark the semicolon as the hinge, bracket the two that-limbs, and label them NOW and BEFORE. Then answer in one line: which limb would survive if Calvin were talking about an angel, and which would not? ⚠️ If you can answer that, you have understood why limb two is the argument.
  2. Composition (4 min). Write one sentence using Formula 3 — ascribe X to Y with a direct object of at least seven words, so that the to-phrase is shifted to the end. Subject: the Spirit's part in the raising of Christ (Rom 8:11). Check yourself on one thing only: apply the field test to your own sentence. Cover the to-phrase. Is what remains incomplete? If it reads as complete, your object is not heavy enough and the construction has not been demonstrated.
  3. Preview (2 min). ⚠️ Tomorrow the register changes, and it changes on a phrase you have seen before.
