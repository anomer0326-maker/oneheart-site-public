Day 35 — Book 1, Chapter 7, §4

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday ended with an empty socket. Augustine, Calvin said, would have the certainty of the godly to rest on a very different foundation — and then he refused to name it. Two sections he has made you wait.

Today he names it. the secret testimony of the Spirit (S5). the testimony of the Spirit is superior to reason (S14). sealed by the inward testimony of the Spirit (S15). Three times, escalating, and the last one gives you the technical term that will run through Reformed theology for four hundred years: testimonium Spiritus Sancti internum.

But look at the shape of the section, because the shape is the argument and almost everybody gets it backwards.

S1–S5    THESIS: the ground of our conviction is the secret testimony of the Spirit
S6–S9    CONCESSION: and yet arguments work! Scripture's divine origin can be
         established by evidence; its majesty will force us to do it homage
S10–S13  THE PIVOT: STILL, HOWEVER — it is PREPOSTEROUS to build full faith by discussion
S14–S18  THE ANSWER RESTATED: the Spirit is superior to reason; God alone can
         witness to his own words; his word is not credited till HE seals it

See what he did? He gives the rational evidences their full due and then demotes them — in that order. S6–S9 is not a throwaway. It is Calvin promising you the whole of Chapter 8, which is nothing but sixty pages of arguments for the credibility of Scripture. He is going to write them. He believes them. And here, one chapter early, he tells you what they can and cannot do. A man who thought reason worthless would not have bothered with Chapter 8. A man who thought reason sufficient would not have written S10.

And watch the one word that carries the whole pivot: preposterous. Not "useless," not "wrong." Prae-posterus — before-behind, back-to-front, out of order. Calvin is not saying the argument is bad. He is saying it has been put in the wrong place in the sequence. Underline it now; we will come back to it at S10 and it will be worth the wait.

Today's 3 Big Points — write them down now:

  1. The AS ... , SO ... correlative (S15) — the sentence that carries the whole doctrine. For **as** God alone can properly bear witness to his own words, **so** these words will not obtain full credit... That so is not "therefore." It is the second leg of a matched pair. And it comes bolted to a not ... until tail — the section's other structural signature (S1, S15, S18).
  2. Old-style negation — boast **not** their own acuteness (no do-support), **nor do they** dwell on reasons (fronted negative → invert), **it does not follow that** we shall forthwith implant (S12). Three different negative machines in one section, and only one of them inverts.
  3. Hypothetical should — although learned men... **should take** the opposite side (S7, = even if they were to) and I **should not find** it difficult (S11, first-person should = would). Neither one means obligation. Plus yesterday's if-deletion returns twice in a single sentence at S11.

Eighteen sentences. One thesis, one honest concession, one pivot, one name. Let's go.

1 — Full Text (the whole § — 18 sentences, verbatim) –

4. It is necessary to attend to what I lately said, that our faith in doctrine is not established until we have a perfect conviction that God is its author. Hence, the highest proof of Scripture is uniformly taken from the character of him whose Word it is. The prophets and apostles boast not their own acuteness or any qualities which win credit to speakers, nor do they dwell on reasons; but they appeal to the sacred name of God, in order that the whole world may be compelled to submission. The next thing to be considered is, how it appears not probable merely, but certain, that the name of God is neither rashly nor cunningly pretended. If, then, we would consult most effectually for our consciences, and save them from being driven about in a whirl of uncertainty, from wavering, and even stumbling at the smallest obstacle, our conviction of the truth of Scripture must be derived from a higher source than human conjectures, Judgments, or reasons; namely, the secret testimony of the Spirit. It is true, indeed, that if we choose to proceed in the way of arguments it is easy to establish, by evidence of various kinds, that if there is a God in heaven, the Law, the Prophecies, and the Gospel, proceeded from him. Nay, although learned men, and men of the greatest talent, should take the opposite side, summoning and ostentatiously displaying all the powers of their genius in the discussion; if they are not possessed of shameless effrontery, they will be compelled to confess that the Scripture exhibits clear evidence of its being spoken by God, and, consequently, of its containing his heavenly doctrine. We shall see a little farther on, that the volume of sacred Scripture very far surpasses all other writings. Nay, if we look at it with clear eyes, and unblessed Judgment, it will forthwith present itself with a divine majesty which will subdue our presumptuous opposition, and force us to do it homage. Still, however, it is preposterous to attempt, by discussion, to rear up a full faith in Scripture. True, were I called to contend with the craftiest despisers of God, I trust, though I am not possessed of the highest ability or eloquence, I should not find it difficult to stop their obstreperous mouths; I could, without much ado, put down the boastings which they mutter in corners, were anything to be gained by refuting their cavils. But although we may maintain the sacred Word of God against gainsayers, it does not follow that we shall forthwith implant the certainty which faith requires in their hearts. Profane men think that religion rests only on opinion, and, therefore, that they may not believe foolishly, or on slight grounds, desire and insist to have it proved by reason that Moses and the prophets were divinely inspired. But I answer, that the testimony of the Spirit is superior to reason. For as God alone can properly bear witness to his own words, so these words will not obtain full credit in the hearts of men, until they are sealed by the inward testimony of the Spirit. The same Spirit, therefore, who spoke by the mouth of the prophets, must penetrate our hearts, in order to convince us that they faithfully delivered the message with which they were divinely entrusted. This connection is most aptly expressed by Isaiah in these words, “My Spirit that is upon thee, and my words which I have put in thy mouth, shall not depart out of thy mouth, nor out of the mouth of thy seed, nor out of the mouth of thy seed’s seed, saith the Lord, from henceforth and for ever,” (Isa. 59:21). Some worthy persons feel disconcerted, because, while the wicked murmur with impunity at the Word of God, they have not a clear proof at hand to silence them, forgetting that the Spirit is called an earnest and seal to confirm the faith of the godly, for this very reason, that, until he enlightens their minds, they are tossed to and fro in a sea of doubts. ⚠️ Text note (transcription, not Calvin): three housekeeping items from our local Beveridge file. (1) The line-break hyphenation divinely in-/spired in S13 has been closed to divinely inspired. (2) The page numbers 72, 76, 77 and the running chapter headers falling inside the section have been stripped; the paragraph break before Still, however is Calvin's own and is preserved. (3) ⚠️ unblessed Judgment in S9 is almost certainly a transcription error in our file for unbiassed judgment — that is the sense the argument requires (look at it with clear eyes and an unprejudiced mind), and unblessed would reverse Calvin's point into nonsense. The capitalised Judgment / Judgments throughout (S5, S9) is likewise an artifact of this electronic edition, not Beveridge's typography. I quote the file as it stands — that is the rule — but read S9 as unbiassed, and if this ever appears on a test, the sense, not the spelling, is what is being examined.

Examiner's Eye — the traps they will set:

Trap 1 (S6–S9 vs. S10): the concession is real. This is the number-one comprehension trap of the entire chapter. An option saying "Calvin holds that rational arguments cannot establish the divine origin of Scripture" is flatly contradicted by S6 (it is easy to establish, by evidence of various kinds) and S7 (they will be compelled to confess). What Calvin denies in S10 is not that arguments work but that they can rear up a full faith. Distinguish: demonstrating the fact (possible) vs. producing the certainty of faith (impossible by argument). Miss this and you will misread Chapter 8 entirely.

Trap 2 (S12): it does not follow that. But although we may maintain the sacred Word of God against gainsayers, **it does not follow that** we shall forthwith implant the certainty... — this negates the inference, not the premise. Calvin is granting that we do successfully maintain the Word against gainsayers. An option reading "Calvin admits we cannot defend Scripture against its despisers" has inverted the sentence. Watch every it does not follow that in the Institutes; it is always a concession wearing a denial's coat.

Trap 3 (S5): the scope of higher source than. human conjectures, Judgments, or reasons — the comparison is about source, i.e. where the conviction comes from, not about worth in the abstract. The distractor will read it as "Calvin ranks the Spirit above reason as a faculty" or worse, "Calvin condemns human reason." He says the conviction must be derived from higher up. Origin, not value.

Trap 4 (S7): should take and if they are not possessed of shameless effrontery. Two conditions stacked. although... should take the opposite side = even supposing they took. if they are not possessed of shameless effrontery = provided they are not utterly brazen — Calvin builds in an escape clause, and it matters: he is not claiming argument converts every opponent. He is claiming the honest ones will be forced to concede. An option that says "Calvin holds all learned opponents will be compelled to confess" has swallowed the escape clause. Over-scope.

Trap 5 (S13): that they may not believe foolishly = LEST. This is a purpose clause, negative: in order that they may not believe foolishly = lest they believe foolishly. It gives the profane men's motive for demanding proof. Read may as permission ("they may not believe") and the sentence collapses into a statement about what they are allowed to do. It is by far the hardest clause in the section.

Trap 6 (S18): earnest. Not "sincere," not "serious." an earnest and seal — ἀρραβών, a down-payment/pledge. Financial-legal vocabulary out of Ephesians 1:14. A trap word of the first order.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas (시험 직전 이것만) –

Formula 1 — AS ... , SO ... correlative + the NOT ... UNTIL tail

   AS + S₁ + V₁ ... ,  SO + S₂ + V₂ ...
   = ~인 것과 꼭 마찬가지로, ~하다            (상관접속 — 두 다리가 한 쌍)

   ▸ For AS God alone can properly bear witness to his own words,
     SO these words will not obtain full credit ... until they are sealed   (S15)

   ⚠️ 이 so 는 "그래서(therefore)" 가 아니다. "so ... that" 의 so 도 아니다.
      ▸ 진단법: as-절을 지우면 so-절이 붕 뜬다. 뜨면 상관접속이 맞다.
   ⚠️ as 를 "~로서/~할 때" 로 읽으면 문장이 무너진다. 여기선 "~이듯이".

   ─────────  그리고 오늘의 다른 축: NOT ... UNTIL  ─────────

   S + is NOT + p.p. / will NOT + V   ...   UNTIL + S + V
   = ~하기 전까지는 ~되지 않는다  (충분조건 제시가 아니라 '문을 닫는' 구문)

   ▸ our faith in doctrine is NOT established UNTIL we have a perfect
     conviction that God is its author                                      (S1)
   ▸ these words will NOT obtain full credit ... UNTIL they are sealed      (S15)
   ▸ UNTIL he enlightens their minds, they are tossed to and fro            (S18)

   ⚠️ 세 번 반복 = 우연이 아니라 논지. "그 전까지는 아직 믿음이 아니다."

Transformation drill: Compose one sentence using both halves — As a seal takes its force from the hand that presses it, so the word takes its full credit from the Spirit who seals it, and no argument can supply that credit until he has sealed it.

Formula 2 — The three negative machines (and only one inverts)

   ① V + NOT              (고어체, do-support 없음, 도치 없음)
      ▸ The prophets and apostles BOAST NOT their own acuteness            (S3)
      ▸ cf. §3 S11  I DENY NOT that ...
      → 현대영어 복원:  do not boast / do not deny
      ⚠️ 명령문 아님! 주어가 앞에 있으면 직설법이다.

   ② NOR + aux + S + V    (부정어 문두 → 반드시 도치)
      ▸ NOR DO THEY dwell on reasons                                        (S3)
      ✗ nor they dwell        ✗ nor dwell they

   ③ IT DOES NOT FOLLOW THAT + S + V   (추론의 부정 ≠ 전제의 부정)
      ▸ although we may maintain the sacred Word ... ,
        IT DOES NOT FOLLOW THAT we shall forthwith implant the certainty    (S12)
      = 전제는 인정, 화살표만 부정 → "그렇다고 ~인 것은 아니다"

   ④ NEITHER A NOR B      (부사 두 개를 묶는 형태)
      ▸ the name of God is NEITHER rashly NOR cunningly pretended           (S4)

   ⚠️ ①과 ②가 S3 한 문장 안에 8단어 간격으로 나란히 있다.
      규칙은 '의미'가 아니라 '위치'로 작동한다 — 문두면 도치, 동사 뒤면 도치 없음.

Transformation drill: Compose in the full ①+②+③ shape — Calvin denies not the force of the evidences; nor does he forbid us to use them; but it does not follow that a man convinced by them has yet the certainty which faith requires.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

What just got named. the secret testimony of the Spirit (S5) + the inward testimony of the Spirit (S15) = testimonium Spiritus Sancti internum. This is the load-bearing wall of Reformed prolegomena, and Calvin has now set it in place. Everything in §§1–3 was demolition — the Church is not the ground. §4 is construction. §5 will finish the wall (Scripture, carrying its own evidence along with it, deigns not to submit to proofs and arguments) and give it its second name, αὐτόπιστος — self-authenticating, believed on its own account.

The 1559 architecture — and why §4's concession is not a lapse. Read the table of contents of what comes next: Chapter 8, "The credibility of Scripture sufficiently proved in so far as natural reason admits" — thirteen sections of hard evidences (antiquity of Moses, fulfilled prophecy, the harmony of the Evangelists, the constancy of the martyrs). Calvin wrote that chapter. S6–S9 is him promising it; S10 is him pre-emptively telling you its rank. The Reformed tradition later named the two tiers: the indicia or secondary proofs (Ch. 8) and the testimonium (Ch. 7) — and insisted the first are genuinely probative but insufficient, the second sufficient but not a substitute for the first. Preposterous (S10) is the hinge word for that whole arrangement. Order, not worth.

Whom he is answering, historically. Two fronts at once, which is why the section looks like it changes its mind. To Rome (§§1–3): the Church's judgment is not the ground. To the rationalist despiser (S13's profani, and behind them the Italian anti-trinitarian and libertine circles Calvin was fighting in Geneva in the 1550s): reason's proof is not the ground either. Deny both and you have to name a third thing — or the whole edifice floats. §4 names it. It is also worth seeing what the section is not: it is not an anti-intellectual reflex. Calvin was a trained humanist lawyer who published on Seneca before he published on God; the man who wrote S11 (I should not find it difficult to stop their obstreperous mouths) was not afraid of an argument.

The circularity objection, and Calvin's actual answer. The standard charge: you believe Scripture because of the Spirit and you know the Spirit from Scripture — a circle. Calvin's reply is in S15: God alone can **properly** bear witness to his own words. The claim is that ultimate authorities are necessarily self-attesting, because anything that certified God's word would thereby be higher than God's word. So the circle is not a defect in the argument; it is the shape any foundation takes when you have actually reached bottom. Whether that satisfies you is a live philosophical question — but do not mistake it for Calvin having failed to notice the problem. He notices it and plants his flag on it.

Where it goes afterwards. Straight into the confessions, almost verbatim: Belgic Confession 5 (we receive these books "not so much because the Church receives them... as because the Holy Spirit witnesses in our hearts"); Westminster 1.5, which is §4 turned into a creed — it lists the evidences (the majesty, the efficacy, the consent of all the parts) as arguments "whereby it doth abundantly evidence itself," and then: "yet notwithstanding, our full persuasion and assurance... is from the inward work of the Holy Spirit." Note the yet notwithstanding — that is S10's Still, however, three generations later. Later: Bavinck systematises it; Barth rebuilds the self-attestation of the Word in Church Dogmatics I/1–I/2 on his own very different terms; Plantinga gives it an analytic re-description as the "internal instigation of the Holy Spirit" producing warranted belief without argument.

⚠️ Guardrail — four over-readings to refuse. 1. "Calvin says reason is worthless / cannot prove anything about Scripture." S6: it is easy to establish, by evidence of various kinds. S7: they will be compelled to confess. And then he writes Chapter 8. What S10 denies is that discussion can rear up a full faith. Distinguish proving the fact from producing the certainty. 2. "The Spirit gives new revelation." S16's **the same** Spirit, and S14's choice of **testimony** over any word for fresh content. A testimony attests what is already given. I.9 will hunt the enthusiasts with exactly this distinction — Calvin is arming himself two chapters in advance. 3. "So it comes down to a private feeling, and anyone can claim it." The object of the testimony is strictly bounded (S16: that they faithfully delivered the message with which they were divinely entrusted) and it is inseparable from the word (S17: Spirit and words, one verb). A "testimony" that certified something other than the prophetic word would, on Calvin's own definition, not be this Spirit's. 4. "Then apologetics is pointless." S11 is Calvin volunteering for the debate and claiming he would win it; S18 is Calvin comforting the believer who lacks a proof at hand, not scolding him for wanting one. The evidences have a real office. They just do not sit in the foundation course.

7 — Scripture Connections –

Only one explicit citation (S17), but the section is soaked in Pauline vocabulary — and the two governing images, sealing and storm at sea, are both borrowed whole.

  1. S17 — “My Spirit that is upon thee, and my words which I have put in thy mouth, shall not depart out of thy mouth...” ← Isaiah 59:21, explicit. Calvin's interest is the compound subject under one verb: רוּחִי rûḥî (my Spirit) and דְּבָרַי dəbāray (my words), joined, shall not depart. He is not proving perpetuity; he is proving inseparability — the exact connection S16 asserted. When Calvin picks a proof text, look at its grammar first.

  2. S15, S18 — sealed / an earnest and seal ← 2 Corinthians 1:22, which supplies both nouns in one verse: ὁ καὶ σφραγισάμενος ἡμᾶς καὶ δοὺς τὸν ἀρραβῶνα τοῦ πνεύματος ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ἡμῶν — "who hath also sealed us, and given the earnest of the Spirit in our hearts." Also Ephesians 1:13–14 (ἐσφραγίσθητε τῷ πνεύματι... ὅς ἐστιν ἀρραβὼν τῆς κληρονομίας) and 2 Cor 5:5. ⚠️ Note ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ἡμῶν — in our hearts, which is S15's in the hearts of men and S16's penetrate our hearts. Calvin's location for the testimony is Paul's location for the seal. That is not a coincidence; it is a citation without quotation marks.

  3. S5, S18 — driven about in a whirl of uncertainty / tossed to and fro in a sea of doubts ← James 1:6 (ὁ γὰρ διακρινόμενος ἔοικεν κλύδωνι θαλάσσης ἀνεμιζομένῳ καὶ ῥιπιζομένῳ — "he that wavereth is like a wave of the sea driven with the wind and tossed") and Ephesians 4:14 (κλυδωνιζόμενοι καὶ περιφερόμενοι παντὶ ἀνέμῳ τῆς διδασκαλίας — KJV: "tossed to and fro, and carried about with every wind of doctrine"). Beveridge's S18 phrase is the KJV's Ephesians wording verbatim. Note that James's word for the doubter is διακρινόμενος — and S5's wavering is its standard English. The section's first and last images are the same verse.

  4. S14–S15 — the testimony of the Spirit is superior to reason ← 1 Corinthians 2:4–5 (οὐκ ἐν πειθοῖς σοφίας λόγοις ἀλλ᾽ ἐν ἀποδείξει πνεύματος καὶ δυνάμεως, ἵνα ἡ πίστις ὑμῶν μὴ ᾖ ἐν σοφίᾳ ἀνθρώπων — "not with enticing words of man's wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power: that your faith should not stand in the wisdom of men") and 2:10–14 (ψυχικὸς ἄνθρωπος οὐ δέχεται). ⚠️ ἀπόδειξις is the technical word for demonstrative proof — Paul sets a Spirit-demonstration against a wisdom-demonstration, which is precisely S14's ranking. And 2:5's purpose clause is S10's preposterous in Paul's own mouth: a faith standing on human wisdom is standing in the wrong place.

  5. S15 — God alone can properly bear witness to his own words ← 1 John 5:9 (εἰ τὴν μαρτυρίαν τῶν ἀνθρώπων λαμβάνομεν, ἡ μαρτυρία τοῦ θεοῦ μείζων ἐστίν — "the witness of God is greater") and Hebrews 6:13 (ἐπεὶ κατ᾽ οὐδενὸς εἶχεν μείζονος ὀμόσαι, ὤμοσεν καθ᾽ ἑαυτοῦ — "because he could swear by no greater, he sware by himself"). Hebrews 6:13 is the whole answer to the circularity charge in one verse, and Calvin's alone... properly is its prose form.

  6. S16 — the same Spirit... who spoke by the mouth of the prophets ← 2 Peter 1:21 (ὑπὸ πνεύματος ἁγίου φερόμενοι ἐλάλησαν ἀπὸ θεοῦ ἄνθρωποι — "holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost") and the Acts formula, by the mouth of (Acts 1:16, 3:18, 3:21, 4:25). Also the Nicene Creed: τὸ λαλῆσαν διὰ τῶν προφητῶν — "who spake by the prophets." Calvin uses the creedal phrase without flagging it, expecting recognition.

  7. S13 — desire and insist to have it proved by reason that Moses and the prophets were divinely inspired ← Luke 16:29–31. "They have Moses and the prophets; let them hear them... If they hear not Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded, though one rose from the dead." ⚠️ The verbal match (Moses and the prophets) is exact, and the doctrine is exact too: Abraham's answer to Dives is that more evidence will not fix a disposition problem. That is S7's shameless effrontery and S12's gap between winning the argument and implanting the certainty. Calvin does not cite it. He does not have to.

  8. S9 — a divine majesty which will subdue our presumptuous opposition, and force us to do it homage ← 1 Corinthians 14:24–25 (the unbeliever entering the assembly: "he is convinced of all, he is judged of all... and so falling down on his face he will worship God") and Hebrews 4:12 (ζῶν ὁ λόγος τοῦ θεοῦ καὶ ἐνεργὴς — "quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword"). Note the reversal in both: the man who came to judge ends up judged.

  9. S13 — divinely inspired ← 2 Timothy 3:16 (πᾶσα γραφὴ θεόπνευστος — "God-breathed"). Beveridge's inspired is in- + spirare, a calque of the Greek. Note that the profane men use the right word and demand the wrong warrant for it.

  10. S1 — a perfect conviction ← Colossians 2:2 / 1 Thessalonians 1:5 (πληροφορία — full assurance, being carried to fullness) and Romans 4:21 (πληροφορηθεὶς ὅτι ὃ ἐπήγγελται δυνατός ἐστιν καὶ ποιῆσαι). ⚠️ πληροφορία is built on πλήρης, full — the same full that S10 (a full faith) and S15 (full credit) turn on. Three occurrences of "full" in this section, all translating one Pauline idea. Track adjectives; they are cheaper to track than doctrines and they get you to the same place.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① 어법 — 밑줄 넷 중 어법상 틀린 것은?

The prophets and apostles ⓐ boast not their own acuteness or any qualities which win credit to speakers; ⓑ nor they dwell on reasons, but they appeal to the sacred name of God. Although the most learned men ⓒ should take the opposite side, they will be compelled to confess that the Scripture was spoken by God. But ⓓ it does not follow that we shall forthwith implant the certainty which faith requires in their hearts.

정답: ⓑ

Nor is a fronted negative adverb, so the auxiliary must precede the subject: nor do they dwell — exactly as Calvin writes it in S3. nor they dwell is impossible.

출제 의도 — ⓐ와의 대비가 전부다. ⓐ boast not looks more wrong to a modern eye (where is the do?), and half the room will pick it. It is correct: old-style post-verbal negation without do-support, and the subject The prophets and apostles sits in front of it, so it is indicative, not imperative. Then eight words later the same author writes a fronted negative and does invert. Position, not meaning, is what fires the rule: 동사 뒤 부정 → 도치 없음 / 문두 부정 → 도치 필수. ⓒ is the hypothetical should after although (Formula 3a) — correct, and looks archaic on purpose. ⓓ is it does not follow that (Formula 2③) — correct, and the students who mistrust it are usually mistrusting the logic, not the grammar.

② 내용일치 — §4의 내용과 일치하는 것은?

  1. Calvin holds that rational arguments can establish nothing whatever about the divine origin of Scripture, and that those who attempt such proofs are profane.
  2. Calvin grants that evidence can compel even able opponents to confess that Scripture was spoken by God, yet holds it back-to-front to build a full faith by discussion, since only the Spirit's inward testimony seals the word in the heart.
  3. Calvin argues that once the gainsayers' mouths have been stopped by argument, the certainty which faith requires follows in their hearts.
  4. Calvin teaches that the Spirit, being superior to reason, supplies believers with a testimony independent of the prophetic word.

정답: ②

S6–S7 (it is easy to establish, by evidence of various kinds / they will be compelled to confess) + S10 (preposterous... to rear up a full faith) + S15 (until they are sealed by the inward testimony of the Spirit). Concession, pivot, and answer — all three in Calvin's own voice.

출제 의도 — 네 개의 서로 다른 함정을 한 문제에 심었다. ① 방향 뒤집기(direction-flip) — 이 장 최대의 오독. Calvin says the exact opposite in S6 and S7, and then writes an entire chapter of such proofs (Ch. 8). Note also the smuggled second clause: profane in S13 describes those who think religion rests only on opinion, not those who offer evidences. 선지 하나에 오독 두 개를 겹쳐 심는 것이 표준 수법이다. ③ 전제/추론 혼동. This is S12 with the negation deleted: it does not follow that we shall forthwith implant the certainty. Calvin grants the premise (we do maintain the Word against gainsayers, S11–S12) and denies only the inference. it does not follow that가 나오면 부정이 어디에 걸리는지부터 확인할 것. ④ 범위 초과 + 용어 무시. S16's **the same** Spirit and S17's Isaiah — Spirit and words joined under one verb, shall not depart — make an independent testimony impossible by definition. Calvin also chose the word **testimony** (증언) rather than any word for new content precisely to block this. 이 선지는 I.9에서 칼빈이 사냥할 열광주의자의 입장이다.

③ 영작 — 오늘의 공식을 적용해 영작하시오.

(a) [Formula 1] "하나님만이 자기 말씀에 대해 본래적 의미로 증언하실 수 있는 것과 꼭 마찬가지로, 그 말씀은 성령께서 마음에 인치시기 전까지는 온전한 신뢰를 얻지 못한다." — as ... so ... + not ... until 을 모두 써서. (b) [Formula 2] "선지자들은 자기 예리함을 자랑하지 않으며, 논거를 길게 늘어놓지도 않는다. 그러나 우리가 반대자들에 맞서 말씀을 옹호한다고 해서, 그들 마음에 믿음이 요구하는 확신을 심어 주게 되는 것은 아니다." — ①·②·③ 형태를 모두 사용. (c) [Formula 3] "설령 가장 학식 있는 자들이 반대편에 선다 해도, 나는 그들에게 답하기가 어렵지 않을 것이다 — 어리석게 믿지 않기 위해서라면 증거를 구하는 것이 잘못은 아니지만."

모범답안: (a) As God alone can properly bear witness to his own words, so those words will not obtain full credit in our hearts until the Spirit seals them there. (b) The prophets boast not their own acuteness, nor do they dwell on reasons; but although we may maintain the Word against gainsayers, it does not follow that we shall implant in their hearts the certainty which faith requires. (c) Although the most learned men should take the opposite side, I should not find it difficult to answer them — though that a man may not believe foolishly, it is no fault in him to seek evidence.

채점 포인트. (a) so를 therefore로 바꿔 쓰면 0점 — 상관접속의 두 번째 다리다. 그리고 until 앞의 부정(will not obtain)이 반드시 있어야 한다. will obtain ... only when으로 바꾸면 뜻은 통해도 오늘 공식이 아니다. (b) 세 기계가 다 보여야 한다: boast not(do-support 없음) / nor do they(도치) / it does not follow that(추론 부정). nor they dwell 쓰면 즉시 0점. in their hearts는 implant 쪽에 붙인다. (c) should take(가정) 와 I should not find(=would) 가 다른 용법임을 알고 둘 다 써야 한다. 마지막 절의 that ... may not 은 목적(=lest)이며, may를 허가로 읽은 답안은 감점.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary. The socket §3 left empty is now filled: since the highest proof of Scripture is uniformly taken from the character of him whose Word it is, our conviction must be derived from a higher source than human conjectures, Judgments, or reasons; namely, the secret testimony of the Spirit — and Calvin then grants the evidences their full force (it is easy to establish, they will be compelled to confess, a divine majesty which will... force us to do it homage) only to rule it preposterous, back-to-front, to rear up a full faith by discussion, because winning an argument and implanting certainty are two different operations (it does not follow that), so that as God alone can properly bear witness to his own words, so these words will not obtain full credit in the hearts of men, until they are sealed by the inward testimony of the Spirit — the same Spirit who spoke by the prophets, an earnest and seal, without whom even worthy men are tossed to and fro in a sea of doubts.

And the three grammar points, once more:

  1. AS ... , SO ... correlative (S15) — so is the second leg of a pair, never "therefore." Bolted to not ... until, which fires three times today (S1, S15, S18) and is a door held shut, not a condition offered.
  2. Three negative machines — boast **not** (post-verbal, no do, no inversion) vs. **nor do they** dwell (fronted, inversion) vs. **it does not follow that** (negates the arrow, not the premise). The first two are eight words apart in S3.
  3. should / may are never obligation here — although... should take = even if (S7); first-person I should = would (S11); although we may maintain = concessive (S12); that they may not believe = lest (S13). And yesterday's if-deletion returns twice in one sentence at S11.

Homework (10 minutes).

① Structure restoration. Take S13 — the hardest sentence in the chapter — and rebuild it as three flat sentences, keeping every element:

Profane men think that religion rests only on opinion, and, therefore, that they may not believe foolishly, or on slight grounds, desire and insist to have it proved by reason that Moses and the prophets were divinely inspired.

Target shape: (i) Profane men hold X. (ii) They wish to avoid Y. (iii) Therefore they demand Z. — Then mark, in the original, exactly where the subject Profane men is and where its finite verbs are, count the words separating them, and say in one line why that they may not believe foolishly cannot be a second object of think.

② Composition. Write two English sentences on this content, using Formula 1 in the first and Formula 2③ in the second: "Just as a king's seal, and not the herald's eloquence, makes a letter his, so the word is not fully credited until the Spirit seals it. And although the herald may silence every objector in the square, it does not follow that he has made one man a subject." — then check your own work against S15 and S12 for the correlative and for where the negation attaches.

③ Tomorrow's preview (one line). §5 opens Let it therefore be held as fixed, that those who are inwardly taught by the Holy Spirit acquiesce implicitly in Scripture — the conclusion Calvin has been building since §1, with the doctrine's second name attached: Scripture, carrying its own evidence along with it, deigns not to submit to proofs and arguments (αὐτόπιστος, self-authenticating), and the sealing metaphor made visual — as much so as if we beheld **the divine image visibly impressed on it**. Bring Isaiah 43:10 and 54:13. And note what closes Chapter 7: none comprehend the mysteries of God save those to whom it is given.
