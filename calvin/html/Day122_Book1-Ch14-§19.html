Day 122 — Book 1, Chapter 14, §19

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday: Satan can wound but never conquer. Today: is there a Satan at all? Calvin has spent six sections on demons. Now he turns and defends the premise: that devils are real spirits, not bad feelings. This is the mirror of Day 112–115's angel-doctrine: just as the "good motions" philosophy dissolves the holy angels (S1, back-reference), the "bad affections" philosophy dissolves the devils. Short section — 12 sentences, one paragraph, and a clean shape: target S1 → method S2 → Scripture's wording S3–S7 → Scripture's punishments S8–S10 → why stop here S11–S12.

Why examiners love it. It is a list-of-evidence paragraph held together by connectors — First, In like manner (twice!), And… still more emphatically, To this corresponds, But the clearest… of all, Again — and by rhetorical questions (S4, S10). The traps are scope and logic: Calvin argues from what the words would mean if devils were mere emotions (a reductio), not from a systematic definition. Constructions: perfect participle with same subject Having… refuted… we must (S1), passages neither few nor obscure (S2), as they are called parenthetical (S3), would not the contrast be absurd if… inverted conditional question (S4), still more emphatically, that… (S5), who have degenerated from their original (S3), How absurd the expressions, that… if there were truly…? exclamatory fragment (S10), But as… while… I believe double-causal opening (S11), and lest any… should imagine (S12).

Text traps. ⚠️ nugatory (S1) = worthless, trifling. ⚠️ allege (S1) = assert (without proof). ⚠️ perturbation (S1) = disturbance of mind. ⚠️ endued (S3) = endowed. ⚠️ original (S3, noun) = origin, not "original copy". ⚠️ excruciated (S10) = tortured. ⚠️ delirious (S11) = raving. ⚠️ remiss (S12) = careless, lax.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. Having above refuted that nugatory philosophy…, we must here likewise refute those who foolishly allege that devils are nothing but bad affections… (S1) → perfect participle (same subject) + nothing but + likewise parallel. Formula 1.
  2. would not the contrast be absurd if the term devil meant nothing more than evil inspirations? (S4); How absurd the expressions, that… if there were truly no devils at all? (S10) → reductio ad absurdum: inverted would not… if… question and the exclamatory How ADJ the N, that…. Formula 2.
  3. …lest any, by embracing that errors should imagine they have no enemy and thereby be more remiss or less cautious in resisting. (S12) → lest… should V (negative purpose) + thereby + comparative pair more remiss or less cautious. Formula 3.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 12 sentences –

Having above refuted that nugatory philosophy concerning the holy angels, which teaches that they are nothing but good motions or inspirations which God excites in the minds of men, we must here likewise refute those who foolishly allege that devils are nothing but bad affections or perturbations suggested by our carnal nature. The brief refutation is to be found in passages of Scripture on this subject, passages neither few nor obscure. First, when they are called unclean spirits and apostate angels (Mt. 12:43; Jude, verse 6), who have degenerated from their original, the very terms sufficiently declare that they are not motions or affections of the mind, but truly, as they are called, minds or spirits endued with sense and intellect. In like manner, when the children of God are contrasted by John, and also by our Saviour, with the children of the devil, would not the contrast be absurd if the term devil meant nothing more than evil inspirations? And John adds still more emphatically, that the devil sinneth from the beginning (1 John 3:8). In like manner, when Jude introduces the archangel Michael contending with the devil (Jude, verse 9), he certainly contrasts a wicked and rebellious with a good angel. To this corresponds the account given in the Book of Job, that Satan appeared in the presence of God with the holy angels. But the clearest passages of all are those which make mention of the punishment which, from the Judgment of God, they already begin to feel, and are to feel more especially at the resurrection, “What have we to do with thee, Jesus, thou Son of God? art thou come hither to torment us before the time?” (Mt. 8:29); and again, “Depart, ye cursed, into everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels,” (Mt. 25:41). Again, “If God spared not the angels that sinned, but cast them down to hell, and delivered them into chains of darkness to be reserved unto Judgment,” &c. (2 Pet. 2:4). How absurd the expressions, that devils are doomed to eternal punishment, that fire is prepared for them, that they are even now excruciated and tormented by the glory of Christ, if there were truly no devils at all? But as all discussion on this subject is superfluous for those who give credit to the Word of God, while little is gained by quoting Scripture to those empty speculators whom nothing but novelty can please, I believe I have already done enough for my purpose, which was to put the pious on their guard against the delirious dreams with which restless men harass themselves and the simple. The subject, however, deserved to be touched upon, lest any, by embracing that errors should imagine they have no enemy and thereby be more remiss or less cautious in resisting.

2 — Structure at a Glance – 

[TARGET]      S1        Mirror error: devils = bad affections of our flesh (just as angels = good motions, already refuted)
[METHOD]      S2        Brief refutation: Scripture's passages — "neither few nor obscure"
[WORDING]     S3–S7     (a) names: unclean spirits, apostate angels, degenerated from their origin = minds with sense and intellect (S3)
                        (b) contrast: children of God vs children of the devil (S4); the devil sinneth from the beginning (S5)
                        (c) Michael contends with the devil (S6); Satan among the holy angels (Job) (S7)
[PUNISHMENT]  S8–S10    Clearest: they feel judgment already and will at the resurrection — Mt 8:29; Mt 25:41; 2 Pet 2:4 → absurd if no devils (S10)
[CLOSE]       S11–S12   Enough for the pious; more is wasted on novelty-lovers (S11) — but the point had to be made, lest we think we have no enemy (S12)

Examiner's Eye. - ⚠️ Target trap (S1): the people refuted now are those who say devils are bad affections of our carnal nature. The angels-as-good-motions view was refuted earlier (Having above refuted). An option swapping the two is a direction-flip. - ⚠️ Method trap (S2–S3): Calvin's argument is from Scripture's own terms, not from philosophy or experience. An option saying he proves devils exist by reason or observation is wrong. - ⚠️ Scope trap (S11): Calvin says he has done enough for his purpose — the pious are guarded. He does not claim to have convinced the speculators (little is gained by quoting Scripture to those…). - ⚠️ Purpose trap (S12): the digression exists so that no one, by embracing error, imagines he has no enemy and grows careless in resisting. It is practical (resistance), not merely doctrinal.

전체 해설 더보기

One point. The method is announced: Scripture, not speculation — and the litotes neither few nor obscure (= many and clear) is Calvin's standard way of saying the matter is settled. The second passages is an appositive; neither… nor joins two negated adjectives.

전체 해설 더보기

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — Having V-ed N, S must V …; (… those who foolishly allege that … are nothing but …) (S1)

Having  (adv)  V-ed  N ,  S  must  (here)  (likewise)  V  those  who  V  [that  X  are  nothing but  Y]
  → perfect participle = prior action, SAME subject as the main clause (not absolute)
  → `nothing but Y` = only Y (exclusion); `likewise` = parallel with the earlier refutation

⚠️ Trap. (a) Having refuted… + a different subject in the main clause = dangling participle — wrong. (b) nothing but ≠ "nothing, but…" (contrast). (c) those who = people who, not "those which." Drill. Having established that angels are real creatures, Calvin must now show that devils are too. → Having shown that the angels are creatures, we must likewise prove that devils are creatures, not metaphors.

Formula 2 — Reductio: would not S be ADJ if S' V-ed … ? and How ADJ the N, that … if there were …? (S4, S10)

Would  not  S  be  ADJ  if  S'  V-ed  nothing more than  X ?          (S4: inverted, unreal condition)
How  ADJ  the  N ,  that  S1 …,  that  S2 …,  if  there  were  no  X  at all ?   (S10: exclamatory fragment)
  → `if + past … (would)` = contrary to fact; main verb ellipted in S10

⚠️ Trap. (a) Inversion: would not the contrast be… (auxiliary before subject), not would the contrast not be. (b) if S' were/meant — past form but present reference. (c) The that-clauses in S10 are appositive to the expressions, not relative. Drill. It would be strange to call Christ "Lord" if he were only a teacher. → Would not the title "Lord" be absurd if Christ were nothing more than a teacher?

Formula 3 — lest S (by V-ing …) should V and thereby be more A or less B (S12)

S  deserved  to be  V-ed ,  lest  any ,  (by  V-ing  N) ,  should  V  [that …]  and  thereby  be  more A  or  less B  (in V-ing)
  → `lest` = so that… not (negation built in; no extra `not`); `thereby` = as a result

⚠️ Trap. (a) lest… not is wrong. (b) subject = any; verbs are should imagine + (should) be. (c) more remiss or less cautious — comparatives point in the same direction. Drill. We study the doctrine of Satan so that no one will become careless. → We study the doctrine of Satan, lest any should thereby be more careless in resisting him.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The doctrine at stake. Is there a personal devil? Calvin refuses the demythologizing move: the devil is not a personification of inner evil. §19 is the demonology's reality check — angels and devils are created spirits with intellect and will (S3), not metaphors for our psychology. Why does this matter pastorally? Because if Satan is only my own flesh, I will fight myself only — and miss that there is an enemy outside who can be resisted (S12; cf. Eph 6:12, 1 Pet 5:8).

The argument shape. A cumulative case from usage (S3–S7), crowned by a case from judgment (S8–S10): Scripture's names, contrasts, history, and punishment all presuppose persons. Then the method-limit (S11): Calvin does not think Scripture-quotes will convert the speculators — his aim is to protect the pious, not to win the debate.

Historical lineage. The "bad affections" reading is an old rationalizing move: the Sadducees denied angels and spirits (Acts 23:8, cf. Day 112); Origen's allegorizing, medieval Platonist currents, and some Renaissance humanists treated demons as psychological or natural forces; later, Enlightenment critics (and, in the 20th century, Bultmann's demythologizing) revived it. Calvin's reply anticipates the modern debate by arguing from the Bible's own idiom rather than from cosmology.

Over-reading guardrail. ⚠️ Calvin does not say that every temptation is demonic — he says demons exist, and that Scripture's language assumes it. He also says (§14–§18) that they act only by God's permission. §19 does not give a theory of how demons influence minds (cf. his reserve in S11: no curious speculation). Also: S12's by embracing that errors is a printing oddity — don't build an argument on the plural.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. S3 — Mt 12:43; Jude 6. "When the unclean spirit is gone out of a man…" (τὸ ἀκάθαρτον πνεῦμα); "the angels which kept not their first estate, but left their own habitation" (τοὺς… ἀγγέλους… μὴ τηρήσαντας τὴν ἑαυτῶν ἀρχήν). Calvin takes spirit and angel as personal designations, and ἀρχή ("first estate") as the original they degenerated from.
  2. S4 — John 8:44; Mt 13:38; 1 John 3:10. "Ye are of your father the devil" (ὑμεῖς ἐκ τοῦ πατρὸς τοῦ διαβόλου ἐστέ); "the tares are the children of the wicked one"; "In this the children of God are manifest, and the children of the devil." A father must be a person.
  3. S5 — 1 John 3:8. "The devil sinneth from the beginning" (ἀπ' ἀρχῆς ὁ διάβολος ἁμαρτάνει — present tense, continuous sin).
  4. S6 — Jude 9. "Yet Michael the archangel, when contending with the devil he disputed about the body of Moses…" (τῷ διαβόλῳ διακρινόμενος). Calvin: good angel vs. wicked angel.
  5. S7 — Job 1:6; 2:1. "There was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan came also among them." Satan appears — a being, not a stirring.
  6. S8 — Mt 8:29; Mt 25:41. "Art thou come hither to torment us before the time?" (πρὸ καιροῦ βασανίσαι ἡμᾶς); "everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels" (τὸ πῦρ τὸ αἰώνιον τὸ ἡτοιμασμένον τῷ διαβόλῳ καὶ τοῖς ἀγγέλοις αὐτοῦ). Calvin: demons know the Judge and a coming time.
  7. S9 — 2 Pet 2:4. "God spared not the angels that sinned, but cast them down to hell [ταρταρώσας — tartarōsas], and delivered them into chains of darkness, to be reserved unto judgment." The only NT use of the verb tartaroō; Calvin uses it as a narrative of judgment, not as a map of hell.
  8. S12 — (echo) Eph 6:11–12; 1 Pet 5:8. The practical end of the argument: we wrestle against real principalities; deny them and we stop resisting. Calvin's aim is vigilance, not curiosity.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① Grammar — which statement about the grammar of §19 is wrong?

(A) In S1, Having above refuted that nugatory philosophy…, we must here likewise refute… the participial phrase shares the subject of the main clause. (B) In S4, would not the contrast be absurd if the term devil meant nothing more than evil inspirations? the if-clause expresses a condition contrary to fact. (C) In S12, lest any… should imagine they have no enemy the word lest already carries a negative sense. (D) In S10, How absurd the expressions, that devils are doomed… the three that-clauses are relative clauses modifying devils.

Answer: (D). Rationale: The that-clauses are appositive to the expressions (they say what the expressions are) — not relative clauses on devils. (A) Formula 1. (B) Formula 2. (C) Formula 3.

② Comprehension — which statement is consistent with §19?

(A) Calvin argues that devils must exist because human experience of temptation cannot be explained by our carnal nature. (B) Calvin points to Scripture's own language — names, contrasts, and the punishment of devils — to show they are real spirits with intellect, and says the point is made to keep believers alert. (C) Calvin argues that the angels refuted earlier are only good motions, while devils alone are real beings. (D) Calvin claims that quoting enough Scripture will finally persuade the speculators.

Answer: (B). Rationale: (A) method-flip — the proof is Scripture's wording, not experience. (C) direction-flip — angels were defended as real; the "good motions" view was refuted. (D) scope-overreach — S11: little is gained by quoting Scripture to those speculators.

③ Composition — apply Formula 3 (lest… should… and thereby be more A or less B):

Write one sentence: Calvin treats the doctrine of devils so that no one will be careless.

Model answer: Calvin treats the doctrine of devils at length, lest any, by denying the enemy, should imagine they are safe and thereby be more careless or less watchful in resisting. Rationale: lest carries the negation (no extra not); any is the subject; thereby marks the consequence; the two comparatives point the same direction.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary: Having refuted the "good motions" view of angels, Calvin refutes the mirror view that devils are only bad affections of our flesh, arguing from Scripture's own wording — unclean spirits and apostate angels who degenerated from their origin, children of the devil, Michael's contest, Satan among the angels in Job — and above all from the punishment already begun and to come (Mt 8:29; 25:41; 2 Pet 2:4), then stops (S11), but defends the digression so that none, embracing error, imagine they have no enemy; the grammar keys are the same-subject perfect participle with nothing but (S1), the reductio would not… if… and How absurd… that… if there were… (S4, S10), and lest… should… and thereby (S12).
