Day 112 — Book 1, Chapter 14, §9

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday Calvin said "stop asking." Today he says "but this you must hold." After eight sections of restraint (don't map the ranks, don't count them, don't guess the guardian), §9 draws the bright line on the other side: angels are real, and they are ministers. There is one point, however… — however is the hinge. The curious may be told to be quiet; the doubters must be told to believe. The target is old: the Sadducees, who said "angel" just means "a movement God impresses on us" (Acts 23:8). Calvin's method is a Scripture avalanche — thousands and legions, bearing up, carrying souls, beholding the face, the Law in angels' hands, the elect like angels, Timothy's charge, Hebrews 1–2, patriarchs' visitors, Mal. 3:1 — and then the pastoral purpose: put the simple on their guard.

Why examiners love it. Fourteen sentences, one long concessive chain, a fronted inversion (To the same effect is their frequent appearance…), and three direction-flip traps: Calvin affirms the reality of angels (not their rank/number — that was §8); he calls the Sadducean view a "dream" (not a "heresy" of the Church); and Christ… is called the Angel does not mean Christ is a created angel. Plus a cluster of old-English tricks: however much… may be twisted, competently, countenance, starting up anew.

Text traps. ⚠️ restless (S1) = unquiet, agitating (not "unable to sleep"). ⚠️ countenance (S3) = support, backing (not "face"). ⚠️ gross ignorance (S3) = crude, glaring ignorance (not "total/net"). ⚠️ competently (S9) = properly, suitably (not "skillfully"). ⚠️ hospitably entertained (S12) = received as guests (not "amused"). ⚠️ ever and anon (S14) = again and again, now and then. ⚠️ simple (S14) = unlearned, unsuspecting people (not "easy").

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. There is one point, however, which though called into doubt by certain restless individuals, we ought to hold for certain—viz. that angels are ministering spirits (S1) → antecedent one point + a relative which that is fronted as the object of hold, with an embedded concession though called into doubt…. Restore: We ought to hold for certain one point, which… is called into doubt. Formula 1.
  2. However much such passages may be twisted, their meaning is plain. (S7) → however much + S + may V = no matter how much… (concessive, subjunctive-flavored may). Formula 2.
  3. To the same effect is their frequent appearance… their speaking, and consenting to be hospitably entertained. (S12) → inversion: predicate is jumps in front of the real subject (their frequent appearance…). Restore: Their frequent appearance… is to the same effect. Formula 3.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 14 sentences –

There is one point, however, which though called into doubt by certain restless individuals, we ought to hold for certain—viz. that angels are ministering spirits (Heb. 1:14); whose service God employs for the protection of his people, and by whose means he distributes his favours among men, and also executes other works. The Sadducees of old maintained, that by angels nothing more was meant than the movements which God impresses on men, or manifestations which he gives of his own power (Acts 23:8). But this dream is contradicted by so many passages of Scriptures that it seems strange how such gross ignorance could have had any countenance among the Jews. To say nothing of the passages I have already quoted, passages which refer to thousands and legions of angels, speak of them as rejoicing, as bearing up the faithful in their hands, carrying their souls to rest, beholding the face of their Father, and so forth: there are other passages which most clearly prove that they are real beings possessed of spiritual essence. Stephen and Paul say that the Law was enacted in the hands of angels. Our Saviour, moreover says that at the resurrection the elect will be like angels; that the day of Judgment is known not even to the angels; that at that time he himself will come with the holy angels. However much such passages may be twisted, their meaning is plain. In like manner, when Paul beseeches Timothy to keep his precepts as before Christ and his elect angels, it is not qualities or inspirations without substance that he speaks of, but true spirits. And when it is said, in the Epistle to the Hebrews, that Christ was made more excellent than the angels, that the world was not made subject to them, that Christ assumed not their nature, but that of man, it is impossible to give a meaning to the passages without understanding that angels are blessed spirits, as to whom such comparisons may competently be made. The author of that Epistle declares the same thing when he places the souls of believers and the holy angels together in the kingdom of heaven. Moreover, in the passages we have already quoted, the angels of children are said to behold the face of God, to defend us by their protection, to rejoice in our salvation, to admire the manifold grace of God in the Church, to be under Christ their head. To the same effect is their frequent appearance to the holy patriarchs in human form, their speaking, and consenting to be hospitably entertained. Christ, too, in consequence of the supremacy which he obtains as Mediator, is called the Angel (Mal. 3:1). It was thought proper to touch on this subject in passing, with the view of putting the simple upon their guard against the foolish and absurd imaginations which, suggested by Satan many centuries ago, are ever and anon starting up anew

2 — Structure at a Glance – 

[THESIS]    S1        Hold for certain: angels = ministering spirits (Heb 1:14); God's service for protection,
                      distribution of favours, other works
[OPPONENT]  S2–S3     Sadducees: "angels" = movements/manifestations (Acts 23:8) → a "dream", contradicted by Scripture
[PROOF A]   S4–S7     Passages already quoted + Law in angels' hands (S5) + Christ on resurrection/judgment (S6)
                      → "However much twisted, the meaning is plain" (S7)
[PROOF B]   S8–S11    Paul/Timothy (S8) · Hebrews 1–2 comparisons (S9) · souls & angels in heaven (S10)
                      · angels of children etc. (S11)
[PROOF C]   S12–S13   Appearances to patriarchs (S12) · Christ called "the Angel" (S13)
[PURPOSE]   S14       Touched in passing: guard the simple against Satan's recurring imaginations

Examiner's Eye. - ⚠️ Scope shift from §8: §8 limited ranks and numbers; §9 affirms reality and function. An option "Calvin says we know nothing certain about angels" is wrong — S1 says hold for certain. - ⚠️ Opponent trap (S2): it is the Sadducees (not the Pharisees, Manichees, or "the Jews" in general) who reduced angels to movements; S3 adds that it is strange they had any countenance among the Jews. - ⚠️ Christology trap (S13): Christ… is called the Angel rests on his supremacy as Mediator — it is not a statement that Christ is a created angel (that contradicts S9's Christ was made more excellent than the angels). - ⚠️ Detail trap (S4/S5/S6): "Law enacted in the hands of angels" = Stephen and Paul (S5); "like angels at the resurrection," "day unknown even to the angels," "come with the holy angels" = our Saviour (S6). - ⚠️ Purpose trap (S14): the section is "touched in passing," aimed at the simple and at imaginations suggested by Satan — not a full angelology.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — Fronted relative + embedded concession (S1)

There is [N], however, which (though p.p. …), S ought to V …—viz. that …
   =  S ought to V [N] (though it is p.p. …)

⚠️ Trap. (a) which is the object of the verb after the inserted though-clause, not its subject. (b) though called into doubt = though (it is) called into doubt (ellipsis of subject + be). (c) however between commas is a sentence adverb ("but"), not the concessive however much. Drill. There is one doctrine that some doubt, but we should treat it as certain. → There is one doctrine, however, which though called into doubt by some, we ought to hold for certain.

Formula 2 — However much + S + may V, main clause (S7)

However much S may V,  main clause.
   =  No matter how much S V,  main clause.

⚠️ Trap. (a) however much is a unit (degree adverb), not the contrast adverb. (b) may V here = concessive subjunctive (not permission). (c) The main clause is plain; don't search for a second verb inside the concession. Drill. No matter how much men twist these verses, the meaning is clear. → However much men may twist these verses, the meaning is clear.

Formula 3 — Inversion To the same effect is X (S12)

(Prepositional phrase) is/are [Subject].
   =  [Subject] is/are (prepositional phrase).

⚠️ Trap. (a) The subject is after the verb; find it before checking agreement. (b) The subject here is a three-item list (appearance, speaking, consenting…); older writers often let the verb agree with the nearest/first noun (is). (c) to the same effect is a predicate adjunct = "pointing the same way." Drill. The angels' visits to Abraham also point the same way. → To the same effect are the angels' visits to Abraham.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The doctrine at stake. Are angels real created persons or merely talk about God's activity? The Sadducees (Acts 23:8: "neither angel, nor spirit") are Calvin's ancient foil, but the target is recurrent: any view that dissolves angels into impulses, ideas, or natural forces. Calvin's reply is not a speculative angelology but a biblical one — a fan of texts whose grammar requires persons: ones who rejoice, behold, defend, appear, speak, eat (Gen 18), come with Christ, and are compared with him.

The argument shape. (1) Thesis with function (S1: ministering spirits, Heb 1:14). (2) Opponent named and ridiculed gently as a "dream" (S2–S3). (3) Cumulative proof (S4–S13). (4) Pastoral aim (S14). Note the discipline: S4 concedes much already shown (Day 105–111) and S7 declares the "plain" sense as the interpretive anchor. This mirrors §8: restraint beyond the Word, certainty within it.

Lineage and later reception. The reality of angels as created spirits is the Church's consensus: Augustine (City of God 11) treats "angel" as an office (messenger) and "spirit" as a nature; Aquinas argues from the same texts (ST I, q. 50). The Reformed tradition (the Belgic Confession art. 12; the Westminster Confession 3 and 8) keeps Calvin's restraint. Later critical theology (Schleiermacher, Bultmann's demythologizing) reopens exactly the Sadducean question, in modern dress. Barth (CD III/3 §51), by contrast, defends the reality of angels but with a strong ministerial, not speculative, emphasis — a generally Calvinian line.

Guardrails against over-reading. (1) §9 does not reverse §8; the reality is certain, the map is not. (2) "Christ is called the Angel" (S13) is a statement about office/mediation, not about Christ's created status. (3) "Ministering spirits" (S1) subordinates angels to God's purposes — they are instruments, not mediators of grace (§10 warns against that superstition). (4) Calvin's wording on the author of Hebrews (S10) is cautious; do not read the author of that Epistle as a denial of Pauline relation.

  11. S13 — Mal 3:1. "The messenger (Heb. malʾāk) of the covenant, whom ye delight in: behold, he shall come." Calvin takes this as Christ.

How Calvin uses Scripture: by accumulation and grammar — each text is made to say what its verbs require. Persons rejoice; movements do not. Then S7: the plain sense stands, however it is twisted.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① Grammar — one underlined part is wrong. Which?

Calvin says (A) there is one point, however, which though called into doubt by certain restless individuals, we ought to hold for certain; (B) however much such passages may be twisted, their meaning are plain; (C) to the same effect is their frequent appearance to the holy patriarchs in human form; and (D) it is impossible to give a meaning to the passages without understanding that angels are blessed spirits.

Answer: (B). Rationale: the subject of the main clause is their meaning (singular), so the verb must be is, not are. (Formula 2: the concession does not change the subject.) (A) is correct: fronted which + embedded though (Formula 1). (C) is correct: inversion with singular subject nearest (Formula 3). (D) is correct: impossible… without + V-ing.

② Comprehension — which statement is consistent with §9?

(A) Calvin holds that angels are only the movements God impresses on men, as the Sadducees taught. (B) Calvin says that because Christ is called "the Angel," Christ is one of the created angels. (C) Calvin affirms that angels are real ministering spirits whom God employs to protect his people and distribute his favours, argues from many Scripture passages that they are real beings, and says the Sadducean reduction cannot stand however the passages are twisted. (D) Calvin concludes that, since angels are real, we can now determine their ranks and numbers from Scripture.

Answer: (C). Rationale: (A) direction-flip — that is the Sadducean view Calvin calls a "dream" (S2–S3). (B) direction-flip/scope overreach — S13 grounds the title in Christ's supremacy as Mediator; S9 says Christ is "more excellent than the angels." (D) scope overreach — §8 forbade the map; §9 affirms reality and function, not rank/number. (C) combines S1, S3, S4, S7.

③ Composition — use Formula 1 and Formula 2.

Compose one English sentence (≈25 words): There is one doctrine that some people doubt, but we should treat it as certain. No matter how much they twist the texts, the meaning is clear.

Model answer: There is one doctrine, however, which though called into doubt by some, we ought to hold for certain; for however much the texts may be twisted, their meaning is plain. Examiner's intent: fronted relative with embedded though + however much… may V + singular subject agreement. Alternative accepted (Formula 3): To the same effect is the testimony of the patriarchs.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary: Calvin insists we hold for certain that angels are ministering spirits (Heb 1:14) — real beings God uses to protect his people, distribute his favours, and carry out other works — refuting the Sadducean reduction of angels to "movements" with a cumulative scriptural proof (the Law in angels' hands, Christ's words, Paul to Timothy, Hebrews' comparisons, patriarchal visits, and Christ as "the Angel"), and says he touches the subject in passing to guard the simple against Satan's recurring imaginations; the grammar keys are the fronted relative with embedded concession (S1), however much… may V (S7), and the inversion To the same effect is… (S12).
