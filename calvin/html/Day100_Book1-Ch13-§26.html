Day 100 — Book 1, Chapter 13, §26

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday: Servetus, the heretic who dissolved the Persons into appearances. Today: the heretic who keeps the Persons and quietly demotes two of them. Look at the opening sentence — Calvin calls this a monster that crawled out of the same pool. Same swamp, opposite species. Servetus said: no real Three. These men say: three, yes — but only One of them is really God. That is the oldest trick in the book (Arius, 4th century) in new dress: grant the word Trinity, deny the equality.

The enemy word is essentiator — one who gives essence. In their system the Father hands over Godhead to the Son and Spirit "when he formed them." Underline the verb transfused (poured across). Calvin's whole answer is one question: if the Godhead is poured over from Father to Son, what exactly did the Son have before it arrived? Watch him close every exit: the essence is cut in pieces → impious; given whole → no distinction left; withheld → a God in name only.

Second — it is a LOGIC lesson, and the logic runs on modal verbs. ⚠️ must be deemed · must be conceded · cannot differ … unless he have · it were impious · there would be a rending. Each one is a trap door in the opponent's floor. If you read must as duty instead of deduction, you lose the argument.

Third — printing traps. ⚠️ the Fathers (S9: a stray s before a line break — sense: the Father), essences (S23: should be essence,), abstracte (S25: should be abstracted), and Ex. 3:4 (S39: Exodus 3:14 — "I AM hath sent me unto you"). We quote what is printed and flag each.

Today's 3 Big Points

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 39 sentences –

This pool has bred another monster not unlike the former. For certain restless spirits, unwilling to share the disgrace and obloquy of the impiety of Servetus, have confessed that there were indeed three Persons, but added, as a reason, that the Father, who alone is truly and properly God, transfused his Divinity into the Son and Spirit when he formed them. Nor do they refrain from expressing themselves in such shocking terms as these: that the Father is essentially distinguished from the Son and Spirit by this; that he is the only essentiator. Their first pretext for this is, that Christ is uniformly called the Son of God. From this they infer, that there is no proper God but the Father. But they forget, that although the name of God is common also to the Son, yet it is sometimes, by way of excellence, ascribed to the Father, as being the source and principle of Divinity; and this is done in order to mark the simple unity of essence. They object, that if the Son is truly God, he must be deemed the Son of a person: which is absurd. I answer, that both are true; namely, that he is the Son of God, because he is the Word, begotten of the Father before all ages; (for we are not now speaking of the Person of the Mediator), and yet, that for the purpose of explanation, regard must be had to the Person, so that the name God may not be understood in its absolute sense, but as equivalent to Father. For if we hold that there is no other God than the Fathers this rank is clearly denied to the Son. In every case where the Godhead is mentioned, we are by no means to admit that there is an antithesis between the Father and the Son, as if to the former only the name of God could competently be applied. For assuredly, the God who appeared to Isaiah was the one true God, and yet John declares that he was Christ (Isa. 6; John 12:41). He who declared, by the mouth of Isaiah, that he was to be “for a stone of stumbling” to the Jews, was the one God; and yet Paul declares that he was Christ (Isa. 8:14; Rom. 9:33). He who proclaims by Isaiah, “Unto me every knee shall bow,” is the one God; yet Paul again explains that he is Christ (Isa. 45:23; Rom. 14:11). To this we may add the passages quoted by an Apostle, “Thou, Lord, hast laid the foundations of the earth;” “Let all the angels of God worship him,” (Heb. 1:10; 10:6; Ps. 102:26; 97:7). All these apply to the one God; and yet the Apostle contends that they are the proper attributes of Christ. There is nothing in the cavil, that what properly applies to God is transferred to Christ, because he is the brightness of his glory. Since the name of Jehovah is everywhere applied to Christ, it follows that, in regard to Deity, he is of himself. For if he is Jehovah, it is impossible to deny that he is the same God who elsewhere proclaims by Isaiah, “I am the first, and I am the last; and beside me there is no God,” (Is. 44:6). We would also do well to ponder the words of Jeremiah, “The gods that have not made the heavens and the earth, even they shall perish from the earth and from under these heavens,” (Jer. 10:11); whence it follows conversely, that He whose divinity Isaiah repeatedly proves from the creation of the world, is none other than the Son of God. And how is it possible that the Creator, who gives to all should not be of himself, but should borrow his essence from another? Whosoever says that the Son was essentiated by the Father, denies his selfexistence. Against this, however, the Holy Spirit protests, when he calls him Jehovah. On the supposition, then, that the whole essence is in the Father only, the essence becomes divisible, or is denied to the Son, who, being thus robbed of his essences will be only a titular God. If we are to believe these triflers, divine essence belongs to the Father only, on the ground that he is sole God, and essentiator of the Son. In this way, the divinity of the Son will be something abstracte from the essence of God, or the derivation of a part from the whole. On the same principle it must also be conceded, that the Spirit belongs to the Father only. For if the derivation is from the primary essence which is proper to none but the Father, the Spirit cannot justly be deemed the Spirit of the Son. This view, however, is refuted by the testimony of Paul, when he makes the Spirit common both to Christ and the Father. Moreover, if the Person of the Father is expunged from the Trinity, in what will he differ from the Son and Spirit, except in being the only God? They confess that Christ is God, and that he differs from the Father. If he differs, there must be some mark of distinction between them. Those who place it in the essence, manifestly reduce the true divinity of Christ to nothing, since divinity cannot exist without essence, and indeed without entire essence. The Father certainly cannot differ from the Son, unless he have something peculiar to himself, and not common to him with the Son. What, then, do these men show as the mark of distinction? If it is in the essence, let them tell whether or not he communicated essence to the Son. This he could not do in part merely, for it were impious to think of a divided God. And besides, on this supposition, there would be a rending of the Divine essence. The whole entire essence must therefore be common to the Father and the Son; and if so, in respect of essence there is no distinction between them. If they reply that the Father, while essentiating, still remains the only God, being the possessor of the essence, then Christ will be a figurative God, one in name or semblance only, and not in reality, because no property can be more peculiar to God than essence, according to the words, “I Am hath sent me unto you,” (Ex. 3:4).

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — Fronted negative + opponent-reporting that-clauses + although … yet.

Nor + AUX + S + V …                       (Nor do they refrain from V-ing)
such N as these: [that …]; [that …]        (colon → the opponent's own words)
They object, that …  /  I answer, that …   (question–answer pair)
although [concession], yet [Calvin's point]   (correlative; yet ≠ however)

⚠️ Traps. (a) Nor do they — after a fronted negative the auxiliary comes BEFORE the subject. Nor they do ✗. (b) Every that-clause after such terms as these: / They object, / they infer, is the OPPONENT's claim. Do not attribute it to Calvin. (c) although … yet is a pair: the second clause is the main point. Drill: Render in Calvin's English: "그들은 아들이 하나님이라고 고백하면서도, 아버지만이 참 하나님이라고 덧붙였다." → They have confessed that the Son is God, but have added, as a reason, that the Father alone is truly God. · "They do not refrain from such terms" → fronted negative: Nor do they refrain from such terms.

Formula 2 — Logical modals in a reductio.

must be + p.p.     = logical necessity (the only conclusion)     (must be deemed / must be conceded)
there must be N    = there has to be … (deduction)                (there must be some mark)
cannot V … unless S + base V (subjunctive)  = only if             (cannot differ … unless he have)
it were + ADJ + to V  = it would be …  (old counterfactual)       (it were impious to think of …)
there would be N   = consequence of a supposition                 (there would be a rending)

⚠️ Traps. (a) must here is deduction, not duty: the Spirit must be conceded to belong to the Father only = it follows that …. (b) unless he have = unless he has — present subjunctive, not an error; modern writers say has. (c) it were impious ≠ it was impious; were = would be. (d) cannot … unless = can only if. Drill: "그 본질이 부분적으로만 나뉜다면 그것은 불경건한 일이다." → It were impious to think that the essence is divided in part. · The Son cannot be God unless he have the whole essence.

Formula 3 — The "it follows" proof frames.

Since P, it follows that Q.                  (Since the name of Jehovah is everywhere applied to Christ, it follows that …)
Whosoever V1 … that …, V2 …                 (Whosoever says that …, denies …)
On the supposition that P, Q becomes R.      (On the supposition … the essence becomes divisible)
If they reply that P, then Q, because R.     (If they reply …, then Christ will be …, because …)
no X can be more ADJ to Y than Z             (negative comparative = superlative)

⚠️ Traps. (a) Whosoever takes a singular verb — Whosoever says, not say. (b) Since = because (causal), not since then. (c) no property can be more peculiar to God than essence = essence is the MOST characteristic of God's attributes. (d) In If they reply that A, then B the then clause is the conclusion Calvin draws; the if clause is the opponents' last move. Drill: "아들의 본질이 아버지에게서 주어졌다고 말하는 자는 누구든 아들의 자존성을 부인하는 것이다." → Whosoever says that the Son received his essence from the Father denies his selfexistence. · No attribute can be more peculiar to God than essence.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The Italians in Geneva. This paragraph answers a live controversy. In the late 1550s the Italian refugee congregation in Geneva produced anti-Trinitarians — notably Giorgio Biandrata, Giovanni Paolo Alciati and Valentino Gentile — who professed three Persons but taught that the Father alone is the essentiator, who bestows his essence on the Son and Spirit. Calvin names no one in this paragraph (and the 1559 edition appeared as the controversy began), so we read it as an answer to the doctrine, not to a particular man. Later he wrote against Gentile (the Impietas Valentini Gentilis, 1561); Gentile was executed at Bern in 1566. ⚠️ Stay with the argument. Our job is not to judge the persons but to follow the logic Calvin gives.

Footnote 47 and the 1554 Defensio. The printed footnote refers to Calvin's Defensio orthodoxae fidei de sacra Trinitate (1554), his answer to Servetus; it belongs to the previous section's context (the pool) and shows Beveridge's cross-reference habit.

The Nicene shape of the answer. S6's source and principle of Divinity is the classical monarchia of the Father (the Eastern pēgē theotētos; the Creed's "God of God, Light of Light"). The Father is the fons of the Persons — order, not rank of essence. S8's begotten … before all ages is Nicene: begotten, not made. The opponents' transfused … when he formed them (S2) puts the Son on the made side of that line: that is why Calvin's reply is so firm.

The autotheos debate. S17 (he is of himself) and S21 (denies his selfexistence) are the loci for Calvin's doctrine that the Son is autotheos — God of himself — as to Deity. Roman Catholic and later Reformed/Remonstrant discussion has argued over whether this fits the Nicene "God of God." The Reformed answer (Calvin; later Turretin and others): the essence is not derived, the person is begotten. Warfield's 1909 essay on Calvin's doctrine of the Trinity is the standard modern discussion. ⚠️ Don't claim Calvin denies eternal generation; S8 affirms it.

The opponents' best texts (not cited in this paragraph). John 17:3 (the only true God), 1 Cor 8:6 (one God, the Father), John 5:26 (so hath he given to the Son to have life in himself). Calvin answers them in the following section (our §27), where the relative use of God for the Father is developed further; S6 and S8 are the first answer.

Reception. The aseity argument shaped later Reformed Trinitarianism (WCF 2.3: "In the unity of the Godhead there be three persons, of one substance, power, and eternity … the Father is of none, neither begotten nor proceeding; the Son is eternally begotten of the Father; the Holy Ghost eternally proceeding"). Note the creed's wording: person derived, essence common. Barth (CD I/1 §9) and later social Trinitarians read Calvin's essence-language differently; they are readers, not sources for this paragraph.

⚠️ Over-reading guardrails (5). (1) Calvin does not deny the Father's pre-eminence of order (S6). (2) He does not deny that the Son is begotten (S8). (3) He does not deny that the Father differs from the Son (S31–S33). (4) S17 is not Sabellian; the Persons are distinct. (5) The monster is a doctrine; do not read Calvin's tone as a verdict on every Italian refugee.
