Day 120 — Book 1, Chapter 14, §17

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday: the devil's malice is depravation, not creation. Today: so is he a rival god? No. §17 is the chapter's answer to every dualism. Calvin grants the language of war — Satan "resists God" — then puts the war on a leash. Five Bible scenes (Job, Ahab, Saul, Egypt, Paul) pile up into one verdict: he cannot move without permission.

Why examiners love it. Twelve sentences, one theme, and the whole thing is a lesson in scope and polarity. Constructions: an opening absolute-qualifier frame (With regard to N, it must be understood with this qualification, that…, S1), a dares not… until limit (S2), a result clause so… that (S7), a concessive though we say… we at the same time maintain (S8), the sharp not X, but only Y (S9), an absolute clause the disposition of the devil being wicked (S10), a This much… he has… that… fronting (S11) and a final But as… , he executes… only… and thus, however unwilling, obeys (S12). The traps: Calvin does not say Satan wants to obey, and he does not say Satan is a puppet with no malice.

Text traps. ⚠️ qualification (S1) = limiting condition. ⚠️ authorised (S2) = given permission. ⚠️ undertook (S3) = took on the task. ⚠️ commissioned (S3) = given a mission. ⚠️ scourge (S4) = whip, instrument of punishment. ⚠️ instrumentality (S5) = agency. ⚠️ at variance with (S8) = in conflict with. ⚠️ contrariety (S8) = opposition. ⚠️ contumacy (S10) = stubborn defiance. ⚠️ of set purpose (S11) = deliberately. ⚠️ fettered (S12) = shackled.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. …it must be understood with this qualification, that Satan cannot possibly do anything against the will and consent of God. (S1) → fronted With regard to N + passive is said to V + it must be understood… that-clause + cannot possibly do anything against. Formula 1.
  2. …Satan is under the power of God, and is so ruled by his authority, that he must yield obedience to it. (S7) → so… that result clause inside a coordinated predicate. Formula 2.
  3. …he executes those things only for which permission has been given him, and thus, however unwilling, obeys his Creator, being forced… to do Him service. (S12) → only for which (stranded-preposition relative) + however + adj concession + participle being forced. Formula 3.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 12 sentences –

With regard to the strife and war which Satan is said to wage with God, it must be understood with this qualification, that Satan cannot possibly do anything against the will and consent of God. For we read in the history of Job, that Satan appears in the presence of God to receive his commands, and dares not proceed to execute any enterprise until he is authorised. In the same way, when Ahab was to be deceived, he undertook to be a lying spirit in the mouth of all the prophets; and on being commissioned by the Lord, proceeds to do so. For this reason, also, the spirit which tormented Saul is said to be an evil spirit from the Lord, because he was, as it were, the scourge by which the misdeeds of the wicked king were punished. In another place it is said that the plagues of Egypt were inflicted by God through the instrumentality of wicked angels. In conformity with these particular examples, Paul declares generally that unbelievers are blinded by God, though he had previously described it as the doing of Satan. It is evident, therefore, that Satan is under the power of God, and is so ruled by his authority, that he must yield obedience to it. Moreover, though we say that Satan resists God, and does works at variance with His works, we at the same time maintain that this contrariety and opposition depend on the permission of God. I now speak not of Satan’s will and endeavour, but only of the result. For the disposition of the devil being wicked, he has no inclination whatever to obey the divine will, but, on the contrary, is wholly bent on contumacy and rebellion. This much, therefore, he has of himself, and his own iniquity, that he eagerly, and of set purpose, opposes God, aiming at those things which he deems most contrary to the will of God. But as God holds him bound and fettered by the curb of his power, he executes those things only for which permission has been given him, and thus, however unwilling, obeys his Creator, being forced, whenever he is required, to do Him service.

2 — Structure at a Glance – 

[THESIS]      S1        Satan's "war" with God has a qualification: he can do nothing against God's will and consent
[PROOF 1]     S2–S3     Job: appears before God to receive commands, dares not act until authorised / Ahab: a lying spirit only when commissioned
[PROOF 2]     S4–S6     Saul's evil spirit "from the Lord" (scourge) / Egypt's plagues via wicked angels / Paul: unbelievers blinded by God (yet Satan's doing)
[CONCLUSION]  S7        Satan is under God's power and so ruled by his authority that he must obey
[CLARIFY]     S8–S9     "resists God" is true, but the opposition depends on God's permission — I speak of the result, not Satan's will
[TWO LEVELS]  S10–S11   will: wicked, no inclination to obey, eager and deliberate opposition (his own iniquity)
[RESULT]      S12       God holds him bound and fettered: he does only what is permitted, and thus, however unwilling, obeys his Creator

Examiner's Eye. - ⚠️ Two-levels trap (S9–S12): Satan's will is rebellious (S10–S11); the result is under God (S12). An option saying Satan wants to obey God, or that God forces him to want good, is wrong. An option saying Satan's malice is absent is wrong too. - ⚠️ Scope trap (S1/S7): cannot possibly do anything against the will and consent — all his actions. An option limiting God's control to some of Satan's acts is wrong. - ⚠️ Source trap (S2–S6): Job (Satan receives commands), Ahab (lying spirit), Saul (evil spirit from the Lord), Egypt (wicked angels), Paul (blinded by God). Five witnesses, not one. - ⚠️ Polarity trap (S6): Paul says God blinds, after saying Satan blinds — Calvin reads the two as not competing: Satan is the instrument. - ⚠️ Direction trap (S8): depend on the permission of God — the dependency runs from Satan to God, not the reverse.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — With regard to N, it must be understood with this qualification, that S cannot possibly V (S1)

With regard to  N (which S is said to V) ,  it  must be understood  with this qualification ,  that  S  cannot possibly  V  anything  against ...
  → fronted topic phrase + formal `it` + `that`-clause (real subject) + emphatic negative `cannot possibly`

⚠️ Trap. (a) is said to wage ≠ denial; it accepts the idiom, then limits it. (b) it is formal; the that-clause carries the content. (c) cannot possibly = emphatic cannot at all; don't drop possibly in a rewrite. (d) with this qualification = limit, not quality. Drill. As for the war the Church is said to wage against sin, we must hold this limit: she cannot overcome it without the grace of God. → With regard to the war which the Church is said to wage against sin, it must be understood with this qualification, that she cannot possibly overcome it without the grace of God.

Formula 2 — S is so ruled by X, that he must V / though we say A, we at the same time maintain B (S7, S8)

S  is  under  N ,  and  is  so  V-ed  by  N ,  that  he  must  V .          (result)
though  we  say  that  A ,  we  at the same time  maintain  that  B .       (concession)
  → `so… that` result clause + concessive `though… we maintain`

⚠️ Trap. (a) The second that in S7 is result, tied to so, not another evident that. (b) though… we maintain concedes the wording, then limits it; don't reverse which clause is the main claim. (c) at the same time = both claims hold together. (d) depend on — direction of dependence: the opposition depends on the permission. Drill. It is clear that the sea is so bound by God's command that it cannot cross the shore. → It is evident that the sea is under the command of God, and is so bound by his word, that it cannot pass its appointed limit.

Formula 3 — he V-s those things only for which N has been given him, and thus, however ADJ, V2-s, being V-ed … to V3 (S12, S10)

S  V1-s  those things  only  for which  N  has been given him ,  and  thus ,  however  ADJ ,  V2-s  N2 ,  being  V-ed ... ,  to  V3
  → `only` + fronted-preposition relative `for which` + `however + adj` concession + participle `being V-ed`

⚠️ Trap. (a) only modifies the whole relative phrase — the actions are exactly the permitted ones. (b) however unwilling = however unwilling he is (verb omitted). (c) The absolute clause in S10 (the disposition of the devil being wicked) has its own subject; it is not a dangling participle. (d) Capital Him = God. Drill. Because the Lord restrains the wicked, they do only what he permits, and so, however reluctant, they serve his purpose. → But as the Lord holds the wicked in check, they do those things only for which permission has been given them, and thus, however reluctant, serve his purpose.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The doctrine at stake. Chapter 14 has just refuted idolatry and Manicheism; §16 said evil in the devil is depravation, not creation. §17 closes the circle: the devil is also not a rival power. The doctrine is divine sovereignty over evil spirits — the groundwork for Calvin's long treatment of providence in Book 1, chapters 16–18, whose chapter 18 (God thus turning the unclean spirits hither and thither at his pleasure…, the next printed section) picks up exactly where S12 stops.

The argument shape. Thesis (S1) → Scripture scenes (S2–S6) → conclusion (S7) → clarification (S8–S9) → two levels (S10–S12). Notice the distinction Calvin guards: will vs result. Satan's will is entirely his own and entirely evil; the outcome is God's.

Historical lineage. Augustine already taught that evil wills cannot act beyond God's permission and that God uses evil wills well (e.g., Enchiridion 95–100). Calvin's Job-1 reading follows a long line, and his talk of the devil as a scourge echoes the common patristic image of Satan as God's executioner. Medieval theology spoke of God permitting evil; Calvin uses permission here but in Book 1.18 he warns that "permission" must not mean an idle God — so read S8 with that caution, not as a sudden retreat.

Later reception. Reformed orthodoxy (e.g., Turretin, Bavinck) keeps the pair: Satan's real malice and God's real rule, with God neither author nor approver of the sin. Modern readers sometimes find the "evil spirit from the Lord" texts harsh; Calvin's answer is the two-level structure (S9–S12), not a softening of the texts.

Guardrails against over-reading. (1) §17 says God is sovereign over Satan; it does not here explain how God rules the evil will without being its author — S9–S12 only distinguishes will from result; more comes in §18 and in Book 1.16–18. (2) permission and consent (S1, S8) are Calvin's words for the limit, not a claim that God is merely a spectator. (3) The Pauline reference in S6 is Calvin's summary of a pair of texts — check the exact verses (see Period 7) before quoting it. (4) The Ahab and Saul scenes are narratives; Calvin reads them as examples of a rule, not as a full account of how spirits operate.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. S1–S2 — Job 1:6–12; 2:1–6. Satan "came also among them" before the LORD; God sets the limit: "all that he hath is in thy power; only upon himself put not forth thine hand" (1:12); "behold, he is in thine hand; but save his life" (2:6). The gate behind dares not proceed… until he is authorised.
  2. S3 — 1 Kgs 22:19–23 (2 Chr 18:18–22). "I will go forth, and I will be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets… Go forth, and do so." Calvin's undertook… on being commissioned… proceeds follows the order of offer → commission.
  3. S4 — 1 Sam 16:14; 18:10; 19:9. "An evil spirit from the LORD troubled him." Calvin's scourge = the punishing role.
  4. S5 — Ps 78:49 (Exod 7–12). "He cast upon them the fierceness of his anger… by sending evil angels among them." The plagues' agent is God; the means are wicked angels.
  5. S6 — 2 Cor 4:4; 2 Thess 2:11 (cf. Rom 11:8). "The god of this world hath blinded the minds of them which believe not" (Satan); "God shall send them strong delusion" (God). Calvin reads the two as compatible: Satan is God's instrument. (Verify before quoting — the printed footnote 64 is not reproduced here.)
  6. S7 — Eph 1:11; Mark 5:12–13; Luke 22:31. God "worketh all things after the counsel of his own will"; the demons "besought him" for leave to enter the swine; Satan "hath desired to have you, that he may sift you" — each shows a request and a limit. (These three are Calvin's wider pattern, not quoted in §17.)
  7. S8–S12 — Jas 2:19; Matt 8:29. "The devils also believe, and tremble"; "Art thou come hither to torment us before the time?" — demons know God's authority and fear it, unwilling yet submitted (cf. however unwilling).

How Calvin uses Scripture: he picks scenes of commission (Job, Ahab, Saul) and lets the narrative rhythm — asks, receives, goes — carry the doctrine; then Paul generalises.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① Grammar — which statement about the grammar of §17 is wrong?

(A) In S1, it in it must be understood with this qualification, that… is a formal subject, and the that-clause supplies the content. (B) In S7, is so ruled by his authority, that he must yield obedience to it contains a result clause, because the second that is paired with so. (C) In S10, For the disposition of the devil being wicked, he has… is an absolute construction meaning since the devil's disposition is wicked. (D) In S12, those things only for which permission has been given him means that he does some things for which permission has been given and also other things for which it has not.

Answer: (D). Rationale: only restricts the whole relative phrase — he does exactly the permitted things; (D) reverses the scope. (A) Formula 1. (B) Formula 2. (C) Formula 3 / S10.

② Comprehension — which statement is consistent with §17?

(A) Calvin says Satan has no real malice, because God controls all his actions and so Satan is not responsible for what he does. (B) Calvin says that Satan's war against God is real in his will and aim, but he can do nothing against God's will and consent: the Job and Ahab scenes, Saul's spirit, Egypt's plagues and Paul show him under God's power, so he does only what is permitted and thus, however unwilling, obeys his Creator. (C) Calvin says that Satan's opposition is independent of God, so that God merely watches and responds after the fact. (D) Calvin says that Satan, when commissioned, willingly cooperates with God's purposes and has the inclination to obey the divine will.

Answer: (B). Rationale: (A) scope-cut — S10–S11: his will is wicked, eager and deliberate. (C) direction-flip — S8: the opposition depend[s] on the permission of God. (D) direction-flip — S10: no inclination whatever to obey; S12: however unwilling.

③ Composition — use Formula 2 and Formula 3.
