Day 82 — Book 1, Chapter 13, §8

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday Calvin proved the Word is God. Today he proves the Word never began. And look at how he opens — it is one of the most quotable sentences in the whole chapter: Here an outcry is made by certain men, who, while they dare not openly deny his divinity, secretly rob him of his eternity. Underline while and underline secretly. That single sentence tells you the entire shape of the section: the opponent is not a frontal attacker. He is a thief. He leaves the word divine in place and walks off with the word eternal. ⚠️ And Calvin's judgement on that is not "they are wrong" but Nothing, therefore, is **more intolerable** (S6). Concede deity, steal eternity — that is the crime being tried today.

Now look at the architecture, because it is unusually clean. Two arguments against the objection, in two movements. Movement one, S2–S6: if the Word began, then God changed — and God cannot change, James 1:17. Movement two, S7–S15: the opponent's Genesis inference is a non-sequitur — manifestation is not origination. And the hinge between them is six words long: **This is the merest trifling.** (S8). One short flat sentence in the middle of a section of long ones. That is not carelessness. That is contempt, deployed as punctuation.

And notice what yesterday planted. §7's last sentence described the Word as being **liable to no variation** — I told you to watch it. Today that word is cashed out as a proof-text: with whom is **no variableness**, neither shadow of turning (James 1:17, S5). Calvin seeds tomorrow's evidence in today's last clause, then harvests it. Same with manifested: §7 S6 said as Christ was **not yet manifested**, and today S9 turns that very word into the logical principle that destroys the objection: because a thing begins to be **manifested** at a certain time, it never existed previously — no, it does not follow.

Grammatically, today is the day of negation and inversion. Count them: they **dare not** openly deny (S1), piety **does not** recognise or admit **any** name (S4), would **cease to be** true (S5), **Nothing** … is **more intolerable** (S6), **till then no** Word existed (S7), It does **not** surely follow … it **never** existed previously (S9), it was **without** beginning (S12), **No** certain period of time is defined (S13), **Nor is this omitted** by John (S14). Nine sentences out of sixteen carry a negative, and one of them inverts. ⚠️ Every one of those is a place where a careless reader flips the direction of the argument, and the examiner knows it.

One warning about the page. Two page-break seams fall inside words (di-|vinity S1, after-|wards S6), and one word-boundary hyphen in S11 is a scan artifact (the Word-was). Details in the text note. No running headers or margin numbers fall inside this section — it sits clean on a single page.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. The counterfactual with a past-tense protasis: if S V-ed …, S' would V — and the delayed appositive that-clause, three times. **For if** any thing adventitious **took place**, the saying of James **would cease** to be true, **that** "every good gift…" (S5). ⚠️⚠️ Two things at once. (i) took place is not a past narrative — it is the past subjunctive of an open hypothesis: if anything adventitious were to take place. The clue is the apodosis: **would** cease, not ceased. Protasis past + apodosis would = counterfactual, not history. Yesterday's it were absurd was the same subjunctive wearing a different coat; today it wears the ordinary past. (ii) Then the appositive: **the saying of James** would cease to be true, **that** "every good gift…" — the that-clause is in apposition to the saying, held back behind the whole predicate. Restore: the saying of James that "every good gift…" would cease to be true. ⚠️ Comma + that after an abstract noun = apposition, not a relative, and not a complement of true. Same shape twice more today, both with verbs of inferring: It does not surely follow, **that** because… (S9) and We, therefore, again conclude, **that** the Word was eternally begotten (S15). Third day running for this pattern. Own it. Formula 1.
  2. Negation and its scope: where the not actually bites. ⚠️⚠️⚠️ Today's central sentence is S9, and it is a trap built out of one adverb: It does **not surely** follow, that because a thing begins to be manifested at a certain time, it **never** existed previously. (i) The negation attaches to follow — to the inference, not to its content. Calvin is not saying the thing never existed; he is saying that conclusion does not follow. (ii) surely = certainly, and Beveridge parks it inside the negation; read it as it certainly does not follow, not as the modern it does not necessarily follow (S10's I draw a **very different** conclusion settles it — he is not hedging, he is reversing). (iii) And the frame itself is a formula worth memorising: **it does not follow, that because** A, **B** = A does not entail B. Then stack the family: Nothing … is **more intolerable than to** fancy (S6) — a negative superlative, the most intolerable thing of all is to fancy; **No** certain period of time **is defined** (S13) — the negation is on certain, i.e. no definite period is specified, not no time at all; and it was **without** beginning (S12). Formula 2.
  3. Negative inversion, and the as … so proportional for the second day running. **Nor is this** omitted by John (S14) ⚠️⚠️ — a fronted negative adverb forces subject–auxiliary inversion: Nor **is this** omitted, never Nor this is omitted. Restore: And John does not omit this either. The same rule governs never have I, not only did he, scarcely had we. This is Exam Problem ①. And beside it, the big correlative of S4: **as** the names of God … began to be ascribed to him…, **so** piety does not recognise or admit any name which might indicate that a change had taken place. ⚠️ Do not read the as-clause as because; it is the proportional — just as A holds, so B holds — and here it carries a concession inside it: Calvin grants the first half in order to bound the second. Yesterday's S19 was the same correlative doing the same job. Formula 3.

Trap words I will test. temerity (S3) ⚠️⚠️⚠️ = Lat. temeritas, reckless rashness — with excessive temerity = with outrageous recklessness. It is the opposite of timidity, which is exactly the word your eye wants. adventitious (S5) ⚠️⚠️⚠️ = coming from outside, accidentally added (ad- + venire, to come to) — if any thing adventitious took place = if anything were added to God from outside. Nothing to do with adventure. This is the technical word of the whole section: an adventitious property is one God did not always have. piety (S4) ⚠️⚠️ = godly reverence, right religion (pietas) — here nearly the religious mind itself; piety does not recognise or admit = reverence refuses to allow. Not sentimentality. fancy (S6) ⚠️⚠️ = a verb: to imagine, to suppose — to fancy a beginning = to imagine a beginning. Not a noun, not a whim, not liking. intimate (S7) ⚠️⚠️⚠️ = a verb: to indicate, to hint, to make known (intimare < intimus, innermost) — must be held to intimate = must be taken to imply. Not the adjective. merest (S8) ⚠️⚠️ = superlative of mere: nothing but, pure and simple — the merest trifling = sheer frivolity, nothing but trifling. Not "smallest." trifling (S8) = here a noun/gerund, frivolous talk — yesterday (§7 S13) the same word was an adjective. acutely (S7) = sharply, shrewdly (acutus, sharpened) — and note it is ironic: they **think** they argue acutely. energy (S11) ⚠️⚠️ = Gk ἐνέργεια, active operation, working — the Word's power in act, not vigour or pep. exerted (S11) = put forth into action (ex- + serere, to put forth). contend (S2) = maintain in argument (con- + tendere, to strain). outcry (S1) = clamour, protest (clamor). have respect to (S4) = have reference to, relate to — not "respect" in the modern sense. descends to (S14) ⚠️ = comes down to (a topic) in the order of his narrative — not spatial, not condescension. from everlasting (S15) = from eternity (KJV mē'ōlām). variableness (S5) = changeableness (KJV for παραλλαγή). sacred mouth (S2) = os sacrum — Calvin using the opponent's own pious phrasing against him.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 16 sentences –

8. Here an outcry is made by certain men, who, while they dare not openly deny his divinity, secretly rob him of his eternity. For they contend that the Word only began to be when God opened his sacred mouth in the creation of the world. Thus, with excessive temerity, they imagine some change in the essence of God. For as the names of God, which have respect to external work, began to be ascribed to him from the existence of the work (as when he is called the Creator of heaven and earth), so piety does not recognise or admit any name which might indicate that a change had taken place in God himself. For if any thing adventitious took place, the saying of James would cease to be true, that “every good gift, and every perfect gift, is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning,” (James 1:17). Nothing, therefore, is more intolerable than to fancy a beginning to that Word which was always God, and afterwards was the Creator of the world. But they think they argue acutely, in maintaining that Moses, when he says that God then spoke for the first time, must be held to intimate that till then no Word existed in him. This is the merest trifling. It does not surely follow, that because a thing begins to be manifested at a certain time, it never existed previously. I draw a very different conclusion. Since at the very moment when God said, “Let there be light,” the energy of the Word-was immediately exerted, it must have existed long before. If any inquire how long, he will find it was without beginning. No certain period of time is defined, when he himself says, “Now O Father, glorify thou me with thine own self with the glory which I had with thee before the world was,” (John 17:5). Nor is this omitted by John: for before he descends to the creation of the world, he says, that “in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God.” We, therefore, again conclude, that the Word was eternally begotten by God, and dwelt with him from everlasting. In this way, his true essence, his eternity, and divinity, are established.

Sentence count: 16 (S1–S16). One paragraph, four moves: the charge (S1–S3) · argument A — if the Word began, God changed (S4–S6) · argument B — the Genesis inference is a non-sequitur (S7–S11) · the proof from eternity, and the conclusion (S12–S16).

The two words are joined silently; nothing else is disturbed. ⚠️ Unlike §7, no page number, running header, or margin number falls inside §8 — the section sits clean between the header above S1 and the start of §9. There is no such compound. (iii) Quotation marks are curly, with the comma inside and the citation outside: “…neither shadow of turning,” (James 1:17). and “…before the world was,” (John 17:5). The engine, in one line: the opponent turned yesterday's Genesis argument against Calvin, and Calvin turns it back by pointing out that first spoken is not first existing. Look at what S2 actually says: the Word only began to be **when God opened his sacred mouth in the creation of the world**. That is §7's own proof — and God said, Let there be light — picked up and reversed. Yesterday Calvin used the creation-speech to show there is a Word; today the objector uses it to show the Word starts there. ⚠️ That is why §8 must follow §7. Calvin is answering the objection his own argument creates. And the answer is beautifully economical: the speech exerts the Word's energy (S11), and a power that is exerted at a moment must have been there before the moment. Exertion presupposes existence.

And notice the two-tier structure of the refutation, because the exam lives in the gap between them. Argument A (S4–S6) is about God: a beginning in the Word would be a change in the divine essence, and Scripture forbids that. Argument B (S7–S11) is about logic: even granting the Genesis text, the inference is invalid. ⚠️ A is theological, B is formal, and Calvin needs both — A alone would look like question-begging against a text, B alone would leave the doctrine of God untouched. Then S12–S14 supply the positive evidence, and S15–S16 total it up. Three moves: you may not, you cannot, and here is what is actually the case.

⚠️ One more thing, and it is the most easily missed sentence in the section. S4 contains a concession, and the concession is the whole reason the argument works. Calvin grants that some names of God began to be ascribed to him from the existence of the work — as when he is called **the Creator of heaven and earth**. God was not always called Creator. Calvin has no problem with that. What he denies is a name that would indicate that **a change had taken place in God himself**. The distinction is between a name that describes God's relation to something outside him and a name that describes what he is. Creator is the first kind. Word is the second. Miss this and S4 looks like a contradiction; catch it and the section is airtight.

Examiner's Eye — six traps.

  1. ⚠️⚠️⚠️ S4 does NOT deny that God may be called Creator — it grants it. Read the parenthesis: (as when he is called the Creator of heaven and earth). That is an example of the permitted class, not of the forbidden one. An option reading "Calvin holds that God cannot properly be called the Creator of heaven and earth, since that name implies change" has inverted the concession into the denial. The forbidden name is one which might indicate that a change had taken place **in God himself** — the last three words carry the whole restriction.
  2. ⚠️⚠️⚠️ S9 denies the inference, not the content — and it is the opponents' conclusion that is being denied. It does not surely follow, that because a thing begins to be manifested at a certain time, **it never existed previously**. An option reading "Calvin argues that a thing which begins to be manifested at a certain time never existed before" has quoted the subordinate clause as if it were the assertion. ⚠️ The main clause is It does not surely follow. Everything after the comma is what does NOT follow. This is the single commonest way to fail this section.
  3. ⚠️⚠️ S7 reports the opponents' reasoning, not Calvin's. **But they think they argue acutely, in maintaining that** Moses … must be held to intimate that till then no Word existed in him. Everything from Moses to in him is inside their claim, and they think is ironic. An option beginning "Calvin infers from Moses that…" has attributed the opponents' syllogism to Calvin. The giveaway is S8: This is the merest trifling. You do not call your own argument trifling.
  4. ⚠️⚠️ S13's No certain period of time is defined does not mean no time is mentioned. It means no definite span is specified — because the span is infinite. certain = definite, fixed (Lat. certus), not some. ⚠️ An option reading "Calvin concedes that Scripture nowhere indicates when the Word existed" has flipped a statement about the absence of a limit into a statement about the absence of evidence — and S12 has just said he will find it was **without beginning**. The lack of a boundary is the finding.
  5. ⚠️⚠️ the energy of the Word … must have existed long before (S11) is an inference from the exertion, not from the speech. The logic is: the energy was exerted at the moment of the command, therefore it pre-existed the moment. An option reading "Calvin argues that the Word existed long before because Moses places the command at the beginning of the narrative" has swapped a metaphysical premise for a textual one. ⚠️ And note **long before** is immediately corrected by S12 to **without beginning** — an option that leaves Calvin at long before has stopped one sentence too early.
  6. ⚠️⚠️ The opponents in S1 are not deniers of Christ's divinity. while they **dare not openly deny** his divinity, secretly rob him of his eternity. They profess the deity. ⚠️ An option describing them as "those who deny that the Word is God" has misidentified the target — that was §§6–7's opponent. Today's opponent concedes more and gives away less. The word while is concessive and does all the work.

                 IF S V-∅ (subjunctive) … ,  S' WILL V'             (S12)

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

Where §8 sits. The chapter's synopsis for this section reads: Objection, that the Λόγος began to be when the creating God spoke. Answer confirmed by Scripture and argument. ⚠️ Note the word Objection in the heading. §7's heading ended Objection. Reply. and §8's begins Objection. The two sections are one continuous movement, and the proof-block runs on: §§7–8 the Word's eternity, §§9–10 the divine names and the angel of the Lord, §§11–13 the New Testament testimonies, §§14–15 the Spirit, §§16–20 the distinctions. Today closes the first sub-argument: the Word is God (§7) and the Word never began (§8).

1. Whose objection is it? ⚠️ Calvin does not name anyone — certain men — and you should resist filling the blank too confidently. But the position is recognisable: concede the divinity, deny the eternity. Three streams feed it. (a) The apologists' distinction between the λόγος ἐνδιάθετος (the word immanent, resident in God) and the λόγος προφορικός (the word uttered, going forth) — a Stoic distinction taken up by Theophilus of Antioch and Tertullian to explain how the Word "proceeds" at creation. ⚠️ In its careless form it makes the Word become a person at the moment of utterance, which is exactly S2's objection, and Calvin is refusing the move — that is why he insists the Word's energy was exerted, not produced, when God spoke. (b) The anti-Trinitarian radicals of Calvin's own decade — above all Servetus, for whom the eternal Word was a disposition or utterance in God, personal only in the incarnate Christ. (c) The residue of Arianism: ἦν ποτε ὅτε οὐκ ἦν, there was when he was not, condemned at Nicaea (325). ⚠️ Guardrail: do not simply label the opponents "Arians." Arius denied the Son was of one substance with the Father; today's opponents grant the divinity outright (S1) and only date it. The heading calls it an objection, not a heresy, and Calvin answers it as one.

2. The real weight of the section: immutability, and the question it raises. Calvin's Argument A is that a beginning in the Word would put change in the divine essence (S3). That commits him to divine immutability — and immediately raises the classic difficulty: if God was not always Creator and then became Creator, has God not changed? ⚠️ S4 is Calvin's answer, and it is the standard one, stated with unusual economy. Names that have respect to external work begin with the work; they say something about God's relation to the creature, not about God himself. The change is in the creature, which comes to stand in a new relation to a God who does not move. Later schoolmen call this a Cambridge property — like being to the left of, which you can acquire without moving, because someone else moved. ⚠️ Guardrail: this is not the claim that God is uninvolved, or that creation is unreal to him. S5's proof-text says the gifts really cometh down. Calvin denies alteration, not action. Anyone who tells you Calvin's God is inert has confused the two.

3. Non sequitur as a theological tool. S9's It does not surely follow, that… is a formal move, and it is worth noticing how cheap it is in the best sense: Calvin does not have to re-exegete Genesis 1, dispute a Hebrew verb, or appeal to authority. He simply points out that the opponents' premise does not carry their conclusion. ⚠️ And the principle he substitutes — manifestation is not origination — is not ad hoc; it is a structural feature of the New Testament's own language about Christ. 1 Peter 1:20: foreordained before the foundation of the world, but manifest in these last times. 1 Timothy 3:16: God was manifest in the flesh. Romans 16:25–26: the mystery kept secret since the world began, but now is made manifest. In every case the appearing is late and the reality is not. Calvin is generalising the apostles' own habit of speech. Guardrail: he is not proving the Word's eternity from the principle — the principle only clears the objection away. The proof is S13–S14, two verses.**

4. John 17:5, and why it is the strongest verse he could have chosen. The high-priestly prayer asks for the restoration of the glory which I had with thee before the world was. ⚠️ Three features do the work. (i) The verb is εἶχον, imperfect indicative — I was having, I possessed — not a verb of promise, appointment, or foreknowledge. (ii) The time-marker πρὸ τοῦ τὸν κόσμον εἶναι puts the possession before the world's existence, which is the exact point S2 disputes. (iii) And it is spoken by Christ about himself, which is why S13 says he **himself** says. ⚠️ The standard counter-reading — that Christ speaks of a glory predestined for him, not actually possessed — was available then and is still argued now; Calvin does not engage it here, because §8 is a reply to one objection, not a full exegesis. Note the gap; the exam may ask what §8 does not argue.

5. Afterlife. Two receptions and a warning. The Reformed scholastics (Turretin, Institutio III.xxviii–xxix) systematised §8's two arguments into the standard treatment of eternal generation, and the pairing — an argument from immutability plus an argument from the invalidity of the temporal inference — became the textbook form. Barth (Church Dogmatics II/1 §31.1) took the harder road: rather than defending immutability by distinguishing relative from absolute names, he redefined it as God's constancy — God is unchangeably himself, which includes his freedom to act and to become. ⚠️ That is not Calvin's argument, and it is not Calvin's opponent's either; it is a third option. And the warning: process theology and much modern kenotic Christology reject S4's distinction outright, holding that a God really related to the world must really change. You do not have to adjudicate that here. ⚠️ What you must see is that Calvin's whole section presupposes the distinction S4 states in one clause, and that if the distinction fails, S5's use of James 1:17 fails with it. Find the load-bearing sentence and you have found where the argument can be attacked. That is what reading well means.

Over-reading guard-rail. ⚠️ Six limits. (a) S4 does not deny that God is truly Creator. The parenthesis grants the name. What is denied is a name implying change in God himself. (b) S9's principle does not prove the Word eternal. It removes an objection. The proof is S11 (the argument) plus S13–S14 (the Scripture) — which is exactly what the heading promises: Answer confirmed by Scripture and argument. (c) long before (S11) is not Calvin's answer. S12 is. Stopping at S11 leaves him saying something he immediately corrects. (d) must have existed (S11) is inference, not obligation, and it rests on an unstated premise (what is exerted already is). Calvin does not defend that premise. Notice the gap; do not pretend it is filled. (e) The opponents of §8 are not the opponents of §§6–7. Those denied the Word's distinct subsistence or its deity; these grant the deity and deny the date. Different target, different argument. (f) eternally begotten (S15) is not "begotten a very long time ago." ⚠️ The adverb cancels the temporal reading of the participle. Any paraphrase that supplies a when has undone the section.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① Grammar — which underlined part is wrong?

ⓐ For if any thing adventitious took place, the saying of James would cease to be true, that “every good gift, and every perfect gift, is from above.” ⓑ It does not surely follow, that because a thing begins to be manifested at a certain time, it never existed previously. ⓒ For as the names of God, which have respect to external work, began to be ascribed to him from the existence of the work, so piety does not recognise or admit any name which might indicate a change. ⓓ Nor this is omitted by John: for before he descends to the creation of the world, he says, that “in the beginning was the Word.”

Answer: ⓓ.

출제 의도. Formula 3(a): negative inversion. A sentence fronted by the negative adverb Nor must invert subject and auxiliary — **Nor is this** omitted by John. Nor this is omitted is not a stylistic variant; it is ungrammatical, then and now. ⚠️ The bait is that the uninverted order looks like ordinary English and the inverted order looks archaic — the instinct is exactly backwards here, and the same rule governs never have I, not only did he, scarcely had we, little did they know. Fix the habit, not the instance: when a clause opens with a negative or restrictive adverbial, put the auxiliary before the subject. Now check why the others stand. ⓐ is Formula 1: the counterfactual if … **took place** …, … **would cease**, with the appositive that-clause extraposed behind the whole predicate; would take place … would cease would double the modal, and takes place … will cease would convert it into an open conditional Calvin does not intend. ⓑ is Formula 2: It does not follow, **that** because A, B — the dummy it, the extraposed that-clause, and surely sitting inside the negation; moving surely to the front (Surely it does not follow) is possible modern English but changes the emphasis, and deleting that would leave because dangling from follow. ⓒ is Formula 3(b): the proportional as … so, with the so retained — modern English may drop it, but with as in the first limb the correlative is correct, and replacing as with because would destroy the concession.

② Comprehension — which statement is TRUE according to the passage?

ⓐ Calvin holds that God cannot rightly be called the Creator of heaven and earth, since any such name would imply that a change had taken place in him. ⓑ Calvin grants that the Word began to be manifested at the creation, but denies that this shows it did not exist before; from the exertion of its energy at that moment he infers that it existed without beginning. ⓒ Calvin argues, with Moses, that because God then spoke for the first time, no Word had existed in him until that moment. ⓓ Calvin concedes that Scripture nowhere indicates how long the Word existed before the world, and therefore rests his case on reason alone.

Answer: ⓑ. (S9 It does not surely follow, that because a thing begins to be manifested at a certain time, it never existed previously; S11 the energy of the Word … was immediately exerted, it must have existed long before; S12 he will find it was without beginning.)

출제 의도. Four options, four failure modes. - ⓐ is the inverted concession — Trap 1. S4's parenthesis (as when he is called **the Creator of heaven and earth**) is an example of what Calvin allows: names that have respect to external work do begin with the work. ⚠️ The bait is that S4's main clause is a negation, so the eye carries the not backwards into the parenthesis. The forbidden class is defined by the last three words of the sentence — in God himself — not by the parenthesis. Getting this backwards makes Calvin deny the first article of the Creed. - ⓑ is true, and note it needs three sentences. S9 alone gives only the non-sequitur; S11 adds the positive inference; S12 pushes long before to without beginning. ⚠️ An option that stopped at S11 would be incomplete but not false — the examiner's favourite grey zone, which is why ⓑ carries the chain through to S12. Trap 5's second half. - ⓒ is the ownership error — Trap 3. That inference is the opponents' (S7: **they** think **they** argue acutely, in maintaining that…), and Calvin's verdict on it is the next sentence: This is the merest trifling. ⚠️ The bait is the phrase with Moses, which smuggles Calvin onto the side of the text he is in fact reinterpreting. Watch every they in S7 — there are three. - ⓓ is the double flip — Trap 4 plus a reversal of the heading. S13's **No certain period** of time is defined means no definite span is fixed (because there is none to fix), not Scripture is silent; and the section is confirmed by Scripture — John 17:5 and John 1:1 are quoted in the very next two sentences, exactly as the argument heading promises (Answer confirmed by Scripture and argument). ⚠️ Any option that leaves Calvin resting on reason alone has ignored half the section.

③ Composition — apply Formulas 1, 2 and 3

Render into Beveridge-style English, using all three of today's formulas:

"만일 무엇이든 밖에서 더해진 것이 하나님께 일어났다면, 사도의 말은 참되기를 그쳤을 것이다, 곧 그분은 변하지 아니하신다는 말이. 어떤 것이 어느 정한 때에 나타나기 시작했다고 해서 그것이 전에 존재하지 않았다는 결론은 결코 따라 나오지 않는다. 바울도 이것을 빠뜨리지 않는다. 그리고 하나님의 이름들이 그 사역이 존재한 때로부터 그분께 돌려지기 시작한 것처럼, 그렇게 경건은 그분 자신 안에 변화가 일어났음을 가리킬 어떤 이름도 인정하거나 허용하지 않는다."

Model answer:

For if any thing adventitious took place in God, the saying of the apostle would cease to be true, that he changeth not. It does not surely follow, that because a thing begins to be manifested at a certain time, it never existed previously. Nor is this omitted by Paul. And as the names of God, which have respect to external work, began to be ascribed to him from the existence of the work, so piety does not recognise or admit any name which might indicate that a change had taken place in God himself.

출제 의도 및 채점 기준. Six things are being checked. 1. Formula 1, the counterfactual. **if** any thing adventitious **took place** …, the saying … **would cease** to be true. ⚠️ Three checks: the past protasis (took place, not takes place, not had taken place); the would apodosis (not will, not ceased); and the fact that they must match — a present protasis with a would apodosis is the commonest error and forfeits the point. 2. Formula 1, the delayed appositive. the saying of the apostle would cease to be true, **that** he changeth not. ⚠️ The that-clause must sit after the whole predicate, with a comma. Writing the saying of the apostle that he changeth not would cease to be true is correct English and loses the Beveridge shape, which is what is being tested. Test yourself with namely. 3. Formula 2, the negation frame. **It does not surely follow, that** because a thing begins to be manifested at a certain time, it never existed previously. ⚠️ Four checks: the dummy It; does not … follow (not it follows not, not it is not following); **surely** inside the negative; and that retained before because. ⚠️ Dropping that is fatal — follow because reads as a causal adjunct of follow, and the sentence then says the opposite of what it means. 4. Formula 3(a), negative inversion. **Nor is** this omitted by Paul. ⚠️ Not Nor this is omitted; not And this is not omitted (grammatical, but forfeits the point); not Nor is this not omitted (double negative). The auxiliary must precede the subject. 5. Formula 3(b), the proportional. **as** the names of God … began to be ascribed …, **so** piety does not recognise or admit any name…. ⚠️ Both limbs required; so must be retained; as must not become because. And the relative **which** have respect to external work must be non-restrictive (commas both sides) — it describes the whole class, it does not select a subset. ⚠️ Writing the names of God that have respect to external work changes the argument: it would imply that some names referring to external work do not begin with the work. 6. Vocabulary and argumentative accuracy — heaviest. adventitious (S5, = coming from outside), cease to be true (S5), manifested (S9), at a certain time (S9, = definite), previously (S9), have respect to (S4, = refer to), ascribed (S4), piety (S4), recognise or admit (S4, the doublet), might indicate (S4, potential). ⚠️ And the closing clause must end in God himself. Drop those three words and you have written the sentence ⓐ of Problem ② — a denial that God may be called Creator, which is the exact inversion of Calvin's concession. Three words carry the whole restriction. Do not lose them.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary. Yesterday the Word was proved God; today it is proved beginningless, against people who steal quietly rather than deny loudly — certain men, who, **while** they dare not openly deny his divinity, **secretly rob him of his eternity** (S1), whose claim is precise: the Word **only began to be** when God opened his sacred mouth in the creation of the world (S2), and whose real offence is upstream — **with excessive temerity**, they imagine **some change in the essence of God** (S3). Argument A, from immutability. Calvin first concedes what must be conceded: **as** the names of God, **which have respect to external work**, began to be ascribed to him from the existence of the work (**as when he is called the Creator of heaven and earth**), **so** piety does not recognise or admit any name which might indicate that a change had taken place **in God himself** (S4) — relative names may begin; absolute ones may not. Then the proof-text §7 planted: **if any thing adventitious took place**, the saying of James **would cease to be true**, that "…**with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning**," (James 1:17) (S5). Verdict: **Nothing** … is **more intolerable** than **to fancy a beginning** to that Word which was **always** God, and **afterwards** was the Creator of the world (S6). Argument B, from logic. Their move is stated fairly — **they think they argue acutely**, in maintaining that Moses … **must be held to intimate** that till then no Word existed in him (S7) — and dismissed in six words: **This is the merest trifling.** (S8). Then the principle the whole section turns on: **It does not surely follow**, that because a thing **begins to be manifested** at a certain time, **it never existed previously** (S9). **I draw a very different conclusion.** (S10): Since at the very moment when God said, "Let there be light," the energy of the Word **was immediately exerted**, it **must have existed long before** (S11) — exertion presupposes existence — and then the correction that abolishes the question: If any inquire **how long**, he will find it was **without beginning** (S12). The evidence. **No certain period** of time is defined, when **he himself** says, "…the glory which I **had** with thee **before the world was**," (John 17:5) (S13); **Nor is this omitted by John**: for **before he descends to the creation** of the world, he says, that "in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God." (S14). Conclusion: the Word was **eternally begotten** by God, and **dwelt with him from everlasting** (S15), so that his true **essence**, his **eternity**, and **divinity**, are established (S16). First spoken is not first existing. Manifestation is not origination.

And the three grammar points, once more. ① The counterfactual with a past protasis, and the delayed appositive — **if** any thing adventitious **took place** …, the saying of James **would cease** to be true, **that** … (S5): past protasis + would apodosis = hypothesis, not history — and read backwards from the apodosis, because only be shows the subjunctive openly. ⚠️ Contrast the open conditional seven sentences later: **if** any **inquire** how long, he **will find** (S12). Plus the appositive three times — the saying of James …, **that** (S5), It does not surely follow, **that** (S9), We … conclude, **that** (S15): abstract noun or dummy it, then the predicate, then the that-clause, testable with namely. ② Negation and its scope — It does **not** surely **follow**, that because A, B (S9): the negation bites on the inference, and B is what does not follow; surely = certainly, settled by S10's a **very different** conclusion. Plus the family: **Nothing** is **more** intolerable **than** = superlative (S6); **No certain** period is defined = no definite span (S13); **without** beginning (S12). Nine of sixteen sentences carry a negative — find it before you translate. ③ Negative inversion and the proportional — **Nor is this** omitted by John (S14): fronted negative adverbial forces subject–auxiliary inversion, obligatory, and the litotes means John states it, as one would expect; plus **as** … , **so** … (S4), second day running, with limb 1 a concession and limb 2 a limit. And five things to keep: temerity = recklessness (not timidity), adventitious = added from outside, intimate = to hint (a verb), the merest = sheer, certain = definite; the concession in S4's parenthesis (Trap 1); the ownership of S7 (Trap 3 — three theys); must have existed = inference, not obligation (S11); and eternally begotten, where the adverb cancels the when (S15).
