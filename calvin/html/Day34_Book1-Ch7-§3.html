Day 34 — Book 1, Chapter 7, §3

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday Calvin swung Ephesians 2:20 and knocked the argument flat. Today the opponent gets back up holding the biggest club in the sixteenth-century arsenal — a single line from Augustine:

Ego vero Evangelio non crederem, nisi me catholicae Ecclesiae commoveret auctoritas. "I would not believe the gospel, were I not moved by the authority of the Church."

Look at the problem. It is not a bad prooftext. It is Augustine — the one Father both sides claim, the one Calvin quotes more than anyone alive or dead. If that sentence means what Rome says it means, Calvin's §2 collapses and the Reformation's foundation goes with it. So §3 is not a doctrinal section. §3 is a hermeneutics lesson. Calvin does not answer Augustine. He answers a misreading of Augustine — and the method he uses is the one you should steal for the rest of your life: go get the context.

Watch the four moves, because they are a template:

  1. State the opponent's citation honestly (S1) — chapter and verse included, no shaving.
  2. Reconstruct the argumentative situation (S3–S4) — whom was he arguing with? What were they demanding? Augustine is fighting Manichees who wanted implicitly believed on their bare say-so, and who were quoting the gospel at him. So he asks: what would you say to a man who doesn't even accept the gospel yet?
  3. Restate the sentence inside that situation (S5) — were he a stranger to the faith. That is the whole key. Augustine is describing the outsider's road in, not the believer's ground of certainty.
  4. Produce the counter-evidence from the same author, a few lines earlier (S8) — where Augustine says the real teacher is God himself internally strengthening and illuminating our minds. Same treatise. Same chapter neighbourhood. Case closed.

And then — this is why Calvin is a great polemicist and not merely a loud one — he concedes what is true (S9, S11, S14). Yes, the Church leads people on. Yes, Augustine really did press the Manichees with the Church's consent. Calvin gives away everything he can afford to give, and keeps only the one thing that matters: the ground of the godly's certainty is a different foundation.

Today's 3 Big Points — write them down now:

  1. If-deletion + inversion in the past subjunctive — THREE times in one section (S1 were he not moved, S5 were he a stranger, S7 were they not influenced). The if is gone; the verb were jumps in front of the subject. Miss this and you will read a conditional as a statement of fact.
  2. not A ... nor B ... but merely C — the concession-and-retention machine. S7 is the triple-decker version (does not here say ... nor does he mean ... he merely says), and S9 is the compressed version (had no intention **to suspend** ... but only **to intimate** — one head noun, two infinitives, one negated).
  3. would have X to V = "wants X to V" (S10). This is volitional have, not a perfect tense and not an obligation. Beveridge's most quietly lethal construction, and it is exactly where the examiner will strike.

Fifteen sentences. One quotation, one context, one boundary line. Let's go.

1 — Full Text (the whole § — 15 sentences, verbatim) –

3. I am aware it is usual to quote a sentence of Augustine in which he says that he would not believe the gospel, were he not moved by the authority of the Church (Aug. Cont. Epist. Fundament. c. 5). But it is easy to discover from the context, how inaccurate and unfair it is to give it such a meaning. He was reasoning against the Manichees, who insisted on being implicitly believed, alleging that they had the truth, though they did not show they had. But as they pretended to appeal to the gospel in support of Manes, he asks what they would do if they fell in with a man who did not even believe the gospel—what kind of argument they would use to bring him over to their opinion. He afterwards adds, “But I would not believe the gospel,” &c.; meaning, that were he a stranger to the faith, the only thing which could induce him to embrace the gospel would be the authority of the Church. And is it any thing wonderful, that one who does not know Christ should pay respect to men? Augustine, therefore, does not here say that the faith of the godly is founded on the authority of the Church; nor does he mean that the certainty of the gospel depends upon it; he merely says that unbelievers would have no certainty of the gospel, so as thereby to win Christ, were they not influenced by the consent of the Church. And he clearly shows this to be his meaning, by thus expressing himself a little before: “When I have praised my own creed, and ridiculed yours, who do you suppose is to judge between us; or what more is to be done than to quit those who, inviting us to certainty, afterwards command us to believe uncertainty, and follow those who invite us, in the first instance, to believe what we are not yet able to comprehend, that waxing stronger through faith itself, we may become able to understand what we believe—no longer men, but God himself internally strengthening and illuminating our minds? These unquestionably are the words of Augustine (August. Cont. Epist. Fundament. cap. 4); and the obvious inference from them is, that this holy man had no intention to suspend our faith in Scripture on the nod or decision of the Church, but only to intimate (what we too admit to be true) that those who are not yet enlightened by the Spirit of God, become teachable by reverence for the Church, and thus submit to learn the faith of Christ from the gospel. In this way, though the authority of the Church leads us on, and prepares us to believe in the gospel, it is plain that Augustine would have the certainty of the godly to rest on a very different foundation. At the same time, I deny not that he often presses the Manichees with the consent of the whole Church, while arguing in support of the Scriptures, which they rejected. Hence he upbraids Faustus (lib. 32) for not submitting to evangelical truth—truth so well founded, so firmly established, so gloriously renowned, and handed down by sure succession from the days of the apostles. But he nowhere insinuates that the authority which we give to the Scriptures depends on the definitions or devices of men. He only brings forward the universal Judgment of the Church, as a point most pertinent to the cause, and one, moreover, in which he had the advantage of his opponents. Any one who desires to see this more fully proved may read his short treatises, De Utilitate Credendi (The Advantages of Believing), where it will be found that the only facility of believing which he recommends is that which affords an introduction, and forms a fit commencement to inquiry; while he declares that we ought not to be satisfied with opinion, but to strive after substantial truth. ⚠️ Text note (transcription, not Calvin): three housekeeping items from our local Beveridge file. (1) The line-break hyphenation au-/thority in S7 has been closed to authority. (2) The footnote markers 19 (after decision of the Church, S9) and 20 (after a very different foundation, S10) have been stripped — both are cited in Period 6, and marker 20 is the De Ordine passage that is the hidden key to the whole section. (3) The long Augustine quotation in S8 opens with a quotation mark at “When I have praised and never closes. The closing mark should fall after illuminating our minds? — Beveridge (or the scan) dropped it. I quote the file as it stands, but read the quotation as ending there, because S9 (These unquestionably are the words of Augustine) proves that is where Calvin stopped quoting.

Examiner's Eye — the traps they will set:

Trap 1 (S1, S5, S7): the three vanished ifs. were he not moved, were he a stranger, were they not influenced — all three are counterfactual conditionals with if deleted and the verb inverted. The distractor will read S5 as a statement — "Augustine was a stranger to the faith" — or read S7's tail as "unbelievers have no certainty of the gospel and are not influenced by the Church." Both destroy the sentence. Restore the if every time.

Trap 2 (S7): the scope of the denial. Calvin denies two things and grants one. Denied: (a) godly faith founded on Church authority; (b) gospel certainty depending on it. Granted: (c) unbelievers would have no certainty so as thereby to win Christ apart from the Church's consent. An option that says "Calvin holds the Church's testimony has no effect on anyone at all" has swallowed (c) along with (a) and (b). Over-scope. Kill it.

Trap 3 (S9–S10): teachable and leads us on are Calvin's own concessions. Note the parenthesis in S9 — (what we too admit to be true). Calvin agrees that reverence for the Church makes the unenlightened teachable, and S10 grants that the Church leads us on, and prepares us to believe. The trap option will present these clauses as the Roman position that Calvin rejects. Direction-flip on a concessive clause — the classic.

Trap 4 (S10): would have ... to rest. This is want, not would have had and not had to. "Augustine had to make the certainty of the godly rest on..." is wrong. "Augustine wanted the certainty of the godly to rest on..." is right.

Trap 5 (S13): nowhere in mid-position does NOT invert. he nowhere insinuates — subject, then the negative adverb, then the verb. Compare S7's nor does he mean, where the negative is fronted and therefore does invert. Same section, both patterns, side by side. That is an exam item waiting to happen.

Trap 6 (S15): facility and substantial. facility of believing = easiness/readiness to believe (Latin facilitas), NOT a building and NOT a skill. substantial truth = truth with substance/reality in it, NOT "a large amount of truth."

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas (시험 직전 이것만) –

Formula 1 — If-deletion + inversion (past subjunctive conditional)

   if + S + WERE / HAD / SHOULD ...
        ↓  delete "if", invert
   WERE / HAD / SHOULD + S + ...

   ▸ WERE he not moved by the authority   = if he were not moved            (S1)
   ▸ WERE he a stranger to the faith      = if he were a stranger           (S5)
   ▸ WERE they not influenced by ...      = if they were not influenced     (S7)

   ⚠️ Only THREE verbs invert this way: were / had / should.
      *"Believed he not" is NOT English. You cannot invert a lexical verb.
   ⚠️ It is a COUNTERFACTUAL, not a statement of fact.
      "were he a stranger" ≠ "he was a stranger"
   ⚠️ Word order looks like a question. It is not. No question mark → conditional.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The text at issue. Augustine, Contra Epistolam Manichaei quam vocant Fundamenti ("Against the Letter of Mani which they call The Foundation"), c. 396/397, chapter 5: Ego vero Evangelio non crederem, nisi me catholicae Ecclesiae commoveret auctoritas. Written a year or so into his episcopate, against a Manichaean tract Augustine knew intimately — because he had been a Manichee auditor for roughly nine years. When he writes were he a stranger to the faith, he is not constructing a thought-experiment. He is remembering the road he actually walked.

Why this one line mattered so much in 1559. It was the standard opening move of Catholic controversialists against the Reformers — in Johann Eck's Enchiridion (1525), in the debates around Trent's fourth session (1546), and later, at full scholastic strength, in Bellarmine. The syllogism was clean: You claim Scripture alone. Augustine says he believes the gospel only because of the Church. Therefore your own greatest authority refutes you. Luther had already wrestled with the passage; Calvin's §3 is the settled Reformed answer, and it survived: you can see its argument reappearing verbatim in Protestant polemics for the next three centuries.

The four hinges of Calvin's reading. (1) Commoveret is a moving-verb, not a founding-verb — an efficient cause of assent, not the formal ground of faith. (2) The line stands inside a counterfactual conditional aimed at a specific hypothetical person (S4's man who did not even believe the gospel). (3) The audience was Manichees demanding implicit belief — so Augustine's appeal to the Church's consent is an argumentum ad hominem in the technical, non-pejorative sense: it works against these opponents by their standards. (4) The decisive counter-evidence is four chapters earlier, where Augustine names the terminal teacher: no longer men, but God himself internally strengthening and illuminating our minds.

The key that Beveridge hides in a footnote. Attached to S10, Beveridge cites De Ordine 2.9: tempore auctoritas, re autem ratio prior est — authority comes first in time, but reason comes first in reality. That single clause is Augustine's own systematic account of the very distinction Calvin is drawing. Augustine consistently separates the pedagogical order (what moves you first) from the ontological order (what actually grounds the thing). Calvin is not rescuing Augustine from Augustine; he is quoting Augustine's own architecture back at the people misusing him. If you remember one thing from Period 6, remember that Latin clause.

The other treatise. De Utilitate Credendi (391), written to his friend Honoratus — still a Manichee — arguing that believing before understanding is rational and necessary. It is the strongest possible text for the "authority first" case, and Calvin sends you to it himself (S15). That is a confident man. His prediction: you will find that the facilitas credendi Augustine recommends is framed as an introduction and a commencement, and that the same treatise insists we must not stop at opinio.

Later reception, briefly. Modern Augustine scholarship largely grants Calvin's contextual point about commoveret and the polemical setting, while noting that Augustine's ecclesiology is more integrated than a strict Reformed reading might wish — for Augustine the Church is not merely a signpost one walks past. The Reformed confessions later codify Calvin's distinction: Belgic Confession 5 (we receive these books "not so much because the Church receives them... as because the Holy Spirit witnesses in our hearts") and Westminster 1.4–1.5 (the Church's testimony may move us to a high esteem — full persuasion comes from the Spirit). Note the shape: the Church's testimony is granted a real function and then explicitly subordinated. That is §3 turned into a creed.

⚠️ Guardrail — three over-readings to refuse. 1. "Calvin denies the Church any role." He states the opposite twice, in his own voice: (what we too admit to be true) (S9) and the authority of the Church leads us on, and prepares us to believe (S10). 2. "Calvin rejects tradition and succession." He quotes handed down by sure succession from the days of the apostles (S12) with approval. What he denies is that our authority for Scripture depends on human definitions (S13). Historical transmission: yes. Juridical constitution: no. 3. "Therefore each individual judges for himself." Not yet, and not ever in Calvin. The alternative foundation is not private judgment — S9 already excluded human judgment on both sides. It is the Spirit's inward testimony (§5), which is neither the Church's decree nor my opinion. If your reading of §3 lands you in subjectivism, you have skipped ahead badly; I.9 will deal with the enthusiasts who did exactly that.

7 — Scripture Connections –

This section is unusual: not one direct Bible citation. It is a patristic argument, fought on patristic ground. But the scriptural bones are showing everywhere — and one verse in particular is the hidden engine of the Augustine quotation.

  1. S8 — that waxing stronger through faith itself, we may become able to understand what we believe ← Isaiah 7:9, in the Old Latin/LXX form Augustine used: nisi credideritis, non intelligetis (ἐὰν μὴ πιστεύσητε, οὐδὲ μὴ συνῆτε) — "unless you believe, you will not understand." This verse is the charter of Augustine's whole crede ut intelligas program, and the clause Calvin quotes is a paraphrase of it. Notice how Calvin uses it: he does not have to name the verse, because the order it prescribes (faith → understanding) is exactly what proves his case. Augustine's sequence is temporal, and a temporal priority is not a foundational one.

  2. S8 — no longer men, but God himself internally strengthening and illuminating our minds ← Ephesians 3:16 (κραταιωθῆναι διὰ τοῦ πνεύματος αὐτοῦ εἰς τὸν ἔσω ἄνθρωπον — "strengthened by his Spirit in the inner man") and Ephesians 1:18 (πεφωτισμένους τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς τῆς καρδίας — "the eyes of your heart being enlightened"). Both verbs of the phrase — strengthening, illuminating — sit in Paul's prayers. Calvin's §5 will build the testimonium internum on precisely this vocabulary; here he gets it from Augustine for free.

  3. S9 — those who are not yet enlightened by the Spirit of God ← 1 Corinthians 2:14 (ψυχικὸς ἄνθρωπος οὐ δέχεται — "the natural man receiveth not the things of the Spirit"). The whole category of "the unenlightened who become teachable by reverence for the Church" presupposes this verse's anthropology.

  4. S3 — alleging that they had the truth, though they did not show they had ← 2 Corinthians 4:2 (τῇ φανερώσει τῆς ἀληθείας — "by manifestation of the truth commending ourselves") and 1 John 4:1 ("try the spirits"). Claim versus manifestation: Calvin's operative distinction against every implicit-faith demand.

  5. S6 — one who does not know Christ should pay respect to men ← John 5:44 ("How can ye believe, which receive honour one of another?") and Galatians 1:10 ("do I seek to please men?"). Human deference is exactly what marks the man who has not yet seen.

  6. S12 — handed down by sure succession from the days of the apostles ← 1 Corinthians 11:23 / 15:3 (παρέλαβον… παρέδωκα — "I received… I delivered"). Calvin lets paradosis language stand unchallenged when it means historical transmission. Watch how carefully he handles this word; he is not afraid of it.

  7. S15 — not to be satisfied with opinion, but to strive after substantial truth ← Hebrews 11:1 (ἔστιν δὲ πίστις ἐλπιζομένων ὑπόστασις — faith is the substance of things hoped for) and Colossians 2:2 (πληροφορία τῆς συνέσεως — "full assurance of understanding"). Opinion versus hypostasis — the chapter's driving antithesis, and the reason §4 opens with a perfect conviction that God is its author.

  8. S10 — rest on a very different foundation ← Ephesians 2:20 (§2's verse) and 1 Corinthians 3:11 (θεμέλιον… οὐδεὶς δύναται θεῖναι — "other foundation can no man lay"). The foundation-metaphor is carried across from §2 on purpose: Calvin is telling you the socket is still empty and someone is coming to fill it.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① 어법 — 밑줄 넷 중 어법상 틀린 것은?

Augustine does not say that the faith of the godly is founded on the Church's authority; ⓐ nor he means that the certainty of the gospel depends upon it. ⓑ Were he a stranger to the faith, the only thing which could induce him would be that authority. He ⓒ nowhere insinuates that our reverence for Scripture rests on human devices; and it is plain that he ⓓ would have the certainty of the godly to rest on a very different foundation.

정답: ⓐ

Nor is a fronted negative adverb, so the auxiliary must precede the subject: nor does he mean (exactly as Calvin writes it in S7). nor he means is impossible.

출제 의도 — 이 문제는 ⓒ와의 대비가 전부다. ⓒ he nowhere insinuates looks like it should invert too, and half the room will "fix" it to nowhere he insinuates or flag it as the error. It is correct as written, because nowhere sits in mid-position after the subject, and mid-position negatives do not trigger inversion. Fronted → invert; mid-position → don't. Both patterns appear in §3, ten lines apart (S7 and S13). ⓑ is the if-deleted subjunctive inversion (Formula 1) — correct. ⓓ is volitional would have + O + to V (Formula 3) — correct, and archaic-looking on purpose, which is why weak students choose it.

② 내용일치 — §3의 내용과 일치하는 것은?

  1. Calvin argues that Augustine's sentence is a forgery, and that the words I would not believe the gospel were never written by him.
  2. Calvin denies that the Church's authority has any effect whatever on those who are not yet enlightened by the Spirit.
  3. Calvin grants that reverence for the Church makes the unenlightened teachable, but holds that Augustine wanted the certainty of the godly to rest on a different foundation.
  4. Calvin concedes that Augustine, in De Utilitate Credendi, recommends resting permanently in the Church's authority rather than pressing on to inquiry.

정답: ③

S9's parenthesis (what we too admit to be true) + S10's though the authority of the Church leads us on, and prepares us to believe... a very different foundation. Concession and reservation, both in Calvin's own voice.

출제 의도 — 세 개의 서로 다른 함정을 한 문제에 심었다. ① 가짜 쟁점(false issue). Calvin never questions the authenticity of the sentence — he cites it with chapter number and quotes it twice. His entire complaint is about meaning, not authorship (S2: inaccurate and unfair ... to give it such a meaning). Students who half-remember "Calvin attacked the quotation" fall straight in. ② 범위 초과(over-scope). This inverts S9 and S10 outright. Calvin explicitly affirms the effect he is here said to deny. Notice how the distractor is built: it takes a limited denial (the Church is not the foundation) and inflates it to an absolute one (no effect whatever). That is the single most common comprehension trap in this whole chapter — watch for any, whatever, at all. ④ 방향 뒤집기(direction-flip) on the last clause. S15 says the opposite twice over: the facility Augustine recommends is an introduction and a fit commencement to inquiry, and he declares that we ought not to be satisfied with opinion, but to strive after substantial truth. The distractor swaps starting point for resting place.

③ 영작 — 오늘의 공식을 적용해 영작하시오.

(a) [Formula 1] "만일 성령이 우리 마음에 그 말씀을 인치지 않으신다면, 어떤 논증도 온전한 믿음을 낳지 못할 것이다." — if를 삭제한 도치 가정법으로. (b) [Formula 2] "칼빈은 교회가 무용하다고 말하지 않으며, 교회의 증언이 무가치하다는 뜻도 아니다. 그는 다만 교회의 권위가 아직 조명되지 못한 자들을 복음 들을 준비가 되게 한다고 말할 뿐이다." (c) [Formula 3] "아우구스티누스는 경건한 자들의 확신이 성령의 내적 증거 위에 놓이기를 원했다."

모범답안: (a) Were the Spirit not to seal his word upon our hearts, no argument would produce a full and settled faith. (b) Calvin does not say that the Church is useless; nor does he mean that her testimony is worthless; he merely says that her authority prepares those who are not yet enlightened to hear the gospel. (c) Augustine would have the certainty of the godly to rest on the inward testimony of the Spirit.

채점 포인트. (a) seal은 일반동사라 직접 도치 불가 — 반드시 were + to V 우회로를 쓴다. Sealed the Spirit not… 은 영어가 아니다. (b) 세 마디가 does not say / nor does he mean / he merely says 순서로 서야 하고, 둘째 마디는 도치 필수. nor he means 쓰면 0점. (c) would have + O + **to** rest — Beveridge 어법대로 to를 살린다. would have had·had to rest 는 오답.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary. Rome's best patristic weapon — Augustine's I would not believe the gospel, were I not moved by the authority of the Church — is a counterfactual spoken about the outsider's road in, not a statement about the believer's ground of certainty; read in context (Manichees demanding implicit belief, a hypothetical unbeliever, and Augustine's own words four chapters earlier naming God himself internally strengthening and illuminating our minds), it grants the Church a pedagogical office that Calvin cheerfully signs — what we too admit to be true — while leaving the certainty of the godly resting on a very different foundation.

And the three grammar points, once more:

  1. If-deleted subjunctive inversion — were he not moved / were he a stranger / were they not influenced. Three times in fifteen sentences. Restore the if every single time.
  2. not A ... nor B ... but merely C — with the paired inversion rule: nor **does he** mean (fronted → invert) vs. he **nowhere insinuates** (mid-position → no inversion).
  3. would have + O + to V = wants O to V — volitional have, not perfect, not obligation.

Homework (10 minutes).

① Structure restoration. Take S9 and rebuild it as three flat sentences, keeping every element:

this holy man had no intention to suspend our faith in Scripture on the nod or decision of the Church, but only to intimate (what we too admit to be true) that those who are not yet enlightened by the Spirit of God, become teachable by reverence for the Church, and thus submit to learn the faith of Christ from the gospel.

Target shape: (i) He did not intend X. (ii) He intended only Y. (iii) And Y is something we ourselves affirm. — Then mark which infinitive the words no intention govern, and say in one line why but here means correction and not addition.

② Composition. Write one English sentence combining Formula 1 and Formula 2, on this content: "Were the Church's consent our only ground, our faith would be precarious; Calvin does not deny her testimony, nor does he despise her office; he merely refuses to make her nod the foundation." — then check your own work against S7 and S10 for the inversion and the volitional have.

③ Tomorrow's preview (one line). §4 opens It is necessary to attend to what I lately said, that our faith in doctrine is not established until we have a perfect conviction that God is its author — and from there Calvin finally names the alternative foundation: the highest proof of Scripture is taken from the character of him whose Word it is, and the testimony of the Spirit is superior to reason. The empty socket of S10 starts to fill. Bring Isaiah 59:21.
