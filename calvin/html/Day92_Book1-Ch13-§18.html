Day 92 — Book 1, Chapter 13, §18

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Day 90 gave you a toolkit. Row ① was the name. Today we finally use row ①. Yesterday proved the Spirit is God by what he does. Today proves it by what he is called — and by whose place he takes in the text. Watch the method, because it is one move repeated five times: take a verse where the subject is God, find a parallel verse where the subject is the Spirit, and show that the predicate is the same. Temple of God / temple of the Spirit. Lied to God / lied to the Spirit. The Lord of Hosts spoke / the Spirit spoke. God provoked / the Spirit grieved. Same predicate, two names. If the predicate belongs to God alone, the two names name the same God. That is the argument. Learn to see it and you can read the next ten pages alone.

Second — the best sentence in the paragraph is one where Calvin throws evidence away. I **designedly omit** several passages which the ancient fathers adduced. ⚠️⚠️ The fathers loved Psalm 33:6 — by the breath (Spirit) of his mouth — as a proof that the Spirit created the world. Calvin says: no. Hebrew poetry says the same thing twice. word and breath of his mouth are one idea, not two Persons. that proof was weak A theologian arguing FOR a doctrine refuses a proof text FOR that doctrine. That is not weakness. That is method. Mark it — you will need it every time someone hands you a proof text that proves too much.

Third — the grammar trap of the day is in S11, and it is a monster. that majesty must certainly be divine **which** it is an inexpiable crime to offend or impair. ⚠️⚠️⚠️ The relative clause has been separated from its antecedent — majesty … [five words of main clause] … which. And inside the relative clause sits a dummy it with a delayed infinitive, and the gap for which is the object of offend. If you cannot rebuild that sentence, you cannot answer the question. Formula 3.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. Negative-initial inversion: Nor + do/does + S + V. **Nor does** the Scripture, in speaking of him, **withhold** the name of God. ⚠️ Nor at the head of a clause forces operator–subject inversion, exactly like a question. Day 91 S12 was the positive cousin (Most clearly … **does Paul** ascribe). And today brings a third inversion: **were we ordered** to make a temple … = if we were ordered — Day 91's if-less conditional, back in Augustine's mouth. Formula 1.
  2. not to A, but to B / not unto A, but unto B — the first limb is DENIED. we are **not to make** a temple to him, **but to be** ourselves that temple · he had **not** lied **unto men**, **but unto God**. ⚠️ Add both to your Day 91 table. No only → A is killed. And the partner construction: how much + comparative + a + NOUN (how much clearer **a proof**) — the adjective jumps in front of the article. Formula 2.
  3. The separated relative clause + it is … to V inside it. **that majesty** must certainly be divine **which** it is an inexpiable crime to offend or impair = that majesty which it is an inexpiable crime to offend … must be divine. ⚠️ The gap: offend ___ / impair ___ — which is their object. Never add it after offend. Plus the frame: while … , whereas … , [MAIN CLAUSE] — two subordinate clauses before the main verb appears. Formula 3.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 14 sentences –

Nor does the Scripture, in speaking of him, withhold the name of God. Paul infers that we are the temple of God, from the fact that “the Spirit of God dwelleth in us,” (1 Cor. 3:16; 6:19; and 2 Cor. 6:16). Now it ought not to be slightly overlooked, that all the promises which God makes of choosing us to himself as a temple, receive their only fulfilment by his Spirit dwelling in us. Surely, as it is admirably expressed by Augustine (Ad Maximinum, Ep. 66), “were we ordered to make a temple of wood and stone to the Spirit, inasmuch as such worship is due to God alone, it would be a clear proof of the Spirit’s divinity; how much clearer a proof in that we are not to make a temple to him, but to be ourselves that temple.” And the Apostle says at one time that we are the temple of God, and at another time, in the same sense, that we are the temple of the Holy Spirit. Peter, when he rebuked Ananias for having lied to the Holy Spirit, said, that he had not lied unto men, but unto God. And when Isaiah had introduced the Lord of Hosts as speaking, Paul says, it was the Holy Spirit that spoke (Acts 28:25, 26). Nay, words uniformly said by the prophets to have been spoken by the Lord of Hosts, are by Christ and his apostles ascribed to the Holy Spirit. Hence it follows that the Spirit is the true Jehovah who dictated the prophecies. Again, when God complains that he was provoked to anger by the stubbornness of the people, in place of Him, Isaiah says that his Holy Spirit was grieved (Isa. 63:10). Lastly, while blasphemy against the Spirit is not forgiven, either in the present life or that which is to come, whereas he who has blasphemed against the Son may obtain pardon, that majesty must certainly be divine which it is an inexpiable crime to offend or impair. I designedly omit several passages which the ancient fathers adduced. They thought it plausible to quote from David, “By the word of the Lord were the heavens made, and all the host of them by the breath (Spirit) of his mouth,” (Ps. 33:6), in order to prove that the world was not less the work of the Holy Spirit than of the Son. But seeing it is usual in the Psalms to repeat the same thing twice, and in Isaiah the spirit (breath) of the mouth is equivalent to word, that proof was weak; and, accordingly, my wish has been to advert briefly to those proofs on which pious minds may securely rest.

Sentence count: 14 (S1–S14). One paragraph, four movements: the thesis — Scripture gives the Spirit the name of God (S1) · the temple argument, with Augustine (S2–S5) · the speech argument — Ananias, Isaiah, the prophets, "the true Jehovah" (S6–S9) · two closing proofs — grief and blasphemy (S10–S11) · and the method note: a weak patristic proof deliberately dropped (S12–S14).

[VERDICT]        S14   "SEEING" parallelism + Isaiah usage → "THAT PROOF WAS WEAK"
                        → only proofs "on which pious minds may SECURELY rest"

The spine in one line: Scripture calls the Spirit God → his indwelling makes us God's temple → lying to him is lying to God; his speaking is the Lord of Hosts' speaking — he is the true Jehovah → grieving him is provoking God; offending him is unforgivable → and a weak proof is dropped, so that faith rests only on solid ones.

Examiner's Eye. ⚠️⚠️⚠️ Four traps. (1) Direction of S2. Premise: the Spirit of God dwells in us. Conclusion: we are the temple of God. Any option saying Paul infers that the Spirit dwells in us from the fact that we are God's temple has flipped the inference. ⚠️ And the argument's hidden premise — only God has a temple — is what makes the Spirit God. (2) S6 not lied unto men, but unto God. ⚠️ The denied limb is men, not the Holy Spirit. Any option saying Ananias lied to God rather than to the Spirit has set the Spirit against God — the exact opposite of Calvin's point (the lie to the Spirit is the lie to God). (3) S11 — the Son-pardon clause. ⚠️⚠️ Scope trap. he who has blasphemed against the Son may obtain pardon does not mean the Spirit ranks above the Son. Calvin's conclusion is only that majesty must certainly be divine — not more divine. An option that ranks the Persons has over-read (see Period 6). (4) S12–S14 — the rejected proof. ⚠️⚠️ Calvin does NOT deny that the Spirit created the world. He denies that Ps 33:6 proves it. Any option saying Calvin rejects the Spirit's role in creation confuses a weak argument with a false conclusion — and Day 90 (Gen 1:2) already gave the Spirit a creation role.

S5★at one time … and at another time, in the same sense, … — the parallel that closes the temple argument›

And the Apostle says at one time that we are the temple of God, and at another time, in the same sense, that we are the temple of the Holy Spirit.

전체 해설 더보기

One point. ⚠️ in the same sense is the whole argument. at one time … at another time = in one place … in another place (1 Cor 3:16 / 1 Cor 6:19) — not two moments in history. Two that-clauses, same verb (says), same predicate (the temple of …), different owner (God / the Holy Spirit) — and Calvin explicitly blocks the escape route Paul meant two different things: in the same sense. Same sense + temples belong to God alone → the Spirit is God. That is the template for S6–S10.

Formula 1 — Inversion after a fronted negative (+ the if-less conditional) · plus the gerund-subject table

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  (A) NEGATIVE FRONTED  →  OPERATOR + SUBJECT + VERB                  │
│      Nor / Neither / Never / Not only / Little / Seldom / No sooner  │
│      NOR  DOES  the Scripture  WITHHOLD  the name of God       (S1)  │
│         ✗ Nor the Scripture withholds …                              │
│         ✗ Nor the Scripture does withhold …                          │
│                                                                       │
│  (B) IF DELETED  →  WERE / HAD / SHOULD + SUBJECT                    │
│      WERE we ordered to make a temple …, it WOULD be …         (S4)  │
│      = If we were ordered …                                           │
│         ✗ If were we ordered …   (inversion REPLACES if)             │
│                                                                       │
│  (C) GERUND SUBJECT — two forms                                       │

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

Where we are. Our §§16–20 are the case for the Spirit's deity. Day 90 set the method (the same sources) and called Genesis 1:2 and Isaiah 48:16. Day 91 gave the argument from works and will. Today gives the argument from name — and closes with a method note. What's left in the Spirit section is short; after it Calvin turns to how the three Persons are distinguished within the one essence.

The argument form: identity by shared predicate. Every proof today has the same shape: Scripture says X of God in one place and X of the Spirit in another, where X belongs to God alone (a temple, the target of a lie that is "not to men", the speaking of prophecy, being provoked/grieved by Israel's rebellion, an unforgivable offence). ⚠️ Guardrail: this does not prove that every sentence about God is about the Spirit as distinct Person. It proves the Spirit is included in the one God named Jehovah. Calvin already proved the Son is Jehovah (§§7–10). One name, one essence — the Persons are distinguished elsewhere (from §21 of ours on).

Augustine (S4). The temple argument is a standard Western anti-Arian proof. Augustine uses it against the Arian bishop Maximinus (the Collatio and Contra Maximinum, c. 427–428). ⚠️ Beveridge prints the reference as Ad Maximinum, Ep. 66. Modern editions number Augustine's letters differently, and the argument as Calvin paraphrases it is usually located in the anti-Maximinus writings rather than a letter. We quote Beveridge's reference as printed; don't treat the letter number as settled. The same temple argument appears earlier in Athanasius (Letters to Serapion) and in Ambrose (On the Holy Spirit) — Calvin is standing in a long line.

Ananias (S6) and the Isaiah 6 transfer (S7). Both are old patristic proofs — Basil and Ambrose use Acts 5:3–4. One detail worth knowing: John 12:39–41 applies the same Isaiah 6 passage to Christ's glory ("These things said Esaias, when he saw his glory"), while Acts 28:25 attributes its words to the Spirit. Isaiah saw the LORD of hosts; John says it was the Son's glory; Paul says the Spirit spoke. Later Reformed writers made much of this triple application. ⚠️ Calvin does not draw that out here — don't put it in his mouth for this section.

dictated the prophecies (S9). ⚠️ Over-reading guardrail. Calvin elsewhere uses "dictation" language for Scripture (the Latin dictare family). Whether he meant a mechanical process (the prophet as a passive stenographer) or used it as a figure for divine authorship is a live scholarly debate — both readings have serious defenders, and his commentaries clearly attend to the human authors' styles and circumstances. In this sentence the only claim needed is: the words of the prophets are the Spirit's words. Stop there.

The unforgivable sin (S11). Matt 12:31–32; Mark 3:28–29; Luke 12:10. Calvin uses it here only as a majesty argument: an inexpiable offence implies a divine object. ⚠️ Guardrails: (1) no ranking of Persons — his conclusion is divine, not greater than the Son; (2) his fuller account (Book III.3.21–24) defines the sin as deliberate, malicious resistance to truth the Spirit has made plain — so the Son-pardon clause concerns ignorance about Christ (e.g. those who opposed him before they understood), not a lower dignity of the Son.

The rejected proof (S12–S14). Psalm 33:6 was a favourite of the fathers — Irenaeus (who calls the Word and Spirit God's "two hands") and Basil (On the Holy Spirit) both read the verse's word and breath as the Son and the Spirit. Calvin rejects the argument, not the doctrine: Hebrew parallelism makes breath of his mouth restate word of the Lord. This is Calvin the humanist exegete: grammar and genre over tradition, even when tradition is on his side. He does the same with Gen 1:26's plural and other favourite Trinitarian proof texts in his commentaries — refusing proofs he thinks won't bear weight, so that opponents cannot mock the doctrine by refuting its weakest supports. ⚠️ Guardrail: Calvin is not saying the Spirit had no part in creation — Day 90 (Gen 1:2, the Spirit … moved upon the face of the waters) already gave him one.

Reception. Calvin's refusal of weak proof texts drew criticism in his own time — some Lutheran opponents (notably Aegidius Hunnius, Calvinus Judaizans, 1593) accused him of "Judaizing" by surrendering classic Trinitarian texts. Modern evangelical and Reformed exegesis largely vindicates his method; the principle those proofs on which pious minds may securely rest became a standard caution against proof-texting.

The one-line takeaway. ⚠️ Today proves the Spirit is God by name and identity of predicate — and shows that a strong doctrine needs strong texts, not many texts.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① 어법 — 밑줄 넷 중 어색한 것 하나

(A) Nor does the Scripture withhold the name of God from the Spirit. All the promises which God makes of choosing us to himself as a temple (B) receive their only fulfilment by his Spirit dwelling in us. Words (C) said by the prophets to have been spoken by the Lord of Hosts are ascribed to the Holy Spirit. So that majesty must certainly be divine (D) which it is an inexpiable crime to offend it.

✨ 답안 보기 (클릭)정답: (D).

출제 의도. ⚠️ Formula 3 — the gap. which is already the object of offend; adding it fills the gap twice (resumptive pronoun). ✗. Correct: which it is an inexpiable crime to offend. ⚠️ The trap: the dummy it earlier in the clause makes a second it feel "balanced". It isn't. Why the others are sound. (A) fronted Nor + operator inversion — Formula 1(A). (B) plural receive agrees with promises, not the decoy temple right before it. (C) reduced relative + perfect passive infinitive (said … to have been spoken) — ⚠️ the "feels wrong, is right" option.

② 내용일치 — 다음 중 §18의 내용과 일치하는 것은?

(1) Calvin argues that because blasphemy against the Son can be pardoned but blasphemy against the Spirit cannot, the Spirit is of higher rank than the Son. (2) Calvin endorses the fathers' use of Psalm 33:6 as a sound proof that the Spirit created the world. (3) From the fact that the Spirit of God dwells in us, Paul infers that we are the temple of God — an inference that presupposes the Spirit's deity. (4) According to Calvin, Peter told Ananias that he had lied to God rather than to the Holy Spirit.

✨ 답안 보기 (클릭)정답: (3).

출제 의도. (1) ✗ Scope overreach. Calvin concludes only that majesty must certainly be divine — no ranking of Persons (Period 6). (2) ✗ Direction flip. Calvin omits the passage and calls the proof weak (S12, S14). (3) ✓ S2 exactly — premise indwelling, conclusion God's temple; and S4 (such worship is due to God alone) supplies the premise that makes it an argument for the Spirit's deity. 9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary. Nor does the Scripture … withhold the name of God from the Spirit: Paul infers we are God's temple from the Spirit's dwelling, and God's temple-promises find their only fulfilment there — how much clearer a proof, says Augustine, in that we are not to make a temple to him, but to be ourselves that temple; the lie to the Spirit is not lied unto men, but unto God; the Lord of Hosts' words are the Spirit's words, so the Spirit is the true Jehovah; in place of God provoked, Isaiah writes the Spirit grieved; and that majesty must certainly be divine which it is an inexpiable crime to offend — while Psalm 33:6, the fathers' favourite, is designedly dropped because parallelism makes that proof … weak.

The three grammar points, once more. ① Fronted negative → operator inversion — Nor does the Scripture withhold; and if-less Were we ordered …, it would be …; plus the gerund-subject pair his dwelling / his Spirit dwelling. ② not A, but B kills A (not to make … but to be, not lied unto men, but unto God) — look for only; plus how much clearer a proof (adjective before a) and in that = because. ③ Separated relative + it is … to V ___ — that majesty … which it is an inexpiable crime to offend ___ — no resumptive it; inside the frame while …, whereas …, MAIN.
