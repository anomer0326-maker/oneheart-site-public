0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday's homework left you holding a live grenade. Here it was:

§9 argued "the text was never interrupted" — hand to hand, unbroken series, double deposit. But §10's subject is an event in which the text was burned. How does Calvin reconcile an obvious interruption with yesterday's continuity argument?

And I gave you the hint: look at §9's S7 — the double deposit. If one copy burns, the chain does not break.

Open today's section and watch Calvin do exactly that — but bigger. He does not deny the burning. ⚠️ Underline this before anything else: Calvin CONCEDES Antiochus's edict. No quibbling about whether it happened. Instead he asks one devastating counter-question (S4): the books were back, complete and undisputed, the moment the persecution ceased (S5) — so in what workshop were they forged, and how fast? ★ The objection assumed a single-copy world — burn the book, kill the book. But §9 already built a many-copy world: temple original, royal archive, every household with the Psalms in its hands. In a many-copy world, fire proves nothing — except providence. The objector's own story becomes Calvin's best exhibit. That is why S1 dares to say the objection is fitted the best possible to confirm it.

And watch the shape of the section, because it does something §9 did not. Seventeen sentences, two paragraphs, and a hinge in the middle:

One more thing before the Big Points. Count the questions. Five, in seventeen sentences: S3 (theirs), S4, S8, S10, S16 (all his). ⚠️ Every one of Calvin's questions is an assertion wearing a question mark — could not possibly have been forged (S4), they do nothing but betray (S8), everyone recognises (S10), the Jews — of all people (S16→S17). ★ When Calvin asks, he is not asking. Train yourself to convert each question into the statement it performs.

Today's 3 Big Points — write them down now:

  1. S1's anatomy: a twelve-word subject, a verb locked in commas, and a superlative that is secretly an adverb. An objection (taken from the history of the Maccabees) (to impugn the credibility of Scripture), **is**, (on the contrary,) fitted **the best possible** to confirm it. ⚠️ Trap (a): the subject head is An objection — everything until the comma is stacked postmodifiers (past-participle phrase + purpose infinitive). Cover them with your hand and the verb is appears. ⚠️ Trap (b): , is, — the commas bracket on the contrary, not an error. ⚠️ Trap (c): the best possible is NOT a noun. Restore: fitted ‹in› the best ‹manner› possible to confirm it — a superlative adverbial without -ly. Read it as a noun and the sentence has two objects and no sense. I promised you this one yesterday. Here it is.
  2. The modal-perfect museum: three modal + have + p.p., three different jobs. ★ S4 **could** they **have been** so quickly **fabricated**? = past possibility denied by rhetorical question (= they could not possibly have been). ★ S9 though it **should have been purchased** at the expense of their lives = concessive subjunctive (= even if it had to be bought with their lives — ⚠️ NOT the modern "ought to have" of regret). ★ S13 it **must have** utterly **perished** = counterfactual certainty in the apodosis of an if-deleted conditional (had not God provided...) (= would inevitably have perished — ⚠️ NOT deductive "evidently did perish"). Same skeleton, three values. The modal decides nothing by itself; the frame around it decides everything.
  3. Clause-scale comparison: Nor does it seem so wonderful that A, as that B (S12). The so ... as correlative you know from adjectives — now stretched across two entire that-clauses, with Nor-inversion on top (third Nor in two days — Day 45's Big Point 2 keeps paying rent). Meaning: not so much A as B — B is the GREATER wonder. ⚠️ Direction trap: A is the dramatic rescue from Antiochus; B is the undramatic survival through centuries of disasters. Calvin ranks the slow providence above the spectacular one. Flip the direction and you flip his theology. ★ Bonus continuity: S13's had not God provided is yesterday's if-deletion family (were / had / should) — negative version, not riding in front of the subject.

Seventeen sentences. S1, S4, S8, S9, S10 and S12 will take real time. Let's go.

1 — Full Text (the whole § — 17 sentences, verbatim) –

10. An objection taken from the history of the Maccabees (1 Macc. 1:57, 58) to impugn the credibility of Scripture, is, on the contrary, fitted the best possible to confirm it. First, however, let us clear away the gloss which is put upon it: having done so, we shall turn the engine which they erect against us upon themselves. As Antiochus ordered all the books of Scripture to be burnt, it is asked, where did the copies we now have come from? I, in my turn, ask, In what workshop could they have been so quickly fabricated? It is certain that they were in existence the moment the persecution ceased, and that they were acknowledged without dispute by all the pious who had been educated in their doctrine, and were familiarly acquainted with them. Nay, while all the wicked so wantonly insulted the Jews as if they had leagued together for the purpose, not one ever dared to charge them with having introduced spurious books. Whatever, in their opinion, the Jewish religion might be, they acknowledged that Moses was the founder of it. What, then, do those babblers, but betray their snarling petulance in falsely alleging the spuriousness of books whose sacred antiquity is proved by the consent of all history? But not to spend labour in vain in refuting these vile calumnies, let us rather attend to the care which the Lord took to preserve his Word, when against all hope he rescued it from the truculence of a most cruel tyrant as from the midst of the flames—inspiring pious priests and others with such constancy that they hesitated not, though it should have been purchased at the expense of their lives, to transmit this treasure to posterity, and defeating the keenest search of prefects and their satellites. Who does not recognise it as a signal and miraculous work of God, that those sacred monuments which the ungodly persuaded themselves had utterly perished, immediately returned to resume their former rights, and, indeed, in greater honour? For the Greek translation appeared to disseminate them over the whole world. Nor does it seem so wonderful that God rescued the tables of his covenant from the sanguinary edicts of Antiochus, as that they remained safe and entire amid the manifold disasters by which the Jewish nation was occasionally crushed, devastated, and almost exterminated. The Hebrew language was in no estimation, and almost unknown; and assuredly, had not God provided for religion, it must have utterly perished. For it is obvious from the prophetical writings of that age, how much the Jews, after their return from the captivity, had lost the genuine use of their native tongue. It is of importance to attend to this, because the comparison more clearly establishes the antiquity of the Law and the Prophets. And whom did God employ to preserve the doctrine of salvation contained in the Law and the Prophets, that Christ might manifest it in its own time? ① 최다 출제 예상: S12 의 방향. ⚠️ 선지에 이런 게 나온다: "Calvin regards the rescue of the Scriptures from Antiochus as a greater miracle than their survival through the nation's later disasters." ✗ 정확히 거꾸로다. Nor does it seem **so** wonderful that ‹rescue›, **as that** ‹survival› — not so A as B 에서 무게는 항상 B 쪽이다. ★ 극적인 한 번의 구출보다 눈에 띄지 않는 수백 년의 보존이 더 큰 기적 — 이것이 칼빈의 섭리론적 감각이다(훗날 I.16–17 의 예고편).

② 두 번째 함정: "칼빈은 분서(焚書)가 없었다고 논증한다." ⚠️ 아니다 — 그는 소각 칙령을 그대로 인정한다(S3 의 As Antiochus ordered... 는 사실로 전제된 절이다). ★ 그의 논증은 사건 이후에서 출발한다: 박해가 그치는 순간 사본들이 있었고(S5), 아무도 위조를 주장하지 않았다(S6). 부정이 아니라 재해석 — S2 의 turn the engine 그대로.

③ 세 번째 함정: S17 의 had not the use. ⚠️ "the Jews did not use their own books" 로 읽으면 틀린다. 유대인들은 그 책을 읽고, 지키고, 필사했다 — 칼빈이 말하는 use 는 구원론적 유익(usus)이다: 소유하되 누리지 못함. ★ 어거스틴의 이미지 그대로: 책가방을 메고 가지만 읽지는 못하는 노예(Period 6). 선지가 possession 과 use 를 바꿔치기하면 그 자리에서 잡아내라.

④ 네 번째 함정: S11 의 appeared to disseminate. ⚠️ "the Greek translation SEEMED to spread them" ✗ — appear 의 인식 용법(~인 듯하다)이 아니라 등장 용법(나타나다)이다. 칠십인역이 출현했고, 그 결과가 온 세상 전파다. "~인 듯 보였다"로 읽으면 논증이 무너진다 — 칼빈은 지금 사실을 쌓는 중이다. 판별법은 S11 에서.

Formula 1 — 무거운 주어의 해부 + 부사적 최상급 the best possible

   [ N ( p.p.구 ) ( to-V구 ) ] , + V단수 , ( 삽입부사구, ) + fitted
   the best possible to V
   ────────────────────────────────────────────────
   ① 주어 머리(head)는 첫 명사 — 수식어를 손으로 가려 동사를 찾아라.
   ② ⚠️ 동사의 수는 머리를 따른다. 동사 옆의 복수 명사는 미끼다.

      부사구다. 명사로 읽으면 수동태 fitted 가 목적어를 갖게 된다. ✗
   ④ `possible` 후치 — 명사 소거의 흔적.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The event. 167 BC. Antiochus IV — self-styled Epiphanēs, "god manifest"; nicknamed by his subjects Epimanēs, "the madman" — outlaws Judaism in Judea: sabbath, circumcision, and the books. 1 Macc 1:56–57 (Vulgate 1:57–58, hence Calvin's citation): the books of the law are torn and burned, and possession is a capital crime. Three years later the Maccabean revolt retakes the temple; the books are back in public use, complete, undisputed. That gap — capital ban to instant restoration — is Calvin's entire argument in S4–S5.

Who is Calvin actually answering? Not Antiochus, and not the Jews. The objection — "the originals burned, so today's text is a Hellenistic reconstruction" — circulated among the 16th-century libertines and "Epicureans" Calvin fought in Geneva (the same miscreants who mutter in corners, §9 S1), with ancient pedigree in Porphyry, the 3rd-century anti-Christian philosopher who had already tried to re-date Daniel to the Maccabean period. ★ Note Calvin's weapon choice: he answers from 1 Maccabees — a book he does NOT regard as canonical (he will say so flatly against Trent, which canonized it in 1546). He argues from the opponents' own accepted history. Concede the source, keep the logic, win the field — the turn the engine method, applied even to the canon question. ⚠️ Over-reading guardrail #1: do not conclude that Calvin "uses the Apocrypha as Scripture." He uses it as history — precisely the status the objection itself gave it.

The Septuagint (S11). The Greek translation of the Hebrew Bible, made in Alexandria from the 3rd century BC. The legend (Letter of Aristeas, embroidered by later writers): seventy-two translators, miraculous agreement. Augustine took the miracle seriously; Jerome, the better philologist, did not. ⚠️ Watch Calvin's restraint: no seventy translators, no miracle-story — just the Greek translation appeared to disseminate them over the whole world. He banks the fact of dissemination and leaves the legend on the table. That restraint is itself a lesson in how he handles secondary helps: maximum argumentative yield, minimum legendary liability.

Augustine's librarians (S17). Enarrationes in Psalmos 56.9 (on Ps 57): the Jew carries the codex from which the Christian believes — like the capsarius, the slave who carried the young master's satchel of books to a school he himself never entered. Augustine's harder prooftext elsewhere is Ps 59:11, "Slay them not, lest my people forget." ⚠️ Over-reading guardrail #2 — handle this history with both hands. In Augustine and in Calvin the point is argumentative (hostile custody certifies authenticity — the books' best witnesses had no motive to help Christianity); the "witness people" tradition also fed centuries of Christian contempt for Jews. Read S17 for what it claims — providential preservation through, not despite, Israel — and remember Paul's fuller frame in Rom 3:1–2 and Rom 9–11: the advantage of the Jew is much every way.

Where this lands in later theology. The claim "God providentially preserved the text" becomes confessional at Westminster (1647): the Scriptures, "by his singular care and providence kept pure in all ages" (WCF 1.8) — this section is the seed. ⚠️ Over-reading guardrail #3: do not conscript Calvin into modern text-criticism debates. He is not claiming autographic perfection for any manuscript family; he is claiming unbroken, providentially guarded custody. And remember where we still are: §§1–13 are the secondary helps. §13 will re-subordinate everything — Maccabees, Septuagint, librarians and all — to the internal testimony of the Spirit (§§4–5 of Ch. 7 already told you why). The engine-turning is brilliant; it is still not the foundation.

① 어법 — 밑줄 친 부분 중 틀린 것은?

An argument (ⓐ)taken from the enemies' silence to impugn the credibility of the Gospels (ⓑ)are, on the contrary, fitted (ⓒ)the best possible to confirm them; for in what workshop (ⓓ)could such books have been so quickly fabricated?

✨ 답안 보기 (클릭)정답: ⓑ (are → is)

출제 의도 해설.

  • ⓑ ⚠️ 오늘의 Formula 1 그대로 — 근접 명사 함정. 주어 머리는 An argument(단수). 동사 바로 앞의 the Gospels(복수)는 수식어 속의 미끼다. 수식어를 손으로 가려라: An argument … is … fitted… ★ S1 에서 예고한 그 문제다.
  • ⓐ 옳다 — 과거분사 후치 수식 (argument [which is] taken from…).
  • ⓒ ⚠️ 맞는데 틀려 보이는 자리 — 그래서 밑줄을 그었다. the best possible = 부사적 최상급 (‹in› the best ‹manner› possible). "명사 같은데?" 하는 순간을 노린 선지. 부사구이므로 수동태 fitted 와 충돌하지 않는다.
  • ⓓ 옳다 — modal perfect ① 형: 수사의문 in what workshop could … have been p.p. = 과거 가능성의 부정.

② 내용일치 — 이 절의 내용과 일치하는 것은?

ⓐ Calvin denies that Antiochus ever succeeded in burning any copies of the Scriptures, since the double deposit in temple and archive kept them out of reach. ⓑ Calvin regards the survival of the Scriptures through the nation's many disasters as a greater wonder than their rescue from Antiochus, and sees a signal providence in the fact that Christ's bitterest enemies preserved the books through which Christ was to be manifested. ⓒ Calvin argues that the Greek translation proves the Hebrew text had perished, since the Jews after the captivity had lost the use of their native tongue. ⓓ Because the enemies of the Jews repeatedly charged them with introducing spurious books, Calvin refutes the charge by appealing to the consent of all history.

✨ 답안 보기 (클릭)정답: ⓑ

출제 의도 해설.

  • ⓐ 범위 초과 + 어제와의 교차 함정. ⚠️ 칼빈은 분서를 부정하지 않는다 — S3 의 As Antiochus ordered… 는 사실로 전제된다. 논증은 사건 이후(S5 의 the moment the persecution ceased)에서 출발한다. ★ double deposit 는 어제(§9 S7)의 논거를 섞어 넣은 미끼 — 그럴듯한 만큼 위험하다. 어느 절의 논거인지까지 물을 수 있다.
  • ⓑ 정답. S12(Nor does it seem so wonderful that … as that … — 잔존 > 구출) + S10(a signal … work of God) + S16–S17(whom did God employ … The Jews, the bitterest enemies of Christ)을 정확히 결합했다. ★ not so A as B 의 방향과 S17 의 역설을 동시에 확인하는 선지.
  • ⓒ 논리 조작. ⚠️ 언어의 쇠퇴(S13–S14)는 본문의 소멸이 아니라 본문의 고대성을 증명하는 재료다(S15 의 the comparison). 칠십인역은 전파의 도구(S11)지 대체물이 아니다. 두 사실을 옳게 인용하며 인과만 뒤집는, 가장 정교한 유형의 오답.
  • ⓓ 방향 뒤집기. ⚠️ S6 이 정반대를 말한다: **not one** ever dared to charge them with having introduced spurious books. 아무도 고발하지 않았다는 침묵이 논거다 — 고발이 있었다는 순간 논증 자체가 사라진다.

③ 영작 — 오늘의 공식을 적용해 옮기시오

문제: 「그 책들이 박해가 그치는 순간 존재하고 있었다는 것은 확실하다. 대체 어느 공방에서 그것들이 그토록 빨리 위조될 수 있었겠는가? 그리고 하나님께서 당신의 말씀을 위해 대비하지 않으셨더라면, 그것은 필연코 완전히 소멸했을 것이다. 그러나 하나님께서 그 책들을 폭군의 칙령에서 구출하신 것보다, 그 숱한 재난 한복판에서 그것들이 온전무결하게 남은 것이 더 놀랍다.」

(조건: It is certain that + the moment 절, Formula 2 ①(In what workshop could … have been p.p.), Formula 2 ③(had not God p.p., … must have p.p.), Formula 3(Nor does it seem so wonderful that A, as that B)을 각각 사용할 것.)

모범 답안:

It is certain that the books were in existence the moment the persecution ceased. In what workshop could they have been so quickly fabricated? And had not God provided for his Word, it must have utterly perished. Nor does it seem so wonderful that God rescued those books from the edicts of the tyrant, as that they remained safe and entire amid so many disasters.

출제 의도 해설. 네 공식이 연속 산문 안에서 값을 유지하는지 본다. 자주 나오는 실패 다섯:

  1. It is certain that the books existed at the moment when… — 문법은 되지만 ⚠️ 오늘의 과제가 아니다. the moment 는 전치사 없이 명사 자체가 접속사로 서야 한다: the moment the persecution ceased.
  2. could they be so quickly fabricated? — ✗ ⚠️ 완료가 빠지면 과거를 쏘지 못한다. 위조는 과거의 (불)가능성이므로 could have been fabricated.
  3. if God had not provided…, it must utterly perish — 조건절은 되지만 ⚠️ 조건은 도치형(had not God provided)으로, 귀결은 modal perfect(must have utterly perished)로 — 둘 다 오늘의 과녁이다.
  4. Nor it seems so wonderful… — ✗ ⚠️ Nor 문두 = 도치. Nor does it seem. 이틀째 같은 자리에서 넘어지는 학생이 제일 많다.
  5. so wonderful that God rescued…, than that they remained… — ✗ ⚠️ so … as 상관구조다. than 은 비교급의 짝이지 so 의 짝이 아니다. as that they remained.

And the three grammar points, restated:

  1. 무거운 주어 + , is, + 부사적 최상급. ★ 주어 머리를 찾고 수식어(p.p.구 + to-V구)를 가려라 — 동사의 수는 머리를 따른다(An objection … is). ⚠️ fitted **the best possible** to confirm it = ‹in› the best ‹manner› possible — 명사 아님, 부사구. possible 후치는 명사 소거의 흔적.
  2. modal + have + p.p. — 골격 하나, 값 셋. ★ 수사의문 + could have been p.p. = 과거 가능성의 부정(S4); though … should have been p.p. = 양보 가정(S9 — ✗ 후회 아님); 도치 조건절 + must have p.p. = 반사실 필연(S13 — ✗ 추론 아님). ⚠️ 조동사가 아니라 둘레(프레임)가 값을 정한다.
  3. Nor does it seem so wonderful that A, as that B. ★ Nor 도치 + 가주어 + that절 두 개에 걸친 so … as = not so much A as B — ⚠️ 무게는 항상 B. 보너스: What do X ‹do›, but V?(S8) = 본동사 do 생략 + but(=except) + 원형부정사 = do nothing but V; had not God provided(S13) = 어제의 if 삭제 도치, 부정어가 주어 앞에 서는 고형(古形).

③ 내일 예습 한 줄. §11 은 이렇게 열린다: When we proceed to the New Testament, how solid are the pillars by which its truth is supported! — ★ 감탄문인데 도치가 있다(how solid ARE the pillars — 정상 감탄문은 how solid the pillars are): 수사적 감탄-의문의 경계선 위 문장이다. 그리고 무대가 바뀐다: 구약 증명 끝, 신약 증명 시작 — 세 복음서의 mean and humble style 이 오히려 하늘의 비밀을 증언하고, 요한은 fulminating in majesty, strikes down more powerfully than any thunderbolt 한다. ⚠️ 준비해 둘 질문 하나: §1–2 에서 칼빈은 성경의 위엄(majesty)을 증거로 삼았다. 그런데 §11 은 복음서의 초라한 문체(simplicity)를 증거로 삼는다. 문체의 낮음이 어떻게 신빙성의 논거가 되는가 — 그리고 이 역설은 오늘 S17 의 역설(적이 보관인이 됨)과 어떤 가족 관계인가? ★ 실마리: 자연적 설명이 막히는 곳에서 섭리적 설명이 선다 — 오늘의 In what workshop…? 과 같은 엔진이다.
