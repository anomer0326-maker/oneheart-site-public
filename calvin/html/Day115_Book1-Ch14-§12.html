Day 115 — Book 1, Chapter 14, §12

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday: God uses angels for our weakness. Today: the rule that keeps that from going wrong. §11 ended on Elisha's servant seeing the chariots and taking courage. A sharp student asks: "So are angels something I should look to?" §12 gives Calvin's one-sentence test: angels must conduct us directly to God — if they pull our eyes toward themselves, the doctrine has been turned upside down. Then comes the memorable image: a ladder, angels going up and down, and Christ at the top.

Why examiners love it. Eight sentences, and every one is a syntax drill: a resultative let us employ… for the purpose of V-ing (S1), a hortative negative let us not be terrified… but let us adopt with as if and archaic notwithstanding of (S2), a fronted exclamatory How preposterous… is it to V (S3), a triple if-chain with dash appositions (S4), the long-delayed-subject concession Accordingly, the servant of Abraham, though he had been commended…, does not… (S6), a correlative As… not…, so… not… with two that…may purposes (S7), and the fronted Away, then, with… (S8). The traps are direction-flips: angels are not denied (they help), they are not mediators (Christ is), and the target is Platonic mediation, not angelic ministry itself.

Text traps. ⚠️ notwithstanding of (S2) = in spite of. ⚠️ sentiment (S2) = opinion, conviction, saying. ⚠️ preposterous (S3) = absurd, backwards (lit. "before-behind"). ⚠️ conduct (S4) = lead. ⚠️ recline (S4) = lean, rest one's weight. ⚠️ commended (S6) = entrusted (not "praised"). ⚠️ instrumentality (S7) = agency, means. ⚠️ courting (S8) = fawning on, wooing. ⚠️ propitious (S8) = favourable, well-disposed.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. How preposterous, therefore, is it to allow ourselves to be led away…? (S3) → exclamatory-rhetorical How + adj + is it to V with fronted complement and inversion; allow ourselves to be led away = causative-passive chain. Formula 1.
  2. But we are so led away, if angels do not conduct us… — if they are not regarded… — if they do not direct us… (S4) → negative if-chain: the condition of being led away is failure to lead us to God; three parallel ifs with dash-glosses. Formula 2.
  3. As God does not make angels the ministers…, that he may share his glory with them, so he does not promise… that we may divide our confidence… (S7) → correlative As… , so… with two parallel purpose clauses that… may (negated purpose). Formula 3.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 8 sentences –

Whatever, therefore, is said as to the ministry of angels, let us employ for the purpose of removing all distrust, and strengthening our confidence in God. Since the Lord has provided us with such protection, let us not be terrified at the multitude of our enemies as if they could prevail notwithstanding of his aid, but let us adopt the sentiment of Elisha, that more are for us than against us. How preposterous, therefore, is it to allow ourselves to be led away from God by angels who have been appointed for the very purpose of assuring us of his more immediate presence to help us? But we are so led away, if angels do not conduct us directly to him—making us look to him, invoke and celebrate him as our only defender—if they are not regarded merely as hands moving to our assistance just as he directs—if they do not direct us to Christ as the only Mediator on whom we must wholly depend and recline, looking towards him, and resting in him. Our minds ought to give thorough heed to what Jacob saw in his vision (Gen. 28:12),—angels descending to the earth to men, and again mounting up from men to heaven, by means of a ladder, at the head of which the Lord of Hosts was seated, intimating that it is solely by the intercession of Christ that the ministry of angels extends to us, as he himself declares, “Hereafter ye shall see heaven open, and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man,” (John 1:51). Accordingly, the servant of Abraham, though he had been commended to the guardianship of an angel (Gen. 24:7), does not therefore invoke that angel to be present with him, but trusting to the commendation, pours out his prayers before the Lord, and entreats him to show mercy to Abraham. As God does not make angels the ministers of his power and goodness, that he may share his glory with them, so he does not promise his assistance by their instrumentality, that we may divide our confidence between him and them. Away, then, with that Platonic philosophy of seeking access to God by means of angels and courting them with the view of making God more propitious (Plat. in Epinomide et Cratylo),—a philosophy which presumptuous and superstitious men attempted at first to introduce into our religion, and which they persist in even to this day.

2 — Structure at a Glance – 

[RULE]       S1–S2    Use the angel doctrine to remove distrust and build confidence: more are for us than against us
[CHALLENGE]  S3       How preposterous to be led AWAY from God by those sent to assure us of his presence
[TEST]       S4       We are so led away unless angels (a) point us to God as sole defender, (b) are seen as mere hands
                      moving as he directs, (c) point us to Christ as sole Mediator
[PROOF 1]    S5       Jacob's ladder: angels ascend and descend; the Lord of Hosts sits at the top → only through Christ's intercession
[PROOF 2]    S6       Abraham's servant: commended to an angel, yet prays to the Lord alone
[PRINCIPLE]  S7       God's ministers are not sharers of his glory; his help through them is not a split of our trust
[REJECT]     S8       Away with Platonic mediation — an imported philosophy still persisting

Examiner's Eye. - ⚠️ Target trap (S3–S4): Calvin does not say angels are unhelpful; he says it is absurd to be led away from God by them. Any option saying "ignore angels" is wrong. - ⚠️ Condition trap (S4): we are so led away, **if** angels do not conduct us directly to him — the if clause describes the failure condition. Do not read it as "angels do not lead us to God". - ⚠️ Mediator trap (S4–S5): the only Mediator is Christ; angels are hands moving to our assistance just as he directs. - ⚠️ Servant trap (S6): the servant of Abraham does not invoke the angel; he prays to the Lord — even though he had been commended to the angel's guardianship. - ⚠️ Philosophy trap (S8): the target is Platonic mediation (Plat. in Epinomide et Cratylo), introduced by presumptuous and superstitious men — not Scripture and not the doctrine of angels.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — How + adj + (therefore) is it to V…? (S3)

How  ADJ  is it  to V ...?         [exclamatory/rhetorical; adj fronted → inversion `is it`]
   =  It is  how ADJ  to V ...     =  To V ... is (so) ADJ.

⚠️ Trap. (a) is it is inversion, not an ordinary question. (b) it is a dummy: the real subject is the infinitive. (c) How = to what degree, not in what way. (d) A passive inside the infinitive (to be led away) keeps its agent by…. Drill. It is absurd to trust in angels instead of Christ. → How absurd, therefore, is it to trust in angels instead of Christ!

Formula 2 — the negative if-chain: S are so V-ed, if A do not V…— if B are not V-ed…— if C do not V… (S4)

We are so led away,  if  X1 does not V1   —   if X2 is not V2   —   if X3 does not V3
                      (three parallel conditions; each describes a FAILURE of the right use)

⚠️ Trap. (a) so points back to the preceding sentence (S3's led away). (b) The if-clauses state the condition under which the error occurs — they do not report what angels actually do. (c) Dashes mark appended glosses/participles (making us look…); keep each if clause together. (d) merely as limits the predicate: as nothing more than. Drill. Sacraments mislead us when they do not point us to Christ and are valued for themselves. → We are so misled, if the sacraments do not point us to Christ — if they are valued for themselves, and not as seals of his promise.

Formula 3 — correlative As S does not V1, that S may V2, so S does not V3, that S' may V4 (S7)

As A does not V1 (in order that A may V2),  so  A does not V3 (in order that B may V4)
         [the negation scopes over the that…may purpose; the two halves are parallel]

⚠️ Trap. (a) that… may = purpose (so that… may), inside the negation — the purpose is denied, the act itself may still occur. (b) As… so… = just as… so… (not time as). (c) Keep the two purpose clauses parallel (share his glory with them // divide our confidence between him and them). Drill. God does not use ministers because he needs help, and likewise the Word is not preached so that we may trust the preacher. → As God does not use ministers, that he may supply his own want, so he does not give us the Word, that we may trust the preacher.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The doctrine at stake. §11 said why God uses angels; §12 says how we must use that teaching: angels must always send us to God and to Christ. Calvin is closing two doors at once: distrust (S1–S2: more are for us than against us) and mediation (S3–S8: angels as go-betweens). The key move is S5: the ladder has Christ at the top — Calvin reads Jacob's ladder through John 1:51 (the Son of man), so Christ is the meeting place of heaven and earth.

The argument shape. Rule (S1–S2) → absurdity of misuse (S3) → three tests (S4) → proof 1: Jacob's ladder (S5) → proof 2: Abraham's servant (S6) → principle: God's glory and our confidence are not to be shared or split (S7) → rejection of Platonic mediation (S8). §13 will move from angels to devils with the mirror rule: Scripture's teaching on demons is meant to arouse vigilance.

Historical lineage. The pagan target is the Platonic doctrine of daimones as intermediaries between gods and men — Plato's Epinomis and Cratylus are the loci Calvin cites; the later Neoplatonists (Iamblichus, Proclus) built it into a ladder of spirits. Within Christianity the idea fed the late-antique pseudo-Dionysian hierarchy (already questioned in §4) and, in Calvin's day, the cult of the saints and angels. Colossians 2:18 (worshipping of angels) is the Pauline background (cf. §10 on the Colossians). Augustine had opposed Platonic mediation by pointing to Christ the one Mediator (City of God IX–X, esp. IX.15–17; Confessions X.42).

Later reception. The Heidelberg Catechism (Q&A 94–95) forbids trusting in any creature in place of God; the Westminster Confession (21.2) says religious worship is to be given to God alone, "and not to angels, saints, or any other creature", and ch. 8 puts mediation in Christ alone. Barth (CD III/3, §51) treats angels as servants of God's Word and warns against any angelology that detaches them from Christ. The Reformed principle is often summarized: no mediator but Christ; no honour but to God.

Guardrails against over-reading. (1) Calvin does not deny angelic ministry — S1 says to employ the doctrine for confidence. (2) The ladder image is read christologically by Calvin (John 1:51); he does not claim every detail of the ladder is allegorically fixed. (3) S6 does not say the angel was absent; it says the servant prayed to the Lord, though commended to an angel. (4) The condemnation in S8 targets the Platonic mediation scheme (seeking access through angels, making God favourable), not the existence or helpfulness of angels. (5) The text does not forbid thanking God for angels, only directing worship or trust to them.

(A) Calvin concludes that angels should be ignored because they only distract from God. Answer: (B). Rationale: (A) scope overreach — S1 says the doctrine is to be employed for confidence; S3–S4 target misuse. (C) direction-flip — S8 Away, then, with that Platonic philosophy. 9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –
