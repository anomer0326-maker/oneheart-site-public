Day 109 — Book 1, Chapter 14, §6

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday Calvin gave you the names. Today he tells you the point. Read S1 slowly: "the point on which the Scriptures specially insist." Not the ranks. Not the numbers. Not the creation date. The point is comfort — "to our comfort, and to the confirmation of our faith." Angelology, for Calvin, is pastoral before it is metaphysical. Underline that, because it is the thesis of the whole section.

Why examiners love it. Thirteen sentences, one paragraph, and a catalogue of Bible stories (Hagar, Abraham's servant, Jacob, Israel's camp, Christ, the women at the tomb, Sennacherib's army) — perfect for detail traps ("which patriarch prays for which blessing?"). But the grammar is where marks are lost: a pseudo-cleft with a relative inside a relative (the point on which … is that which …, S1), a namely, that … appositive doing the real work of the sentence, a we are told how … passive with embedded how-clauses (S2), and a fronted object (the protection of those whom he has undertaken to defend he has delegated to his angels, S5).

Text traps. ⚠️ bounty (S1) = generosity, goodness freely given — not a pirate's reward. ⚠️ dispensers = those who weigh out and hand out (L. dispensare) — not "people who get rid of." watch for our safety = keep guard over it. in fine (S10) = in short, to sum up. avengers (S9) = those who punish on God's behalf. encampeth / delivereth = archaic third-person -eth.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. Pseudo-cleft + appositive: the point on which the Scriptures specially insist is that which tends most to our comfort … namely, that angels are the ministers and dispensers of the divine bounty (S1) → Subject = the point (on which the Scriptures specially insist); complement = that which tends… (= the thing that tends…); namely, that … then names the point. Formula 1.
  2. Passive we are told how … + parallel indirect-question clauses: we are told how they watch for our safety, how they undertake our defence, direct our path, and take heed that no evil befall us (S2) → After how, the word order is statement order (they watch), not question order. Formula 2.
  3. Fronted object + as often as + gerund adverbial: the protection of those whom he has undertaken to defend he has delegated to his angels (S5); as often as God was pleased… (S9); In discharging the office of our protectors, they war… (S12) → Restore the base pattern; match the gerund's logical subject to the main subject. Formula 3.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 13 sentences –

But the point on which the Scriptures specially insist is that which tends most to our comfort, and to the confirmation of our faith, namely, that angels are the ministers and dispensers of the divine bounty towards us. Accordingly, we are told how they watch for our safety, how they undertake our defence, direct our path, and take heed that no evil befall us. There are whole passages which relate, in the first instance, to Christ, the Head of the Church, and after him to all believers. “He shall give his angels charge over thee, to keep thee in all thy ways. They shall bear thee up in their hands, lest thou dash thy foot against a stone.” Again, “The angel of the Lord encampeth round about them that fear him, and delivereth them.” By these passages the Lord shows that the protection of those whom he has undertaken to defend he has delegated to his angels. Accordingly, an angel of the Lord consoles Hagar in her flight, and bids her be reconciled to her mistress. Abraham promises to his servant that an angel will be the guide of his journey. Jacob, in blessing Ephraim and Manasseh, prays “The angel which redeemed me from all evil bless the lads.” So an angel was appointed to guard the camp of the Israelites; and as often as God was pleased to deliver Israel from the hands of his enemies, he stirred up avengers by the ministry of angels. Thus, in fine (not to mention more), angels ministered to Christ, and were present with him in all straits. To the women they announced his resurrection; to the disciples they foretold his glorious advent. In discharging the office of our protectors, they war against the devil and all our enemies, and execute vengeance upon those who afflict us. Thus we read that an angel of the Lord, to deliver Jerusalem from siege, slew one hundred and eighty-five thousand men in the camp of the king of Assyria in a single night.

2 — Structure at a Glance – 

[THESIS]     S1      The point Scripture presses: angels = ministers and dispensers of God's bounty to us (comfort + faith)
[SPECIFICS]  S2      They watch, defend, guide, guard against evil
[PROOF-TEXT] S3–S4   Psalm passages: first Christ the Head, then all believers
[PRINCIPLE]  S5      God has DELEGATED the protection of his people to angels
[CASES]      S6–S9   Hagar · Abraham's servant · Jacob's prayer · Israel's camp and avengers
[CHRIST]     S10–S11 Angels ministered to Christ in his straits; announced resurrection and return
[WAR]        S12–S13 Protectors who war against the devil; 185,000 in one night

Examiner's Eye. - ⚠️ Direction-flip (who is protected first?): S3 says the Psalm passages relate "in the first instance, to Christ, the Head of the Church, and after him to all believers." An option claiming they concern believers and not Christ is wrong. - ⚠️ Scope overreach (delegation): S5 says God has delegated protection to angels. It does not say God has withdrawn from it, nor that we may pray to angels. The ground is God's undertaking (has undertaken to defend); the angels are means. - ⚠️ Scope overreach (guardian angel): Nothing in S1–S13 says each believer has one personal angel. That question is §7 (I dare not positively affirm). Do not import it here. - ⚠️ Detail trap (Jacob): the prayer in S8 is for Ephraim and Manasseh ("bless the lads"), spoken by Jacob; the "angel which redeemed me" is the one who has delivered Jacob himself from all evil.

전체 해설 더보기

One point: bids her be reconciled = commands her to be reconciled (bid + bare infinitive; be reconciled is passive, "to become at peace with"). Gen 16:9: "Return to thy mistress, and submit thyself." The angel comforts and directs — both of S2's verbs in one scene.

전체 해설 더보기

One point: promises to his servant that … — the verb takes a recipient (to his servant) plus a that-clause; will be the guide of his journey = will lead him on his way (Gen 24:7, 40). Note that it is Abraham's faith in what God does that Calvin is citing.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — Pseudo-cleft + namely, that … appositive (S1)

[the N (on which S V)] is [that which V …], namely, [that S V …]
the point (on which the Scriptures insist) is that which tends to our comfort, namely, that angels are the ministers of God's bounty
   =  The Scriptures insist on a point. The point tends to our comfort. The point is this: angels are the ministers…

⚠️ Trap. that which = the thing that — do not split it as that (demonstrative) + which. The namely, that… clause is in apposition to the point; it is not a new main clause. Drill. Calvin presses one thing most of all; it strengthens the conscience; that thing is God's fatherly care. → The point on which Calvin most insists is that which strengthens the conscience, namely, that God cares for us as a father.

Formula 2 — Passive be told how S V + statement order after how (S2) / subjunctive after care (S2)

S1 tells us how S2 V1, how S2 V2, V3, and V4.
→  we are told how they watch…, how they undertake…, direct…, and take heed that no evil befall us

⚠️ Trap. After how inside a clause, use statement order: how they watch, never how do they watch. A bare form in the that-clause after take heed (befall, not befalls) is the old subjunctive; modern English prefers will befall / should befall. Drill. Scripture teaches us. God directs our path. God keeps us from evil. → We are taught how God directs our path and takes heed that no evil befall us.

Formula 3 — Fronted object + as often as + gerund adverbial (S5, S9, S12)

[Object (long)] S V [Indirect Obj]    ←  S V Object [Indirect Obj]
the protection of those whom he has undertaken to defend he has delegated to his angels
   =  he has delegated to his angels the protection of those whom he has undertaken to defend

as often as S1 V1, S2 V2   =   whenever S1 V1, S2 V2
In V-ing X, S V …           (S must be the doer of V-ing)

⚠️ Trap. A fronted object makes the sentence look like NP + S + V — find the verb, then ask "who does it?" Also: In discharging…, they war… is fine because they discharge; In discharging the office, the devil is defeated would be a dangling gerund. Drill. He gave the care of the Church to pastors. → The care of the Church he has delegated to pastors; and as often as the flock is in danger, they watch.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The doctrine at stake. S1 is Calvin's pastoral key to the whole doctrine of angels. After §4 closed the door on speculation (Dionysius' hierarchy; the "third heaven" silence) and §5 reinterpreted the names as one office, §6 tells us what Scripture is "specially" interested in: comfort and confirmation of faith by showing that God's care for his people is carried out through means.

The Christological hinge (S3). Calvin's reading of Ps 91 — "in the first instance" Christ, then believers — is the key exegetical move. If the promise is first the Head's, then every believer shares it in him. This is not an incidental remark: it keeps angelology under Christology. (Scripture itself shows Ps 91:11–12 being applied to Christ in the Temptation narratives, Mt 4:6 / Lk 4:10–11.)

Delegation, not abdication (S5). The word delegated is a precise theological choice. The undertaking is God's (has undertaken to defend); the angels are his means (compare Heb 1:14). It protects against two errors at once: that the angels act on their own, and that God needs no means.

Lineage. Pseudo-Dionysius' Celestial Hierarchy (the target of §4) treats angels as a ladder of knowledge; the Reformed tradition, with Calvin, treats them as a ministry of protection and service. Later Reformed writers extended these themes; Karl Barth (CD III/3, §51) takes up the doctrine of angels at length, with a similar insistence that they serve and point beyond themselves to God's action. Be careful with the lineage: Calvin's text here does not discuss guardian angels in the technical sense — that is §7.

Guardrails against over-reading. (1) S5 says delegated, not transferred: no prayer to angels is licensed. (2) S9 and S12–S13 describe angelic judgement acts as narrated in Scripture; they are not a pattern for human vengeance. (3) "Whole passages" (S3) does not say every promise applies to Christ first; it speaks of certain texts, the Psalm 91 case chief among them.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. S1 — Heb 1:14. "Are they not all ministering spirits, sent forth to minister for them who shall be heirs of salvation?" The verse behind "ministers and dispensers of the divine bounty."
  2. S2 — Ps 34:7; Ps 91:11. The "watch/defend" verbs (S2) are the Psalms' own verbs, quoted outright in S4.
  3. S3–S4 — Ps 91:11–12. "He shall give his angels charge over thee … They shall bear thee up in their hands, lest thou dash thy foot against a stone." Calvin reads this "in the first instance" of Christ (cf. the devil's use of it in Mt 4:6; Lk 4:10–11) and then of all believers. Gk (LXX) entelleitai … peri sou ("he will command concerning you").
  4. S4 — Ps 34:7. "The angel of the Lord encampeth round about them that fear him, and delivereth them." The Psalm's own turn from "thee" to "them that fear him."
  5. S6 — Gen 16:7–9. The angel finds Hagar by the fountain in the wilderness: "Return to thy mistress, and submit thyself under her hands."
  6. S7 — Gen 24:7, 40. Abraham to his servant: "He shall send his angel before thee."
  7. S8 — Gen 48:15–16. Jacob's blessing of Ephraim and Manasseh: "The Angel which redeemed me from all evil, bless the lads." (Calvin reads the Redeemer-Angel as the one who has delivered Jacob; see the Christ-as-angel question already raised at §5 S11.)
  8. S9 — Ex 14:19; Judg 2:1; 6:11–12; 13:3. "The angel of God, which went before the camp of Israel"; the deliverers raised up in Judges.
  9. S10 — Mt 4:11; Lk 22:43. "Angels came and ministered unto him" (wilderness); "there appeared an angel unto him from heaven, strengthening him" (Gethsemane).
  10. S11 — Mt 28:5–6; Lk 24:4–7; Acts 1:10–11. The angels at the tomb announce the resurrection; two men in white "shall so come in like manner."
  11. S13 — 2 Kgs 19:35; Isa 37:36. "The angel of the Lord went out, and smote in the camp of the Assyrians a hundred fourscore and five thousand."

How Calvin uses Scripture: he lets Scripture itself pick the examples. S4 gives two Psalm texts, S6–S13 give narrative evidence; no extra-biblical tradition is adduced here — the contrast with Dionysius is the entire point.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① Grammar — one underlined part is wrong. Which?

Calvin says (A) the point on which the Scriptures specially insist is that which tends to our comfort; we are told (B) how do they watch for our safety; (C) the protection of those whom he has undertaken to defend he has delegated to his angels; and (D) in discharging the office of our protectors, they war against the devil.

Answer: (B). Rationale: inside an embedded how-clause the word order is statement order: how they watch for our safety (Formula 2). (A) is the S1 pseudo-cleft (Formula 1); (C) is the S5 fronted object (Formula 3) and whom correctly stays the object of to defend; (D) is correct because the logical subject of discharging is they (Formula 3).

② Comprehension — which statement is consistent with §6?

(A) Calvin holds that the Psalm passages about angelic protection concern believers only, and that Christ stands outside them. (B) Calvin teaches that each believer has exactly one personal guardian angel, as the examples of Hagar and Jacob prove. (C) Calvin says Scripture especially stresses, for our comfort and the confirmation of our faith, that angels are ministers of God's bounty, and that God has delegated the protection of those he has undertaken to defend to his angels. (D) Calvin says angels, being delegated by God, may be addressed in prayer in place of God.

Answer: (C). Rationale: (A) direction-flip — S3: "in the first instance, to Christ … and after him to all believers." (B) scope overreach — the single-guardian question is §7 (I dare not positively affirm). (D) scope overreach / direction-flip — nothing in S5 licenses prayer to angels; delegated describes God's use of means, and the undertaking remains his.

③ Composition — use Formula 3 (and Formula 1 if you can).

Compose one English sentence (≈20 words): God has promised to defend his people. He has given the care of them to angels. Scripture especially stresses this, and it comforts us.

Model answer: The point on which Scripture especially insists is that which comforts us, namely, that the protection of those whom God has undertaken to defend he has delegated to his angels. Examiner's intent: pseudo-cleft + namely, that… appositive + fronted object with nested whom relative. Alternative accepted: As often as God was pleased to deliver his people, he stirred up avengers by the ministry of angels, who, in discharging the office of our protectors, war against our enemies. (Formula 3: as often as + subject-matched gerund.)

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary: The point Scripture most presses — for our comfort and the confirmation of our faith — is that angels are God's ministers and dispensers of his bounty: God, who has undertaken to defend his own, delegates that protection to angels, as the Psalms (first of Christ, then of believers) and the stories of Hagar, Jacob, Israel, Christ and Jerusalem all show; the grammar keys are the pseudo-cleft with namely, that (S1), the be told how S V statement order (S2), and the fronted object with as often as / subject-matched gerund (S5, S9, S12).
