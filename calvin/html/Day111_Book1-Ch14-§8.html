Day 111 — Book 1, Chapter 14, §8

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday: "Does each of us have one angel?" — Calvin: "I dare not positively affirm." Today the same discipline gets turned on a bigger target: the org chart of heaven. Medieval theology had a detailed chart — nine orders, three triads, seraphim at the top. Calvin opens the section with a warning aimed at the people who drew it: "would do well to consider on what foundation they rest." Then he does something clever. He doesn't deny anything. He walks through every text the chart-makers could use — Michael, the archangel's trumpet, the names, the legions, the chariots, the wings — and shows what each one actually delivers. Answer: comfort and assurance, yes; a table of ranks, no. The last sentence is the rule: class them among mysterious subjects… refrain from inquiring too curiously, or talking presumptuously.

Why examiners love it. Nine sentences, a dense run of Bible references (Daniel, Jude, 1 Thessalonians, Tobit, Matthew, 2 Kings, Psalm 34), and an argument with a built-in direction-flip trap: Calvin says the names and wings are accommodation — not that angels are fictions or have no real rank. And he says Scripture shows many — not that it gives a count. Plus a rhetorical question that means "no one can" (S4), a concession buried in a though-clause (S5), a litotes (not without cause, S8), and a closing relative clause with the full revelation of which (S9).

Text traps. ⚠️ dogmatize (S1) = to assert positively, as settled doctrine — not "to be dogmatic about food." ⚠️ ascertain (S4) = find out for certain — not "to make sure of." ⚠️ insignia (S4) = badges of rank (pl. of L. insigne) — treat as a plural noun here. ⚠️ accommodation (S5, S8) = adaptation to our limited capacity (Calvin's key word). ⚠️ myriads (S6) = ten-thousands (Gk myrias) — an exact number word used as "countless." ⚠️ curiously (S9) = with excessive, meddling inquisitiveness (L. curiosus) — a vice word, not "interestingly." ⚠️ Farther (S9) = further (older spelling variant).

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. Those who presume to V … would do well to V … (S1) → a long plural subject with a who-relative, then a comma before the verb (Beveridge's punctuation), then the polite-warning idiom would do well to = had better. Find the main verb would do well before you read the relative clause. Formula 1.
  2. how is it possible … to ascertain … to determine …, and assign …? (S4) → dummy it; the real subject is the infinitive chain; the question is rhetorical (= "it is not possible"). Three parallel infinitives: ascertain / determine / assign. Formula 2.
  3. the full revelation of which is deferred … + let us class them among …, and … refrain from … (S9) → of which attaches to the noun subjects; the verb agrees with revelation, not with subjects. Then a let us imperative with two coordinated verbs. Formula 3.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 9 sentences –

Those who presume to dogmatize on the ranks and numbers of angels, would do well to consider on what foundation they rest. As to their rank, I admit that Michael is described by David as a mighty Prince, and by Jude as an Archangel. Paul also tells us, that an archangel will blow the trumpet which is to summon the world to Judgment. But how is it possible from such passages to ascertain the gradations of honour among the angels to determine the insignia, and assign the place and station of each? Even the two names, Michael and Gabriel, mentioned in Scripture, or a third, if you choose to add it from the history of Tobit, seem to intimate by their meaning that they are given to angels in accommodation to the weakness of our capacity, though I rather choose not to speak positively on the point. As to the number of angels, we learn from the mouth of our Saviour that there are many legions, and from Daniel that there are many myriads. Elisha’s servant saw a multitude of chariots, and their vast number is declared by the fact, that they encamp round about those that fear the Lord. It is certain that spirits have no bodily shape, and yet Scripture, in accommodation to us, describes them under the form of winged Cherubim and Seraphim; not without cause, to assure us that when occasion requires, they will hasten to our aid with incredible swiftness, winging their way to us with the speed of lightning. Farther than this, in regard both to the ranks and numbers of angels, let us class them among those mysterious subjects, the full revelation of which is deferred to the last day, and accordingly refrain from inquiring too curiously, or talking presumptuously.

2 — Structure at a Glance – 

[WARNING]    S1      Dogmatizers on ranks & numbers: check your foundation
[RANK]       S2–S5   What Scripture gives: Michael "prince/archangel" (S2), archangel's trumpet (S3)
                     → but NOT a table of gradations, insignia, stations (S4)
                     → even the names (Michael, Gabriel, [Raphael]) are accommodation (S5)
[NUMBER]     S6–S8   What Scripture gives: many legions, many myriads (S6); chariots, encamping (S7)
                     → wings of Cherubim/Seraphim = accommodation: speed of help (S8)
[RULE]       S9      Beyond this: mysterious; revelation deferred to the last day → don't inquire curiously or talk presumptuously

Examiner's Eye. - ⚠️ Direction-flip (denial vs. restraint): Calvin does not deny that Michael is a prince/archangel or that angels are many (S2, S6). He denies that these texts yield a hierarchy (S4). An option "Calvin denies angels have any rank or number" is wrong. - ⚠️ Detail trap (S5): the names are said to be given "in accommodation to the weakness of our capacity" — but the sentence ends though I rather choose not to speak positively on the point. It is a hedged claim ("seem to intimate"), not a doctrine. - ⚠️ Scope overreach (S8): "no bodily shape" refers to spirits (It is certain). Cherubim and Seraphim with wings are Scripture's accommodated depiction, whose purpose is to show swiftness of help — not to say angels literally have wings. - ⚠️ Detail trap (S3): it is Paul (not David or Jude) who speaks of the archangel's trumpet; David (S2) gives "mighty Prince," Jude gives "Archangel." - ⚠️ Scope overreach (S9): the rule is about ranks and numbers ("in regard both to the ranks and numbers of angels"), not about angels in general — the ministering function is affirmed in §9.

3 — Sentence-by-Sentence Live Teaching –

S1★★Those who presume to dogmatize … would do well to consider … — the warning idiom›

Those who presume to dogmatize on the ranks and numbers of angels, would do well to consider on what foundation they rest.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • dogmatize < Gk dogma(opinion, decree) + -ize; presume < L. prae- + sumere(take beforehand) → "take upon oneself"; foundation < L. fundus(bottom).
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • would do well to V = had better V; presume to V = dare, take it upon oneself to V.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

People who claim to teach firm doctrines about the ranks and numbers of angels should ask themselves what evidence they are building on.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • presume to dogmatize → take it upon themselves to lay down as doctrine
  • would do well to consider → should think about
  • rest → are based.
S2★★As to their rank, I admit that … — concession with gapping›

As to their rank, I admit that Michael is described by David as a mighty Prince, and by Jude as an Archangel.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • archangel < Gk arch-(chief) + angelos(messenger); Michael = Heb. mî-kā-ʾēl "who is like God?".
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • as to X = regarding X; I admit that = I grant that.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

Regarding rank, I grant that David calls Michael a mighty prince and Jude calls him an archangel.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • As to → regarding
  • I admit that → I grant that
  • is described … as → is called.
S3★Paul's trumpet — 1 Thess 4:16›

Paul also tells us, that an archangel will blow the trumpet which is to summon the world to Judgment.

전체 해설 더보기

One point: which is to summon = which is destined to summon (be + to V of destiny/plan) — the trumpet at the last day. Note the loose paraphrase: 1 Thess 4:16 has "the voice of the archangel, and … the trump of God" — Calvin summarises it as the archangel's trumpet. He only needs: an archangel has a role at the end.

S4★★★⚠️⚠️ how is it possible … to ascertain … to determine …, and assign …? — rhetorical question; infinitive chain›

But how is it possible from such passages to ascertain the gradations of honour among the angels to determine the insignia, and assign the place and station of each?

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • ascertain < O.Fr. a- + certain (make certain); gradation < L. gradus(step) → grade, degree; insignia < L. in- + signum(mark); station < L. stare(stand).
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • how is it possible to V? = (rhetorical) it is not possible to V; place and station = rank and standing.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

But how could anyone work out from such passages the levels of honour among the angels, fix their badges of rank, and assign each one's place?

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • how is it possible … to ascertain → how could anyone find out
  • gradations of honour → levels of rank
  • insignia → badges of rank
  • station → post.
S5★★★⚠️⚠️ long subject + inserted or a third, if you choose to add it + though clause›

Even the two names, Michael and Gabriel, mentioned in Scripture, or a third, if you choose to add it from the history of Tobit, seem to intimate by their meaning that they are given to angels in accommodation to the weakness of our capacity, though I rather choose not to speak positively on the point.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • intimate < L. intimare(announce) ← intimus(innermost); capacity < L. capax(able to hold); accommodation < L. ad- + commodus(fitting) → commodity, accommodate.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • seem to intimate that … = appear to suggest that …; speak positively on the point = make a definite statement about it.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

Even the two names found in Scripture — Michael and Gabriel, or a third, Raphael, if you count Tobit — seem to be hinting through their meanings that they are given to angels to suit our limited understanding, though I would rather not claim that for certain.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • intimate → hint
  • in accommodation to → to suit
  • weakness of our capacity → limits of our understanding
  • rather choose not to speak positively → prefer not to claim definitively
  • the history of Tobit → the book of Tobit.
S6★★As to the number … we learn … that … and from Daniel that … — two sources, gapping›

As to the number of angels, we learn from the mouth of our Saviour that there are many legions, and from Daniel that there are many myriads.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • legion < L. legio(levy; a Roman unit of ~6,000); myriad < Gk myrias(10,000).
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • from the mouth of X = on X's own testimony; as to X = regarding X.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

As for how many angels there are, Christ tells us there are many legions and Daniel tells us there are many thousands upon thousands.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • As to → regarding
  • from the mouth of → from the words of
  • myriads → ten-thousands (countless thousands).
S7★Elisha's chariots — 2 Kgs 6:17; Ps 34:7›

Elisha’s servant saw a multitude of chariots, and their vast number is declared by the fact, that they encamp round about those that fear the Lord.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • encamp — en- + camp; cf. campus (field); 진을 치다.
전체 해설 더보기

One point: their vast number is declared by the fact, that they encamp … — the number is inferred from the verb: an army must be large to encamp round about (Ps 34:7) a whole people. the fact, that … = appositive clause. those that fear the Lord = those who fear the Lord (older relative that for persons).

S8★★★⚠️⚠️ It is certain that …, and yet …; not without cause, to assure us that … — accommodation of wings›

It is certain that spirits have no bodily shape, and yet Scripture, in accommodation to us, describes them under the form of winged Cherubim and Seraphim; not without cause, to assure us that when occasion requires, they will hasten to our aid with incredible swiftness, winging their way to us with the speed of lightning.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • swiftness < OE swift; incredible < L. in- + credere(believe); cherubim / seraphim = Heb. plurals in -im (sg. cherub, seraph "burning one").
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • not without cause = for good reason; when occasion requires = when the need arises; wing one's way = fly.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

We know spirits have no physical bodies, yet Scripture pictures them as winged cherubim and seraphim, and for good reason: to assure us that when we need help they will rush to us with amazing speed, flying like lightning.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • in accommodation to us → to suit our understanding
  • not without cause → for good reason
  • when occasion requires → when the need arises
  • winging their way → flying.
S9★★★⚠️⚠️ Farther than this, … let us class them among those mysterious subjects, the full revelation of which is deferred … — the closing rule›

Farther than this, in regard both to the ranks and numbers of angels, let us class them among those mysterious subjects, the full revelation of which is deferred to the last day, and accordingly refrain from inquiring too curiously, or talking presumptuously.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • mysterious < Gk mysterion(secret rite); deferred < L. de- + ferre(carry away → put off) — cf. defer, differ; curiously < L. cura(care) → anxious, over-eager care; presumptuously < L. prae- + sumere.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • in regard to X = concerning X; class A among B = put A in the category B; refrain from V-ing = abstain from V-ing.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

Beyond this, about both the ranks and the numbers of angels, let us treat them as mysteries that will be fully revealed on the last day, and so refrain from prying too much or speaking too boldly.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • Farther → further
  • class them among → treat them as
  • deferred → postponed
  • inquiring too curiously → prying too much
  • presumptuously → arrogantly.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — Those who V …, would do well to V (S1)

[Those (who presume to V …)] (,) would do well to V [wh-clause].
   =  People who presume to V … had better V …

⚠️ Trap. (a) The subject is long; the real predicate would do well comes after the relative clause. (b) Beveridge's comma between subject and verb is old punctuation — ignore it for parsing. (c) do well here is not "perform admirably"; it's the idiom would do well to = had better. Drill. Anyone who claims to know the day of the Lord's return should be careful about the basis. → Those who claim to know the day of the Lord's return would do well to consider on what basis they speak.

Formula 2 — Dummy it + infinitive chain in a rhetorical question (S4)

How is it possible (from X) to V1 A, (to) V2 B, and (to) V3 C?
   =  To V1 A, V2 B, and V3 C is not possible from X.

⚠️ Trap. (a) Rhetorical: the answer is no. (b) The real subject is the whole infinitive chain, not it. (c) The final and links the last two infinitives; the earlier link may lack a comma in older print. Keep the three verbs parallel in form. Drill. Nobody can read the day of judgment from these verses, calculate the hour, or fix the date. → How is it possible from these verses to read the day of judgment, calculate the hour, and fix the date?

Formula 3 — NP, the N of which is V-ed + let us V1 … and (accordingly) V2 (S9)

let us V1 O among [those N (,) the N2 of which is p.p. …], and accordingly V2 …

⚠️ Trap. (a) In the full revelation of which is deferred, the verb agrees with revelation (singular), not with the antecedent subjects. (b) of which = of those subjects. (c) let us + two verbs: class and refrain (both after let us; the second without a repeated us). Don't read refrain as a new main clause. Drill. Let us put the timing of the end among mysteries; the full explanation of them is postponed to the last day. Let us stop speculating. → Let us class the timing of the end among those mysteries, the full explanation of which is postponed to the last day, and accordingly refrain from speculating.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The doctrine at stake. Angelology as a system: how many orders, who ranks above whom. The most influential chart is Pseudo-Dionysius, The Celestial Hierarchy (c. 500): nine orders in three triads — Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones / Dominions, Virtues, Powers / Principalities, Archangels, Angels — built partly from Pauline lists (Eph 1:21; Col 1:16). Gregory the Great and Aquinas (ST I, q. 108) took it over. Calvin's reply (here and in §4–5, with S4 being the sharp edge): the names in Scripture (thrones, powers, principalities) describe the one office of service, not a ladder of ranks. Today's section is the method behind that reply: read each text for what it delivers, and stop there.

The method (S2–S8). For each candidate text, Calvin names what it does give: Michael is a prince/archangel (S2); an archangel has a role at the last day (S3); angels are called by names that signify (S5); there are legions and myriads (S6); a chariot-army encamps (S7); wings signify speed (S8). He then says what it does not give: a table (S4). Then the rule (S9).

Accommodation. S5 and S8 are two of Calvin's clearest uses of the word. Spirits have no body (S8) — Scripture gives them wings for us. Names (S5) are meaning-laden for us. The key point: accommodation is not pretence; it is God stooping to speak truthfully at our level. Later Reformed theology (and Barth's debates about "analogy") develops this; Calvin uses it narrowly and pastorally.

Lineage and later reception. Medieval: the nine-order chart (Dionysius → Gregory → Aquinas) was nearly universal. Reformation: Luther was also cautious about hierarchies; Reformed writers followed Calvin's restraint, though some Puritan works (e.g. on the "ministry of angels") indulged in more detail. Be careful: this is a general summary, not a claim about any individual author's complete view.

Guardrails against over-reading. (1) Calvin does not say angels lack rank or names; he says Scripture does not give us enough to map them (S4–S5). (2) "No bodily shape" (S8) is not a denial that angels are real — it concerns how Scripture depicts them. (3) "Deferred to the last day" (S9) is a limit on our curiosity now, not a claim that angels will be catalogued then. (4) Tobit is mentioned as a source of a third name (S5) with an "if you choose" — Calvin does not canonise Tobit; he leaves the choice to the reader.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. S2 — Dan 12:1; Dan 10:13, 21; Jude 9. "Michael, the great prince which standeth for the children of thy people" (Dan 12:1); "Michael, one of the chief princes" (Dan 10:13); "Michael the archangel" (Jude 9). Calvin takes David's "prince" as shorthand for Daniel's language (the source's footnote groups these under one heading).
  2. S3 — 1 Thess 4:16. "…with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God." Calvin summarises it as the archangel's trumpet summoning the world to Judgment; the point is only that an archangel is involved.
  3. S5 — Luke 1:19, 26; Tobit 3:17; 5:5; 12:15. Gabriel announces John's and Jesus's births (Lk 1:19, 26); Raphael in Tobit (an apocryphal book — Calvin offers it as an optional "if you choose"). Names with meanings: Michael (mî-kā-ʾēl, "who is like God?"), Gabriel (gabrîʾēl, "God is my strength"), Raphael (rāpāʾ-ʾēl, "God heals").
  4. S6 — Mt 26:53. "…more than twelve legions of angels." The number is a rhetorical maximum of divine help, not a census.
  5. S6 — Dan 7:10. "Thousand thousands ministered unto him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him." Gk myriades myriadōn → "myriads."
  6. S7 — 2 Kgs 6:17. "…the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire round about Elisha." The same text used in yesterday's §7 (S8) — here for number, there for specific allotment.
  7. S7 — Ps 34:7. "The angel of the Lord encampeth round about them that fear him." The verb encamp implies an army.
  8. S8 — Isa 6:2; Ps 18:10; Ps 104:4. Seraphim with six wings; "he rode upon a cherub, and did fly: yea, he did fly upon the wings of the wind"; "who maketh his angels spirits." Calvin: wings = swiftness of help.
  9. S9 — 1 Cor 13:12; Deut 29:29. "Now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known"; "The secret things belong unto the LORD our God." The biblical basis for "deferred to the last day."

How Calvin uses Scripture: he lines up each text, says what it proves and what it doesn't, and puts a boundary where the texts stop. The last sentence is the boundary sign.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① Grammar — one underlined part is wrong. Which?

Calvin says (A) those who presume to dogmatize on the ranks of angels would do well to consider on what foundation they rest; (B) how is it possible from such passages to ascertain the gradations of honour among the angels; (C) let us class them among those mysterious subjects, the full revelation of which are deferred to the last day; and (D) accordingly refrain from inquiring too curiously.

Answer: (C). Rationale: the subject of is deferred is the full revelation (singular), so the verb must be is, not are. of which does not change the subject (Formula 3). (A) is correct: would do well to + long subject (Formula 1). (B) is correct: dummy it + infinitive (Formula 2). (D) is correct: refrain from V-ing.

② Comprehension — which statement is consistent with §8?

(A) Calvin accepts the medieval nine-order hierarchy because Paul mentions an archangel who blows a trumpet. (B) Calvin denies that angels have names, since the names in Scripture are only symbols. (C) Calvin admits that Scripture names Michael as prince and archangel and speaks of many legions and myriads, but denies that such texts yield a table of ranks, regards the names and wings as accommodation to our weakness, and counsels us to leave further detail to the last day. (D) Calvin teaches that Cherubim and Seraphim literally have wings, which is why they can reach us with the speed of lightning.

Answer: (C). Rationale: (A) direction-flip — S4 is a rhetorical "how is it possible?" against exactly that move. (B) scope overreach — S5 says the names seem to intimate accommodation, hedged by I rather choose not to speak positively; he doesn't deny names. (D) direction-flip — S8: It is certain that spirits have no bodily shape; the wings are Scripture's accommodated picture, meaning swiftness. (C) combines S2, S4–S6, S8–S9.

③ Composition — use Formula 1 and Formula 2.

Compose one English sentence (≈25 words): People who claim to know the exact ranks of heaven should think about their basis. It is not possible to find these ranks from the biblical passages.

Model answer: Those who claim to know the exact ranks of heaven would do well to consider on what foundation they rest; for how is it possible from such passages to ascertain them? Examiner's intent: long plural subject + would do well to + indirect question on what foundation … + rhetorical how is it possible … to V. Alternative accepted (Formula 3): Let us class the ranks of heaven among those mysteries, the full revelation of which is deferred to the last day.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary: Calvin warns those who dogmatize on the ranks and numbers of angels to check their foundation: Scripture names Michael a prince and archangel, mentions an archangel's trumpet, and speaks of many legions and myriads, but it does not yield a table of gradations or insignia; even the names and the wings of Cherubim and Seraphim are accommodation to our weakness (the wings = swiftness of help); beyond this, the matter is a mystery whose full revelation is deferred to the last day, so let us refrain from inquiring too curiously or talking presumptuously; the grammar keys are Those who presume to V … would do well to V (S1), the rhetorical how is it possible … to V1, V2, and V3 (S4), and the full revelation of which is deferred + let us class … and refrain (S9).
