Day 58 — Book 1, Chapter 11, §3

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Look at the first four words. "It is true that…"

That is the sound of a man handing his opponent a loaded gun. §1 was the ruling. §2 was the evidence hearing. §3 is where the defence finally gets to speak — and Calvin speaks for them, out loud, better than they could. The objection is obvious and it is strong: "You say God has no visible form? Then what were the cloud and the fire? What was the dove? What was the man who wrestled Jacob? And what, exactly, were those two golden Cherubim that God Himself commanded Moses to hammer out and set on the ark?"

Calvin does not dodge a single one. He grants all of them. And then he does the thing that makes this section one of the finest pages in Book 1: he shows that every one of those appearances was built to conceal.

Cloud. Smoke. Flame. Those are not windows. Those are curtains. The dove — it vanished. The Cherubim — their wings were covering the mercy-seat. The Seraphim — their faces were veiled. Do you see the pattern? The defence's own exhibits, examined closely, all say the same thing: stop here, you cannot go farther.

This is Calvin's doctrine of accommodation, and here is the sentence I want burned into your brain, because students get it exactly backwards every year:

Accommodation LIMITS. It does not LICENSE.

God stoops to our smallness — yes. But the shape of the stooping is itself a "no farther." The sign is a bridle, not a doorway.

Then the closing third turns brutal. S13–S15: a pagan satirist who mocked the Jews understood Scripture better than the Papists do. Juvenal — after Seneca in §2 — is the third hostile witness Calvin has called in this chapter. He never gets tired of that move.

Grammatically, this is the densest section we have had in a while. S6 and S8 are long, colon-broken, multi-clause monsters. S16 is a full periodic sentence — the main verb is buried in the middle and the sting is at the very end. Take these slowly.

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. The result-design family: so + adj/p.p. … as to V and so … that … (S6, S8, S9). was **so** framed, **as to** intimate…; they were **so** formed **as to** cover…; the refulgence … is **so** great, **that** even the angels cannot gaze…. This is not a purpose clause about somebody's intention. It describes a built-in design consequence. Get the nuance and you get the whole argument of the section.
  2. Interrogative-as-assertion (S4, S8). **who does not see that** …? = everybody sees that. **what** … did these figures mean, **if not that** …? = they meant precisely that. Do not answer these questions. Translate them as emphatic statements.
  3. Subject–verb separation across a parenthesis + relative clause (S3, S12, S16). S12: For that period of puerility, (if I may so express it), [to which such rudiments were adapted], **has passed away**. Twelve words between subject and verb. If you grab the verb inside the brackets you will translate the sentence inside out.

Bonus trap I will absolutely test you on: pray in S8 has nothing to do with prayer. infatuation in S7 has nothing to do with love. prate in S15 and puerility in S12 are both insults, and you need to know exactly how sharp. Four archaic landmines in one section.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 16 sentences –

3. It is true that the Lord occasionally manifested his presence by certain signs, so that he was said to be seen face to face; but all the signs he ever employed were in apt accordance with the scheme of doctrine, and, at the same time, gave plain intimation of his incomprehensible essence. For the cloud, and smoke, and flame, though they were symbols of heavenly glory (Deut. 4:11), curbed men’s minds as with a bridle, that they might not attempt to penetrate farther. Therefore, even Moses (to whom, of all men, God manifested himself most familiarly) was not permitted though he prayed for it, to behold that face, but received for answer, that the refulgence was too great for man (Exod. 33:20). The Holy Spirit appeared under the form of a dove, but as it instantly vanished, who does not see that in this symbol of a moment, the faithful were admonished to regard the Spirit as invisible, to be contented with his power and grace, and not call for any external figure? God sometimes appeared in the form of a man, but this was in anticipation of the future revelation in Christ, and, therefore, did not give the Jews the least pretext for setting up a symbol of Deity under the human form. The mercy-seat, also (Exod. 25:17, 18, 21), where, under the Law, God exhibited the presence of his power, was so framed, as to intimate that God is best seen when the mind rises in admiration above itself: the Cherubim with outstretched wings shaded, and the veil covered it, while the remoteness of the place was in itself a sufficient concealment. It is therefore mere infatuation to attempt to defend images of God and the saints by the example of the Cherubim. For what, pray, did these figures mean, if not that images are unfit to represent the mysteries of God, since they were so formed as to cover the mercy-seat with their wings, thereby concealing the view of God, not only from the eye, but from every human sense, and curbing presumption? To this we may add, that the prophets depict the Seraphim, who are exhibited to us in vision, as having their faces veiled; thus intimating, that the refulgence of the divine glory is so great, that even the angels cannot gaze upon it directly, while the minute beams which sparkle in the face of angels are shrouded from our view. Moreover, all men of sound Judgment acknowledge that the Cherubim in question belonged to the old tutelage of the law. It is absurd, therefore, to bring them forward as an example for our age. For that period of puerility, if I may so express it, to which such rudiments were adapted, has passed away. And surely it is disgraceful, that heathen writers should be more skilful interpreters of Scripture than the Papists. Juvenal (Sat. 14) holds up the Jews to derision for worshipping the thin clouds and firmament. This he does perversely and impiously; still, in denying that any visible shape of Deity existed among them, he speaks more accurately than the Papists, who prate about there having been some visible image. In the fact that the people every now and then rushed forth with boiling haste in pursuit of idols, just like water gushing forth with violence from a copious spring, let us learn how prone our nature is to idolatry, that we may not, by throwing the whole blame of a common vice upon the Jews, be led away by vain and sinful enticements to sleep the sleep of death.

Examiner's Eye — three traps I would build from this section

Trap 1 — the direction flip (the big one). A test-writer will offer: "Calvin argues that God never manifested himself in visible form." False. S1 grants it in the first four words: It is true that the Lord occasionally manifested his presence by certain signs. Calvin's claim is not about whether but about what the signs were designed to do — they "gave plain intimation of his incomprehensible essence." The concession is not a slip; it is the platform for the argument.

Trap 2 — scope overreach on the Cherubim. "Calvin condemns the Cherubim of the ark as idolatrous." False, and doubly so. (a) He never says they were wrong — God commanded them (Exod 25). (b) His argument is that their design preaches the opposite of what image-defenders want, and that they belonged to a superseded pedagogical era (S10–S12). Two separate arguments, neither of them "the Cherubim were sin."

Trap 3 — the Juvenal double-edge. "Calvin commends Juvenal as a pious interpreter." False. S15 is exactly balanced and you must hold both halves: This he does perversely and impiously; **still**, … he speaks more accurately than the Papists. The praise is comparative and grudging, and it is aimed at the Papists, not at Juvenal. The rhetorical force is shame, not endorsement.

3 — Sentence-by-Sentence Live Teaching –

S1★★★the concession frame — grant the fact, seize the interpretation›

It is true that the Lord occasionally manifested his presence by certain signs, so that he was said to be seen face to face; but all the signs he ever employed were in apt accordance with the scheme of doctrine, and, at the same time, gave plain intimation of his incomprehensible essence.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • intimation < Latin intimare (to make known, from intimus, innermost) — an announcement from the inside out, hence "hint" in modern English but "plain declaration" here; note `plain intimation` would be an oxymoron in today's usage. incomprehensible = in- + com- + prehendere (to seize) — literally "not able to be grasped-together"; the metaphor is a hand closing, not a mind puzzling.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • in accordance with = ~에 부합하여; at the same time here = 그와 동시에 (simultaneity, not concession — do not read it as "however").
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

True, God did sometimes show that he was present through particular signs — so much so that the Bible can say he was "seen face to face." But every sign he used was exactly suited to what he was teaching at the time, and each one also made it perfectly clear that his own being is beyond our grasp.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • It is true that → Admittedly / True,
  • manifested his presence → showed that he was there
  • in apt accordance with → exactly suited to
  • gave plain intimation of → made clear
  • he was said to be seen → the Bible describes him as being seen.
S2★★the bridle — the sign as restraint›

For the cloud, and smoke, and flame, though they were symbols of heavenly glory (Deut. 4:11), curbed men’s minds as with a bridle, that they might not attempt to penetrate farther.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • curb < Old French courbe < Latin curvus (bent) — originally the curved chain under a horse's jaw; the modern abstract sense ("restrain") is this hardware gone metaphorical. penetrate = penitus (inwardly) + -trare — to go all the way in; that "all the way" is why `farther` is the operative word.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • as with a bridle = 재갈 물리듯; penetrate farther = 더 깊이 파고들다 (with the connotation of trespass, not research).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

The cloud, the smoke, and the fire really were signs of heavenly glory — but they worked like a bridle on people's minds, holding them back from trying to push in any deeper.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • though they were → even though they were / while they really were
  • curbed → held back
  • as with a bridle → like a bridle
  • that they might not attempt to → so that they would not try to.
S3★★★a fortiori — the ceiling case, and a subject–verb split›

Therefore, even Moses (to whom, of all men, God manifested himself most familiarly) was not permitted though he prayed for it, to behold that face, but received for answer, that the refulgence was too great for man (Exod. 33:20).

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • refulgence = re- (back) + fulgere (to flash, cognate with fulmen, lightning) — light flashing back at you; cf. effulgent, fulminate. familiarly < familia (household) — "on household terms," far stronger than modern "familiar"; the sense is intimacy of access, not casual acquaintance.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • receive X for answer = 답으로 ~을 받다; of all men = (강조 삽입) 그 누구보다도; a fortiori — 더 강한 이유로.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

So even Moses — the one person God revealed himself to most intimately — was not allowed to see that face, even though he asked. The answer he got was that the brightness was more than any human could take.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • was not permitted … to behold → was not allowed to see
  • though he prayed for it → even though he asked
  • received for answer, that → was told that
  • the refulgence → the brightness
  • too great for man → more than a human could bear.
S4★★★the dove — and the interrogative that asserts›

The Holy Spirit appeared under the form of a dove, but as it instantly vanished, who does not see that in this symbol of a moment, the faithful were admonished to regard the Spirit as invisible, to be contented with his power and grace, and not call for any external figure?

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • admonish = ad- (toward) + monere (to warn; cf. monitor, monument = a thing that reminds) — a warning delivered at someone. vanish < Old French evanir < Latin evanescere (ex- + vanus, empty) — to go empty, not merely to leave.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • under the form of = ~의 형태로 (Latinate: sub specie); be contented with = ~로 만족하다; call for = 요구하다 (not "shout for"); symbol of a moment = 한순간짜리 표징.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

The Holy Spirit did appear in the shape of a dove — but it disappeared immediately. Isn't it obvious that this one-moment symbol was warning believers to think of the Spirit as invisible, to be satisfied with his power and grace, and not to demand any outward image?

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • under the form of → in the shape of
  • instantly vanished → disappeared immediately
  • who does not see that → isn't it obvious that
  • were admonished to → were being warned to
  • not call for any external figure → not to demand any outward image.
S5★★the human form — anticipation, not authorisation›

God sometimes appeared in the form of a man, but this was in anticipation of the future revelation in Christ, and, therefore, did not give the Jews the least pretext for setting up a symbol of Deity under the human form.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • pretext = prae- (before) + texere (to weave; cf. textile, text) — something woven in front to hide what is behind. anticipation = ante- + capere (to take) — to take beforehand.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • give someone the least pretext for = ~할 최소한의 빌미도 주다 (negated: 빌미조차 주지 않다); in anticipation of = ~을 앞서 내다보고.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

God did sometimes appear in human form, but that was looking ahead to Christ's coming — so it gave the Jews no excuse whatsoever for making a human-shaped image of God.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • in the form of a man → in human form
  • in anticipation of the future revelation in Christ → looking ahead to Christ's coming
  • did not give … the least pretext → gave … no excuse whatsoever
  • setting up a symbol of Deity → making an image of God.
S6★★★the mercy-seat — so framed as to, and a colon that carries three proofs›

The mercy-seat, also (Exod. 25:17, 18, 21), where, under the Law, God exhibited the presence of his power, was so framed, as to intimate that God is best seen when the mind rises in admiration above itself: the Cherubim with outstretched wings shaded, and the veil covered it, while the remoteness of the place was in itself a sufficient concealment.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • mercy-seat = Tyndale's coinage for Hebrew kappōreṯ (< kāp̄ar, to cover / atone), rendered ἱλαστήριον in the LXX and picked up at Rom 3:25 — so the covering is built into the very name. admiration = ad- + mirari (to wonder) — 16c. awe, not modern approval; cf. miracle, mirror. conceal = com- + celare (to hide; cognate with cell, hell = the covered place).
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • be so framed as to V = ~하도록 만들어지다 (설계상 그렇게 되어 있다); in itself = 그것만으로도; rise above oneself = 자기 수준 위로 올라서다.
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

The mercy-seat too (Exodus 25:17, 18, 21) — where God displayed his powerful presence under the Law — was built to signal that God is seen best when the mind is lifted in awe beyond itself. The Cherubim shaded it with their spread wings, the veil covered it, and the sheer distance of the place hid it well enough by itself.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • was so framed, as to intimate → was built to signal
  • exhibited the presence of his power → displayed his powerful presence
  • rises in admiration above itself → is lifted in awe beyond itself
  • the remoteness of the place → the sheer distance of the place
  • a sufficient concealment → hid it well enough.
S7★the verdict — and a trap word›

It is therefore mere infatuation to attempt to defend images of God and the saints by the example of the Cherubim.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • infatuation < in- + fatuus (foolish) — cf. fatuous; the romantic sense is a much later drift.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • by the example of = ~을 선례로 삼아; mere = ~에 지나지 않는 (dismissive, not "small").
전체 해설 더보기

One point, then move.

infatuation is NOT romantic obsession. Latin infatuare = to make a fool of (fatuus = foolish, silly, insipid — the same root that gives fatuous). Calvin's word means downright folly, besotted stupidity. Translate: "nothing but folly." If you carry the modern meaning in, the sentence turns bafflingly mild — and Calvin is not being mild.

S8★★★what … if not that …? — the exhibit that testifies for the prosecution›

For what, pray, did these figures mean, if not that images are unfit to represent the mysteries of God, since they were so formed as to cover the mercy-seat with their wings, thereby concealing the view of God, not only from the eye, but from every human sense, and curbing presumption?

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • presumption = prae- (before) + sumere (to take) — taking before it is given; the theological vice of this chapter in one etymology. pray here = reduced I pray you → prithee; a discourse particle, not a verb.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • what, pray, …? = 도대체 ~란 말인가? (challenge); if not that = 그것이 아니라면 무엇이겠는가; be unfit to V = ~하기에 부적합하다; not only … but … = 눈뿐 아니라 (escalation, not mere addition).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

So tell me — what did those figures mean, if not that images can't represent God's mysteries? They were made precisely to cover the mercy-seat with their wings, hiding the sight of God not just from the eye but from every human sense, and holding back our presumption.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • what, pray, did these figures mean → tell me, what did those figures mean
  • unfit to represent → can't represent
  • so formed as to → made precisely to
  • thereby concealing → hiding
  • curbing presumption → holding back our presumption.
S9★★the Seraphim — so great, that, and the a fortiori repeated on angels›

To this we may add, that the prophets depict the Seraphim, who are exhibited to us in vision, as having their faces veiled; thus intimating, that the refulgence of the divine glory is so great, that even the angels cannot gaze upon it directly, while the minute beams which sparkle in the face of angels are shrouded from our view.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • seraphim < Hebrew śārap̄ (to burn) — the burning ones; that is why the sentence is about unbearable brightness. shroud < Old English scrūd (garment) — later the burial-cloth; the darker connotation is live here. minute (adj.) < minutus (made small), from minuere — the noun "minute" is the same root (pars minuta prima).
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • to this we may add that = 여기에 덧붙이자면; depict X as V-ing = X를 ~하는 모습으로 그리다; gaze upon = 응시하다 (sustained looking — that is precisely what is denied).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

We can add that the prophets picture the Seraphim — shown to us in visions — with their faces covered. That signals that the brightness of God's glory is so overwhelming that even angels can't look at it straight on, while the tiny gleams that shine on angels' faces are hidden from us.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • To this we may add → We can add
  • depict … as having their faces veiled → picture … with their faces covered
  • thus intimating → that signals
  • refulgence → brightness
  • minute beams which sparkle → tiny gleams that shine
  • shrouded from our view → hidden from us.
S10★the pivot to redemptive history›

Moreover, all men of sound Judgment acknowledge that the Cherubim in question belonged to the old tutelage of the law.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • tutelage < tutela < tueri (to watch over) — cf. tutor, tuition; the legal sense is wardship over a minor.
전체 해설 더보기

One point. tutelage = guardianship of a legal minor (< tutela, from tueri, to watch over). This is not a throwaway metaphor — it is Paul's vocabulary from Gal 3:24 (παιδαγωγός, the slave who walked the child to school) and Gal 4:1–3 (the heir who, while a minor, differs nothing from a servant). Calvin has switched arguments here: S1–S9 were the Cherubim mean the opposite of what you claim; S10–S12 are and in any case that era is over. Two independent lines of attack. Note them separately — an exam question will ask which one a given sentence belongs to.

(Judgment with a capital J is the source's own; preserved.)

S11★the inference›

It is absurd, therefore, to bring them forward as an example for our age.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • absurd — ab- + surdus (deaf) — literally discordant to the ear. 터무니없는
전체 해설 더보기

One point. bring forward as an example = to cite as precedent — courtroom register, continuing the trial metaphor that has run since §2. absurd (< ab- + surdus, deaf) originally = out of tune, jarring to the ear; the word still has that grating edge.

S12★★the periodic sentence — find the verb›

For that period of puerility, if I may so express it, to which such rudiments were adapted, has passed away.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • puerility < puer (boy) — cf. puerile; describes the learner's stage, not the teacher's quality. rudiment < rudis (rough, unformed) — the rough first shaping, hence "ABCs."
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • if I may so express it = 이렇게 표현해도 된다면 (hedging a strong word); pass away here = 지나가 버리다 (of an era — not the modern euphemism for dying).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

That childhood stage — if I can put it that way — which those beginner's lessons were designed for, is over.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • that period of puerility → that childhood stage
  • if I may so express it → if I can put it that way
  • to which such rudiments were adapted → which those beginner's lessons were designed for
  • has passed away → is over.
S13★the shame move›

And surely it is disgraceful, that heathen writers should be more skilful interpreters of Scripture than the Papists.

💬 Idiom · 관용
  • it is disgraceful that S should V = ~하다니 수치스러운 일이다 (emotive should, not obligation).
전체 해설 더보기

One point. should here is not obligation. It is the emotive/evaluative subjunctive after an expression of feeling: it is disgraceful that S **should** V = it is a scandal that S actually does V. The should adds indignation, not duty. Compare It is strange that he should say so — meaning he did say so, and it is strange. A classic underline-the-error item.

S14★the witness enters›

Juvenal (Sat. 14) holds up the Jews to derision for worshipping the thin clouds and firmament.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • derision — de- + ridere (to laugh) → ridiculous, risible. Laughing down at. 조롱
전체 해설 더보기

One point. hold X up to derision = to expose X to ridicule — the phrasal frame is hold up to + [reaction noun] (to ridicule / to scorn / to contempt). And note that Calvin is quoting his hostile witness accurately, including the hostility: Juvenal (Sat. 14.96–106) genuinely sneers at the Jews for worshipping nothing but clouds and the divinity of the sky. Calvin does not launder the quotation. That is what makes S15 work.

S15★★still — the two-edged verdict›

This he does perversely and impiously; still, in denying that any visible shape of Deity existed among them, he speaks more accurately than the Papists, who prate about there having been some visible image.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • prate — Middle Dutch praten (to chatter); onomatopoeic and always pejorative. perverse = per- (thoroughly, awry) + vertere (to turn) — turned the wrong way round, which is exactly Calvin's charge: Juvenal has the fact right and the direction wrong.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • still (sentence-initial) = 그럼에도; prate about = ~에 대해 지껄이다; there having been X = X가 있었다는 것 (gerund clause with its own subject).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

He does this in a twisted and irreverent way; even so, when he denies that any visible form of God existed among them, he is more accurate than the Papists, who go on babbling about some visible image having been there.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • This he does → He does this
  • perversely and impiously → in a twisted and irreverent way
  • still → even so
  • prate about → go on babbling about
  • there having been some visible image → some visible image having been there.
S16★★★the closing periodic sentence — the turn onto us›

In the fact that the people every now and then rushed forth with boiling haste in pursuit of idols, just like water gushing forth with violence from a copious spring, let us learn how prone our nature is to idolatry, that we may not, by throwing the whole blame of a common vice upon the Jews, be led away by vain and sinful enticements to sleep the sleep of death.

🔤 Morphology · 어형
  • enticement < Old French enticier, probably < Vulgar Latin intitiare (to set on fire, from titio, a firebrand) — the seduction metaphor is originally kindling. copious < copia (abundance; cf. cornucopia) — the spring never runs dry, which is the whole point. prone < pronus* (leaning forward, face-down) — a postural word: not "likely to" but already tilted that way.
💬 Idiom · 관용
  • rush forth in pursuit of = ~을 좇아 뛰쳐나가다; every now and then = 이따금씩 (here: 반복적으로); throw the blame upon = ~에게 책임을 떠넘기다; be led away by = ~에 이끌려 빗나가다; sleep the sleep of death = 죽음의 잠을 자다 (cognate object, 시13:3).
쉬운 영어 / Modern English

From the way the people kept bolting after idols in a boiling rush — like water bursting out of a full spring — let's learn how strongly our own nature leans toward idolatry. Then we won't dump the whole blame for a shared fault onto the Jews and get lured by empty, sinful attractions into sleeping the sleep of death.

Key changes · 올·현대 표현
  • In the fact that → From the way that
  • rushed forth with boiling haste in pursuit of → kept bolting after
  • gushing forth with violence from a copious spring → bursting out of a full spring
  • how prone our nature is → how strongly our nature leans
  • that we may not … be led away by → so we won't get lured by
  • vain and sinful enticements → empty, sinful attractions.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — The result-design family

① S + be + SO + adj/p.p.  +  AS TO + V(원형)
       "was so framed, as to intimate…"   "were so formed as to cover…"
   ⟹ built in such a way that it V-s.  DESIGN CONSEQUENCE.

② S + be + SO + adj  +  THAT + S′ + V′
       "the refulgence … is so great, that even the angels cannot gaze…"
   ⟹ same logic, finite clause, more expansive.

⚠️ Trap 1: ① is not a purpose clause. Do not translate so framed as to intimate as "framed in order to intimate." The teaching is a property of the construction, not a separate intention laid on top of it. Calvin's whole argument in S6/S8 depends on this: the furniture preaches by being what it is. ⚠️ Trap 2: in ① the as to takes a bare infinitive verb, never -ing. so formed as to cover ✓ / so formed as to covering ✗. ⚠️ Trap 3: don't confuse with so as to V (no adjective) = in order to, which is purpose. The adjective/participle is the tell.

Transformation drill. Combine into ①, then rewrite as ②:

The veil hid the ark. It taught the people that God is not to be stared at. → ① The veil was so hung as to teach the people that God is not to be stared at. → ② The veil was so heavy that the people could not so much as glimpse the ark.

Formula 2 — The interrogative that asserts

① WHO does not see THAT S + V ?        ⟹ Everyone sees that S + V.
② WHAT did X mean, IF NOT THAT S + V ? ⟹ X meant precisely that S + V.
③ (cousin) And is it strange that …?   ⟹ It is not strange at all.   [§2 S7]

⚠️ Trap 1: never render these as genuine questions. The question mark is rhetorical punctuation, not an inquiry. ⚠️ Trap 2: ② is stronger than a plain assertion because it shifts the burden: it invites an alternative and implies none exists. Your translation should keep that challenge. ⚠️ Trap 3: pray inserted inside (what, pray, did these figures mean) is a discourse particle = tell me. It is not the verb, and it is not a parenthetical clause. Do not translate it as prayer.

Transformation drill. Turn each assertion into the rhetorical form:

It is obvious that the vanishing of the dove taught the Spirit's invisibility. → Who does not see that the vanishing of the dove taught the Spirit's invisibility? The veiled faces of the Seraphim mean that the divine glory cannot be looked at. → What, pray, did those veiled faces mean, if not that the divine glory cannot be looked at?

Formula 3 — Subject–verb separation (find the finite verb)

S + ((parenthesis)) + [[relative clause]] + … + MAIN VERB
   "that period of puerility, (if I may so express it), [to which such rudiments were adapted],
       HAS PASSED AWAY."
   "even Moses (to whom, of all men, God manifested himself most familiarly)
       WAS NOT PERMITTED … but RECEIVED …"
   "In [the fact that …], LET US LEARN …"

⚠️ Trap 1: a verb inside [[ ]] (relative clause) or (( )) (parenthesis) can never be the main verb. were adapted and manifested are decoys. ⚠️ Trap 2: the main verb may be coordinated — S3 has two (was not permitted … but received). Find one and keep looking. ⚠️ Trap 3: when the sentence opens with a fronted adverbial (In the fact that…), the main verb can be thirty words in. Bracket the adverbial before you start hunting.

Transformation drill. Expand this bare sentence using the pattern, keeping the main verb at the end:

The Cherubim have been superseded. → The Cherubim, if we may put it plainly, which belonged to the tutelage of a bygone age and were fitted to its rudiments, have been superseded.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The objection Calvin is answering — and why it is the strongest one. Everything so far in Ch. 11 has been the prosecution. §3 is the defence's turn, and the defence's case is not stupid. It runs: Scripture itself records visible manifestations of God, and Scripture itself commands the manufacture of religious images (the Cherubim, and later the bronze serpent). You cannot forbid what God commanded. This is not a straw man Calvin invented — it is, in outline, the argument of Nicaea II (787), the council that settled the iconoclast controversy for the East and, through Carolingian and later scholastic mediation, underwrote Western image-practice. Nicaea II's defence of icons leaned on two legs: the incarnation (God became visible, so he may be depicted) and Old Testament precedent (the cherubim, the ark). §3 is Calvin's answer to the second leg. He will return to the first later in the chapter.

The doctrine that organises the section: accommodatio. Calvin's most characteristic hermeneutical principle is that God attempers himself (Deus se attemperat) to our capacity — most famously at I.13.1, where God is said to lisp with us (balbutire) as nurses do with infants. Every theophany in §3 is an instance. But note the direction of the argument, because this is where readers go wrong. Accommodation is often invoked as a permission — God came down to our level, therefore level-appropriate representations are fine. Calvin uses it the opposite way. The very fact that a sign is accommodated advertises the gap it is bridging. The cloud is God-shaped-for-us, and the shape it takes is opacity. in apt accordance with the scheme of doctrine, and, at the same time, gave plain intimation of his incomprehensible essence (S1) — that "at the same time" is the whole doctrine. Accommodation limits; it does not license.

The two independent arguments — keep them apart.

Argument Sentences Claim
A Semiotic S1–S9 The signs, read correctly, teach concealment. The exhibits testify for the prosecution.
B Redemptive-historical S10–S12 Even granting them, they belonged to a superseded pedagogical era.

Argument B is where Calvin's covenant theology surfaces. old tutelage of the law, period of puerility, rudiments — all three are Pauline terms (Gal 3:24 παιδαγωγός; Gal 4:1–3 νήπιοι and στοιχεῖα; Col 2:20). Calvin is not improvising a periodisation; he is deploying Paul's. It will be worked out in full at Book 2, chs. 10–11, where he insists the substance of the old and new covenants is one and the difference is one of administration. Hold that, and S12 cannot be misread as contempt for the Old Testament.

The lineage. The self-concealing theophany is patristic bedrock. Chrysostom's De incomprehensibili Dei natura (386–87, against the Anomoeans, who claimed to know God's essence as God knows it) argues exactly Calvin's S3 and S9: even the seraphim veil their faces. Augustine, De Trinitate II–III, works through the OT theophanies and refuses to identify any of them with the divine substance directly. Pseudo-Dionysius supplies the medieval vocabulary of the divine darkness — though Calvin has no taste for the apophatic mysticism it grew into, and you should not read S6's rises in admiration above itself as Dionysian ascent. For Calvin the raising is done by the Word working on the mind, not by negation working on the intellect.

Juvenal, and the hostile-witness habit. Satire 14.96–106 mocks the Jews for worshipping nil praeter nubes et caeli numen — "nothing but clouds and the divinity of heaven." Calvin has now called three pagan witnesses in short order: Cicero (I.3.1), Seneca via Augustine (I.11.2), Juvenal (I.11.3). The rhetorical logic never varies: the hostile witness has no motive to help me, so his agreement is worth more than a friend's. Here the sting is sharper than usual, because the pagan's accuracy is set against the Papists' inaccuracy on a matter of plain fact.

Reception. The concealing-theophany argument becomes structural for the Reformed regulative principle of worship (Westminster Confession XXI.1) — worship is limited to what God prescribes, and images are excluded not merely as unhelpful but as prescriptively forbidden. In the 20th century Karl Barth makes the Bildverbot a matter of prolegomena rather than merely of the second commandment: the ban on images becomes a ban on the theological method of grasping God from below (CD I/1, and the treatment of the "analogia entis"). Calvin's S6 — sight replaced by the mind's being raised — is a recognisable ancestor of that move, though Barth radicalises it well beyond anything Calvin says.

⚠️ Over-reading guardrails — four lines you must not cross.

  1. Calvin does not deny the theophanies. S1, S4, S5 grant every one of them explicitly. Anyone who reports Calvin as saying "God never appeared visibly" has misread the first four words of the section.
  2. Calvin does not condemn the Cherubim. They were divinely commanded. His two claims are that their design argues against images, and that their era has ended. Neither is "they were idolatrous."
  3. puerility is not contempt for the Old Testament. It describes the mode of administration, and Calvin hedges the word himself as he writes it. Read against Book 2, ch. 10, where the covenant's substance is one.
  4. This is not an argument against visual art. Calvin says so plainly nine sections later, at I.11.12, where sculpture and painting are called gifts of God. The target throughout is the representation of God and the worship of images — not the eye, not the hand, not culture.

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. Exod 33:11 / Num 12:8 — "face to face" (S1). Hebrew pānîm ’el-pānîm; Num 12:8 has peh ’el-peh, "mouth to mouth." Calvin's careful passive — he **was said** to be seen face to face — grants Scripture's own idiom while declining the literalist inference. Watch the technique: he never contradicts a biblical phrase; he bounds it with another.

  2. Deut 4:11 — the cloud, darkness, thick darkness (S2). ‘ānān (cloud), ‘ărāp̄el (thick gloom). Note that Calvin has now used both halves of Deut 4: v. 15 in §2 ("ye saw no manner of similitude") and v. 11 here ("the mountain burned with fire… with darkness, clouds, and thick darkness"). One passage, two functions — the prohibition and its rationale.

  3. Exod 33:20 — "there shall no man see me, and live" (S3). The a fortiori ceiling. Calvin's the refulgence was too great for man renders the verse's logic rather than its words: the danger is not that God is too dim to see but too bright to survive. Compare 1 Tim 6:16, φῶς οἰκῶν ἀπρόσιτον — dwelling in unapproachable light.

  4. Matt 3:16 / Luke 3:22 — the dove (S4). Luke has the loaded phrase: σωματικῷ εἴδει ὡς περιστεράν — "in bodily form as a dove." That is the image-defender's strongest single verse, and Calvin does not touch its reality. He attacks its duration: as it instantly vanished … this symbol of a moment. A form that cannot be held cannot be enshrined.

  5. Gen 18; Gen 32:24–30; Judg 13 — the human-form theophanies (S5). Calvin reads them as praeludia of John 1:14, ὁ λόγος σὰρξ ἐγένετο. A sign pointing forward is spent when the thing arrives — which is why it can generate no pretext afterward.

  6. Exod 25:17, 18, 21 — the mercy-seat (S6). kappōreṯ, from kāp̄ar (to cover / make atonement); LXX ἱλαστήριον, picked up at Rom 3:25 of Christ. The cherubim are sōkəkîm — "overshadowing" — v. 20: "covering the mercy seat with their wings." Calvin's argument is straight exegesis of the verb. The furniture's own participle is his evidence.

  7. Isa 6:2 — the Seraphim (S9). śərāp̄îm (< śārap̄, to burn): "with twain he covered his face, and with twain he covered his feet." Calvin's a fortiori goes up a storey: not merely no man, but no angel, may gaze directly. Cf. also Ezek 1:22–28, where the vision terminates in "the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD" — three layers of hedging in one phrase.

  8. Gal 3:24; Gal 4:1–3; Col 2:20 — tutelage, puerility, rudiments (S10, S12). παιδαγωγός (the slave who escorted the child, not a teacher), νήπιοι (minors), τὰ στοιχεῖα (elementary things). All three of Calvin's key terms in this stretch are transliterations of Paul's. The redemptive-historical argument is therefore not a Reformation innovation but an apostolic one — which is precisely why Calvin can deploy it against a church claiming apostolic continuity.

  9. Exod 32; Ps 106:19–20 (S16). The golden calf and its liturgical memory: "They made a calf in Horeb… Thus they changed their glory into the similitude of an ox that eateth grass." Note that Ps 106 uses the same word-field (tabnît, similitude) as Deut 4:15–16. Calvin's every now and then rushed forth compresses the whole Judges cycle (Judg 2:11–13) and 1 Kings 12:28.

  10. Ps 13:3 — "the sleep of death" (S16). "Lighten mine eyes, lest I sleep the sleep of death" — Hebrew pen-’îšan ha-māweṯ, a cognate-object construction that Beveridge preserves rather than smoothing away. And look at the irony Calvin has engineered: David asks God to lighten his eyes; idolatry is the choice to have one's eyes darkened by something eye-shaped. The chapter that began by forbidding a visible form ends by praying, in effect, for real sight.

8 — Exam Problems –

① 어법 (grammar) — find the ONE error

The mercy-seat was ⓐ so framed as to intimate that God is best seen from above oneself; the Cherubim were ⓑ so formed as to covering the ark with their wings; the glory is ⓒ so great that even angels cannot gaze on it; and that period of puerility ⓓ has passed away.

정답 및 출제 의도 **정답: ⓑ** — `so formed as to **covering**` → **`so formed as to cover`**. **출제 의도.** Formula 1's Trap 2, straight. In the `so + adj/p.p. + as to + V` construction the `to` is an **infinitival** `to`, so it takes the **bare verb**. The `-ing` is only tempting because `as to` *looks* like the preposition `as to` (= "regarding"), which of course does take a gerund. That homograph is the entire trap. Distinguish: - `so formed **as to cover**` — result/design construction, infinitive. ✓ - `no decision **as to covering** the ark` — preposition `as to` = "concerning," gerund. ✓ ⓐ and ⓒ are the two legitimate branches of the family (infinitive vs. finite clause) and are both correct. ⓓ is correct present perfect for a state reached and continuing — and it is placed last as a decoy, since students who spent Day 58 hunting for the main verb in S12 tend to suspect it.

② 내용일치 (comprehension) — which ONE is correct?

  1. Calvin argues that God never manifested his presence in any visible way, since Scripture nowhere describes him as seen.
  2. Calvin holds that the Cherubim of the mercy-seat were themselves idolatrous objects which Moses should not have made.
  3. Calvin grants the visible manifestations but argues that each was constructed so as to signal God's incomprehensibility, and that the Cherubim in any case belonged to a superseded era of the Law.
  4. Calvin commends Juvenal as a pious interpreter whose reverence for Scripture exceeded that of the Papists.
정답 및 출제 의도 **정답: 3** **오답 해설 — 세 개의 함정 유형이 하나씩 들어 있다.** **① 방향 뒤집기 (direction flip).** The section's opening words are `It is true that the Lord occasionally manifested his presence by certain signs, so that he was said to be seen face to face` (S1). Calvin **concedes** the appearances. The added clause "since Scripture nowhere describes him as seen" makes the falsehood explicit — S1 says Scripture **does** so describe him. This is the trap most students fall for, because they arrive at §3 carrying §1's absolute prohibition and assume §3 must extend it. **② 범위 초과 (scope overreach).** Calvin never condemns the Cherubim. They are the **defence's exhibit**, which he turns — `what, pray, did these figures mean, if not that images are unfit…` (S8). His second argument is chronological (`belonged to the old tutelage of the law`, S10), not moral. Note how the distractor smuggles in "which Moses should not have made" — a claim found nowhere in the text and flatly at odds with Exod 25. **④ 부분 인용의 반전 (half-quotation inverted).** S15 is deliberately two-edged: `This he does perversely and impiously; **still**, … he speaks more accurately than the Papists.` The distractor keeps the comparative and deletes the concessive. Calvin's praise is **narrow** (one factual point), **grudging** (`still`), and aimed **at the Papists**. "Pious" is precisely the word Calvin denies him. **③ is correct** because it holds both of Calvin's independent arguments in the right relation: the **semiotic** one (S1–S9) and the **redemptive-historical** one (S10–S12), joined by "and in any case."

③ 영작 (composition) — apply today's formulas

Korean: 그 휘장은 지성소를 가리도록 만들어져 있어서, 아무도 감히 더 들어가려 하지 않았다. 도대체 그것이 무엇을 뜻하겠는가 — 하나님은 눈이 아니라 경외 가운데 들린 마음으로 뵙는 분이라는 뜻이 아니라면?

모범답안 및 출제 의도 **모범답안:** > **The veil was so hung as to shut off the Holy of Holies, that none might presume to penetrate farther. What, pray, did it mean, if not that God is beheld, not by the eye, but by a mind raised in admiration above itself?** **채점 포인트 (배점 순).** 1. **Formula 1** — `was **so** hung **as to** shut off` (bare infinitive after `as to`; adjective/participle before it). `so hung that it shut off` also accepted. ✗ `so hung as to shutting off`. 2. **Formula 2** — `What, pray, did it mean, **if not that** …?` Both parts required: the `what … if not that` frame **and** the discourse particle `pray` correctly placed between commas. Rendering it as a genuine question, or translating `pray` as a verb, loses the point. 3. **S2's purpose clause** — `**that** none **might** presume to penetrate farther` (bare `that + might`, the archaic final clause). Modern `so that no one would dare go any farther` accepted but flagged as a register drop. 4. **Vocabulary reuse** — `penetrate farther` (S2), `presume` / `presumption` (S8), `raised in admiration above itself` (S6). Using today's own word-stock is what makes the sentence sound like Calvin rather than like a translation of Calvin. 5. **Structural echo** — the model answer deliberately reproduces the section's own rhythm: *design-statement → purpose clause → rhetorical question that supplies the meaning.* That is the S6→S8 movement in miniature.

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary. Calvin concedes every visible manifestation of God his opponents can name — cloud, dove, human form, Cherubim — and shows that each was built to conceal: accommodation announces the gap it bridges, the Cherubim's own wings argue against images, and in any case that childhood era of the Law has passed; so the lesson of Israel's repeated rush to idols is not their peculiar weakness but ours.

Today's 3 grammar points, one more time.

  1. so + adj/p.p. + **as to** + V (design consequence) ‖ so + adj + **that** + S + V (finite consequence). — S6, S8, S9.
  2. **who does not see that** …? = everyone sees ‖ what … **if not that** …? = it means precisely that. And pray = tell me, never prayer. — S4, S8.
  3. S ((parenthesis)) [[relative]] … **MAIN VERB** — the verb inside the brackets is a decoy. Bracket first, then hunt. — S3, S12, S16.

Homework (10 minutes)

1 — Structure restoration. Take S16 and rebuild it without looking. Start from the skeleton From X, let us learn Y, that we may not Z and fill in: what is X (with its simile), what is Y, what is Z (with both the means — blaming the Jews — and the end — the sleep of death)? Then check the placement of the main verb: how many words into the sentence does let us learn appear? Getting that number right is the point of the exercise.

2 — Composition. Write one sentence in Calvin's voice about the bronze serpent (Num 21:8–9; 2 Kings 18:4 — Hezekiah broke it in pieces because Israel had begun burning incense to it), using Formula 1 and Formula 3: the object must be described with so … as to …, and the main verb must come after at least one parenthesis and one relative clause. Ten minutes, one sentence, no more.

3 — Preview of tomorrow (§4). Calvin drops the theophany objection and turns to the raw materials. Tomorrow opens: "To the same effect are the words of the Psalmist (Psalms 115:4, 135:15), 'Their idols are silver and gold, the works of men's hands.'" Watch for the argument-from-substance — silver and gold rather than clay or stone, and why that choice matters — and get ready for Horace's fig-tree log (Sat.
