Day 108 — Book 1, Chapter 14, §5

0 — Orientation (1 minute) –

Yesterday Calvin shut the door on speculation. Today he opens the other door: what Scripture itself says about angels. And he does it with a trick you must catch. The names are the same ones Pseudo-Dionysius used to build his nine-rank hierarchy — hosts, virtues, principalities, powers, dominions, thrones. Calvin keeps the vocabulary but changes the job of the words: they are not rungs on a ladder; they are descriptions of one office ("The names … have reference to the same office," S2). Same words, opposite method. Underline that.

Why examiners love it. Fourteen sentences, one paragraph, and a perfect stage for scope traps: Thrones ("I am unwilling to speak positively," S7), Christ as the Angel of the Lord ("I am rather inclined," S11), angels called Gods (S10 — a reason is given, and it is not worship). The grammar is equally rich: a whose relative with a fronted object (S1), so … that result with a self-correction or rather (S3), a though as to … , as … concession chain (S7), and the negative-inversion pair Nor is it right … / Nor ought this … closing on an a fortiori inversion (with far greater reason may it be given, S14).

Text traps. ⚠️ virtues (S5) = powers, mighty acts (L. virtus, Gk dynamis) — not moral virtues. Thrones (S7) is a name, capitalized as a title. congruous = fitting. vicegerents (S14) = deputies who rule in someone's place. hence their name (S1) = that is why they are called … — the word angel itself means "messenger."

Today's 3 Big Points

  1. whose relative with a fronted object + hence chain: angels are heavenly spirits, whose obedience and ministry God employs to execute all the purposes which he has decreed, and hence their name … (S1) → ⚠️ In whose obedience and ministry God employs, God is the subject and whose obedience and ministry is the fronted object. Restore: God employs their obedience and ministry. Formula 1.
  2. so ADJ … that result clause with self-correction and as-reason clauses: are so ready and prompt to execute his orders, that the moment he gives the nod, they prepare for, or rather are actually at work (S3); As … they are called virtues (S5); as the glory of God … dwells in them (S7) → so … that = result; as at the front = because, not "while." Formula 2.
  3. Negative/emphatic inversion + a fortiori: Nor is it right to pass by … (S9), Nor ought this to seem strange (S13), with far greater reason may it be given to angels (S14) → after nor and after a fronted adverbial of degree, the auxiliary jumps before the subject. Formula 3.

1 — Full Text (⚠️ invariable) — 14 sentences –

In Scripture, then, we uniformly read that angels are heavenly spirits, whose obedience and ministry God employs to execute all the purposes which he has decreed, and hence their name as being a kind of intermediate messengers to manifest his will to men. The names by which several of them are distinguished have reference to the same office. They are called hosts, because they surround their Prince as his court,—adorn and display his majesty,—like soldiers, have their eyes always turned to their leader’s standard, and are so ready and prompt to execute his orders, that the moment he gives the nod, they prepare for, or rather are actually at work. In declaring the magnificence of the divine throne, similar representations are given by the prophets, and especially by Daniel, when he says, that when God stood up to Judgment, “thousand thousands ministered unto him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him,” (Dan. 7:10). As by these means the Lord wonderfully exerts and declares the power and might of his hand, they are called virtues. Again, as his government of the world is exercised and administered by them, they are called at one time Principalities, at another Powers, at another Dominions (Col. 1:16; Eph. 1:21). Lastly, as the glory of God in some measure dwells in them, they are also termed Thrones; though as to this last designation I am unwilling to speak positively, as a different interpretation is equally, if not more congruous. To say nothing, therefore, of the name of Thrones, the former names are often employed by the Holy Spirit in commendation of the dignity of angelic service. Nor is it right to pass by unhonoured those instruments by whom God specially manifests the presence of his power. Nay, they are more than once called Gods, because the Deity is in some measure represented to us in their service, as in a mirror. I am rather inclined, however, to agree with ancient writers, that in those passages wherein it is stated that the angel of the Lord appeared to Abraham, Jacob, and Moses, Christ was that angel. Still it is true, that when mention is made of all the angels, they are frequently so designated. Nor ought this to seem strange. For if princes and rulers have this honour given them, because in their office they are vicegerents of God, the supreme King and Judge, with far greater reason may it be given to angels, in whom the brightness of the divine glory is much more conspicuously displayed.

2 — Structure at a Glance – 

[THESIS]     S1   Angels = heavenly spirits; God uses their obedience; "angel" = messenger
[PRINCIPLE]  S2   Their various names all point to one office
[NAMES 1]    S3   Hosts: court, soldiers, eyes on the standard, ready at the nod
[PROOF]      S4   Daniel 7:10 — thousands upon thousands ministering before the throne
[NAMES 2]    S5–S6  Virtues (power displayed); Principalities / Powers / Dominions (government)
[NAMES 3]    S7–S8  Thrones (glory dwelling in them) — Calvin hesitates; the rest commend the dignity of service
[HONOUR]     S9–S10  Don't pass by the instruments of God's power; they are even called Gods (mirror)
[CASE STUDY] S11–S12  The Angel of the Lord = Christ (inclined) — yet "angel" also means the created angels
[A FORTIORI] S13–S14  Not strange: if rulers are called gods as vicegerents, how much more angels

Examiner's Eye. - ⚠️ Scope overreach (Thrones): Calvin lists Thrones but is unwilling to speak positively (S7). Any option saying "Calvin teaches that angels are Thrones because God's glory dwells in them" is wrong. - ⚠️ Scope overreach (Christ): S11 is "rather inclined … to agree with ancient writers" — a preference, not a dogma — and S12 immediately adds that "angel of the Lord" is frequently said of angels generally. - ⚠️ Direction-flip (worship): S10 says angels are called Gods "because the Deity is … represented to us in their service, as in a mirror." That is a mirror claim, not a worship claim. (Contrast the Manichee and Colossian error already refused in §3.) - ⚠️ Contrast with yesterday: §4 refused to arrange the angels in ranks. §5 uses the same names but reads them as functions of one office (S2). Calvin does not contradict himself; he disciplines the names by Scripture.

4 — Today's Grammar Formulas –

Formula 1 — Relative clause with fronted object (whose N S V) (S1, S10, S14)

N, whose X S V   =   N, and S V N’s X
heavenly spirits, whose obedience and ministry God employs   =   God employs the spirits’ obedience and ministry

⚠️ Trap. The word after whose N is not the verb of N — it is the subject (God) followed by the verb (employs). Always ask: who does the verb? Same for S14: angels, in whom the brightness … is displayed — prepositional relative, subject brightness. Drill. The Lord uses the obedience of angels. → angels, whose obedience the Lord uses to execute his decrees.

Formula 2 — so ADJ … that result + as-fronted reason + or rather (S3, S5, S6, S7)

S V so ADJ (to V) that S V        ⇒ result ("so quick to obey that at a nod they are at work")
As [reason], S V                  ⇒ "Since [reason], S V"  (NOT "while")
X, or rather Y                    ⇒ self-correction; Y is the endorsed wording

⚠️ Trap. the moment he gives the nod works as a conjunction (as soon as). The result clause can hide a second clause before its own that. In S7 the as-clause is inside a though clause — track which as belongs to what. Drill. The angels obey so quickly. At God’s nod they are already working. → The angels are so prompt to obey that, the moment God nods, they are, or rather already were, at work.

Formula 3 — Inversion after nor and fronted degree adverbials (S9, S13, S14)

Nor + aux + S + V        ⇒   not … either / and it is also not the case that
Nor is it right to pass by …        =   It is also not right to pass by …
With far greater reason may it be given to angels   =   It may be given to angels with far greater reason

⚠️ Trap. In inverted order the auxiliary (is / ought / may) comes before the subject; the main verb stays in the participle or infinitive (be given, seem). Don’t read Nor ought this as a noun phrase. Drill. We must not neglect the ministers of God’s power. It must not seem strange that they bear titles. → Nor must we neglect the ministers of God’s power, nor ought it to seem strange that they bear titles.

6 — Background in 5 Minutes –

The doctrinal stakes. Section 5 begins Calvin’s positive angelology after §4’s negative rules. The architecture of the chapter (see Beveridge’s own outline at the head of Ch. 14): angels considered (§§3–13); §5 gives nature, offices, and various names; §6 will give function (dispensers of God’s bounty to us); §§7–8 guardianship and numbers. So §5 is the foundation: what angels are and what their titles mean.

The target (unnamed). Pseudo-Dionysius’s Celestial Hierarchy (c. 500) arranged angels in three triads — Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones; Dominions, Virtues, Powers; Principalities, Archangels, Angels — and made them a ladder of mediation toward God. Calvin (in §4) calls it “idle talk.” In §5 he uses the same Pauline names (Col 1:16; Eph 1:21) but reads them as titles of one ministry. Reception: the Scholastics (Aquinas, ST I q.108) kept the Dionysian ladder; Reformed theology followed Calvin’s restraint; Barth’s angelology (CD III/3 §51) still counts the Reformers’ caution about angelic metaphysics but keeps the Biblical witness.

The “Angel of the Lord.” Calvin’s “rather inclined” stands in the long Christological reading of theophanies (Justin, Irenaeus) that Reformed exegesis largely retained. Later interpreters differ on individual passages. Guardrail: do not turn S11 into a universal rule — S12 limits it immediately, and Calvin’s own phrase is “rather inclined.”

Why this matters pastorally. Calvin will not let angelology become either speculation (§4) or a replacement mediator (§3). The names’ point is service and dignity — which sets up §6, where the angels’ care for us is "the point on which the Scriptures specially insist" (Heb 1:14).

7 — Scripture Connections –

  1. S1 — Heb 1:14. “Are they not all ministering spirits, sent forth to minister …?” Calvin’s angelology in one verse: spirits (nature) + ministering (office). Gk angelos = messenger.
  2. S1 — Ps 103:20–21. “Bless the Lord, ye his angels … that do his commandments, hearkening unto the voice of his word … ye ministers of his, that do his pleasure.” The model for obedience (S3).
  3. S3 — Ps 148:2; 1 Kgs 22:19; Lk 2:13; Josh 5:14. “Praise ye him, all his angels … all his hosts”; “host of heaven standing by him”; “multitude of the heavenly host”; “captain of the host of the Lord.” The military-court imagery.
  4. S4 — Dan 7:10. Quoted directly: “thousand thousands ministered unto him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him.” Calvin’s source for multitude and court.
  5. S5–S6 — Eph 1:21; Col 1:16. Principality, power, might, dominion; thrones, dominions, principalities, powers. Cited by Calvin; Paul lists, does not rank.
  6. S10 — Ps 8:5; 97:7; 138:1 (LXX); Ps 82:6; Jn 10:34–35. Heb. ’elohim used for heavenly beings and rulers. Calvin: the title is representative, not essential.
  7. S11 — Gen 16:7–13; 22:11–15; 31:11–13; Ex 3:2–6; Ex 23:20–21. The Angel of the Lord speaks as God. Calvin: rather inclined to read these Christologically.
  8. S14 — Ps 82:6; Ex 22:8–9. Rulers called ’elohim as God’s deputies; the a fortiori base-case.

How Calvin uses Scripture here: he lets the text’s own words (names, quotations) define angelic dignity, and he marks his own certainty (positive / inclined / unwilling to speak positively) — an exegete who labels his confidence level.

8 — Exam Problems (출제자의 눈) –

① Grammar — one underlined part is wrong. Which?

Calvin says that angels, (A) whose service God employs, are (B) so prompt as the moment he nods they act; (C) nor ought this to seem strange; and (D) with far greater reason may the title be given to them.

Answer: (B). Rationale: the result structure is so ADJ that S V, not so ADJ as …. Correct: are so prompt that the moment he nods they act. (A) fronted-object relative (Formula 1) is correct; (C) and (D) are correct negative / degree inversions (Formula 3).

② Comprehension — which statement is consistent with §5?

(A) Calvin concludes that the name Thrones proves God’s glory dwells in angels as in a temple. (B) Calvin holds that since angels are called Gods, they may be worshipped in their own right. (C) The various names of angels commend the dignity of their service to God, and being called Gods is explained by the Deity being represented in that service, as in a mirror. (D) Calvin teaches that every appearance of “the angel of the Lord” in Scripture was Christ.

Answer: (C). Rationale: (A) scope overreach — S7: “unwilling to speak positively.” (B) direction-flip — S10 explains the title by mirror/representation; §3 already rejected angel-worship. (D) scope overreach — S11 is “rather inclined,” and S12 says “angel” is also frequently said of all the angels.

③ Composition — use Formula 3 (and Formula 2 if you can).

Compose one English sentence (≈20 words): It is also wrong to ignore those through whom God shows his power. It also should not seem strange that they carry honorific titles.

Model answer: Nor is it right to pass by unhonoured those instruments through whom God shows his power, nor ought it to seem strange that they bear honorific titles. Examiner’s intent: double Nor inversion (aux before subject). Alternative accepted: With far greater reason may such titles be given to angels, since they are so prompt that the moment God nods they are at work. (Formula 3 + Formula 2.)

9 — One-Line Wrap-up + Homework –

One-line summary: Scripture presents angels as heavenly spirits whom God employs to carry out his decrees and to make his will known, and every title they bear — hosts, virtues, principalities, powers, dominions, even thrones and “gods” — commends the dignity of that one service; Calvin keeps the names but refuses to turn them into a ladder of ranks and marks his own certainty at each point. Three grammar points: ① whose N S V relative with fronted object ② so ADJ … that result / fronted as reason / or rather self-correction ③ Nor + aux + S and fronted-degree inversion (a fortiori).
